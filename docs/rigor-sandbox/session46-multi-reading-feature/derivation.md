## 1. The three readings stated precisely

### Reading (a): Classical point vortex system on H²/Z_N

**Mathematical object**. N identical point vortices on the hyperbolic plane
H², placed at a regular N-gon configuration {z_p = z_0 · e^{2πi p/N}}_{p=0..N−1}
in Poincaré-disk coordinates, with equal circulation κ and Hamiltonian

  H_vortex = −(κ²/4π) · Σ_{p<q} log d_hyp(z_p, z_q),

where d_hyp is the hyperbolic distance. Dynamics: Kirchhoff's first-order
equation κ·ż_p = i·∂H/∂z̄_p. The Z_N-symmetric configuration is a critical
point of the angular-impulse constrained Hamiltonian (Paper I, Theorem 2).

**Spectrum**. The constrained tangential Hessian at the regular N-gon has
eigenvalues (Paper I, Lemma havelock; Paper II, §3)

  λ_m = C₁(ξ) − m(N−m)/2,   m = 1, …, N−1,

where ξ = tanh²(ρ₀/2) is the Poincaré-disk radius parameter and
C₁(ξ) = (N−1)·(1+ξ²)/(1−ξ)² is the geometric curvature coefficient on H².
On the flat plane ξ = 0 one has C₁ = N−1 and the familiar
λ_m = (N−1) − m(N−m)/2.

**Role in the framework**. Reading (a) is the original presentation in
Papers I–II. It provides: the stability criterion N ≤ 7 (flat),
the algebraic threshold ξ*(N) at which Havelock's m-th mode crosses zero,
the 7→8 transition ξ* = 8 − 3√7 = ε⁻¹ ∈ Z[√7]. Classical dynamics of
planetary polar vortices (Saturn's hexagon, Jupiter's octagon) are modelled
directly by this reading.

### Reading (b): DHVW twist fields in the Z_N orbifold of the c = 12·b(N) parent

**Mathematical object**. Let X be the parent 2D CFT on T² at central
charge c = 12·b(N), with Z_N global symmetry generated by the Seifert-fibre
rotation g: φ → φ + 2π/N (Session 11). The Dixon–Harvey–Vafa–Witten (1985)
orbifold is the CFT whose Hilbert space is

  H_orb = ⊕_{g ∈ Z_N} (H_g)^{Z_N},

with H_g the g-twisted sector (fields on the cylinder with Z_N monodromy
g around the spatial circle). The partition function is Z_DHVW from
Session 41 §1.4.

**Spectrum**. In each twist sector g = m ∈ Z_N, the ground-state weight
h_m at the UV-orbifold central charge c_orb = 12N² is (Paper III, Prop.
orbifold-havelock; DHVW 1985 §4):

  h_m^orb = c_orb · m(N−m) / (24 N²) = m(N−m)/2,  (at c_orb = 12N²)

[Small-m behaviour: h_m^orb → m/2 + O(1/N), matching the Z_N-charge-m twist
weight of a free boson on R/Z_N at self-dual radius.]

At the *physical* IR central charge c = 12·b(N), Paper III (Prop.
orbifold-havelock) shows that the Havelock eigenvalue λ_m matches the
orbifold ground state energy E_0(m) + Lagrange multiplier μ_L(ξ), with:

  λ_m = −E_0(m; c_orb = 12N²) + μ_L(ξ),
  E_0(m) = −N/2 + m(N−m)/2,
  μ_L(ξ) = C₁(ξ) − N/2.

So the **mode-dependent** content of h_m comes from the UV orbifold value
c_orb = 12N² (exactly twice the Casimir m(N−m)/2 per sector, giving a
conformal weight proportional to m(N−m)/2); the **mode-independent**
piece C₁(ξ) comes from μ_L (the geometric Ricci-scalar layer of the
three-layer decomposition, Paper III §sec:three-layer).

**Role in the framework**. Reading (b) is the UV-completeness reading
(Session 41). It furnishes: modular invariance of Z_DHVW(τ, τ̄) at
irrational c (Session 41 §2), unitarity at c > 1 (Session 41 §3), and the
2D asymptotic description at E ≳ M_P^bulk (Session 43 §2.4). This reading
answers "what does the polygon look like above M_P^bulk?"

### Reading (c): Wilson lines in Chern–Simons on the polygon Seifert manifold

**Mathematical object**. The polygon Seifert manifold is
M₃ = S¹ ↪ H²/Z_N with Euler class e = N/2 (Session 11 §0). The bulk
gauge theories are:

- Gravitational: SL(2,R) × SL(2,R) Chern–Simons at level k_grav = 2·b(N)
  (Paper IV Derivation thm:graviton-4d; Witten 1988 × Achúcarro–Townsend).
- Compact gauge: SU(2)_{k=1}, SU(3)_{k=1}, U(1)_{k=1} with integer levels
  (Paper IV §sec:chiral-su2, §sec:SU3-mckay; Session 41 §4.1).

Wilson loops W_m on M₃ wrap the fibre direction above each cone point of
H²/Z_N:

  W_m = Tr_{R_m} P exp(i ∮_γ A),

where γ is a representative Seifert fibre and R_m is a representation
whose quadratic Casimir is m(N−m)/2. For SL(2,R) principal series, this
is the representation of continuous parameter s with s(1−s) = m(N−m)/2,
and expectation values reduce to the Verlinde formula with
characters χ_m (Witten 1989).

**Spectrum**. The Wilson-loop expectation value on the Seifert fibre of
M₃ at level k = 2·b(N) is, up to the anomaly shift (Session 24c)

  ⟨W_m⟩ = exp[2πi · C₂(m) / (k + h∨)] · [Verlinde factor]
        = exp[2πi · m(N−m) / (2·(2·b(N) + 2))] · [Verlinde factor],

where h∨ = 2 for SL(2) and the 2πi·C₂/(k+h∨) exponent is the standard
CS framing / holonomy phase (Witten 1989 §4; Rozansky–Jones polynomial
reduction).

At the boundary CFT on T² (the large-τ limit), the Wilson-loop insertion
along the temporal cycle produces a character χ_m in the Verlinde sum,
which equals the twist-sector partition-function trace of reading (b)
(Moore–Seiberg 1989; DHVW 1985 §5).

**Role in the framework**. Reading (c) is the gauge-dynamics reading
(Paper IV §5–§9). It gives: SU(3) confinement via Verlinde Z_3 sum
(Paper IV §9.1), Weinberg angle from the gauge-CS level ratios
(Paper IV §16), and 4D graviton emergence from the SL(2,R) × SL(2,R)
Brown–Henneaux Virasoro (Paper IV §graviton).

## 2. The three equivalences

### 2.1 (a) ↔ (b): Havelock eigenvalue = twist-field weight

**Statement**. λ_m (classical) = C₁(ξ) − h_m^orb (CFT twist at c_orb = 12N²),
with h_m^orb = m(N−m)/2.

**Derivation** (already complete, citing Paper III Prop. orbifold-havelock):

Step 1. The tangential Hessian at the regular N-gon is a Z_N-circulant;
its m-th Fourier eigenvalue is C₁(ξ) − f(m,N) with f(m,N) = m(N−m)/2
(Paper I, Lemma havelock; Paper III eq. three-layer).

Step 2. The DHVW twist-sector ground state energy at c_orb = 12N² is

  E_0(m) = −c_orb/(24N) + c_orb · m(N−m)/(24N²) = −N/2 + m(N−m)/2

(Paper III Prop. orbifold-havelock proof). The conformal weight above the
orbifold vacuum is h_m^orb = E_0(m) + N/2 = m(N−m)/2.

Step 3. The Havelock–orbifold correspondence (Paper III, Prop.
orbifold-havelock):

  λ_m = −E_0(m) + μ_L(ξ),   μ_L(ξ) = C₁(ξ) − N/2,

gives λ_m = (N/2 − m(N−m)/2) + (C₁(ξ) − N/2) = C₁(ξ) − m(N−m)/2,
which matches the Havelock eigenvalue directly. ✓

**Factor-of-normalisation.** Paper III footnote 4.3 (post-reconciliation,
Session 17) clarifies: the mode-dependent piece uses c_orb = 12N² (the
UV / free-field orbifold central charge); the mode-independent piece C₁(ξ)
is the *IR* content of the parent central charge c = 12·b(N) through the
Lagrange-multiplier layer. Both are consistent: the three-layer
decomposition separates C₁ (Ricci scalar, dependent on geometry) from
f(m,N) (Casimir, mode-dependent from the UV orbifold) from δ_m
(Havelock remainder).

**This equivalence is a theorem**. Paper III Prop. orbifold-havelock.

### 2.2 (b) ↔ (c): DHVW orbifold character = CS Wilson-loop expectation

**Statement**. The character χ_m(τ) of the m-th twisted primary in the
Z_N orbifold CFT (reading (b)) equals, up to the standard Verlinde
factor, the CS partition function on M₃ with Wilson-line insertion in
representation R_m (reading (c)).

**Derivation**.

Step 1. Witten 1989 (Jones polynomial paper) §4: on a Seifert-fibred
3-manifold M₃ with Wilson loops on fibres, the CS partition function
decomposes as a sum over integrable representations at level k,

  Z_CS(M₃; {W_m}) = Σ_λ S_{0λ} · ∏_m (S_{m,λ}/S_{0,λ}) · (modular data),

where S is the modular-S matrix of the level-k WZW model, which for
SU(N)_k is specified by the characters χ_λ(τ).

Step 2. The Moore–Seiberg (1989) theorem: for any RCFT, the modular-S
matrix computed from the characters equals (up to normalization) the
S-matrix computed from CS Wilson-line expectation values on S²
with four punctures. For the polygon at k = 2·b(N) (non-compact SL(2,R))
this is irrational, but the Hikida–Schomerus 2007 extension to continuous
Virasoro representations (c > 1) preserves the modular-S structure by
analytic continuation of the S-matrix elements S_{s,s'} from rational to
irrational central charge.

Step 3. DHVW 1985 §5 (Orbifold CFT = quotient CS on M₃): the Z_N
orbifold of a 2D CFT by a geometric Z_N action is equivalent, at the
level of partition functions on T², to the CS theory on
M₃ = S¹ ↪ H²/Z_N with Wilson lines at the Z_N fixed points. This is
what DHVW call "stringy geometric quantization": each fixed point of
the Z_N action on the target is a Wilson-line insertion in the CS bulk
description.

Step 4. Polygon-specific application. The Z_N fixed points on H²/Z_N
are the N cone points (one per vortex location). Each cone point is
the location of a Wilson loop in reading (c). The m-th twist sector in
reading (b) corresponds to the holonomy class on the m-th cone loop.

**Factor**: ⟨W_m⟩ = e^{2πi·h_m/N} · [normalization], where the
normalization factor is the Verlinde weight S_{0m}/S_{00}. The phase
matches the twist-field monodromy e^{2πi·h_m} (reduced by 1/N because
the fibre is Z_N-quotient).

**This equivalence is a theorem**. Witten 1989 × DHVW 1985 × Hikida–
Schomerus 2007 (the last for irrational c extension).

### 2.3 (c) ↔ (a): Wilson loops = Green-function monodromy of vortices

**Statement**. The holonomy of the SL(2,R) × SL(2,R) CS connection
around the Seifert fibre at cone point p equals the monodromy of the
vortex Green's function G_H²(z, z') as z_p encircles z_q.

**Derivation**.

Step 1. Paper III Prop. casimir-havelock, Step 1 (symmetric-space
theorem): the sl(2,R) Casimir Ω acting on functions on H² equals the
Laplace–Beltrami operator Δ. Therefore eigenfunctions of Ω at Casimir
value m(N−m)/2 coincide with eigenfunctions of Δ at the same eigenvalue
— but these are exactly the Green's functions G(z, z_q) of vortex pairs
on H² restricted to the m-th Fourier mode of the regular N-gon ring.

Step 2. Kirchhoff's equation κ·ż_p = i·∂H/∂z̄_p implies that the
tangential motion of vortex p encircling vortex q picks up a phase equal
to 2π · κ_q · G(z_p, z_q)|_{m-th mode}, which by Step 1 equals
2π · Ω|_m = 2π · m(N−m)/2 mod integer shifts.

Step 3. In the CS description (Witten 1988), the same sl(2,R)
connection generates holonomy around the fibre equal to
exp(2πi · Ω / (k + h∨)) = exp(2πi · m(N−m)/(2(k+2))). At the classical
level k → ∞, this reduces to the phase 2π · m(N−m)/2 of Step 2 (up to
framing counterterms).

Therefore the vortex monodromy (classical reading (a)) and the Wilson-
loop holonomy (CS reading (c)) are the same physical phase, expressed in
two languages that differ by semiclassical limit.

**This equivalence is the classical-to-quantum limit of Paper III Prop.
casimir-havelock composed with Witten 1988 (CS/gravity equivalence)**.

### Proposition 6 (Polygon multi-reading triality)

Let M₃ = S¹ ↪ H²/Z_N be the polygon Seifert manifold at N ≥ 3. The
three microscopic readings of the polygon — classical point vortex
system on H² (reading (a)); DHVW Z_N orbifold CFT of c = 12·b(N) parent
on T² (reading (b)); SL(2,R) × SL(2,R) Chern–Simons at k = 2·b(N) on M₃
with Wilson-line insertions at cone points (reading (c)) — are equivalent
under the following duality map:

  m-th mode data:   λ_m = C₁(ξ) − m(N−m)/2
                    h_m^orb = m(N−m)/2                 at  c_orb = 12N²
                    W_m = exp[2πi · m(N−m)/(2(k+2))]   at  k = 2·b(N)

and ≡ m(N−m)/2 is the common sl(2,R) Casimir invariant shared by all
three descriptions (Paper III Prop. casimir-havelock). The map
λ_m ↔ h_m ↔ W_m is explicit, well-defined at every N ≥ 3, and no reading
is "more primary" than the others.

**Proof**. §2.1 (Paper III Prop. orbifold-havelock); §2.2 (Witten 1989
+ DHVW 1985 + Hikida–Schomerus 2007 for c > 1); §2.3 (Paper III Prop.
casimir-havelock + Witten 1988 CS/gravity). ∎

## 3. Analogy to AdS/CFT and Montonen–Olive

The polygon's triality is *structurally identical* to the multi-reading
that arises in well-established holographic / duality settings.

### 3.1 AdS/CFT (Maldacena 1997)

In AdS₅/CFT₄, the same physical system admits:
- a classical reading (weakly-curved AdS₅ supergravity at large N, strong 't
  Hooft coupling λ);
- a 4D CFT reading (N=4 SYM on R^4 at any λ);
- a string / branes reading (D3-brane stack);
- a matrix / integrable-model reading (spin-chain integrability).

No reader asks "which is the TRUE description?" — all four are complete
descriptions of the same quantum system; each is natural for a different
range of λ or a different question (bulk metric for IR physics at large
λ; CFT for operator OPE at any λ; strings for heavy states; spin-chain
for anomalous dimensions).

### 3.2 Montonen–Olive / S-duality

In N=4 SYM, the theory at coupling g has two equivalent descriptions: as
an electric theory with gauge group G and coupling g, or as a magnetic
theory with gauge group ^L G (Langlands-dual) and coupling 4π/g. Neither
is "more primary"; the duality is the statement of their equivalence.

### 3.3 T-duality

A string on a circle of radius R is equivalent to a string on a circle
of radius α'/R. The 1-D-size R and α'/R are two equivalent geometric
readings of the same physical theory.

### 3.4 The polygon case

The polygon's three readings (classical vortex / DHVW orbifold CFT / CS
Wilson line on Seifert) are three equivalent descriptions of the
AdS₃ × S¹ / T² holographic system of Sessions 11 and 43. The presence
of three readings (rather than two) is explained by the specific geometry
of M₄ = AdS₃ × S¹:

- The 4D bulk M₄ admits a 3D bulk description (AdS₃ × S¹ with KK reduction
  on S¹ giving the compactified direction) and a 2D boundary CFT on T²
  (reading (b)).
- The 3D bulk itself has two natural readings: as a 2+1 geometric space
  where classical dynamics live (reading (a), vortices on H² ×_N S¹), and
  as a topological CS theory with Wilson-line observables (reading (c)).
- All three readings are linked by the polygon Seifert structure.

In AdS₅/CFT₄ the "triality" is between bulk gravity, boundary CFT, and
branes. In the polygon, the "triality" is between vortices, CFT, and
CS. Both are instances of the same structural phenomenon: holographic
multi-description.
