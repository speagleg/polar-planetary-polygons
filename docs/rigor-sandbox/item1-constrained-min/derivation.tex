\section*{Main result}

\begin{theorem}[Havelock Lagrangian eigenvalues]\label{thm:havelock}
On the unit ring $z_k = e^{2\pi i k / N}$, $k = 0, \ldots, N - 1$, of
unit-circulation point vortices with energy
$H = -\sum_{j<k} \ln|z_j - z_k|$, the Lagrangian Hessian
$\nabla^2 \mathcal{L} = \nabla^2 H - 2\mu_L I$ with
Lagrange multiplier $\mu_L = -(N - 1)/4$ is block-diagonal in the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier basis. At each mode
$m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$ the $2 \times 2$ block in the normalized
(radial, tangential) basis has vanishing off-diagonal entry and
eigenvalues
\[
  \lambda_m^+ = (N - 1) - \frac{m(N-m)}{2},
  \qquad
  \lambda_m^- = \frac{m(N-m)}{2}.
\]
In particular, $\lambda_m^+ + \lambda_m^- = N - 1$ is mode-independent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:offdiag} (off-diagonal vanishing), the mode-$m$
block is diagonal, so its eigenvalues are the two diagonal entries.
The radial-radial entry is computed in \S\ref{sec:rr},
equation~\eqref{eq:rr-unshifted}: $(\nabla^2 H)_{rr}^{(m)}
= (N - 1 - m(N-m))/2$, built from the Havelock identity
(Lemma~\ref{lem:havelock}) and the cosecant-squared sum
(Lemma~\ref{lem:csc}, Corollary~\ref{cor:cos-over-sin}).
The tangential-tangential entry is computed in \S\ref{sec:tt},
equation~\eqref{eq:tt-unshifted}: $(\nabla^2 H)_{tt}^{(m)}
= (m(N-m) - (N-1))/2 = C(1) - C(m)$. The Lagrange shift
$(N-1)/2$ applied to each diagonal entry (\S\ref{sec:lag-shift})
yields \eqref{eq:final-eigs}. The trace identity
(Lemma~\ref{lem:trace}) then follows by direct addition.
\end{proof}

The remainder of this document gives the derivation in full, each step
verified numerically against the definition-based oracle in
\texttt{numerical\_check.py} (symbolic identities to 30 decimal digits;
oracle matches the full-diagonalization Hessian to $10^{-2}$,
finite-difference precision) for $N \in \{3, \ldots, 12\}$ and
$m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$.

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

Let $z_k = e^{i\theta_k}$, $\theta_k = 2\pi k / N$, $k = 0, \ldots, N-1$,
be the regular $N$-gon of unit-circulation point vortices on the unit
circle. The Thomson energy is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thomson}
  H = -\sum_{j<k} \ln |z_j - z_k|.
\end{equation}
We perturb each vortex in its \emph{local} (radial, tangential) frame:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perturb}
  z_k \;\longmapsto\; z_k\bigl(1 + \delta r_k\bigr) + i\,z_k\,\delta t_k,
\end{equation}
with $\delta r_k, \delta t_k \in \mathbb{R}$ small.

\paragraph{$\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariance.}
The cyclic action $\phi : k \mapsto k + 1 \pmod N$ on vortex labels
is a symmetry of the equilibrium ring and of $H$: relabeling vortices
permutes the pair sum \eqref{eq:thomson} invariantly. Under $\phi$
the perturbation data transforms as $(\delta r_k, \delta t_k) \mapsto
(\delta r_{k-1}, \delta t_{k-1})$, because the \emph{local} radial
and tangential directions at vertex $k + 1$ are the images of the
corresponding directions at vertex $k$ under the Euclidean rotation
by $2\pi/N$ that maps $z_k \mapsto z_{k+1}$. The Hessian $\nabla^2 H$
is therefore $\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariant on the $2N$-dimensional
perturbation space $\mathbb{R}^{2N} \cong \mathbb{R}^N \oplus \mathbb{R}^N$
(radial $\oplus$ tangential). By the standard finite-group
representation theory, $\nabla^2 H$ block-diagonalizes in the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ isotypic components, indexed by
$m \in \{0, 1, \ldots, N-1\}$.

\paragraph{Real Fourier modes.}
The $\mathbb{Z}_N$-irreducibles over $\mathbb{R}$ are two copies of
the trivial rep at $m = 0$, two copies of the sign rep at $m = N/2$
(even $N$ only), and for
$m \in \{1, \ldots, \lfloor (N-1)/2 \rfloor\}$ a paired complex
representation $(m, N-m)$ of real dimension $4$ per pair, spanned by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-m}
  \delta r_k = a_c\cos\!\tfrac{2\pi m k}{N} + a_s\sin\!\tfrac{2\pi m k}{N},
  \quad
  \delta t_k = b_c\cos\!\tfrac{2\pi m k}{N} + b_s\sin\!\tfrac{2\pi m k}{N}.
\end{equation}
So at each $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$ with $m \ne N/2$, the Hessian
restricts to a $4 \times 4$ block in the $(a_c, a_s, b_c, b_s)$ basis.

\begin{lemma}[Cos-sin equivalence]\label{lem:cos-sin}
At every $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$ with $m \ne N/2$, the
$4 \times 4$ Hessian block decomposes as two identical $2 \times 2$
blocks, one on $(a_c, b_c)$ and one on $(a_s, b_s)$, and it suffices
to compute the cosine sub-block.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The SO(2) rotation $R_\beta : z_k \mapsto e^{i\beta} z_k$ (rigid rotation
of the ring) is a symmetry of $H$ and preserves the equilibrium for
every $\beta$. It acts on mode-$m$ amplitudes by
$(a_c + i a_s) \mapsto e^{-i\beta m}(a_c + i a_s)$ (and similarly on
$(b_c, b_s)$), because the cos/sin mode at $m$ is the real/imaginary
part of $e^{2\pi i m k / N}$, and a rotation by $\beta$ shifts the
vertex angles by $\beta$. In particular, the rotation $R_{\pi/(2m)}$
maps $(a_c, a_s) \to (a_s, -a_c)$ and $(b_c, b_s) \to (b_s, -b_c)$.
Since $R_{\pi/(2m)}$ is a symmetry of $\nabla^2 H$, the $4 \times 4$
block commutes with this swap, so its $(a_c, b_c)$ and $(a_s, b_s)$
$2 \times 2$ sub-blocks are equal, and any $(a_c, b_s)$ or $(a_s, b_c)$
cross entries are zero. Palindromic symmetry $m \leftrightarrow N - m$
is the complex conjugate of this statement.
\end{proof}

The mode-$m$ cosine sub-block — which is what we compute below — is
therefore sufficient. In the notation we use throughout,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cos-block}
  \delta r_k = a\,\cos(2\pi m k / N), \qquad
  \delta t_k = b\,\cos(2\pi m k / N),
\end{equation}
parametrized by $(a, b)$, which yields the $2 \times 2$ block
computed in \S\ref{sec:rr}--\S\ref{sec:lag-shift}.

\paragraph{The case $m = N/2$ (even $N$).}
When $N$ is even and $m = N/2$, the paired representation degenerates
to a single real dimension per vertex direction
($\sin(2\pi \cdot N/2 \cdot k / N) = \sin(\pi k) = 0$, so only the
cosine mode exists). In this case $\alpha_k := \cos(\pi k) = (-1)^k$,
so $\sum_k \alpha_k^2 = N$ (not $N/2$) and the normalization of the
mode vectors doubles. However, the elementary Fourier sums also
double correspondingly ($\sum_j \alpha_j \alpha_{j+p} = N (-1)^p
= N \cos(\pi p)$, twice the generic formula $(N/2) \cos(2\pi m p / N)$
evaluated at $m = N/2$), and the two factors of two cancel in the
normalized Hessian entry. The final eigenvalue formula thus extends
uniformly to $m = N/2$; we record this explicitly as
Remark~\ref{rem:m-half} after the main derivation.

\section{Two auxiliary identities}\label{sec:aux}

Two closed-form trigonometric sums enter the block computation and are
recorded here. Both are verified to 30 decimal digits in
\texttt{numerical\_check.py} (\texttt{check\_havelock\_identity\_rr},
\texttt{check\_cosecant\_squared\_sum}).

\begin{lemma}[Havelock identity]\label{lem:havelock}
For all integers $N \ge 2$ and $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:havelock}
  S(m) \;:=\; \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \frac{1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)}{4 \sin^2(\pi p / N)}
  \;=\; \frac{m(N-m)}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Using $1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N) = 2 \sin^2(\pi m p / N)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sm-sin-form}
  S(m) = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \frac{\sin^2(\pi m p / N)}{2 \sin^2(\pi p / N)}.
\end{equation}
We derive the identity
$\sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \sin^2(\pi m p / N) / \sin^2(\pi p / N) = m(N - m)$
from first principles. Write the telescoping geometric sum
\[
  e^{-i(m-1)\pi p/N} + e^{-i(m-3)\pi p/N} + \cdots + e^{i(m-1)\pi p/N}
   = \sum_{k=0}^{m-1} e^{i(2k - m + 1)\pi p / N}
   = \frac{e^{i m \pi p / N} - e^{-i m \pi p / N}}{e^{i \pi p / N} - e^{-i \pi p / N}}
   = \frac{\sin(\pi m p / N)}{\sin(\pi p / N)}.
\]
Squaring the modulus,
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\sin^2(\pi m p / N)}{\sin^2(\pi p / N)}
  &= \sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1} e^{i(2k - 2\ell)\pi p / N}
   = \sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1} e^{2\pi i (k-\ell) p / N}.
\end{align*}
Summing over $p = 1, \ldots, N-1$ and using the orthogonality
$\sum_{p=0}^{N-1} e^{2\pi i q p / N} = N\,\mathbf{1}\{q \equiv 0\!\!\pmod N\}$
(hence $\sum_{p=1}^{N-1} e^{2\pi i q p / N} = -1 + N\,\mathbf{1}\{q \equiv 0\}$),
\[
  \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\frac{\sin^2(\pi m p / N)}{\sin^2(\pi p / N)}
  = \sum_{k, \ell = 0}^{m-1}\bigl(N\,\mathbf{1}\{k \equiv \ell\!\!\pmod N\} - 1\bigr)
  = N \cdot m - m^2 = m(N - m),
\]
since for $k, \ell \in \{0, \ldots, m-1\}$ with $m \le N - 1$, the
condition $k \equiv \ell \pmod N$ is equivalent to $k = \ell$, giving
$m$ diagonal contributions each of value $N$, minus $m^2$ constant
$-1$'s. Dividing by $2$ gives $S(m) = m(N-m)/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cosecant-squared sum]\label{lem:csc}
For $N \ge 2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:csc}
  \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \frac{1}{4 \sin^2(\pi p / N)}
     \;=\; \frac{N^2 - 1}{12}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Sum Lemma~\ref{lem:havelock} over $m = 1, \ldots, N - 1$:
\[
  \sum_{m=1}^{N-1} S(m)
   = \sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\frac{m(N-m)}{2}
   = \frac{1}{2}\bigl[N \cdot {\textstyle\frac{N(N-1)}{2}} - {\textstyle\frac{(N-1)N(2N-1)}{6}}\bigr]
   = \frac{(N-1)N(N+1)}{12}.
\]
On the other hand, interchanging summation order in the definition of $S(m)$
and using $\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)) = (N - 1) - (-1) = N$
for each fixed $p \in \{1, \ldots, N - 1\}$ (since
$\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\cos(2\pi m p / N) = 0$ and the $m = 0$ term contributes $1$),
\[
  \sum_{m=1}^{N-1} S(m)
   = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N))}{4\sin^2(\pi p / N)}
   = N \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{4\sin^2(\pi p / N)}.
\]
Equating the two expressions and dividing by $N$ gives
$\sum_{p=1}^{N-1} 1/(4\sin^2(\pi p / N)) = (N^2 - 1)/12$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cosine-weighted inverse-sine sum]\label{cor:cos-over-sin}
For $N \ge 2$ and any integer $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cos-over-sin}
  C(k) \;:=\; \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \frac{\cos(2\pi k p / N)}{4 \sin^2(\pi p / N)}
       \;=\; \frac{N^2 - 1}{12} - \frac{k(N-k)}{2}
       \quad \text{for } 0 \le k \le N.
\end{equation}
The expression is periodic in $k$ modulo $N$, i.e.\ $C(k + N) = C(k)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Subtract \eqref{eq:havelock} from \eqref{eq:csc}:
$C(k) = (N^2-1)/12 - S(k) = (N^2-1)/12 - k(N-k)/2$.
Periodicity is immediate from the integrand.
\end{proof}

\section{Radial-radial block}\label{sec:rr}

Under a purely radial mode-$m$ perturbation
$\delta r_k = a\,\alpha_k$ with $\alpha_k = \cos(2\pi m k / N)$
(and $b = 0$ in \eqref{eq:mode-m}), the squared pair distance expands as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-expand}
  |z_j(a) - z_k(a)|^2
  = |z_j - z_k|^2 \bigl[1 + a(\alpha_j + \alpha_k)\bigr]
    + a^2\,Q_{jk} + O(a^3),
\end{equation}
with
\[
  Q_{jk} := \alpha_j^2 + \alpha_k^2
            - 2 \alpha_j \alpha_k \cos(\theta_j - \theta_k),
  \qquad
  |z_j - z_k|^2 = 4 \sin^2\!\bigl(\pi(j-k)/N\bigr).
\]
(To see this, write $z_k(a) = z_k(1 + a\alpha_k)$, compute
$|z_j(a) - z_k(a)|^2 = |z_j - z_k|^2 + 2 a \operatorname{Re}\bigl[\overline{(z_j-z_k)}(\alpha_j z_j - \alpha_k z_k)\bigr] + a^2 |\alpha_j z_j - \alpha_k z_k|^2$,
and simplify the real part using
$\operatorname{Re}[(e^{-i\theta_j} - e^{-i\theta_k})(\alpha_j e^{i\theta_j} - \alpha_k e^{i\theta_k})]
 = (\alpha_j + \alpha_k)(1 - \cos(\theta_j - \theta_k))$.)

Expanding $-\ln|z_j(a) - z_k(a)| = -\tfrac12 \ln(|z_j(a) - z_k(a)|^2)$ to
second order in $a$ via $\ln(1 + x) = x - x^2/2 + O(x^3)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-expand}
  -\ln|z_j(a) - z_k(a)|
   = -\ln|z_j - z_k|
     - \tfrac{a}{2}(\alpha_j + \alpha_k)
     + a^2\!\left[\tfrac{(\alpha_j + \alpha_k)^2}{4}
                  - \tfrac{Q_{jk}}{2 |z_j - z_k|^2}\right] + O(a^3).
\end{equation}
The linear term, summed over pairs $j < k$, gives
$-(a/2)(N-1)\sum_k \alpha_k = 0$ since $\sum_k \cos(2\pi m k/N) = 0$ for
$m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$ (equilibrium).

Write $\beta_m$ for the coefficient of $a^2$ in $H(a) - H(0)$:
\[
  \beta_m = \sum_{j<k}\!\left[\frac{(\alpha_j + \alpha_k)^2}{4}
                              - \frac{Q_{jk}}{2 |z_j - z_k|^2}\right].
\]
The unit-normalized radial Fourier mode vector $\hat{e}_r$ (in the
$2N$-dimensional Cartesian basis) has squared norm
$\|\hat{e}_r\|^2 = \sum_k \alpha_k^2 = N/2$ for $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$
with $m \ne N/2$; for even $N$ at $m = N/2$ the norm is $N$ — see
Remark~\ref{rem:m-half} below for the explicit parallel computation
that yields the same final formula.
The Hessian entry in this normalized direction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rr-norm}
  (\nabla^2 H)_{rr}^{(m)}
     = \frac{H''(0)}{\|\hat{e}_r\|^2}
     = \frac{2\,\beta_m}{N/2}
     = \frac{4\,\beta_m}{N}
     = \frac{2}{N}\sum_{j \ne k}\!\left[\frac{(\alpha_j + \alpha_k)^2}{4}
                                        - \frac{Q_{jk}}{2 |z_j - z_k|^2}\right],
\end{equation}
where we used $H''(0) = 2\beta_m$ (for a Taylor expansion
$H(a) - H(0) = \beta_m a^2 + O(a^3)$) and
$\sum_{j<k} = \tfrac12 \sum_{j \ne k}$ (the summand is symmetric in
$j \leftrightarrow k$).

Parametrize pairs by $p = k - j \pmod N$ with $p \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$
and apply the $N$-fold cyclic symmetry. Two elementary sums are needed:
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \alpha_j^2 = \frac{N}{2},
  \qquad
  \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \alpha_j \alpha_{j+p} = \frac{N}{2}\cos(2\pi m p / N).
\]
From these,
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} (\alpha_j + \alpha_{j+p})^2
     = N \bigl(1 + \cos(2\pi m p / N)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} Q_{j, j+p}
     = N \bigl(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N) \cos(2\pi p / N)\bigr).
\]
Substituting into \eqref{eq:rr-norm},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rr-intermediate}
  (\nabla^2 H)_{rr}^{(m)}
     = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \frac{1 + \cos(2\pi m p / N)}{2}
       \;-\; \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \frac{1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)\cos(2\pi p / N)}
                                   {4 \sin^2(\pi p / N)}.
\end{equation}

The first sum is elementary:
$\sum_{p=1}^{N-1} (1 + \cos(2\pi m p / N))/2 = (N-1)/2 - 1/2 = (N-2)/2$,
since $\sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \cos(2\pi m p / N) = -1$ for $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$.

The second sum reduces via the product-to-sum identity
$\cos A \cos B = \tfrac12[\cos(A-B) + \cos(A+B)]$:
\[
  1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)\cos(2\pi p / N)
     = 1 - \tfrac12 \cos(2\pi(m-1) p / N) - \tfrac12 \cos(2\pi(m+1) p / N).
\]
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:csc} and Corollary~\ref{cor:cos-over-sin},
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\!\frac{1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)\cos(2\pi p / N)}{4 \sin^2(\pi p / N)}
  &= \frac{N^2 - 1}{12}
     - \frac{1}{2}\Bigl[\frac{N^2 - 1}{12} - \frac{(m-1)(N-m+1)}{2}\Bigr] \\
  &\phantom{=} - \frac{1}{2}\Bigl[\frac{N^2 - 1}{12} - \frac{(m+1)(N-m-1)}{2}\Bigr] \\
  &= \frac{(m-1)(N-m+1) + (m+1)(N-m-1)}{4}
   = \frac{m(N-m) - 1}{2}.
\end{align*}
Combining,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rr-unshifted}
  (\nabla^2 H)_{rr}^{(m)}
     = \frac{N - 2}{2} - \frac{m(N-m) - 1}{2}
     = \frac{N - 1 - m(N-m)}{2}.
\end{equation}
This is the radial-radial block eigenvalue of the \emph{unshifted}
energy Hessian $\nabla^2 H$. The Lagrange shift is applied in
\S\ref{sec:lag-shift}.

\section{Tangential-tangential block}\label{sec:tt}

Under a purely tangential mode-$m$ perturbation
$\delta t_k = b\,\alpha_k$ with $\alpha_k = \cos(2\pi m k / N)$
(and $a = 0$ in \eqref{eq:mode-m}), the perturbation is
$\delta z_k = i z_k\,b\,\alpha_k$, so
$z_k(b) = z_k(1 + i\,b\,\alpha_k)$. A direct computation yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tt-pair-expand}
  |z_j(b) - z_k(b)|^2
   = |z_j - z_k|^2
     + 2b\,(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k)
     + b^2\,Q_{jk} + O(b^3),
\end{equation}
with the same $Q_{jk}$ as in~\eqref{eq:pair-expand}. With
$A = z_j - z_k$ and $B = \alpha_j z_j - \alpha_k z_k$,
\[
  |z_j(b) - z_k(b)|^2 = |A + ibB|^2
    = |A|^2 - 2b\,\operatorname{Im}(\bar{A} B) + b^2 |B|^2,
\]
and $\operatorname{Im}(\bar{A} B)
 = \operatorname{Im}\!\bigl[(z_j - z_k)^{\!*}(\alpha_j z_j - \alpha_k z_k)\bigr]
 = -(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k)$
(from $\operatorname{Im}[\alpha_j + \alpha_k - \alpha_k e^{i(\theta_k - \theta_j)} - \alpha_j e^{i(\theta_j - \theta_k)}]$).
The sign in \eqref{eq:tt-pair-expand} follows: $-2b \cdot [-(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k)] = +2b(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k)$.

Expanding $-\tfrac12 \ln|z_j(b) - z_k(b)|^2$ to second order in~$b$
(with $M_{jk} = 2(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k)$
and $U_{jk} = |z_j - z_k|^2$),
\[
  -\ln|z_j(b) - z_k(b)|
   = -\ln|z_j - z_k|
     - \frac{b M_{jk}}{2 U_{jk}}
     + b^2\!\left[\frac{M_{jk}^2}{4 U_{jk}^2}
                   - \frac{Q_{jk}}{2 U_{jk}}\right] + O(b^3).
\]
The linear term, summed over pairs, vanishes: setting $p = k - j$ and
pairing $p$ with $N - p$, the factor $\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k) = -\sin(2\pi p / N)$
is odd in $p$ while $(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)/U_{jk}$ is even in $p$, so
$\sum_{j<k} M_{jk}/U_{jk} = 0$ term by term.

With $p = k - j \pmod N$ and $\theta_j - \theta_k = -2\pi p / N$,
\[
  U_{jk} = 4 \sin^2(\pi p / N),
  \qquad
  M_{jk}^2 = 4(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)^2 \sin^2(2\pi p / N)
           = 4(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)^2 \cdot U_{jk} \cos^2(\pi p / N),
\]
using $\sin^2(2\pi p / N) = 4 \sin^2(\pi p / N) \cos^2(\pi p / N)$.
Therefore $M_{jk}^2 / (4 U_{jk}^2) = (\alpha_j - \alpha_k)^2
\cos^2(\pi p / N) / U_{jk}$.

Proceeding as in \S\ref{sec:rr}, applying
$\sum_j (\alpha_j - \alpha_{j+p})^2 = N(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N))$
and $\sum_j Q_{j, j+p} = N(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)\cos(2\pi p / N))$,
the tangential-tangential block entry is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tt-intermediate}
  (\nabla^2 H)_{tt}^{(m)}
   = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}
     \frac{-\bigl(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)\cos(2\pi p / N)\bigr)
           + 2\bigl(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)\bigr)\cos^2(\pi p / N)}
          {4 \sin^2(\pi p / N)}.
\end{equation}
The bracketed numerator simplifies via
$2\cos^2(\pi p / N) = 1 + \cos(2\pi p / N)$:
\begin{align*}
  &-\bigl(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)\cos(2\pi p / N)\bigr)
     + \bigl(1 - \cos(2\pi m p / N)\bigr)\bigl(1 + \cos(2\pi p / N)\bigr) \\
  &\qquad = \cos(2\pi p / N) - \cos(2\pi m p / N).
\end{align*}
(The $\cos(2\pi m p / N)\cos(2\pi p / N)$ cross terms cancel,
$-1 + 1 = 0$, and the remaining terms are as stated.)

Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tt-diff-C}
  (\nabla^2 H)_{tt}^{(m)}
   = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\frac{\cos(2\pi p / N) - \cos(2\pi m p / N)}
                          {4 \sin^2(\pi p / N)}
   = C(1) - C(m),
\end{equation}
where $C(k)$ is as in Corollary~\ref{cor:cos-over-sin}. Substituting,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tt-unshifted}
  (\nabla^2 H)_{tt}^{(m)}
   = \frac{m(N-m)}{2} - \frac{N-1}{2}
   = \frac{m(N-m) - (N-1)}{2}.
\end{equation}
For $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$, $m(N-m) \ge N-1$ with equality exactly
at $m \in \{1, N-1\}$, so $(\nabla^2 H)_{tt}^{(m)} \ge 0$ with equality
at the translation modes (consistent with the translation Goldstone
structure: modes $m = 1$ and $m = N - 1$ generate infinitesimal
rigid translations, which lie in the kernel of $\nabla^2 H$).

\section{Off-diagonal vanishing and trace identity}\label{sec:offdiag-trace}

\begin{lemma}[Off-diagonal vanishing]\label{lem:offdiag}
For every $N \ge 2$ and every $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$ the
radial-tangential cross entry of $\nabla^2 H$ in the
mode-$m$ normalized Fourier basis vanishes:
\[
  (\nabla^2 H)_{rt}^{(m)} = 0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Under the combined perturbation
$\delta z_k = z_k\,\alpha_k(a + ib)$ with $\alpha_k = \cos(2\pi m k/N)$,
write $A = z_j - z_k$ and $B = \alpha_j z_j - \alpha_k z_k$. Then
$z_j(a,b) - z_k(a,b) = A + (a + ib)B$, so
\[
  |z_j(a,b) - z_k(a,b)|^2
   = |A|^2 + 2a\,\operatorname{Re}(A\bar B) + 2b\,\operatorname{Im}(A\bar B)
     + (a^2 + b^2)\,|B|^2.
\]
Crucially, there is \emph{no} $ab$ term in $|v(a,b)|^2$ itself.
The $ab$ term in $-\tfrac12 \ln|v(a,b)|^2$ therefore comes entirely
from the cross product of the two linear terms in the expansion
$-\tfrac12 \ln(1 + x) = -x/2 + x^2/4 - \cdots$, namely
\[
  \text{coeff of } ab \text{ in } -\tfrac12\ln|v(a,b)|^2
   = \frac{P_{jk} R_{jk}}{2\,U_{jk}^2},
\]
with $P_{jk} = 2\operatorname{Re}(A\bar B)
            = 2(\alpha_j + \alpha_k)(1 - \cos(\theta_j - \theta_k))$
and $R_{jk} = 2\operatorname{Im}(A\bar B)
            = 2(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k)$.
Multiplying,
\[
  P_{jk} R_{jk}
   = 4\,(\alpha_j + \alpha_k)(\alpha_j - \alpha_k)\,(1 - \cos(\theta_j - \theta_k))\,\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k)
   = 4\,(\alpha_j^2 - \alpha_k^2)\,(1 - \cos(\theta_j - \theta_k))\,\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k).
\]
Using $1 - \cos(\theta_j - \theta_k) = U_{jk}/2$,
\[
  \frac{P_{jk} R_{jk}}{2\,U_{jk}^2}
   = \frac{2\,(\alpha_j^2 - \alpha_k^2)\,\sin(\theta_j - \theta_k)}{U_{jk}}.
\]
Summing over pairs with $p = k - j$ and fixing $p$, the dependence
on $j$ is only through $\alpha_j^2 - \alpha_{j+p}^2$. But
$\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \alpha_j^2 = N/2 = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \alpha_{j+p}^2$
(the second sum is a cyclic shift of the first), so
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \bigl(\alpha_j^2 - \alpha_{j+p}^2\bigr) = 0
  \quad \text{for every } p \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}.
\]
Therefore the $ab$ coefficient in $H(a, b)$ vanishes, which
(after unit normalization of $\hat e_r, \hat e_t$) gives
$(\nabla^2 H)_{rt}^{(m)} = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trace identity]\label{lem:trace}
$\lambda_m^+ + \lambda_m^- = N - 1$ for every $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct from \eqref{eq:final-eigs}:
$\bigl[(N-1) - m(N-m)/2\bigr] + \bigl[m(N-m)/2\bigr] = N - 1$.
Equivalently, $\lambda_m^+ + \lambda_m^- = (\nabla^2 H)_{rr}^{(m)}
+ (\nabla^2 H)_{tt}^{(m)} + 2 \cdot (N-1)/2$, and the two
unshifted sums telescope via \eqref{eq:rr-unshifted} and
\eqref{eq:tt-unshifted}:
$(N - 1 - m(N-m))/2 + (m(N-m) - (N-1))/2 = 0$, leaving $N - 1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Lagrange shift and eigenvalues}\label{sec:lag-shift}

The Lagrangian is $\mathcal{L} = H - \mu_L L$ with angular impulse
$L = \sum_k |z_k|^2$ and $\mu_L = -(N-1)/4$ at the unit-ring equilibrium.
Since $\nabla^2 L = 2 I$, the Lagrangian Hessian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-shift}
  \nabla^2 \mathcal{L}
   = \nabla^2 H - 2\mu_L I
   = \nabla^2 H + \frac{N-1}{2}\,I.
\end{equation}
Adding $(N-1)/2$ to each of \eqref{eq:rr-unshifted} and
\eqref{eq:tt-unshifted} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final-eigs}
  \boxed{\;
  \lambda_m^+ = \frac{N-1 - m(N-m)}{2} + \frac{N-1}{2}
             = (N-1) - \frac{m(N-m)}{2},
  \qquad
  \lambda_m^- = \frac{m(N-m) - (N-1)}{2} + \frac{N-1}{2}
             = \frac{m(N-m)}{2}.
  \;}
\end{equation}
The trace identity $\lambda_m^+ + \lambda_m^- = N - 1$ falls out
automatically — it is not an independent check but a consequence of
the $(N-1)/2$ shift doubling across the two diagonal entries.

\begin{remark}[The case $m = N/2$ for even $N$]\label{rem:m-half}
When $N$ is even and $m = N/2$, set $\alpha_k = \cos(\pi k) = (-1)^k$.
The mode vector $\hat e_r$ has squared norm
$\|\hat e_r\|^2 = \sum_k \alpha_k^2 = N$
(because $\alpha_k^2 = 1$ for every $k$, not averaged to $1/2$).
The key Fourier sums change correspondingly to
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \alpha_j \alpha_{j+p}
   = \sum_j (-1)^{2j + p} = N\,(-1)^p
   = N \cos(2\pi \cdot \tfrac{N}{2} \cdot p / N),
\]
which is exactly twice the generic expression
$(N/2)\cos(2\pi m p / N)$ evaluated at $m = N/2$. Consequently
$\sum_j(\alpha_j + \alpha_{j+p})^2 = 2 N (1 + \cos(\pi p))$
and $\sum_j Q_{j, j+p} = 2 N (1 - \cos(\pi p)\cos(2\pi p / N))$ —
each double the generic value. Substituting into the normalized
Hessian entry with $\|\hat e_r\|^2 = N$ (so the prefactor is $2/N$,
not $4/N$), the two factors of $2$ cancel and the same algebra goes
through. Explicitly,
\[
  (\nabla^2 H)_{rr}^{(N/2)}
   = \sum_p\tfrac{1 + \cos(\pi p)}{2}
     - \sum_p\tfrac{1 - \cos(\pi p)\cos(2\pi p / N)}{4\sin^2(\pi p / N)}
   = \tfrac{N - 1 - (N/2)(N/2)}{2}
   = \tfrac{N - 1 - N^2/4}{2},
\]
matching $(N - 1 - m(N-m))/2$ at $m = N/2$. The tangential-tangential
entry follows identically. The final eigenvalue formula
\eqref{eq:final-eigs} is thus uniform in $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$,
including $m = N/2$.
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical verification}\label{sec:verification}

The derivation above is self-contained; the script
\texttt{numerical\_check.py} in this directory corroborates each
step with machine-checked identity verification.

\emph{Primary verification — symbolic to 30 decimal digits.}
Every intermediate identity used in the derivation is verified via
\texttt{sympy} arbitrary-precision evaluation for $N \in \{3, \ldots, 10\}$
and $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$: the Havelock identity \eqref{eq:havelock},
the cosecant-squared sum \eqref{eq:csc}, the radial-radial and
tangential-tangential unshifted expressions \eqref{eq:rr-unshifted} and
\eqref{eq:tt-unshifted}, and the $\alpha^2$-translation identity in
the off-diagonal proof. Each is confirmed to differ from its closed
form by less than $10^{-25}$.

\emph{Corroborating sanity check — finite-difference oracle.}
As an independent cross-check that the block-level algebra is
correctly assembled, the script diagonalizes the full $2N \times 2N$
Hessian $\nabla^2 H$ at the unit ring (via central differences),
projects into the normalized mode-$m$ (radial, tangential) basis,
applies the Lagrange shift, and compares to \eqref{eq:final-eigs}.
For $N \in \{3, \ldots, 12\}$ and all $m \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$,
the observed eigenvalues match to $10^{-2}$ (finite-difference
precision). The three canonical cases
$(N, m) \in \{(5, 2), (7, 3), (11, 5)\}$ match to machine precision.
