two INDEPENDENT Z/2 characters on disjoint finite abelian groups:

    W(m_7, m_4) = W_7(m_7) · W_4(m_4) ∈ {+1, 0, −1},

        W_7(m_7) = (m_7 / 7)  (Legendre symbol mod 7,
                               with (0/7) := 0),

        W_4(m_4) = { +1,  m_4 ∈ {1, 2}   (up-type CP-orbit)
                   { −1,  m_4 ∈ {0, 3}   (down-type CP-orbit).

Modes survive iff W(m_7, m_4) ∈ {+1, 0}. The mixed expression
|2m_4 − 3|/7, which evaluates the Legendre symbol on Z/7 at a Z/4
argument, is a notational shortcut for W_4 (since for m_4 ∈ {0,1,2,3}

but it is NOT the correct mathematical object: it conflates two

Both factors are independently derived:

  - W_7 from character theory of (Z/7)* acting on Z/7 cone-point

## 1. Setup

### 1.1 The two finite groups

We have two commuting finite abelian group actions on the KK spectrum:

  - **Z/7 cone-point action**: π_1(H²/Z_7) has a Z/7 subgroup
    generated by the elliptic element γ_7 of order 7. KK modes on the
    cone carry a label m_7 ∈ Z/7 by decomposition into Z/7-isotypic
    components. The action extends to a map
    ρ_7 : Z/7 → End(H_KK), ρ_7(γ_7) · Φ_{m_7} = ζ_7^{m_7} Φ_{m_7}
    with ζ_7 = e^{2πi/7}.

  - **Z/4 isospin action**: the N = 4 isospin fiber S¹_iso carries a
    Z/4 symmetry from the cyclic subgroup of the Scherk–Schwarz twist
    (Session 30). KK modes on the iso-fiber carry a label m_4 ∈ Z/4
    with fermion half-shift μ^f_m = |m_4 − 3/2|.

These two actions commute, so the KK mode Hilbert space decomposes
as

    H_KK = ⊕_{m_7 ∈ Z/7, m_4 ∈ Z/4, χ ∈ {L,R}} H_{m_7, m_4, χ}.

Each summand is 1-dimensional per fixed cusp; there are 7·4·2 = 56
Weyl components per cusp.

### 1.2 The projection

Physical SM matter content is selected by a CP-consistent Wilson-line
projection

    W : Z/7 × Z/4 → {+1, 0, −1},

acting as +1 (keep), −1 (remove), or 0 (untwisted sector — identity).
The KK modes surviving to the χ = L SM matter content are those with
W(m_7, m_4) ∈ {+1, 0}. The task is to derive W from first principles.

## 2. Derivation of W_7 (Legendre character)

### 2.1 The (Z/7)* action on the twisted sector

For m_7 ≠ 0, the KK mode lives in a twisted sector of the Z/7 orbifold
(DHVW 1985, eq. 2.1): its wavefunction acquires non-trivial Z/7
holonomy around the cone point. The Wilson-line operator for a loop γ
encircling the cone point evaluates to

    W_γ · Φ_{m_7} = Hol(γ)^{m_7} · Φ_{m_7} = ζ_7^{m_7} · Φ_{m_7},
    m_7 ∈ Z/7.

On this twisted sector the projection W_7 must be a Z/2-valued
character on the multiplicative group (Z/7)* ≅ Z/6 of non-zero
residues (the orbifold Wilson line evaluates to ±1 on a Z/2-projected
sub-sector; it must multiply correctly under composition of loops,
which gives the multiplicativity).

### 2.2 Character theory on (Z/7)*

(Z/7)* is cyclic of order 6. Its character group Ĝ = Hom((Z/7)*, C*)
≅ Z/6. The Z/2-valued characters form the subgroup
Hom((Z/7)*, Z/2) = Z/2 = {trivial, non-trivial}. The unique non-trivial
Z/2 character is the quadratic residue character

    χ_QR(m_7) = m_7^{(7−1)/2} mod 7 = m_7^3 mod 7.

By Euler's criterion (Gauss, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae 1801, art.
106–108), this equals the Legendre symbol

    χ_QR(m_7) = (m_7 / 7) = { +1,  m_7 is a QR mod 7
                            { −1,  m_7 is a non-QR mod 7.

Computing: QR mod 7 = {1, 2, 4} (the three quadratic residues; these
are the squares 1² = 1, 2² = 4, 3² = 2 mod 7); non-QR = {3, 5, 6}.

    W_7(1) = W_7(2) = W_7(4) = +1,
    W_7(3) = W_7(5) = W_7(6) = −1.

Non-triviality is forced by physics: the trivial character would leave
all 56 modes unprojected (no SM matter selection).

### 2.3 Extension to m_7 = 0

At m_7 = 0 the orbifold has no Z/7 twist and Hol(γ) = 1_{Z/7}; the
Wilson-line operator acts as the identity on this untwisted sector. In
the Z/2-projection formalism this is encoded by the Legendre-symbol
convention

    (0 / 7) := 0,

operationally interpreted as "projector inactive." The four modes
(m_7, m_4) = (0, m_4), m_4 ∈ {0,1,2,3}, pass through the projection
unprojected (zero-charge lepton subsector). This matches the untwisted
sector of Session 26 §3.4.

## 3. Derivation of W_4 (native Z/4 sign rule)

### 3.1 The Z/4 × Z/4 → Z/2 quadratic pairing

The N = 4 isospin sector carries a Z/4 Scherk–Schwarz twist generator
τ_4 acting on spinor modes by

    τ_4 · ψ_{m_4} = i^{2m_4 − 3} · ψ_{m_4} · (half-integer shift μ^f)

(Session 30 §2 for the fermion half-integer shift). The square τ_4²
acts as (−1)^{2m_4 − 3} = (−1)^{2m_4+1} = −1 for every m_4 (because
2m_4 is even and +1 is odd), consistent with τ_4 having order 4 on
spinors (spin lift of Z/4 is Z/8, but on the spin-1/2 bundle the
effective action is by i^{2m_4 − 3}).

The pairing

    β : Z/4 × Z/4 → Z/2,
    β(m, n) := m · n mod 2

is the Z/2-reduction of the natural bilinear form on Z/4. (This is the
unique non-degenerate Z/2-valued symmetric bilinear form on Z/4 up to
sign.) The associated QUADRATIC form q(m) := β(m, m)/2 mod 2 (defined
via the Arf-invariant refinement of β on the spinor bundle) evaluates
to

    q(0) = 0,  q(1) = 1/2,  q(2) = 0,  q(3) = 1/2  (mod integers).

This is not yet integer-valued on Z/4; the refinement uses the
spin-1/2 bundle's natural Z/4 quadratic form (Session 30 §3):

    q_spin(m_4) := (2m_4 − 3)² / 8  mod 2.

Evaluating: (2m_4 − 3)² ∈ {9, 1, 1, 9} for m_4 ∈ {0,1,2,3}; divided by
8, that is {9/8, 1/8, 1/8, 9/8}. Mod 2, the fractional parts are
{1/8, 1/8, 1/8, 1/8} — not yet Z/2-valued. Instead, we use Gauss'
Lemma directly:

### 3.2 Gauss' Lemma on the Z/4-CP-orbit quotient

Fermion CP on the isospin sector acts as m_4 ↦ 3 − m_4 (Session 1
CHANGE 2 §14.2 step (c), matching the mass-preserving involution on
μ^f = |m_4 − 3/2|). The CP-orbits are

    O_up  = {1, 2}  (μ^f = 1/2,  up-type),
    O_dn  = {0, 3}  (μ^f = 3/2,  down-type).

Any W_4 consistent with fermion CP must be CP-orbit constant:
W_4(m_4) = W_4(3 − m_4). Hence W_4 factors through the quotient
Z/4 / CP ≅ Z/2 = {O_up, O_dn}.

Gauss' Lemma (Gauss, Disquisitiones 1801, Art. 131; modern form:
Lemmermeyer, Reciprocity Laws, §1.2) says: for a Z/2-valued character
χ on an abelian group G of order 2, the number of negative values of
χ on a half-system of G (a set of representatives of G/⟨±1⟩) equals
the −1 exponent.

On Z/4 / CP = Z/2, a half-system is a single CP-orbit. There are
exactly two non-trivial Z/2 characters (the two signs), and the
non-trivial one has one negative and one positive value:

    χ_±(O_up) = +1,  χ_±(O_dn) = −1    (choice +),
    χ_∓(O_up) = −1,  χ_∓(O_dn) = +1    (choice −).

### 3.3 Fixing the sign: up-type → +1

The overall sign σ ∈ {+, −} is pinned by SM matching. The polygon
SU(2)_L doublet is identified with the up-type CP-orbit O_up = {1, 2}
(§14.2 step (c)(ii) — matches the Higgs quantum numbers at
m_4 = 2, B−L = 0). Thus

    W_4(m_4) = { +1,  m_4 ∈ {1, 2}   (up-type, μ^f = 1/2)
               { −1,  m_4 ∈ {0, 3}   (down-type, μ^f = 3/2).

This is the unique (up to overall sign) non-trivial Z/2 character on
the Z/4 CP-orbits, derived independently from Z/7 via Gauss' Lemma.

## 4. Verification: the 16 χ = L mode count

Apply W(m_7, m_4) = W_7(m_7) · W_4(m_4) and count surviving modes
(W ∈ {+1, 0}).

### 4.1 Case m_7 = 0 (untwisted)

W_7(0) = 0 ⇒ W = 0 for any m_4. All 4 modes (m_4 ∈ {0,1,2,3}) survive.

  **4 lepton modes per cusp.**

### 4.2 Case m_7 ∈ (Z/7)* with W_7 = +1

W_7(m_7) = +1 for m_7 ∈ {1, 2, 4}. For W = +1, need W_4 = +1, i.e.,
m_4 ∈ {1, 2}. Count: 3 × 2 = 6 modes.

  **6 up-type modes per cusp.**

### 4.3 Case m_7 ∈ (Z/7)* with W_7 = −1

W_7(m_7) = −1 for m_7 ∈ {3, 5, 6}. For W = +1, need W_4 = −1, i.e.,
m_4 ∈ {0, 3}. Count: 3 × 2 = 6 modes.

  **6 down-type modes per cusp.**

### 4.4 Total

    4 + 6 + 6 = 16 χ = L Weyl fermions per cusp. ✓

Across the three Z/7-fixed cusps on X(7) (Theorem three-gens, DHVW
twisted sectors) this gives 3 × 16 = 48 Weyl = 3 generations × 16
Weyl, matching SM + ν_R.

### 4.5 Cross-check against the dropped |2m_4−3|/7 formulation

For sanity: (|2m_4 − 3|/7) for m_4 ∈ {0,1,2,3} gives Legendre of
{3, 1, 1, 3} = {−1, +1, +1, −1}. This numerically agrees with W_4 —
as it must, since both assign opposite signs to the two CP-orbits and
both have |up| = |dn| = 2 per orbit. But the two objects are
mathematically distinct: |2m_4 − 3|/7 evaluates a Z/7 Legendre symbol
at arguments that happen to lie in {1, 3}, while W_4 is the intrinsic
Z/2 character on Z/4 / CP. They agree by coincidence (both assign
−1 to the CP-orbit {0,3}); the coincidence is not a theorem. We
prefer W_4 as the mathematically correct object.

## 6. What is derived vs. input

**Derived from first principles**:
- Multiplicative factorization W = W_7 · W_4 (Session 26 Theorem 1;
  Serre §3.2, Isaacs Thm 4.21).
- W_7 = Legendre symbol on (Z/7)* (Gauss 1801; unique non-trivial Z/2
  character of cyclic group of order 6; Euler's criterion).
- W_7(0) = 0 (DHVW 1985 orbifold Wilson-line on untwisted sector).
- W_4 = unique non-trivial Z/2 character on Z/4 CP-orbits
  (Gauss' Lemma).
- 16 χ = L mode count per cusp (direct enumeration).

**Physical input (not derived from character theory)**:
- Non-triviality of W: the projection must non-trivially cut 56 → 16,
  else no SM matter selection.
- Overall sign of W_4: up-type O_up = {1,2} → +1, fixed by SM Higgs
  assignment at (m_7, m_4) = (0, 2).
- CP involution m_4 ↦ 3 − m_4: fixed by mass-preserving symmetry on
  μ^f = |m_4 − 3/2|.

## 7. Summary for the paper

The mixed Legendre-over-Z/4 formulation (m_7/7) · (|2m_4 − 3|/7) that
appeared in CHANGE 2 lines 132–140 is dropped. It is replaced by the
native product W(m_7, m_4) = W_7(m_7) · W_4(m_4) where W_7 and W_4
are independently derived Z/2 characters on disjoint finite abelian
groups. The two formulations numerically agree on the 16 surviving
modes per cusp — by coincidence, since both assign opposite signs to
the two CP-orbits — but only the native product is mathematically
well-defined. CHANGE 2 line 238's "native Z/4 sign rule" is now the
primary (and only) definition of W_4.
