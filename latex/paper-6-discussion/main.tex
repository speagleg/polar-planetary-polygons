\subsection{Cumulative uncertainty}
\label{sec:uncertainty}
The derivation chain from polygon stability to the Standard
Model passes through three distinct types of link:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Theorem links} (Havelock identity, McKay
  correspondence, CS/WZW, Liouville--Arnold):
  mathematically proved.  No propagated uncertainty.
\item \emph{Structural identifications}, ranked from
  strongest to weakest:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{(a)}] 2+1D gravity $=$ $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
    CS theory (\citealt{Witten1988}; a theorem);
  \item[\textbf{(b)}] CS on punctured surface $\to$ KZ $\to$ CMS
    (proved operator isomorphism, EFK 1998);
  \item[\textbf{(c)}] CMS Casimir $=$ Havelock eigenvalue
    (Proposition~\ref{III-prop:casimir-havelock}; a theorem);
  \item[\textbf{(d)}] dreibein $=$ linearized Havelock mode
    (proved at linearized level; nonlinear extension
    guaranteed by target-space symmetry of the WZW model).
  \end{itemize}
  All four links are mathematical theorems.
  The composition (a)--(c) proves the identification
  Regge vertex $=$ point vortex: conical singularities
  in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ CS theory have
  Casimir eigenvalues $m(N{-}m)/2$, which are exactly
  the Havelock vortex eigenvalues (Paper~I).

  structure).

  $\sin^2\theta_W$): the numerical values are exact consequences

  identifications.
