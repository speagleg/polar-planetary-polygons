\begin{abstract}
The polygon vortex framework (Papers~I--IV) operates on two-dimensional
surfaces.  We lift it to the three-sphere~$S^3$ via the Hopf fibration,
showing that the 600-cell---the 120 vertices of the binary icosahedral
group~$I^*$ viewed as unit quaternions---is the natural UV configuration.
The scalar Green's function on~$S^3$ is derived from first principles
($G(\chi) = (1/4\pi^2)(\pi - \chi)\cot\chi$, verified by direct PDE
substitution), and its spectral decomposition reveals all nine~$I^*$
irreps with degeneracies~$d_\rho^2$ (the regular representation).
The Onsager selection on~$S^3$ and the Schur conservation theorem
(the non-abelian analogue of Lax integrability) protect the 600-cell
phase.  The Hopf projection $S^3 \to S^2$ recovers the icosahedron.
At $K = 0$---the topological transition from $S^3$ spatial geometry
to~$\mathbb{R}^3$---the dominant saddle changes from the icosahedron
($N = 12$, $A_5$, $E_8$) to the heptagon ($N = 7$, $\mathbb{Z}_7$, SM),
a first-order phase transition in the Ehrenfest sense.  The integer-spin
sector (120~dimensions) is the classical vortex physics visible
on~$S^2$; the half-integer sector (128~dimensions) is the $E_8$
spinor, invisible classically but revealed on~$S^3$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The scalar Green's function on $S^3$}
\label{sec:green}
%% ====================================================================

\subsection{Eigenvalues and eigenfunctions}

On the unit three-sphere, the scalar Laplacian has eigenvalues
$-\Delta f = l(l+2)\,f$ for $l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$, with
degeneracy $(l+1)^2$.  The zonal eigenfunctions (depending only on
geodesic distance~$\chi$) are the Gegenbauer polynomials $C_l^1(\cos\chi)$,
also known as Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind.
They satisfy the recurrence $C_0^1 = 1$, $C_1^1 = 2x$,
$C_{l+1}^1 = 2x\,C_l^1 - C_{l-1}^1$,
with $C_l^1(1) = l + 1$ and $C_l^1(\cos\chi) = \sin((l{+}1)\chi)/\sin\chi$.

\subsection{Closed form and verification}

The Green's function $G(\chi)$ satisfying
$\Delta G = -\delta + 1/\mathrm{Vol}(S^3)$ (where $\mathrm{Vol}(S^3) = 2\pi^2$)
is constructed from the eigenfunction expansion using the $S^3$ addition
theorem and summed to a closed form.

\begin{theorem}[$S^3$ Green's function]\label{thm:green-s3}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:green-s3}
  G(\chi) = \frac{1}{4\pi^2}\,(\pi - \chi)\,\cot\chi.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution into the $S^3$ radial Laplacian
$\Delta f = f'' + 2\cot(\chi)\,f'$.  Let $h(\chi) = (\pi - \chi)\cot\chi$.
Then $h' = -\cot\chi - (\pi - \chi)\csc^2\chi$ and
$h'' = 2\csc^2\chi + 2(\pi - \chi)\csc^2\chi\,\cot\chi$.
Computing $\Delta h = h'' + 2\cot\chi\cdot h'$:
the $(\pi - \chi)$ terms cancel, leaving
$\Delta h = 2(\csc^2\chi - \cot^2\chi) = 2$.
Therefore $\Delta G = 2/(4\pi^2) = 1/(2\pi^2) = 1/\mathrm{Vol}(S^3)$.
\end{proof}

Properties: $G(0^+) \to +\infty$ (Coulomb singularity,
$\sim \pi/(4\pi^2\chi)$); $G(\pi/2) = 0$;
$G(\pi^-) = -1/(4\pi^2)$ (finite at the antipode).

\begin{remark}
The $S^3$ Laplacian identity $\Delta_{S^3}(-G) = -1/(2\pi^2)$ (constant)
is the three-dimensional analogue of the $S^2$ identity
$\Delta_{S^2}[-\ln\sin(d/2)] = 1/2$ that underlies the eigenvalue
pairing theorem (Theorem~I-\ref{I-thm:hessian-pairing}).
\end{remark}

\section{The 600-cell: $I^*$ on $S^3$}
\label{sec:600cell}
%% ====================================================================

The binary icosahedral group $I^* \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ has 120
elements, which we represent as unit quaternions
$q = w + x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k} \in S^3$.
These 120 quaternions are the vertices of the 600-cell, a regular
4-dimensional polytope with 120~vertices, 720~edges,
1200~triangular faces, and 600~tetrahedral cells.

The construction: 8~quaternion units ($\pm 1, \pm\mathbf{i},
\pm\mathbf{j}, \pm\mathbf{k}$), 16~half-integer quaternions
$(\pm 1 \pm \mathbf{i} \pm \mathbf{j} \pm \mathbf{k})/2$,
and 96~golden quaternions (even permutations of
$(0, \pm 1, \pm\varphi, \pm 1/\varphi)/2$ where
$\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$).

\subsection{Distance classes and conjugacy classes}

The geodesic distance between quaternions $q_1, q_2 \in S^3$ is
$\chi = \arccos(q_1 \cdot q_2)$ (the $\mathbb{R}^4$ inner product).
The 119~non-identity 600-cell vertices fall into exactly eight
distance classes from any reference vertex, with sizes
$\{12, 20, 12, 30, 12, 20, 12, 1\}$.

These eight distance classes are precisely the eight nontrivial
conjugacy classes of~$I^*$ (the ninth class is the identity).
The geometry of the 600-cell IS the group theory of~$I^*$.

\section{Energy decomposition: the regular representation}
\label{sec:energy-decomp}
%% ====================================================================

The energy weight matrix $K_{jk} = -G(\chi_{jk})$ for $j \neq k$,
$K_{jj} = -\sum_{l \neq j} K_{jl}$ (zero-sum rows), gives the
decomposition of the interaction energy by~$I^*$ irreps.

\begin{theorem}[Regular representation]\label{thm:regular-rep}
The eigenvalue degeneracies of the energy weight matrix on the
600-cell are $d_\rho^2$ for each $I^*$ irrep~$\rho$:
$\{1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 16, 4, 9\}$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
This is the regular representation structure: each irrep~$\rho$ of
dimension~$d_\rho$ appears with multiplicity~$d_\rho$ in the
permutation representation of $I^*$ acting on itself.

The nine distinct eigenvalues correspond to the nine $I^*$ irreps,
giving the energy-weighted Havelock Casimir $T_\rho$ for \emph{all}
nine irreps---including the four half-integer-spin irreps
($\rho_1, \rho_3, \rho_5, \rho_7$) that are invisible on~$S^2$
(where the icosahedron permutation representation factors through
$A_5 = I^*/\mathbb{Z}_2$ and sees only integer-spin irreps).

\begin{remark}
On~$S^2$, the icosahedron's 12-vertex permutation representation
decomposes as $1 \oplus 3 \oplus 5 \oplus 3'$ (four $A_5$ irreps,
all integer-spin).  On~$S^3$, the 600-cell's 120-vertex regular
representation decomposes into all nine $I^*$ irreps with multiplicity
$d_\rho$.  The $S^3$ framework \emph{completes} the $S^2$ picture.
\end{remark}

\section{Onsager selection and Schur conservation}
\label{sec:onsager-schur}
%% ====================================================================

\subsection{Onsager selection on $S^3$}

At negative temperature (the Onsager regime, microcanonical
ensemble at fixed angular impulse~$L$), the equilibrium maximises
the energy~$H = -\sum_{j<k} G(\chi_{jk})$.  Among $I^*$-symmetric
configurations on~$S^3$, the 600-cell has the largest interaction magnitude
among $I^*$-symmetric configurations ($|H_{600}| = 67.9$ vs.\
$|H_{24}| = 1.2$ for the 24-cell), reflecting its 7140~pairwise
interactions at $I^*$-determined distances.

\subsection{Schur conservation theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Dynamical Schur conservation]\label{thm:schur}
Let $H$ be an $I^*$-invariant Hamiltonian on~$S^3$.  If the initial
configuration is $I^*$-symmetric (i.e., lies on the $I^*$ orbit),
then the Hamiltonian flow preserves $I^*$ symmetry for all time,
and the energy weight matrix decomposes into $I^*$ irrep blocks
with degeneracies~$d_\rho^2$ that are conserved along the flow.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step~1 (Symmetry preservation).}
The Hamiltonian~$H$ is $I^*$-invariant:
$H(g \cdot \mathbf{q}) = H(\mathbf{q})$ for all $g \in I^*$.
By equivariance of Hamilton's equations, $g \in I^*$ maps
solutions to solutions.  An $I^*$-symmetric initial condition
$\mathbf{q}_0 = g \cdot \mathbf{q}_0$ evolves to
$\mathbf{q}(t) = g \cdot \mathbf{q}(t)$ for all~$t$ (uniqueness
of solutions).  Therefore the orbit remains $I^*$-symmetric.

\emph{Step~2 (Block structure).}
At each time~$t$, the configuration is $I^*$-symmetric, so the
energy weight matrix commutes with~$I^*$.  By Schur's lemma, its
eigenspaces decompose by $I^*$ irreps with degeneracies~$d_\rho^2$.
The eigenvalues vary continuously; the discrete degeneracies
cannot change.
\end{proof}

This is stronger than instantaneous Schur's lemma: it is a
\emph{dynamical} conservation law.  The $I^*$ block structure is
preserved not just under equivariant perturbations, but under
the full Hamiltonian time evolution starting from any $I^*$-symmetric
state.  The analogy with Lax conservation (Paper~III, \S3) is:
Lax conserves eigenvalues of the Lax matrix; Schur conserves the
\emph{block structure} (degeneracy pattern) of the interaction matrix.

\section{The phase transition at $K = 0$}
\label{sec:phase-transition}
%% ====================================================================

\begin{theorem}[Topological phase transition]\label{thm:phase-transition}
The dominant vortex configuration changes discontinuously at $K = 0$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $K > 0$ ($S^2$): Icosahedron ($N = 12$, $A_5$, $E_8$ via McKay).
\item $K = 0$ ($\mathbb{R}^2$): Heptagon ($N = 7$, $\mathbb{Z}_7$,
  $\mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$
  via Frobenius).
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\emph{Thermodynamic framework.}\enspace
The transition is \emph{topological}: the configuration space changes
from $\mathrm{Conf}_{N}(S^2)$ ($K > 0$, Platonic solids exist) to
$\mathrm{Conf}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^2)$ ($K = 0$, only polygon rings).
The order parameter $\sigma(K) = $ symmetry group of the dominant
saddle is discontinuous: $\sigma(K > 0) = A_5$ ($|G| = 60$),
$\sigma(K = 0) = \mathbb{Z}_7$ ($|G| = 7$).
Note: $K = 0$ is a singular limit ($R = 1/\sqrt{K} \to \infty$),
so the standard Ehrenfest classification (which requires a smooth
free energy) does not directly apply.  The transition is classified
by the \emph{discontinuity of the order parameter} and the
\emph{change of configuration space topology}, not by derivatives of
a thermodynamic potential.

\emph{Icosahedron dominance (over all configurations).}\enspace
The icosahedron is universally optimal on~$S^2$ for $N = 12$:
it simultaneously minimises every completely monotone potential
energy, including both the Coulomb kernel $1/\sin(d/2)$ and the
log-sine kernel $-\ln\sin(d/2)$
(\citealt{CohnKumar2007}, Theorem~1.2).
The log-energy maximum is therefore the icosahedron,
which is the Onsager ground state at negative temperature.
Among stable configurations of \emph{any} $N$ on~$S^2$,
$N = 12$ (icosahedron) is selected by the Onsager
maximum-$N$ principle (Paper~II, \S11): the icosahedron is
stable while the dodecahedron ($N = 20$) is not.

\emph{The mechanism.}\enspace
On $S^2(R)$ for any $R > 0$, the icosahedron has higher energy
than the heptagon:
$\Delta E(R) = \Delta E_{\mathrm{unit}} + 45\ln R \to \infty$.
Polygon rings placed at the equator of~$S^2$ are unstable for all
$N \ge 4$ (the tangential Hessian has negative eigenvalues from
out-of-plane perturbations; verified computationally for $N = 4$--$8$),
while the icosahedron is stable (zero Morse index).
At $K = 0$, the topology changes: $S^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, and
Platonic configurations cease to exist as finite-energy objects.
The 236~coset generators ($E_8 \to \mathrm{SM}$,
$\dim(E_8) - \dim(\mathrm{SU}(3){\times}\mathrm{SU}(2){\times}\mathrm{U}(1))
= 248 - 12$; the embedding chain
$E_8 \supset E_6 {\times} \mathrm{SU}(3) \supset \cdots \supset \mathrm{SM}$
is standard in the GUT literature) become massive at the transition.

\section{The $120/128$ split: classical and quantum sectors}
\label{sec:split}
%% ====================================================================

Two different decompositions illuminate the integer/half-integer
structure.

\paragraph{The Coxeter decomposition of $E_8$ (248-dimensional).}
Theorem~I-\ref{I-thm:coxeter} gives multiplicities
$\{2, 6, 6, 8, 10, 12, 8, 6, 6\}$ for the nine $I^*$ irreps.
Summing $\mathrm{mult}_i \times d_i$ over integer-spin irreps
($\rho_0, \rho_2, \rho_4, \rho_6, \rho_8$ with dims $1,3,5,4,3$):
$2 + 18 + 50 + 32 + 18 = 120$.
Over half-integer ($\rho_1, \rho_3, \rho_5, \rho_7$ with dims $2,4,6,2$):
$12 + 32 + 72 + 12 = 128$.
This gives $248 = 120 + 128$, matching $E_8 \supset \mathrm{SO}(16)$:
$248 = 120_{\mathrm{adj}} \oplus 128_{\mathrm{spinor}}$.

\paragraph{The regular representation on $S^3$ (120-dimensional).}
On the 600-cell, the $K$-matrix has degeneracies $d_\rho^2$.
The integer-spin sector contributes
$1^2 + 3^2 + 5^2 + 4^2 + 3^2 = 60$ dimensions;
the half-integer sector contributes
$2^2 + 4^2 + 6^2 + 2^2 = 60$ dimensions.
The regular representation splits $120 = 60 + 60$
(evenly between integer and half-integer).

\paragraph{The physical distinction.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Integer-spin sector} (5~irreps):
  visible on~$S^2$ as eigenvalues of the icosahedron's interaction
  matrix.  These are the classical vortex modes.
\item \textbf{Half-integer-spin sector} (4~irreps):
  invisible on~$S^2$ (the permutation representation factors through
  $A_5 = I^*/\mathbb{Z}_2$, proved by the $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$
  stabilizer character sum vanishing for half-integer~$\rho$).
  Revealed on~$S^3$ by the regular representation.
\end{itemize}
The $120/128$ split in the \emph{Coxeter decomposition} (not the
regular representation) is the mathematical origin of the
$\mathrm{SO}(16)$ adjoint/spinor distinction in the $E_8$ framework.

\begin{theorem}[The derived framework]\label{thm:master}
Let $a = 4$ be the spectral diameter
(Theorem~I-\ref{I-thm:spectral-diameter}).
Onsager stability (Proposition~\ref{prop:onsager}) selects the
icosahedron, whose binary lift gives the $(2,3,5)$ triangle group
with spherical excess $\varepsilon = 1/30$.
Then
\begin{alignat}{3}
  b &= a+1 &&= 5  &\quad&(\text{number of Platonic solids}),\\
  c &= a+2 &&= 6  &&(\text{maximum mark}),\\
  |I^*| &= abc &&= 120 = 5!,\\
  h &= 6b  &&= 30, \\
  \operatorname{rank} &= 2a &&= 8 = \dim(\mathbb{O}),\\
  \textstyle\sum\text{pivots} &= a{+}a/2{+}a/4 &&= 7 = N_{\mathrm{crit}}, \\
  \dim(E_8) &= abc + 2^{7a/4} &&= 120 + 128 = 248.
\end{alignat}
All quantities are derived from two inputs: the spectral diameter~$a = 4$
and the Onsager selection of the $(2,3,5)$ triangle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$|I^*| = 4/\varepsilon = 4 \times 30 = 120$
(Gauss--Bonnet: $|\Gamma^*|\varepsilon = 4$ for all binary polyhedral groups).
$h = |I^*|/a = 120/4 = 30$ (mark-balance $k_1 = a$,
Theorem~I-\ref{I-thm:mark-balance}).
The class equation of~$I^*$ gives $9$ conjugacy classes
($\{e\}$, $\{-I\}$, and $7$ non-central classes with sizes
$12, 12, 20, 20, 30, 12, 12$);
by Burnside, $9$ irreps, so $\operatorname{rank} = 9 - 1 = 8$.
$\sum\text{pivots} = a + a/2 + a/4 = 7 = N_{\mathrm{crit}}$
(Theorem~I-\ref{I-thm:division-chain} and Proposition~I-\ref{I-prop:ncrit-rank}).
The split $248 = 120 + 128$ is the $\mathrm{SO}(16)$ branching
$E_8 \supset D_8$: $120$ = adjoint, $128 = 2^7$ = spinor.
\end{proof}

Prime & Eigenvalues & Count & Physical sector \\

$p=5$ (golden)    & $\pm\varphi,\;\pm 1/\varphi$ & 4 & Strong $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ \\
$p=3$ (triangle)  & $\pm 1$                       & 2 & Weak $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ \\
$p=2$ (Majorana)  & $0$                           & 1 & Gravity (geometric) \\
boundary          & $\pm 2$                        & 2 & EM $\mathrm{U}(1)$ \\
\end{tabular}

The effective spectral denominators $\{1,2,3,5\}$ have
$\sum = 11$ and $\prod = 30 = h$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The affine $m$-values $\{0,6,10,12,15,18,20,24,30\}$ group by

\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Bipartite conjugation]\label{thm:bipartite}
For each Cartan eigenvalue~$\mu$, the eigenvector at~$4-\mu$ equals
$\pm$ the eigenvector at~$\mu$ multiplied by~$(-1)^{\ell}$
(where~$\ell$ is the breadth-first-search level from~$\rho_0$).
The nine eigenvalues thus reduce to five independent modes:
four conjugate pairs $(\mu, 4-\mu)$ plus one self-conjugate mode at~$\mu = 2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\tilde{E}_8$ is bipartite (a tree).  For bipartite graphs,
if $Av = \lambda v$ then $A(Sv) = -\lambda(Sv)$ where $S = \mathrm{diag}((-1)^\ell)$.
For $C = 2I - A$: $Cv = \mu v$ implies $C(Sv) = (4-\mu)(Sv)$.
Verified to machine precision for all four pairs.
The self-conjugate mode at $\mu = 2$ satisfies $Sv = +v$ (verified).
\end{proof}

The five independent modes are in bijection with the five Platonic solids:
the icosahedron/dodecahedron (dual pair) correspond to the $0 \leftrightarrow 4$
pair (matter/antimatter), the octahedron/cube to $1 \leftrightarrow 3$,
and the tetrahedron (self-dual) to the self-conjugate mode at~$\mu = 2$.

\begin{corollary}[$\mu = 2$ vanishing on even-dimensional nodes]\label{cor:mu2-vanishing}
The $\mu = 2$ eigenvector of the $\tilde{E}_8$ Cartan matrix is
$(1, 0, -1, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0, -1)$, which vanishes exactly on the
\emph{even-dimensional} nodes $\{\rho_1, \rho_3, \rho_5, \rho_6, \rho_7\}$
(marks $\{2, 4, 6, 4, 2\}$) and is nonzero on the odd-dimensional nodes
$\{\rho_0, \rho_2, \rho_4, \rho_8\}$ (marks $\{1, 3, 5, 3\}$).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\mu = 2$ means $Cv = 2v$, i.e., $Av = 0$ (since $C = 2I - A$).
Solving $Av = 0$ node-by-node on the $\tilde{E}_8$ graph:
$\rho_0$: $v_1 = 0$;
$\rho_1$: $v_0 + v_2 = 0 \Rightarrow v_2 = -v_0$;
$\rho_2$: $v_1 + v_3 = 0 \Rightarrow v_3 = 0$;
$\rho_3$: $v_2 + v_4 = 0 \Rightarrow v_4 = v_0$;
$\rho_4$: $v_3 + v_5 = 0 \Rightarrow v_5 = 0$;
$\rho_7$: $v_6 = 0$;
$\rho_6$: $v_5 + v_7 = 0 \Rightarrow v_7 = 0$;
$\rho_5$: $v_4 + v_6 + v_8 = 0 \Rightarrow v_8 = -v_0$.
Setting $v_0 = 1$ gives $(1, 0, -1, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0, -1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Golden decoherence]\label{thm:decoherence}
Decompose the nine Cartan eigenvalues into integer-frequency modes
$\mathcal{O} = \{\mu \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}\}$ (5~modes) and
golden-frequency modes
$\mathcal{H} = \{\mu \in \{1/\varphi^2, 3{-}\varphi, 2{+}1/\varphi, 2{+}\varphi\}\}$ (4~modes).
For any initial state $|\psi\rangle$ with nonzero projection onto both sectors,
the time-averaged reduced density matrix of~$\mathcal{O}$ is diagonal:
\[
  \overline{\rho}_{\mathcal{O}}
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \lim_{T\to\infty} \frac{1}{T} \int_0^T
  \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(|\psi(t)\rangle\langle\psi(t)|\bigr)\,dt
  \;=\; \rho_{\mathrm{diag}},
\]
with $S(\rho_{\mathrm{diag}}) > 0 = S(|\psi\rangle)$.
The decoherence arises because the golden-to-integer frequency differences
$\mu_h - \mu_o$ are irrational for all $\mu_h \in \mathcal{H}$,
$\mu_o \in \mathcal{O}$, so the off-diagonal coherences average to zero
by the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The off-diagonal matrix elements of~$\rho_\mathcal{O}(t)$ contain
phases $e^{i(\mu_h - \mu_o)t}$ with $\mu_h - \mu_o \notin \mathbb{Q}$
(since $\varphi \notin \mathbb{Q}$ by Niven's theorem, and
all golden eigenvalues involve~$\varphi$).
The time average of $e^{i\omega t}$ over $[0,T]$ tends to~$0$
as $T \to \infty$ for any $\omega \neq 0$ (Riemann--Lebesgue).
The resulting diagonal~$\rho_{\mathrm{diag}}$ has strictly positive
von Neumann entropy whenever the initial state has nontrivial
projection onto at least two eigenmodes of different frequency.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The golden ratio's extremal irrationality (continued-fraction
coefficients all~$1$, giving the slowest rational approximation)
makes $\varphi$ the worst-case for recurrence.  Among all algebraic
irrational frequencies, the golden modes produce the most uniform
time-averaged decoherence (Weyl equidistribution).
\end{remark}
