\section{The spacetime: $\mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$}
\label{sec:spacetime}

\begin{proposition}[The spacetime geometry is uniquely determined by $N$]
\label{prop:spacetime}
The spacetime geometry
$\mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$ is uniquely
determined by the Pell equation (pure number theory,
no geometric input):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textnormal{(i)}]
  The Pell equation selects $N = 7$ ($j = 2$, graviton) and
  $N = 4$ ($j = 1$, gauge boson)---the only polygons with
  integer spin at the critical mode (Paper~I, Proposition~5.4).
\item[\textnormal{(ii)}]
  KK flux additivity gives $N = 11 = 4 + 7$
  (Paper~V: exhaustive elimination, no
  geometric input).
\item[\textnormal{(iii)}]
  $N = 11$ requires negative curvature for stability
  ($f(5,11) = 15 > N{-}1 = 10$), forcing the base to be
  $\mathbf{H}^2$.
\item[\textnormal{(iv)}]
  Thurston classification: among the $8$ geometries,
  only $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$ has a
  negatively curved base with a \emph{non-trivial}
  compact fiber.
  (The product $\mathbf{H}^2 \times S^1$ also has a
  negatively curved base, but has Euler class $e = 0$.
  The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbifold action $\varphi \to
  \varphi + 2\pi/N$ on the fiber generates $N$ cone
  points on the base, each with isotropy
  $\mathbb{Z}_N$.
  Each of the $N$ cone points has isotropy order~$N$
  and holonomy $e^{2\pi i/N}$ around the fiber.
  The Seifert Euler class is
  $e = \sum_{k=1}^N \beta_k/\alpha_k = N \cdot (1/N) = 1$
  in the standard normalization, or $e = N/2$ in the
  Chern class convention ($c_1 = N$, $e = c_1/2$).
  The flux quantization
  $\oint\!dA = \pi N$ (from $c_1 = N$ with the KK
  normalization $\oint\!dA = \pi c_1$) gives
  $|F|^2 = N^2/4$; this differs from the Besse normalization
  ($\oint\!dA = 2\pi e$) by a factor of~$N/(2e) = N/2$,
  because the Seifert Chern class $c_1 = N = 2e$.
  The consistency check
  $\Lambda_3 = 0$ at $N = 4$ (Paper~V, eq.~\ref*{V-eq:lambda})
  verifies the normalization; the Besse convention would give
  $\Lambda_3 = 0$ at $N = 2$, which does not correspond to
  any gauge sector.
  Since $e \ne 0$, the manifold is
  Seifert (non-product).)
\end{enumerate}
No step requires knowing the geometry in advance:
the logical flow is
Pell $\to$ $N = 4, 7$ $\to$ $N = 11$
(KK flux additivity on the single $S^1$ fiber)
$\to$ $\mathbf{H}^2$ (stability) $\to$ Seifert ($e \ne 0$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Steps~(i)--(ii) are number theory (Paper~I + Paper~V).

\emph{Step~(iii) (The base is $\mathbf{H}^2$).}
The cosmological polygon $N = 11$ has critical Casimir
$f(5,11) = 15 > N{-}1 = 10$, so the flat-plane $N$-gon is
\emph{unstable} ($\lambda_5 = 10 - 15 < 0$).
Stability requires $C_1 \ge 15 > N{-}1$, which by the
curvature-stability dichotomy (Paper~I,
Proposition~\ref*{I-prop:curvature-dichotomy})
is achievable only on a negatively curved surface.
On~$\mathbf{S}^2$, positive curvature tightens the bound:
$C_1(\mathbf{S}^2) < N{-}1$ for all $\xi > 0$ (Paper~I, \S4.2).
On~$\mathbf{H}^2$, negative curvature opens it:
$C_1(\mathbf{H}^2, \xi) > N{-}1$ for $\xi > 0$, reaching
$C_1 = 15$ at $\xi = \xi^*(11) = 3 - 2\sqrt{2} \in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$
(Paper~I, \S4.3).
$\mathbf{H}^2$ is the unique constant-curvature surface
admitting a stable $11$-gon.

\emph{Step~(iv) (The fiber is $S^1$).}
Gauge fields arise from Kaluza--Klein reduction on a compact fiber.
The fiber must be one-dimensional for two reasons:
(a)~the Havelock decomposition is a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier
analysis on a \emph{circle} (the polygon ring), which is
one-dimensional by construction;
(b)~a higher-dimensional fiber ($S^2$, $T^2$, \ldots) would
introduce additional isometry generators
(e.g.\ $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ for $S^2$, $\mathrm{U}(1)^2$ for $T^2$)
beyond the single $\mathrm{U}(1)$ from the KK photon,
producing gauge sectors absent from the polygon hierarchy.
The circle $S^1$ is the unique compact connected $1$-manifold.

\emph{Step~(iv) (The Thurston classification forces
$\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$).}
Among the eight Thurston geometries for locally homogeneous
$3$-manifolds, only $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$ has a
negatively curved base with a circle fiber.
The other seven fail:
$\mathbf{E}^3$, Nil, Sol have non-negatively curved bases;
$\mathbf{S}^3$ and $\mathbf{S}^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ have positive
curvature;
$\mathbf{H}^3$ has no fibration;
$\mathbf{H}^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ has Euler class $e = 0$
(trivial fibration, no holonomy) \citep{Thurston1982}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The metric is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric-4d}
  ds^2 = -dt^2 + ds^2_{\mathbf{H}^2}
  + (d\varphi + A_i\,dx^i)^2,
\end{equation}
where $ds^2_{\mathbf{H}^2} = d\rho^2 + \sinh^2\!\rho\;d\theta^2$
($\kappa = -1$) and $A$ is a $\mathrm{U}(1)$ connection on the $S^1$ fiber
with flux
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flux}
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint dA = \frac{N}{2}.
\end{equation}
For odd~$N$, the flux is half-integral; the fiber carries a
$\mathrm{spin}^c$ structure rather than a line bundle
(equivalently, the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbifold of
the integer-flux bundle at level~$N$).
The Kaluza--Klein reduction on $S^1$ decomposes any field into
$N$ modes with masses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kk-masses}
  \mu_m = |m - N/2|, \qquad m = 0, 1, \ldots, N{-}1.
\end{equation}
The critical mode $m = N/2$ (even $N$) is massless: it is the
Havelock critical mode.
The KK energy $E_{\mathrm{KK}} = (m{-}N/2)^2/2 = N^2/8 - f(m,N)$
is complementary to the Casimir: their sum $N^2/8$ is
mode-independent.

\begin{remark}[Low-energy limit is $3{+}1$D Minkowski]
\label{rem:minkowski}
The Seifert spacetime reduces to $3{+}1$D Minkowski at
laboratory scales.  Two length scales control deviations:
(i)~$\ell \sim 10^{26}\,$m (the $\mathbf{H}^2$ curvature radius):

(ii)~$R \sim 10^{-21}\,$m (the $S^1$ radius):
at $r \gg R$, KK modes decouple,
leaving $3{+}1$D gravity $+$ the Standard Model.
Cosmological expansion arises from the time-dependent

\end{remark}

\section{Emergence of the 4D spin-$2$ graviton}
\label{sec:graviton}

Paper~III identified the graviton in $2{+}1$D as the marginal
Havelock mode at $N = 7$.  The central question of this section
is whether the graviton survives the lift to four dimensions.

In $2{+}1$D, pure gravity is topological
\citep{Witten1988} with zero propagating bulk modes.
The $4$D spin-$2$ graviton emerges from the boundary
Virasoro algebra of the Chern--Simons theory.

\begin{derivation}[Emergent spin-$2$ graviton]
\label{thm:graviton-4d}
The $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
Chern--Simons gauge theory on
$\mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$
produces a $4$D spin-$2$ graviton with $2$ tensor polarizations
($+$~and~$\times$).
The spin-$2$ character is derived from the Virasoro algebra
of the boundary CFT; no $4$D gravitational dynamics are assumed.
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
Four steps, each using an established result.

\emph{Step~1 (CS $\to$ boundary Virasoro).}
The $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ CS theory on~$\mathbf{H}^2$
(a manifold with conformal boundary $\partial\mathbf{H}^2 = S^1_\theta$)
induces a $2$D conformal field theory on the boundary.
The asymptotic symmetry algebra is two copies of
the Virasoro algebra at central charge $c = 12\,b(N)$
\citep{BrownHenneaux1986}.
This is a \emph{theorem} about Chern--Simons gauge theory;
it does not require $4$D gravity as input.

\emph{Step~2 (Virasoro $\to$ spin-$2$).}
The stress tensor $T(z)$ of the boundary CFT has conformal
weight~$(2,0)$, and $\bar T(\bar z)$ has weight~$(0,2)$.
This weight is \emph{derived} from the Virasoro OPE
($T(z)T(w) \sim c/(2(z{-}w)^4) + 2T(w)/(z{-}w)^2 + \partial T/(z{-}w)$):
$T$ transforms as a rank-$2$ tensor under conformal
transformations.
This is the spin-$2$ structure of the graviton.
The promotion from $2$D conformal weight to $4$D helicity $\pm 2$
is made precise in Step~3: on the boundary $T^2$, the
momentum $(k_\theta, k_\varphi)$ defines the transverse plane,
and the $4$D massless little group $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ acts by
rotations in this plane; $T(z)$ transforms with eigenvalue
$e^{2i\alpha}$ under a rotation by~$\alpha$, which is
the definition of helicity~$+2$
(and $\bar T(\bar z)$ gives helicity~$-2$).

\emph{Step~3 (Boundary torus $=$ transverse plane).}
The conformal boundary of the Seifert manifold is
$T^2 = S^1_\theta \times S^1_\varphi$
(boundary of $\mathbf{H}^2$ $\times$ fiber $S^1$).
At energies $E \ll M_{\mathrm{poly}}$, the KK tower
is frozen and both circles have wavelength
$\gg 1/E$; the boundary $T^2$ is indistinguishable
from the Euclidean transverse plane~$\mathbb{R}^2$
to corrections $O(E/M_{\mathrm{poly}})^2$.
(The exponent is~$2$, not~$1$: the first KK mode
at momentum $k_1 = M_{\mathrm{poly}}$ shifts the
flat-space propagator $1/k^2$ by
$\delta(1/k^2) = 1/(k^2 + M_{\mathrm{poly}}^2) - 1/k^2
= -M_{\mathrm{poly}}^2/(k^2(k^2 + M_{\mathrm{poly}}^2))
= O(M_{\mathrm{poly}}^2/k^4)$, giving a relative
correction $O(E^2/M_{\mathrm{poly}}^2)$ at energy~$E$.)
The helicity identification is local
(independent of the global topology):
$T(z)$ has conformal weight~$(2,0)$ on $T^2$ or
$\mathbb{R}^2$ equally, and the $\mathrm{SO}(2)$
rotation $z \to e^{i\alpha}z$ gives
$T(z) \to e^{2i\alpha}\,T(z)$, the definition of
helicity~$+2$.
The stress tensor on $T^2$ has
$2 = d(d{-}3)/2|_{d=4}$ independent components
(Remark~\ref{rem:FG-WW} below).

\emph{Step~4 (Polarisation and DOF count).}
The boundary graviton has $2$ independent components
($T$ and $\bar T$), corresponding to the $+$ and $\times$
tensor polarizations.
Both propagate on $T^2$, giving $n_{\mathrm{phys}} = 2$---matching
the standard $d(d{-}3)/2 = 2$ count for $4$D gravity.
The graviton is a boundary mode of a $3$D topological
theory (Remark~\ref{rem:FG-WW}).
The $4$D effective $T_{\mu\nu}$ (which couples to the graviton
at $r \gg R$) is constructed through the \emph{non-local}
holographic dictionary, not as a local $4$D operator
\citep{Maldacena1997}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[FG expansion and Weinberg--Witten]
\label{rem:FG-WW}
The Fefferman--Graham expansion holds for the Seifert metric
\citep{deHaroSolodukhinSkenderis2001, ToldoWillett2018};
for odd~$N$ the gravitational anomaly modifies $T_{ab}$
\citep{KrausLarsen2006} but the DOF count ($2$ polarizations)
is unchanged.
The $4$D graviton is a boundary mode of a $3$D topological
theory: $T(z)$ lives on the $2$D boundary, and the
Weinberg--Witten assumptions (Lorentz-covariant $T^{\mu\nu}$
in the bulk) do not hold in the topological sector.
\end{remark}

At macroscopic distances ($r \gg R \sim 10^{-21}$\,m),

\begin{remark}[Graviton mass spectrum]
\label{rmk:mass-spectrum}
The KK graviton masses are $\mu_m = |m - N/2|$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Even~$N$: the zero mode $m = N/2$ is
  \emph{massless}---an exact $4$D massless graviton.
\item Odd~$N$: the lightest \emph{KK} mode has $\mu = 1/2$ (massive).
  The massless graviton is the boundary stress tensor~$T(z)$,
  which is not a KK mode but the asymptotic symmetry
  generator of the CS theory.
  Its masslessness is topologically protected
  (the Virasoro algebra forbids a mass term for~$T$).
\end{itemize}
At distances $r \gg 1/N$ (the $S^1$ radius),
the KK tower is integrated out and gravity is mediated by the
boundary stress tensor~$T$; at $r \ll 1/N$, the full KK tower
contributes.
The graviton propagator crossover from 3D to 4D
is derived in Proposition~\ref{prop:graviton-propagator}.
\end{remark}

\section{The couplings: all from $c = 12\,b(N)$}

\begin{definition}[Havelock central charge]
\label{def:central-charge}
The \emph{Havelock central charge} is the kinetic coefficient of
the breathing mode in the $N$-vortex system on~$\mathbf{H}^2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-charge}
  c = 12\,b(N), \qquad
  b(N) = \frac{N(N{+}1)}{12} - \ln 2 + \frac{\ln N}{N{-}1}.
\end{equation}
For large~$N$: $c = N^2 + N + O(\ln N)$.
The \emph{instanton fugacity} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:instanton-K}
  \mathcal{K} = e^{-2\pi k_{\mathrm{frac}}}, \qquad
  k_{\mathrm{frac}} = \mathrm{frac}(c/6 - N/2),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{frac}(\cdot)$ denotes the fractional part.
At $N = 7$: $k_{\mathrm{frac}} = 0.096$, $\mathcal{K} = 0.548$.
The instanton fugacity $\mathcal{K}$ enters the fermion mass hierarchy
(\S\ref{sec:mass-hierarchy}), the baryon fraction
(Paper~V), and the Higgs quartic
(\S\ref{sec:consistency}).

\begin{proposition}[Exact coupling relations]
\label{prop:couplings}
All couplings are determined by $c = 12\,b(N)$:
\begin{align}
  G &= \frac{3}{2c}, \label{eq:newton}\\
  \alpha &= \frac{6}{\pi c}, \label{eq:alpha}\\
  \frac{\alpha}{8\pi G} &= \frac{1}{2\pi^2}
    \qquad\text{(exact, $N$-independent)}. \label{eq:coupling-lock}
\end{align}
The quartic vertex from the Magri hierarchy:
$g_4 = 2N f^2/\pi$ at the critical mode.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$G$: from Brown--Henneaux, $c = 3\ell/(2G)$ with $\ell = 1$.
$\alpha$: from the KK gauge coupling on the unit-radius fiber
(this is $\alpha_{\mathrm{U}(1)}$, the KK photon coupling).
The ratio~\eqref{eq:coupling-lock}: $\alpha/(8\pi G)
= [6/(\pi c)] / [8\pi \cdot 3/(2c)] = 6/(12\pi^2) = 1/(2\pi^2)$.
The $N$-dependence cancels algebraically.

The strong coupling: the $3$D CS coupling for $\mathrm{SU}(3)$
at level~$k = 1$ is $\alpha_s^{3\mathrm{D}} = 1/(k + h^\vee)
= 1/4$ (\citealt{Witten1989Jones}), where $h^\vee = 3$
is the dual Coxeter number (the standard one-loop level shift).
The KK reduction on $\Sigma_g \times S^1$ promotes the
$3$D coupling to a $4$D coupling by dividing by the
effective volume $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of the internal space:
$\alpha_s^{4\mathrm{D}}
= \alpha_s^{3\mathrm{D}} \times (k{+}h^\vee) / V_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
The effective volume has three factors, each with an
independent origin:
(i)~the bare CS level $k = 1$ (the topological charge
from $\pi_3(\mathrm{SU}(3)) = \mathbb{Z}$, counting
the number of gauge-field windings per $S^1$ fiber);
(ii)~the Bolza surface area
$4\pi(g{-}1) = 4\pi$ ($g = 2$, $K = -1$),
which is the Gauss--Bonnet volume of the base
$\mathbf{H}^2/\Gamma$; and
(iii)~the CS partition function $Z(S^3) = \sqrt{2}$,
derived from the exact Witten formula
(\citealt{Witten1989Jones}, eq.~(2.21)):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:z-s3}
  Z(S^3, \mathrm{SU}(N), k)
  = \Bigl(\frac{2}{k{+}N}\Bigr)^{\!N/2}
    \prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\bigl(2\sin(\pi j/(k{+}N))\bigr)^{N-j}.
\end{equation}
For $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$: $k{+}N = 4$,
$(2/4)^{3/2} = 1/(2\sqrt{2})$,
$[2\sin(\pi/4)]^2 = 2$,
$[2\sin(\pi/2)]^1 = 2$,
giving $Z = \sqrt{2}$.
This is not a convention choice---it is the exact non-perturbative
result of the CS path integral.
The alternative ``surgery'' normalization
$Z_{\mathrm{surg}} = (k{+}N)^{-N/2}\prod(\cdots) = 1/2$
omits the prefactor $\sqrt{2}^N$; our formula~\eqref{eq:z-s3}
follows Witten's original normalization.
The product $V_{\mathrm{eff}} = k \times 4\pi(g{-}1) \times
Z(S^3)$ is the dimensionless volume that converts the
$3$D CS coupling $g_3^2 = 4\pi/(k{+}h^\vee)$ into
the $4$D YM coupling $g_4^2 = g_3^2/V_{\mathrm{eff}}$
via KK reduction, with $Z(S^3)$ arising from the
zero-mode integral over the flat connection moduli space
(\citealt{Witten1989Jones}, eq.~(2.21)).
Explicitly:
$\alpha_s^{4\mathrm{D}}
= \alpha_s^{3\mathrm{D}} \times (k{+}h^\vee)/V_{\mathrm{eff}}
= [1/(k{+}h^\vee)] \times (k{+}h^\vee) / [k \times 4\pi \times \sqrt{2}]
= 1/(k \times 4\pi\sqrt{2})$.
The $(k{+}h^\vee)$ cancel:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-s}
  \alpha_s(M_{\mathrm{poly}})
  = \frac{1}{k \times 4\pi(g{-}1) \times Z(S^3)}
  = \frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{2}}
  = 0.0563.
\end{equation}
The value $\alpha_s = 1/(4\pi\sqrt{2})$ is derived from
the exact Witten partition function~\eqref{eq:z-s3}
with no convention ambiguity.
One-loop running to $M_Z$ gives
$\alpha_s(M_Z) = 0.115$;
two-loop (RK4 integration of the two-loop
$\beta$-function with threshold matching at $m_t$)
gives $\alpha_s(M_Z) = 0.122$.
The observed value $0.1180 \pm 0.0009$ is bracketed,
consistent within the perturbative uncertainty.
\end{proof}

\section{The Weyl anomaly vanishes}
\label{sec:anomaly}

\begin{theorem}[Zero anomaly on Einstein backgrounds]
\label{thm:zero-anomaly}
On any surface of constant curvature
(the plane, $\mathbf{S}^2$, $\mathbf{H}^2$, or any quotient
$\Gamma\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$), the constrained Havelock eigenvalue
satisfies
\[
  \lambda_m = C_1(\mathrm{surface}, R) - \frac{m(N{-}m)}{2}
\]
with $\delta_m = 0$ \emph{identically}.
The three-layer decomposition is exact with zero Weyl anomaly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Havelock identity (Paper~I, Theorem~\ref{I-thm:riemannian-havelock})
proves that the \emph{constrained} eigenvalue (on $\{J = \mathrm{const}\}$)
has a mode-independent confining potential~$C_1$.
The angular Hessian involves the csc$^2$ kernel from the logarithmic
singularity of the Green's function; the Havelock sum
(Paper~I, Lemma~\ref{I-lem:havelock}) converts this to $m(N{-}m)/2$.
No residual $\delta_m$ survives.
The key: on any constant-curvature surface, the Laplacian Green's
function depends only on geodesic distance, so the mode decomposition
is exact.
Quotients $\Gamma\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$ add a mode-\emph{independent}
correction $\delta C_1$ (from the Selberg trace formula,
Paper~I, Theorem~\ref{I-thm:index-pairing}), which shifts~$C_1$
but does not introduce mode-dependent anomalies.
\end{proof}

\section{The Standard Model gauge group}
\label{sec:gauge-group}

The polygon hierarchy realizes
$\mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$
through three mechanisms, each grounded in established mathematics.

\subsection{$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ from the Chern--Simons formulation of
$2{+}1$D gravity}
\label{sec:chiral-su2}

\begin{derivation}[$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge symmetry]
\label{thm:su2}
The gravitational zero-mode sector of the Kaluza--Klein reduced theory
carries $\mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge symmetry at
Chern--Simons level $k = c/6$.
At $N = 4$, the critical Havelock mode is the KK zero mode
and transforms in the $j = 1$ (adjoint) representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
The proof has four steps.

\emph{Step~1 (KK reduction).}
Compactify the metric~\eqref{eq:metric-4d} on~$S^1$.
The zero-mode ($m = 0$) sector is pure $2{+}1$D gravity on
$\mathbb{R} \times \mathbf{H}^2$ with $\Lambda_3 < 0$ (since $\kappa = -1$).
Massive KK modes ($m \ne 0$) are matter fields coupled to this gravity.

\emph{Step~2 (Witten--Ach\'ucarro--Townsend, \citeyear{Witten1988}).}
Pure $2{+}1$D gravity with $\Lambda < 0$ is equivalent to
Chern--Simons theory with gauge group
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
in Lorentzian signature.
The Euclidean continuation replaces
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ by $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
(\citealt{Witten1988}, \S2.3): the CS path integral
converges for the compact group, and the Lorentzian
observables are recovered by analytic continuation of
the CS partition function.
The level is $k = \ell/(4G)$;
with $\ell = 1$ and $G = 3/(2c)$: $k = c/6 = 2\,b(N)$.
The gravitational sector uses the non-compact
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, which admits irrational level
(Paper~III, \S\ref*{III-sec:graviton});
the compact gauge sectors ($\mathrm{SU}(2)$,
$\mathrm{SU}(3)$, $\mathrm{U}(1)$) are quantized
at $k = 1$ by large gauge invariance.
The representation theory, coupling constants, and
conformal weights used below are all computed in the
Euclidean formulation --- the standard procedure in
gauge theory (all SM cross-sections use Euclidean
Feynman rules, analytically continued to
Minkowski signature).
The compact gauge sectors have integer levels
$k = 1$ for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and
$\mathrm{U}(1)$ (large gauge invariance).
The sectors coexist because their gauge algebras are orthogonal:
$\mathrm{Tr}(T_a^{G_i} T_b^{G_j}) = 0$ for $i \ne j$, so
$S_{\mathrm{total}} = \sum_i S_i$ and $Z = \prod_i Z_i$.
The boundary CFT is
$\mathrm{WZW}_{\mathrm{grav}} \otimes \mathrm{WZW}_{\mathrm{gauge}}$
with $c_{\mathrm{total}} = \underbrace{(12b(N){-}4)}_{c_{\mathrm{grav}}}
+ \underbrace{2}_{c_{\mathrm{SU}(3)}}
+ \underbrace{1}_{c_{\mathrm{SU}(2)}}
+ \underbrace{1}_{c_{\mathrm{U}(1)}} = 12b(N)$
(verified numerically; see Code Availability).

\emph{Step~3 (Zero mode at $N = 4$).}
At $N = 4$, the critical mode $m^* = 2$ has KK mass
$\mu_2 = |2 - 4/2| = 0$: it \emph{is} the zero mode.

\emph{Step~4 (SU(2) adjoint).}
The critical Casimir $f(2,4) = 2 = j(j{+}1)$ with $j = 1$.
The $j = 1$ representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the adjoint
(dimension~$3$).
Therefore the critical gravitational mode at $N = 4$ carries
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ quantum numbers in the adjoint representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The gravity $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts chirally on matter]
\label{prop:chiral-su2}
In the CS formulation, the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R$
gauge connections couple chirally to KK matter modes:
four-dimensional left-handed Weyl fermions transform under
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$, right-handed under $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$.
The Seifert Euler class $e = N/2$ breaks
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ via the parity anomaly, leaving
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ as the unbroken gauge factor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof has three steps.
Witten's CS formulation splits gravity into $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L
\times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ (Step~1).
KK reduction maps 4D chirality to 3D CS sector (Step~2).
The Seifert Euler class $e = N/2$ is parity-odd, breaking
$L \leftrightarrow R$ via the fermion parity anomaly and
the gravitational eta invariant, gapping $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$
(Step~3).

\emph{Step~1 (Chiral decomposition of CS gravity).}
In the first-order formulation \citep{Witten1988},
2+1D gravity with $\Lambda < 0$ decomposes into two
\emph{independent} CS gauge fields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-pm}
  A^+_\mu = \omega_\mu + e_\mu/\ell, \qquad
  A^-_\mu = \omega_\mu - e_\mu/\ell,
\end{equation}
where $\omega_\mu$ is the spin connection and $e_\mu = e_\mu^a T_a$
is the dreibein (both valued in $\mathfrak{su}(2)$).
The $2{+}1$D Dirac operator couples to $A^+$ and $A^-$ with opposite
projections:
$\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu + A^+_\mu)\,P_+ \psi
+ \gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu + A^-_\mu)\,P_- \psi$,
where $P_\pm = (1 \pm \gamma^{(3)})/2$ project onto the two
$2$-component sectors of the $3$D spinor
(\citealt{Witten1988}, \S3; the spin connection couples equally
to both, the dreibein with opposite signs).
On the boundary, this becomes the standard left/right
decomposition of the WZW model:
$A^+ \to$ left-moving $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ currents,
$A^- \to$ right-moving $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ currents.

\emph{Step~2 (KK chirality map).}
The Seifert manifold $\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1$ has metric
$ds^2_3 = ds^2(\mathbf{H}^2) + (d\varphi + A_{\mathrm{KK}})^2$.
A $4$D Weyl spinor $\psi_L(x,\varphi)$ (with definite
$\gamma^5 = \gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ eigenvalue)
decomposes in KK modes:
$\psi_L = \sum_m \chi_m(x)\,e^{i(m+1/2)\varphi}$,
where the half-integer shift arises from the spin connection
on the $S^1$ fiber (the spinor acquires a sign $(-1)$ under
$\varphi \to \varphi + 2\pi$;
see Code Availability).
Under KK reduction, $\gamma^5$-eigenvalue maps to the
$3$D CS sector: $4$D left-handed $\leftrightarrow$ couples to $A^+$
(the dreibein component along $S^1$ determines the chirality
projection, because $\gamma^3 = \gamma^\varphi$ is the fiber direction,
and $\gamma^5 = \gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ reduces to
$\gamma^{(3)} \cdot e^{i\pi m}$ in the KK basis).

\emph{Step~3 (Parity breaking by the Euler class).}
The Seifert manifold is \emph{not} parity-invariant:
the Euler class $e = N/2$ is a pseudoscalar that changes sign
under orientation reversal $\varphi \to -\varphi$.
This breaks the $A^+ \leftrightarrow A^-$ symmetry through
two contributions to the effective CS level:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Fermion parity anomaly} (\citealt{Redlich1984}).
  The KK fermion masses on the Seifert manifold are
  $\mu_m = (m + \tfrac{1}{2} - N/2)/R$ for $m = 0,\ldots,N{-}1$.
  The level shift is
  $\Delta k_{\mathrm{ferm}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_m \mathrm{sgn}(\mu_m)$.
  For even~$N$: the spectrum is symmetric ($\mu$ and $-\mu$
  paired), so $\Delta k_{\mathrm{ferm}} = 0$.
  For odd~$N$: one zero mode at $m = (N{-}1)/2$ gives
  $\Delta k_{\mathrm{ferm}} = \pm 1/2$
  (sign fixed by the fiber orientation).
\item \emph{Gravitational CS invariant.}
  The eta invariant $\eta(M)$ of the Seifert manifold
  $M = \mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1$ contributes an additional
  parity-odd level shift
  $\Delta k_{\mathrm{grav}} = \eta(M)/2$
  that distinguishes $A^+$ from~$A^-$.

  \emph{Derivation.}
  The Seifert manifold~$M$ is a circle bundle over the
  orbifold $\Sigma = \mathbf{H}^2/\mathbb{Z}_N$, which has
  one cone point of order~$N$.
  The adiabatic limit theorem
  \citep{BismutCheeger1989,Dai1991}
  decomposes the eta invariant of the Dirac operator
  on a circle bundle into a base contribution
  and singular-fiber corrections:
  $\eta(M) = \eta(\Sigma) + \eta_{\mathrm{sing}}$.
  The base contribution vanishes:
  $\eta(\Sigma) = 0$ because the Dirac operator on a
  $2$D surface has symmetric spectrum
  ($\lambda \leftrightarrow -\lambda$).
  The singular-fiber correction is determined by the
  local lens-space geometry $L(N,1)$ around the cone
  point:
  $\eta_{\mathrm{sing}} = -4\,s(1,N) + (N{-}1)/(6N)$,
  where $s(a,N) = (1/4N)\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}
  \cot(\pi k/N)\cot(\pi ak/N)$ is the Dedekind sum.
  For $a = 1$: the identity
  $\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\cot^2(\pi k/N) = (N{-}1)(N{-}2)/3$
  gives $s(1,N) = (N{-}1)(N{-}2)/(12N)$, hence
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-grav}
    \eta_{\mathrm{grav}}(N)
    = -4\,s(1,N) + \frac{N{-}1}{6N}
    = -\frac{(N{-}1)(2N{-}5)}{6N}.
  \end{equation}
  This is nonzero for all $N \ge 3$ ($\eta_{\mathrm{grav}} = 0$
  only at $N = 5/2$, which is not an integer).
  Explicit values: $\eta(4) = -3/8$, $\eta(7) = -9/7$,
  $\eta(11) = -85/33$
  (verified numerically; see Code Availability).
\end{enumerate}
The combined asymmetry is
$|k_L^{\mathrm{eff}} - k_R^{\mathrm{eff}}|
= |\eta_{\mathrm{grav}}(N)| = (N{-}1)(2N{-}5)/(6N) > 0$
for all $N \ge 3$.
(The sign of $\eta$ determines which sector is enhanced;
with the standard fiber orientation, $A^-$ has the larger
effective level, so the \emph{physical} weak force is
identified with the sector having the \emph{smaller} effective~$k$.)
The mass gap follows from topologically massive gauge theory
\citep{DeserJackiwTempleton1982}:
a $2{+}1$D gauge field with both a YM kinetic term and
a CS term at level~$k$ acquires a topological mass
$m_{\mathrm{top}} = |k_{\mathrm{eff}}|\,g^2/(4\pi)$.
With $g^2 = 4\pi/k_{\mathrm{bare}}$ from KK reduction,
this gives $m_{\mathrm{top}} = |k_{\mathrm{eff}}/k_{\mathrm{bare}}|/\ell$.
At $N = 7$ with $k_{\mathrm{bare}} = 1$ and
$|\eta_{\mathrm{grav}}| = 9/7$:
$k_R^{\mathrm{eff}} = 1 - 9/14 = 5/14 \approx 0.36$
and $k_L^{\mathrm{eff}} = 1 + 9/14 = 23/14 \approx 1.64$,
giving topological masses
$m_R \approx 0.36/\ell$ and $m_L \approx 1.64/\ell$
(ratio $m_L/m_R \approx 4.6$).
At $\ell \sim 1/M_{\mathrm{poly}}$:
$m_L \sim 490$\,TeV, $m_R \sim 107$\,TeV.
The heavier sector ($m_L$) exceeds
$M_{\mathrm{poly}} \approx 300$\,TeV;
the lighter ($m_R$) is below $M_{\mathrm{poly}}$ but
above the electroweak scale by a factor of~$440$,
unobservable at the LHC.
The assignment of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ to the
\emph{heavier} sector ($k_L = 23/14$) is derived from the
sign of~$\eta$: $\eta < 0$ at $N = 7$ means the sector
with the larger $|k_{\mathrm{eff}}|$ couples more strongly
to left-handed fermions (through the Euler class).
This is determined by the fiber orientation, not a convention.
Both topological masses scale as $|\eta|/(k\ell)$
and exceed $M_{\mathrm{EW}}$ for all $N \ge 4$
(since $|\eta(N)| \ge 1/N > 0$).

\emph{Level quantization.}
The effective levels $k_R^{\mathrm{eff}} = 5/14$
and $k_L^{\mathrm{eff}} = 23/14$ are not integers.
This is standard in topologically massive gauge theory
(\citealt{DeserJackiwTempleton1982}):
the \emph{bare} level $k_{\mathrm{bare}} = 1 \in \mathbb{Z}$
is quantized by $\pi_3(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = \mathbb{Z}$
(invariance under large gauge transformations),
while the eta-invariant shift $\eta/2$ is a one-loop
(perturbative, local) correction that modifies the
effective coupling without changing the topological charge.
The standard Witten shift $k \to k + h^\vee$ ($= k + 2$
for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$) is the same mechanism: a one-loop
renormalization of the coupling, not a new topological
constraint
(\citealt{Dunne1999}, \S3.4).
The sector with the larger effective level has heavier
gauge bosons; at energies $E \ll m_R$, only the
lighter sector propagates.
This is the origin of parity violation in the weak interaction:
\emph{the Seifert geometry is not parity-invariant, so the
gauge theory it generates is not either.}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Confinement and the QCD string tension}
\label{sec:confinement}

\begin{proposition}[Confinement]
\label{prop:confinement}
In the polygon phase ($T < T_{\mathrm{HP}}$, selected by the
Onsager principle~(H2b)), the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gauge theory
is confining: the Wilson loop in the fundamental representation
vanishes exactly, $\langle W_{\mathrm{fund}}\rangle = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Two independent arguments:

\emph{(a)~Verlinde formula (exact in the TQFT sector).}
The $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$ S-matrix has
$S_{0\lambda} = 1/\sqrt{3}$ for all three integrable
representations $\lambda$.
The Wilson loop in the fundamental:
$S_{\mathrm{fund},\lambda}/S_{0,\lambda}
= \omega^{\lambda_1}$ where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$.
On any genus-$g$ surface:
$\langle W_{\mathrm{fund}}\rangle
= (1/Z)\sum_\lambda (S_{\mathrm{fund},\lambda}/S_{0,\lambda})
\,(S_{0,\lambda})^{2-2g}
\propto 1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0$.
This is the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ character sum: the fundamental
has non-trivial center charge, so its expectation value
vanishes identically. No approximation is involved.

\emph{(b)~Center symmetry (conditional on H2b).}
The Polyakov loop transforms as $P \to \omega\,P$ under
$\mathbb{Z}_3$.
The polygon phase has $\mathbb{Z}_7$ symmetry.
Since $\gcd(7, 3) = 1$, the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ center is
unbroken, forcing $\langle P\rangle = 0$.
This argument requires the Onsager principle~(H2b) to
select the polygon saddle---the same conditionality as the
Einstein equation derivation
(Theorem~\ref{III-thm:polygon-einstein}).

In the BTZ phase ($T > T_{\mathrm{HP}}$): the black hole
breaks all discrete symmetries, and
$\langle P\rangle \ne 0$ (deconfined).
The deconfinement transition is the polygon--BTZ
Hawking--Page transition (\S\ref{sec:btz-transition}).
\end{proof}

\begin{derivation}[QCD string tension from the CS path integral]
\label{der:string-tension}
The Wilson loop in the fundamental of $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$
on any genus-$g$ surface vanishes \emph{exactly}
by the Verlinde formula:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verlinde-wilson}
  \langle W_{\mathrm{fund}}\rangle_{\mathrm{TQFT}}
  = \frac{1}{Z}\sum_\lambda
    \frac{S_{\mathrm{fund},\lambda}}{S_{0,\lambda}}
    \,(S_{0,\lambda})^{2-2g}
  = \frac{1+\omega+\omega^2}{Z} \times (\cdots) = 0,
\end{equation}
where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ and $1+\omega+\omega^2 = 0$
is the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ character sum.
This is \emph{exact topological confinement}: the TQFT
sector has infinite string tension.

The \emph{finite} physical string tension arises from
the Yang--Mills sector (the KK kinetic term).
The CS partition function on $\Sigma_g \times S^1$
decomposes as
$Z(\Sigma_g \times S^1) = Z_{\mathrm{free}} \times
Z_{\mathrm{non\text{-}pert}}$,
where $Z_{\mathrm{free}} = Z(S^3)^2$
(the perturbative/reference partition function)
and $Z_{\mathrm{non\text{-}pert}} = \dim\mathcal{H}$
(the Verlinde dimension, from the sum over flat connections).
The physical string tension is the \emph{bare} YM string
tension times the non-perturbative CS enhancement:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:string-tension}
  \frac{\sigma}{\Lambda^2}
  = \sigma_{\mathrm{YM}}
    \times \frac{Z(\Sigma_g \times S^1)}{Z(S^3)^2}
  = \sigma_{\mathrm{YM}}
    \times \frac{\dim\mathcal{H}}{Z(S^3)^2}.
\end{equation}
The ratio $\dim\mathcal{H}/Z^2$ converts from the
CS-regulated bare coupling to the physical normalization:
it counts the number of confining vacua weighted by the
CS measure.

\emph{(i)~The bare YM string tension $\sigma_{\mathrm{YM}}$.}
This has two components on $\mathbf{H}^2$:

\emph{Geometric:}
$\sigma_{\mathrm{polygon}}
= \langle\lambda_{m^*}\rangle_{\mathrm{Airy}}
= 0.1015/\ell^2$
(the Havelock eigenvalue at the WDW ground state,
with $\langle z\rangle_{\mathrm{Ai}} = 0.457$
and Airy scale $a = (2c\,\coth\rho^*)^{1/3} = 4.79$).

\emph{Monopole (Polyakov mechanism,
\citealt{Polyakov1977}; \citealt{AHW1982}):}
On $\mathbf{H}^2$ ($K = -1$), the CS instanton
has curvature-corrected action
\[
  S_{\mathrm{mon}} = 2\pi k\Bigl(1 + \frac{K}{6k^2}\Bigr)
  = 2\pi\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{6}\Bigr) = \frac{5\pi}{3},
\]
where $k = 1$ and the curvature correction
$K/(6k^2) = -1/6$ is the standard Seeley--DeWitt
$a_1 = R/6 = K/3 = -1/3$ contribution
to the instanton determinant
(\citealt{Polyakov1977}, eq.~(7);
$S = 2\pi k(1 + a_1/(2k)) = 2\pi(1 - 1/6)$;
negative curvature reduces the barrier).
determinant prefactor $(5/6)^{3/2} = 0.761$,
and dressed fugacity $y = 0.00405$.
The Polyakov string tension:
$\sigma_{\mathrm{Polyakov}}
= [N^2/(8\pi^2)]\,g^4\,m\,\sqrt{y} = 0.612/\ell^2$.
With $\mathrm{rank}(\mathrm{SU}(3)) = 2$ independent
monopole species (one per simple root):
$\sigma_{\mathrm{YM}}
= 0.1015 + 2 \times 0.612 = 1.326/\ell^2$.

\emph{(ii)~The CS non-perturbative enhancement.}
The CS path integral on $\Sigma_g \times S^1$ gives the
Hilbert-space dimension
$Z(\Sigma_g \times S^1) = \dim\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_g)
= \sum_\lambda (S_{0\lambda})^{2-2g}$
(Verlinde formula; \citealt{Verlinde1988}).
For $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$ on $\Sigma_2$:
three integrable representations with $S_{0\lambda} = 1/\sqrt{3}$,
giving $\dim\mathcal{H} = 3 \times (1/\sqrt{3})^{-2} = 9$.
The $S^3$ partition function $Z(S^3)^2 = 2$ normalises the
free propagator (two $S^3$ caps bounding $\Sigma_g$).
The ratio $\dim\mathcal{H}/Z(S^3)^2 = 9/2$ counts the
number of confining vacua per unit CS propagator weight:
$\sigma_{\mathrm{total}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{YM}} \times
\dim\mathcal{H}/Z(S^3)^2$.

\emph{(iii)~Result.}
\[
  \frac{\sigma}{\Lambda^2}
  = 1.326 \times \frac{9}{2}
  = 5.97.
\]
The lattice value (\citealt{Bali2001}, pure-gauge $\mathrm{SU}(3)$):
$\sigma/\Lambda^2 = 6.25 \pm 0.5$.
The match is $5\%$ with zero free parameters.
The one-loop correction (Supplement~S, \S5.3)---from the
Seeley--DeWitt $a_2 = 4/135$ coefficient, the zero-mode
Jacobian on $\mathbf{H}^2$, and the monopole--monopole
interaction---shifts the tree-level value to
$\sigma/\Lambda^2 = 6.46$, within the lattice error bar.
\end{derivation}

\subsection{$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ from the McKay correspondence at $N = 7$}
\label{sec:su3-mckay}

\begin{derivation}[$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gauge symmetry
  from the Klein quartic]
\label{thm:su3}
The $N = 7$ vortex system determines the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$
gauge symmetry --- representation structure and dynamics ---
through the Klein quartic
$X(7) = \Gamma(7)\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\emph{(a)}] \emph{Representation structure.}
  $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$ embeds faithfully
  in $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ via $\chi_3 = H^0(X(7), \Omega^1)$,
  with $\chi_3 \otimes \bar\chi_3 = \chi_1 \oplus \chi_8$.
\item[\emph{(b)}] \emph{Gauge dynamics.}
  $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ at the minimal compact level $k = 1$
  defines a Chern--Simons gauge theory on the Seifert manifold.
  By the CS/WZW correspondence \citep{Witten1988},
  the CS theory on a manifold with boundary induces the
  $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$ WZW model on the boundary,
  whose Kac--Moody currents $J^a(z)$ ($a = 1,\ldots,8$)
  are the dynamical gluons (conformal weight $(1,0)$ = spin~$1$).
\end{enumerate}
Part~(a) is representation theory; part~(b) is the
Witten CS/WZW theorem \citep{Witten1989Jones}.
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}[Proof of part~(a): representation structure]
Five algebraic steps.

\emph{1.\ Frobenius orbits (derived, not chosen).}
The multiplicative group
$(\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$
has exactly four subgroups.
A non-abelian gauge symmetry requires a proper non-trivial
subgroup $\Gamma$ whose orbits form conjugate multiplets
under CP (the palindromic involution $\sigma_6\colon m \mapsto 7{-}m$).
We show $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ is the unique choice
by exhaustive exclusion:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $\{1\}$: trivial --- no gauge symmetry.
\item $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \{1, 6\}$:
  acts by $m \mapsto {-}m$ --- this \emph{is} the palindromic
  involution, already identified with CP/conformal inversion
  (Paper~I, Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:palindrome}).
  Using it as a gauge symmetry would conflate color with CP.
\item $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$: the full group ---
  gives a single orbit of size~$6$ with no complementary
  decomposition into $\mathbf{3} \oplus \bar{\mathbf{3}}$.
\item $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} = \{1, 2, 4\}$:
  the unique remaining subgroup.
  Orbits $O_+ = \{1, 2, 4\}$, $O_- = \{3, 5, 6\}$
  are exchanged by CP ($\sigma_6(O_+) = O_-$),
  giving a genuine $\mathbf{3} \oplus \bar{\mathbf{3}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\end{equation}

with $k_Y = 1$ the U(1) compact-boson level
(distinguished from $K$ for Gaussian curvature).
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
Three steps: derive the quantum numbers from the Casimir,
derive the CS gauge couplings from the DHVW levels,
compute the mixing angle from the KK-reduced action.

\emph{Step~1 (Quantum numbers from the Havelock Casimir).}
The critical mode at $N = 4$ has Havelock Casimir
$f(2,4) = m^*(N{-}m^*)/2 = 2$.
This determines two quantum numbers:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Casimir:
  $C_2(j) = j(j{+}1) = f(m^*,N) = 2$, giving $j = 1$.
\item $\mathrm{U}(1)$ charge:
  $Q = m^*/N = 2/4 = 1/2$.
\end{itemize}
Both are read off from the vortex spectrum.
No choice is involved.  The identification
$C_2^{\mathrm{SU}(2)} = f(m^*,N)$ is the Helgason symmetric
space theorem (Proposition~III-2.1): on $\mathbf{H}^2 = \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2)$,
the $\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ Casimir equals the Laplacian, which
restricted to the $m$-th $\mathbb{Z}_N$ mode gives $m(N{-}m)/2$.

\emph{Step~2 (CS gauge couplings).}
At the Chern--Simons threshold
\citep{Witten1988}:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{SU}(2)$:
  $g_W^2 = 2\pi/((k{+}h^\vee)\,R) = 2\pi/(3R)$
  \quad($k = 1$, $h^\vee(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = 2$);
\item $\mathrm{U}(1)$:
  $g_Y^2 = 2\pi/(k_Y\,R) = 2\pi/R$
  \quad($k_Y = 1$).
\end{itemize}
The level $k = k_Y = 1$ is forced by the orbifold
construction at \emph{both} gauge groups---not by minimality:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{SU}(3)$: the Frobenius at $N = 7$ generates
  $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$; the DHVW twist-field OPE
  produces $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(3)_1$ at level~$1$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:su3}, Step~B3).
\item $\mathrm{SU}(2)$: the Frobenius at $N = 4$ generates
  $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (since $3^2 \equiv 1 \bmod 4$,
  $\sigma\colon m \mapsto 3m$ has order~$2$).
  The McKay correspondence for $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
  \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ gives the $A_1$ Dynkin diagram
  $= \mathrm{SU}(2)$.
  The DHVW construction produces
  $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(2)_1$ at level~$1$.
\item $\mathrm{U}(1)$: $k_Y = 1$ is fixed by single-valued holonomy
  on~$S^1$.  In the $S^3$ regime, $k_Y = e = 1$ from the Euler class
  of the Hopf bundle; in the Seifert regime, $k_Y = e/N$ from the
  orbifold normalization.
\end{itemize}
In all three cases, $k = 1$ follows from the
DHVW orbifold construction, independently of the
parent theory's level.
The mechanism: adjacent twist fields
$\sigma_k \times \sigma_{k+1}$ produce Kac--Moody
currents $J^a(z)$ at conformal weight $h = 1$.
The current--current OPE
$J^a(z)\,J^b(w) \sim k\,\delta^{ab}/(z{-}w)^2 + \ldots$
has level $k$ determined by the twist-field normalization,
which for the standard DHVW construction gives $k = 1$
for any $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$
\citep{DixonHarveyVafaWitten1985}.
The parent central charge $c = 12\,b(N)$ enters the
gravitational sector but does not affect the gauge level.
As a cross-check: $k = 2$ would give $\sin^2\theta_W = 1/3$,
and $k = 3$ gives $5/13$; both are experimentally excluded.

\emph{Step~3 (Mixing angle from KK-reduced Yang--Mills couplings).}
The KK reduction of $\mathrm{CS}_3$ on $\mathbf{H}^2 \times S^1(R)$
at level~$k$ gives $\mathrm{YM}_2$ on~$\mathbf{H}^2$
with bare coupling $g^2 = 2\pi/((k{+}h^\vee)\,R)$
(\citealt{BlauThompson1993}; the shift $k \to k{+}h^\vee$
is the standard one-loop CS renormalization).
The gauge--matter vertex couples with strength $g \times T^a_R$
(the Lie-algebra generator in representation~$R$),
so the \emph{effective} coupling for matter in representation~$R$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conformal-coupling}
  \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}(R)
  \;=\; g^2 \times C_2(R)
  \;=\; \frac{2\pi\,C_2(R)}{(k{+}h^\vee)\,R}.
\end{equation}
The Lie-algebra Casimir $C_2(R) = \sum_a (T^a_R)^2$ is a property
of the representation, independent of~$k$.
The fiber radius~$R$ cancels in the mixing angle ratio:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weinberg-cs}
  \sin^2\theta_W
  = \frac{g_Y^2\,C_2(Y)}{g_Y^2\,C_2(Y) + g_W^2\,C_2(W)}
  = \frac{Q^2/k_Y}{\;Q^2/k_Y \;+\; j(j{+}1)/(k{+}h^\vee)\;}.
\end{equation}
The numerator and denominator are products of two
independent factors: the YM coupling $g^2 = 2\pi/((k{+}h^\vee)R)$
(determined by the CS level via KK reduction) and the
Lie-algebra Casimir $C_2$ (a property of the representation,
independent of~$k$).
Substituting from Steps~1--2
($Q^2 = 1/4$, $k_Y = 1$, $j = 1$, $k{+}h^\vee = 3$):
\[
  \sin^2\theta_W
  = \frac{1/4}{1/4 + 2/3}
  = \frac{3}{11}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gauge--matter factorization]\label{thm:gauge-matter}
The theory has three cleanly separated sectors:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\emph{(T)}] \emph{Topological sector} (3D CS):
  determines the gauge group and level~$k$.
  The WZW integrability bound $j \le k/2$ constrains this sector
  (conformal blocks, Verlinde formula, anyonic braiding).
\item[\emph{(D)}] \emph{Dynamical sector} (2D KK-reduced YM):
  the KK reduction of $\mathrm{CS}_3$ on
  $\mathbf{H}^2 \times S^1(R)$ produces $\mathrm{YM}_2$
  on~$\mathbf{H}^2$ with coupling
  $g^2 = 2\pi/((k{+}h^\vee)\,R)$ (Blau--Thompson).
\item[\emph{(G)}] \emph{Geometric sector} (polygon on~$\mathbf{H}^2$):
  the Havelock critical mode has Casimir
  $C_2 = f(m^*,N) = m^*(N{-}m^*)/2$, an eigenvalue of the
  $\mathbf{H}^2$ Laplacian determined by the polygon alone.
\end{enumerate}
The effective coupling factorizes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge-matter}
  \alpha(R) = \underbrace{g^2}_{\text{(D) gauge}} \times
  \underbrace{C_2(R)}_{\text{(G) matter}},
\end{equation}
where $g^2$ comes from sector~(D) and $C_2$ from sector~(G).
The integrability bound from sector~(T) is irrelevant: it
constrains topological excitations (Wilson loops),
not geometric excitations (polygon displacements).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(a) From CS to YM (sectors T$\to$D).}
The CS action
$S_{\mathrm{CS}} = (k/4\pi)\int \mathrm{Tr}(A \wedge dA + \tfrac{2}{3}A^3)$
on $\mathbf{H}^2 \times S^1(R)$ has no propagating degrees of freedom
in~$3$D.  The KK reduction on~$S^1(R)$ produces a $2$D YM kinetic term
$\tfrac{1}{4g^2}\int F^2$ with coupling
$g^2 = 2\pi/((k{+}h^\vee)\,R)$ (\citealt{BlauThompson1993};
the shift $k \to k{+}h^\vee$ is the standard one-loop CS
renormalization).
This is the step that creates a propagating gauge field.

\emph{(b) Geometric matter (sector G).}
The vortex critical mode at $N = 4$ has Casimir
$f(2,4) = 2 \times 2 / 2 = 2 = j(j{+}1)$ with $j = 1$.
This is a \emph{geometric} quantity: the eigenvalue of the
$\mathbf{H}^2$ Laplacian restricted to the $m = 2$ Fourier mode.
The mode exists independently of the gauge field ---
it is a polygon displacement on~$\mathbf{H}^2$,
present before the CS theory is introduced.
It couples to the gauge field through the standard
covariant derivative $D_\mu = \partial_\mu - ig\,A^a_\mu\,T^a_R$
of the KK-reduced YM theory, giving
$\alpha = g^2 C_2(R)$ at one-gluon exchange.

\emph{(c) Why the integrability bound is irrelevant.}
The quantum dimension
$d_j = \sin\bigl((2j{+}1)\pi/(k{+}2)\bigr)/\sin(\pi/(k{+}2))$
is the norm of a \emph{Wilson loop} (a topological
excitation in sector~T).
At $k = 1$, $j = 1$: $d_1 = \sin(3\pi/3)/\sin(\pi/3) = 0$
--- the $j = 1$ Wilson loop has zero norm and does not exist
as a state in the CS Hilbert space.
But the vortex mode is \emph{not a Wilson loop}.
It is a localized excitation on~$\mathbf{H}^2$
(sector~G), coupled to the gauge field through the
YM vertex (sector~D).
The vanishing $d_1 = 0$ confirms the clean separation:
the $j = 1$ representation has no topological content
and enters the physics purely through the perturbative
YM coupling.

\emph{(d) The Weinberg angle uses only sectors D and G.}
The effective couplings are
$\alpha_W = g_W^2 \cdot C_2(j{=}1) = (2\pi/3R) \cdot 2$
and $\alpha_Y = g_Y^2 \cdot Q^2 = (2\pi/R) \cdot 1/4$.
The common factor $2\pi/R$ cancels in the mixing ratio:
$\sin^2\theta_W = \alpha_Y/(\alpha_Y + \alpha_W) = 3/11$.
The topological sector (where $d_1 = 0$) does not enter.

\emph{(e) Cross-check: $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$.}
Gluons have $C_2(\mathrm{adj}) = 3$, also non-integrable at $k = 1$.
They propagate through the YM kinetic term (sector~D),
not through topological braiding (sector~T).
This is standard in all CS--matter theories
\citep{ABJM2008}: the CS level determines the gauge coupling,
the matter representation is a separate physical input.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral derivation of the electroweak couplings}
\label{sec:spectral-weinberg}

The CS inverse couplings that enter the Weinberg angle can be derived
independently from the spectral structure of the $\tilde{E}_8$ Cartan matrix
(Paper~I, \S\ref{I-sec:mark-distribution};
Paper~V, \S\ref{V-sec:spectral-primes}).
This provides a graph-theoretic derivation of the coupling constants
that enter~\eqref{eq:weinberg-cs}, without invoking Chern--Simons theory
directly.

\begin{lemma}[Spectral--CS bridge]\label{lem:spectral-bridge}
The spectral denominators $\{1, 2, 3, 5\}$ of the $\tilde{E}_8$
Cartan matrix are the \emph{stabilizer orders} of the icosahedral
symmetry axes (trivial, edge~$\mathbb{Z}_2$, face~$\mathbb{Z}_3$,
vertex~$\mathbb{Z}_5$).
The dual Coxeter numbers of the SM gauge groups equal
the corresponding stabilizer orders:
$h^\vee(\mathrm{SU}(3)) = 3 = |\mathbb{Z}_3|$ (face),
$h^\vee(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = 2 = |\mathbb{Z}_2|$ (edge),
$h^\vee(\mathrm{U}(1)) = 0$ (trivial).
Consequently, the CS inverse couplings at level~$k = 1$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-cs-bridge}
  1/g^2(\mathrm{SU}(3)) = 1 + 3 = 4, \quad
  1/g^2(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = 1 + 2 = 3, \quad
  k_Y = 1.
\end{equation}
The vertex stabilizer $\mathbb{Z}_5$ gives
$h^\vee(\mathrm{SU}(5)) = 5$ (the GUT group),
which breaks to $\mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$
at the $K = 0$ transition.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The spectral denominators $k = h/\gcd(m,h)$ for the affine
$m$-values are: $k = 1$ (boundary, $m \in \{0,30\}$),
$k = 2$ (Majorana, $m = 15$),
$k = 3$ (triangle, $m \in \{10,20\}$),
$k = 5$ (golden, $m \in \{6,12,18,24\}$).

Each prime $p \mid h = 30$ corresponds to an icosahedral
rotation axis with stabilizer $\mathbb{Z}_p$:
$p = 5$ (vertices, $12$ elements of order~$5$ or~$10$),
$p = 3$ (faces, $20$ elements of order~$3$ or~$6$),
$p = 2$ (edges, $30$ elements of order~$4$).
The orbifold gauge group at each axis is $\mathrm{SU}(p)$ via
the McKay correspondence $\mathbb{Z}_p \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)
\to \tilde{A}_{p-1}$, with $h^\vee(\mathrm{SU}(p)) = p$.

For the SM gauge groups after breaking: $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ retains
$h^\vee = 3$ from the face stabilizer,
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ retains $h^\vee = 2$ from the edge stabilizer,
and $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is abelian ($h^\vee = 0$).
The universal CS level $k = 1$ (Hopf Euler class, \S\ref{sec:weinberg})
gives~\eqref{eq:spectral-cs-bridge}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Spectral Weinberg angle]\label{thm:spectral-weinberg}
The spectral denominators of the electroweak sectors recover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-weinberg}
  \sin^2\theta_W
  = \frac{Q^2/k_Y}{Q^2/k_Y + j(j{+}1)/(k{+}h^\vee)}
  = \frac{1/4}{1/4 + 2/3}
  = \frac{3}{11}
  = \frac{a-1}{a + N_{\mathrm{crit}}},
\end{equation}
where $a = k_1(E_8) = 4$ and $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$.
The formula factorizes into two independent contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Spectral (from the $\tilde{E}_8$ graph):}
  $k_W/k_Y = 3$ (the CS coupling ratio from Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral-bridge}).
\item \emph{Havelock (from the vortex spectrum on~$\mathbf{H}^2$):}
  $C_2/Q^2 = 2/(1/4) = 8$ (the Casimir-to-charge ratio from the
  critical mode $m^* = 2$, $N = 4$).
\end{itemize}
The Weinberg angle is $\sin^2\theta_W = 3/(3 + 8) = 3/11$:
the spectral ratio divided by the sum of spectral and Havelock ratios.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the spectral--CS bridge (Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral-bridge}):
$k + h^\vee(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = 3 = k_W$ and $k_Y = 1$.
By the Havelock Casimir (Step~1, \S\ref{sec:weinberg}):
$j = 1$ ($C_2 = 2$) and $Q = 1/2$ ($Q^2 = 1/4$),
where $N = 4$ is the unique polygon in $[3, N_{\mathrm{crit}}]$
whose critical Casimir $f(\lfloor N/2\rfloor, N)$ gives a
positive integer $j < 2$ (gauge boson, not graviton;
see Step~1 for the exhaustive check).
Then $\alpha_Y/\alpha_W = (Q^2 \cdot k_W)/(C_2 \cdot k_Y)
= (1/4 \cdot 3)/(2 \cdot 1) = 3/8$,
and $\sin^2\theta_W = (3/8)/(1 + 3/8) = 3/11$.
The numerator $3 = k_W/k_Y$ is the spectral contribution;
the denominator $11 = 3 + 8$ adds the Havelock contribution
$C_2 \cdot k_Y / Q^2 = 8$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bernoulli--Coxeter identity (classical)]\label{lem:bernoulli-coxeter}
$B_4 = B_8 = -1/30 = -1/h(E_8)$.
Consequently $\zeta(-3) = 1/120 = 1/|I^*|$ and
$\zeta(-7) = 1/240 = 1/|\text{roots}(E_8)|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By von Staudt--Clausen, $\mathrm{denom}(B_{2k}) = \prod_{(p-1)|2k} p$.
For $B_4$: $(p{-}1)|4$ gives $p \in \{2,3,5\}$, denom${}= 30$.
For $B_8$: $(p{-}1)|8$ gives $p \in \{2,3,5\}$
(since $p = 9 = 3^2$ is not prime), denom${}= 30$.
Both numerators are~$-1$ by the Bernoulli recursion.
Then $\zeta(-3) = -B_4/4 = (1/30)/4 = 1/120$
and $\zeta(-7) = -B_8/8 = (1/30)/8 = 1/240$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The identity $B_4 = B_8$ relies on $\operatorname{rank}(E_8) + 1 = 9 = 3^2$
being composite.  If~$9$ were prime, $\mathrm{denom}(B_8) = 270 \neq 30$,
and the Riemann zeta would not encode~$|I^*|$ at~$s = -3$.
The coincidence $B_4 = B_8$ depends on the specific value
$\operatorname{rank}(E_8) + 1 = 9 = 3^2$ (composite, with $3 \mid h$).
\end{remark}

\section{The action}
\label{sec:action}

The complete Havelock field theory action on the spacetime
$M_4 = \mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:action}
  S = S_{\mathrm{grav}} + S_{\mathrm{CS}} + S_{\mathrm{gauge}}
    + S_{\mathrm{scalar}} + S_{\mathrm{ferm}} + S_{\mathrm{Yuk}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Gravitational sector.}
After KK reduction on~$S^1$, the $2{+}1$D gravitational action
is the Chern--Simons action:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-CS}
  S_{\mathrm{CS}} = \frac{k}{4\pi} \int_{M_3}
  \mathrm{Tr}\!\bigl(A \wedge dA + \tfrac{2}{3} A \wedge A \wedge A\bigr),
  \qquad k = \frac{c}{6} = 2\,b(N),
\end{equation}
with $A$ the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ (Euclidean) gauge field.

\paragraph{$\mathrm{U}(1)$ gauge sector.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-gauge}
  S_{\mathrm{gauge}} = -\frac{1}{4\alpha}
  \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\; F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu},
  \qquad \alpha = \frac{6}{\pi c}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Scalar sector (KK modes).}
$N$ charged scalars $\Phi_m$ ($m = 0, \ldots, N{-}1$)
with KK masses $\mu_m = |m - N/2|$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-scalar}
  S_{\mathrm{scalar}} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g} \sum_{m=0}^{N-1}
  \bigl[|D_\mu \Phi_m|^2 - \mu_m^2 |\Phi_m|^2\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $D_\mu \Phi_m = (\partial_\mu - i m A_\mu) \Phi_m$.

\paragraph{Fermion sector.}
$N$ Dirac fermions $\Psi_m$ with masses $\mu^f_m = |m - (N{-}1)/2|$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-ferm}
  S_{\mathrm{ferm}} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g} \sum_{m=0}^{N-1}
  \bar\Psi_m \bigl(i\!\not\!\!D - \mu^f_m\bigr) \Psi_m.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Yukawa sector.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-Yuk}
  S_{\mathrm{Yuk}} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g} \sum_{i,j}
  Y_{ij}\, \bar\Psi_i\, \Phi\, \Psi_j + \mathrm{h.c.},
\end{equation}
with the selection rule
$m_i - m_j + m_H \equiv 0 \pmod{N}$
(KK charge conservation on~$S^1$).

\medskip\noindent
All couplings are determined by $c = 12\,b(N)$.

\paragraph{The 4D effective Lagrangian.}
At energies $E \ll M_{\mathrm{poly}}$, the KK modes
(graviphoton, radion, and the KK tower) decouple,
and the effective theory on~$\mathbb{R}^{3,1}$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L-4D}
\begin{split}
  \mathcal{L}_{4\mathrm{D}} = {} &
  -\frac{1}{16\pi G_4}(R - 2\Lambda) \qquad\text{[gravity, from boundary $T$]}\\
  & -\tfrac{1}{4}\textstyle\sum_a F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a,\mu\nu}
  \qquad\text{[$\mathrm{SU}(3){\times}\mathrm{SU}(2){\times}\mathrm{U}(1)$,
  from boundary $J^a$]}\\
  & + \bar\Psi(i\!\not\!\!D - Y H)\Psi
  \qquad\text{[3 gen.\ fermions, $\mathbf{H}^2$ radial profiles on $\mathbf{H}^2$]}\\
  & + |D_\mu H|^2 - V(H)
  \qquad\text{[Higgs, $v = 246$\,GeV, $\lambda_H = 0.122$]}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
All couplings in~\eqref{eq:L-4D} are determined by $N = 7$
and $M_P$; no free parameters remain.

\section{UV completion}
\label{sec:uv}

The theory is UV-complete in the following precise sense.

\paragraph{Non-perturbative definition.}
The boundary CFT is a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbifold theory at
central charge $c = 12\,b(N)$, with
known partition function, OPE coefficients, and conformal blocks.
By the CS/WZW theorem \citep{Witten1988},
this boundary theory defines the bulk Chern--Simons theory
at $k = c/6 = 2\,b(N)$ non-perturbatively.
At leading order, $c = N(N{+}1) + O(\ln N)$; the
subleading corrections ($-12\ln 2 + 12(\ln N)/(N{-}1)$) encode
the regularized vortex self-energy.
The $c$-theorem is satisfied: the free-field UV limit
has $c_{\mathrm{UV}} = N(N{+}1)$, and $c_{\mathrm{IR}} = 12\,b(N)
< N(N{+}1)$ for all $N \ge 3$.
There is a single central charge throughout: $c = 12\,b(N)$
enters both the Brown--Henneaux relation ($G = 3/(2c)$)
and the WDW breathing-mode equation.

\paragraph{Super-renormalisability.}
After KK reduction to $1{+}1$D, the superficial degree of divergence
is $D = 2 - E$ ($E$ = external legs).
Only the $2$-point function ($D = 0$) has a logarithmic divergence;
all vertices with $E \ge 4$ are UV-finite.
Two renormalization parameters suffice: the vortex core size~$a$
(UV cutoff for self-energy) and the central charge~$c$ (overall
energy scale).

\paragraph{Physical observables.}
All physical observables---Havelock eigenvalues, partition function,

\subsection{The CKM phase \textnormal{[exact]}}

\begin{derivation}[CKM phase from the scattering phase on~$\mathbf{H}^2$]
\label{thm:ckm-phase}
The CKM CP-violating phase is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta-ckm}

\end{equation}
The observed value is $\delta = 69^\circ \pm 3^\circ$ (PDG~2024);
the discrepancy is~$1.2^\circ$ ($40\%$ of~$1\sigma$).
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
Four steps: determine which mode contributes, derive the
spectral parameter from the KK structure, compute the overlap
integral, evaluate the phase.

\emph{Step~1 (Only the BF-crossing mode contributes).}
For generation~3, mode $m = 3$ ($\mu_7 = 0$):
the conformal dimension transitions from
$c_{\mathrm{up}} = \mu_4 - T_3 = \tfrac{1}{2}$ (up-type)
to $c_{\mathrm{dn}} = \mu_4 + T_3 = \tfrac{3}{2}$ (down-type),
crossing the BF bound $c = 1$ ($M^2 = c(c{-}1) = 0$).
All other modes have $c_{\mathrm{up}} > 1$
(since $\mu_7 \ge 1$ gives $c \ge 1.12$);
for these, both $c_{\mathrm{up}}$ and $c_{\mathrm{dn}}$
are real and positive, so $\arg\Gamma(c) = 0$---no
phase difference and no contribution to~$\delta$.
The CKM phase comes entirely from generation~3.

\emph{Step~2 (The spectral parameter $m = 1$ from the
conformal dimension sweep).}
The $\mathbf{H}^2$ radial profile at conformal dimension~$c$
is the conical (Mehler) function
$P_{-1/2+is}(\cosh\rho)$ with spectral parameter
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-param}
  s \;=\; c - \tfrac{1}{2}.
\end{equation}
This is the \emph{standard parameterization}
of the Mehler function (DLMF~\S14.20): $c$ and $s$ are
related by an additive constant.

As the conformal dimension sweeps from
$c_{\mathrm{up}} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ to
$c_{\mathrm{dn}} = \tfrac{3}{2}$
(the BF crossing of Step~1),
the spectral parameter sweeps from
$s_{\mathrm{up}} = 0$ to $s_{\mathrm{dn}} = 1$:
\[
  \Delta s \;=\; \Delta c
  \;=\; c_{\mathrm{dn}} - c_{\mathrm{up}}
  \;=\; \tfrac{3}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2} \;=\; 1.
\]
The $\mathbf{H}^2$ scattering matrix for the Dirac operator
at spectral parameter~$s$ is
$S(s) = \Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+is)/\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-is)$,
with scattering phase $\arg S(s) = 2\arg\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+is)$.
At $s = 0$: $S(0) = 1$ (zero phase; $\Gamma(1/2)$ is real).
At $s = 1$: $S(1) = \Gamma(1/2+i)/\Gamma(1/2-i)$, which
is well-defined ($\Gamma$ has no poles on the line
$\mathrm{Re}(z) = 1/2$).
The accumulated phase from $s = 0$ to $s = 1$ is
$\delta = \int_0^1 \tfrac{d}{ds}\arg\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+is)\,ds
= \int_0^1 \mathrm{Im}\,\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}+is)\,ds$,
where $\mathrm{Im}\,\psi(1/2+is) = (\pi/2)\tanh(\pi s)$
is smooth on $[0,1]$ (no singularity at $s = 0$).

The only input is the standard parameterization
$s = c - \tfrac{1}{2}$; the value $\Delta s = 1$ follows
by arithmetic.

\emph{Step~3 (The overlap integral is real).}
The conical function $P_{-1/2+im}(\cosh\rho)$ is real for
all real~$m$ and~$\rho > 0$
(it is the Laplace integral
$(1/\pi)\!\int_0^\pi\!(\cosh\rho + \sinh\rho\cos t)^{-1/2}
\cos(m\log(\cosh\rho + \sinh\rho\cos t))\,dt$,
a real integrand).
The Yukawa overlap
$I(m) = \int_0^{\rho^*}\! P_{-1/2+im}(\cosh\rho)\,w(\rho)\,
\sinh\rho\;d\rho$
(with $w = f_R\,\varphi_H > 0$) is therefore real
and positive ($I(1) = 0.426 > 0$).
The positivity $I(m) > 0$ follows analytically: the conical function
satisfies $P_{-1/2+im}(\cosh\rho) > 0$ for all $\rho > 0$ and
$m \in \mathbb{R}$, by the integral representation
$P_\nu(x) = (2/\pi)\!\int_0^{\pi/2}\!(x + \sqrt{x^2{-}1}\cos\theta)^\nu\,d\theta > 0$

\begin{proposition}[4D graviton propagator]
\label{prop:graviton-propagator}
The long-range $4$D graviton propagator is the standard linearized
GR result:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graviton-propagator}
  \langle h^{(4)}_{ij}(k)\,h^{(4)}_{kl}(-k)\rangle
  = \frac{16\pi G_4}{k_4^2}\,\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ijkl},
\end{equation}
with $2$ tensor polarizations ($+$, $\times$) and no scalar or vector
admixture at $k \ll M_{\mathrm{poly}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Four steps, each using a standard result.

\emph{Step~1 (Boundary $\langle TT\rangle$).}
In the orbifold CFT at central charge~$c$, the stress tensor
$2$-point function is fixed by conformal invariance:
$\langle T_{ij}(k)\,T_{kl}({-}k)\rangle
  = (c/24\pi)\,|k|^3\,\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ijkl}$,
where $\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{TT}}$ is the transverse-traceless projector.
This follows from conservation ($k^i\langle T_{ij}\rangle = 0$),
tracelessness ($\delta^{ij}\langle T_{ij}\rangle = 0$),
and conformal dimension~$2$.

\emph{Step~2 (Brown--Henneaux map).}
The normalizable bulk metric perturbation in AdS$_3$ satisfies
$h^{(2)}_{ij}(k) = (16\pi G_3/c)\,\langle T_{ij}(k)\rangle$
\citep{BrownHenneaux1986}.
This is a \emph{tensor} equation: $h$ inherits the
$\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{TT}}$ structure of~$T$.

\emph{Step~3 (KK mode sum).}
The boundary $\mathbb{R}{\times}S^1_\theta$ lifts on $S^1_\varphi$
(circumference $2\pi R = 2\pi/M_{\mathrm{poly}}$) to
$\mathbb{R}{\times}T^2$.
The KK mode sum
$G(k_3, x_\varphi) = \sum_n G_{2\mathrm{D}}(k_3, m_n{=}n/R)\,
e^{in x_\varphi/R}$
converts the $2$D propagator to a $4$D one:
in the regime $k \gg 1/R$, the sum yields
$G^{(4\mathrm{D})} = G_3/(k_4^2 \times 2\pi R)
= G_4/k_4^2$.
The $\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{TT}}$ projector is \emph{preserved}:
$S^1_\varphi$ is orthogonal to the TT projection directions
(which act on the $\mathbf{H}^2$ spatial coordinates).

\emph{Step~4 (Assembly).}
Combining Steps~1--3:
$\langle h^{(4)}_{ij}\,h^{(4)}_{kl}\rangle
= (16\pi G_4/k_4^2)\,\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ijkl}$,
with $G_4 = G_3/(2\pi R) = 1/M_P^2$ (matching the hierarchy formula).
In position space, the standard Fourier transform
$\int \frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\,e^{i\boldsymbol{k}{\cdot}\boldsymbol{r}}/k^2
= 1/(4\pi r)$ yields Newton's $1/r$ potential
(Corollary~\ref{cor:newton}).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Newton's law from the Seifert geometry]
\label{cor:newton}
The static gravitational potential between two masses $m_1$, $m_2$
at separation~$r$ on the Seifert spacetime is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-potential}
  V(r) = -\frac{G_N\,m_1\,m_2}{r}
  \qquad (R \ll r \ll \ell),
\end{equation}
with $G_N = G_4 = G_3/(2\pi R)$ and $R = 1/M_{\mathrm{poly}}$.
Three distance regimes exist:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $r \ll R \approx 10^{-21}$\,m: the $S^1$ fiber is resolved,
  the geometry is effectively $2{+}1$D, and the dimensionally
  reduced $3$D Newton potential gives
  $V(r) \sim -(G_3/2\pi)\,m_1 m_2\,\log(r/R)$;
\item $R \ll r \ll \ell$:
  the KK mode sum yields the standard $1/r$ Newton
  potential~\eqref{eq:newton};
\item $r \gg \ell$: a logarithmic correction
  $\delta V \sim -(G_3/2\pi)\,\log(r/\ell)$ from the CS boundary term,
  with $\delta V/V \sim R/r \times \log(r/\ell) \sim 10^{-19}$

\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}

$V(r) = -G_N m_1 m_2/r$

combined with the TT-projector contraction against $T^{00}_1 T^{00}_2$,

The short-distance regime ($r \ll R$) follows because at distances below
the $S^1$ circumference, the KK modes are unresolved and the zero mode
(topological CS sector with no local propagating DOF) dominates,

The long-distance correction ($r \gg \ell$) arises from the CS boundary
term, which contributes a non-local logarithmic potential suppressed by
$G_3/G_4 = 2\pi R \approx 6 \times 10^{-21}$\,m.
\end{proof}

\begin{derivation}[Full nonlinear $4$D Einstein equations]
\label{thm:nonlinear-einstein}

\begin{proof}
All three steps are self-contained in $2{+}1$D.

$R^{(4)}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\frac{1}{4}F^2$
(constant for uniform flux).
The $\varphi\varphi$ component gives
$\Lambda^{(4)} = N^2/16$ (in units of $1/\ell^2$).
The $ij$ components give $R^{(3)}_{ij} = \Lambda_3\,g_{ij}$

\end{enumerate}

--- the RT formula, derived from CFT$_2$ and geometry.

\paragraph{Proton stability.}
\label{cor:proton-stability}%
The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ sectors live in different
polygon numbers ($N = 7$ and $N = 4$).
Cross-sector Yukawa couplings require
$8(m_1 + m_2) \equiv 28 \pmod{56}$,
which has no integer solution
($\gcd(8, 56) = 8$ does not divide~$28$).
This is exact $\mathbb{Z}_{56}$ charge conservation.

long-lived---distinguishing this theory from $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ GUTs
($\tau_p \sim 10^{34}$\,yr) and consistent with Super-Kamiokande
bounds ($\tau_p > 1.6 \times 10^{34}$\,yr for $p \to e^+\pi^0$).

\subsection{The Higgs mass}
\label{sec:higgs}
The electroweak VEV $v = 246$\,GeV is predicted
(Theorem~\ref*{V-thm:cc}, $0.12\%$~match).
Standard Model RG running of $\lambda_H$ from $M_{\mathrm{poly}}$
to $M_Z$ gives $\lambda_H \approx 0.10$,
yielding $m_H \approx 110$\,GeV at tree level.
Two corrections, both derived from the orbifold structure, raise this:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Twisted-sector quartic.}
  The $\mathbb{Z}_7$ orbifold has $N - 1 = 6$ twisted sectors.
  Each twisted-sector operator is localized at the orbifold fixed point
  with OPE coefficient $C_{\mathrm{tw}} \propto 1/\sqrt{N}$
  (the localization suppression from DHVW
  \citep{DixonHarveyVafaWitten1985}).
  The four-point function receives a correction from all twisted
  sectors:
  $N \times |C_{\mathrm{tw}}|^4 = N \times 1/N^2 = 1/N$.
  The charge selection rule ($2m_3 + 2m_4 = 14 \equiv 0 \bmod 7$)
  restricts the quartic to $|\varphi_3|^2|\varphi_4|^2$
  (the unique $\mathbb{Z}_7$-invariant quartic).
  Therefore:
  $\delta\lambda_{\mathrm{tw}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{SM}}/N
  = 0.10/7 = 0.014$.
\item \emph{Instanton quartic.}
  The physical CS level
  $k_{\mathrm{phys}} = c/6 - 1/2 = 8.096$ (at $N = 7$)
  has fractional part $\kappa = 0.096$.
  The instanton fugacity is
  $\mathcal{K} = 0.548$~\eqref{eq:instanton-K}.
  The quartic vertex involves two instanton--anti-instanton pairs
  (amplitude $\mathcal{K}^2 = 0.300$), mediated by the
  critical mode ($j = 1$, conformal weight
  $h = j(j{+}1)/(k{+}h^\vee) = 2/3$).
  The critical mode dominates because it is the lightest excitation
  at the BF threshold ($\lambda_{m^*} = 0$).
  The central-charge suppression $1/c_{\mathrm{EW}} = 1/17.2$
  (the inverse gravitational coupling in the EW sector) gives:
  $\delta\lambda_{\mathrm{inst}} = \mathcal{K}^2\,h^2/c_{\mathrm{EW}}
  = 0.300 \times 0.444 / 17.2 = 0.008$.
\end{enumerate}
At leading order:
$\lambda_H^{\mathrm{LO}} = 0.10 + 0.014 + 0.008 = 0.122$,
giving $m_H^{\mathrm{LO}} = \sqrt{2\lambda_H}\,v = 122$\,GeV.
Four next-to-leading corrections raise this:
\begin{enumerate}
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\item \emph{$O(1/N^2)$ twisted sector}: $\delta\lambda = \lambda/N^2 = 0.002$
  (subleading DHVW OPE coefficient).
\item \emph{$O(\mathcal{K}^4)$ instanton}: $\delta\lambda = 6 \times 10^{-5}$
  (negligible).
\item \emph{Two-loop SM running}: $\delta\lambda = +0.007$
  (the standard top-quark two-loop correction
  $3y_t^4/(16\pi^2)^2 \times [16g_s^2 - 9y_t^2/2]
  \times \ln(M_{\mathrm{poly}}/m_t)$).
\item \emph{Top threshold}: $\delta\lambda = +0.004$
  (finite EW-scale matching).
\end{enumerate}
At next-to-leading order:
$\lambda_H^{\mathrm{NLO}} = 0.122 + 0.013 = 0.135$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mH}
  m_H = \sqrt{2\lambda_H^{\mathrm{NLO}}}\,v
  = 128\;\mathrm{GeV}.
\end{equation}
The observed $m_H = 125.25$\,GeV lies between the LO ($122$)
and NLO ($128$) values; the theoretical uncertainty from
$O(1/N^3)$ and three-loop corrections is $\pm\,3$\,GeV.
Every coefficient ($1/N$, $\kappa$, $h$, $c_{\mathrm{EW}}$,
$y_t$, $g_s$) is determined by~$N = 7$ and the SM
particle content.

\paragraph{Black hole entropy.}
\label{sec:btz-transition}
The Cardy formula of the orbifold CFT at $c = 12\,b(N)$ gives

  S_{\mathrm{BH}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{c}{6}\,E_L}

\end{equation}

$S = \pi r_+/(2G_3)$ via the Brown--Henneaux relation $c = 3\ell/(2G_3)$.

the BTZ black hole.
The microstates are Virasoro descendants of the orbifold CFT;
the Havelock central charge $c = 51.57$ gives $S = 18.4\sqrt{E}$

The $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ palindromic symmetry ($m \leftrightarrow N{-}m$)
gives $1$~bit per self-paired mode ($N$~even) and $2$~bits per paired
mode ($N$~odd).

Under the KK lift to~$4$D, the BTZ entropy becomes the $4$D
Bekenstein--Hawking entropy via $G_4 = G_3/(2\pi R)$, giving
$S_{4\mathrm{D}} = A/(4G_4)$ for the $4$D horizon area~$A$.
