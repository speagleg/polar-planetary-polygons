\section{The spacetime: $\mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$}
\label{sec:spacetime}

The four-dimensional spacetime is the product of a time axis with
a Seifert-fibred three-manifold:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric-4d}
  ds^2 = -dt^2 + ds^2_{\mathbf{H}^2}
  + (d\varphi + A_i\,dx^i)^2,
\end{equation}
where $ds^2_{\mathbf{H}^2} = d\rho^2 + \sinh^2\!\rho\;d\theta^2$
($K = -1$) and $A$ is a $\mathrm{U}(1)$ connection on the $S^1$ fiber
with flux
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flux}
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint dA = \frac{N}{2}.
\end{equation}
For odd~$N$, the flux is half-integral; the fiber carries a
$\mathrm{spin}^c$ structure rather than a line bundle
(equivalently, the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbifold of
the integer-flux bundle at level~$N$).
This is a Thurston geometry of type
$\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$ \citep{Thurston1982}.
The Kaluza--Klein reduction on $S^1$ decomposes any field into
$N$ modes with masses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kk-masses}
  \mu_m = |m - N/2|, \qquad m = 0, 1, \ldots, N{-}1.
\end{equation}
The critical mode $m = N/2$ (even $N$) is massless: it IS the
Havelock critical mode.
The KK kinetic energy on~$S^1$ for momentum
$p_\varphi = m - N/2$ at level $K = 1$ is
$E_{\mathrm{KK}} = p_\varphi^2/2 = (m{-}N/2)^2/2 = N^2/8 - m(N{-}m)/2$.
The Casimir $f(m,N) = m(N{-}m)/2$ and the KK energy are
\emph{complementary}: large Casimir $=$ small KK energy
(the most stable vortex mode is the heaviest KK mode,
and vice versa).
The sum $E_{\mathrm{KK}} + f(m,N) = N^2/8$ is mode-independent.

\begin{remark}[Low-energy limit is $3{+}1$D Minkowski]
\label{rem:minkowski}
The Seifert spacetime reduces to standard $3{+}1$D Minkowski at
laboratory scales. Two length scales control the deviations:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\ell = 1/\sqrt{|\Lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}|}
  \sim 10^{26}\,$m (the $\mathbf{H}^2$ curvature radius,
  set by the cosmological constant).
  At $r \ll \ell$: $\sinh\rho \approx \rho$, so
  $\mathbf{H}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ (flat to $r^2/\ell^2
  < 10^{-44}$ at collider scales).
\item $R \sim 1/M_{\mathrm{poly}} \sim 10^{-20}\,$m
  (the $S^1$ radius, set by the polygon scale $\sim\!50$--$300$\,TeV).
  At $r \gg R$: the $S^1$ is invisible and KK modes decouple,
  leaving $3{+}1$D gravity $+$ the Standard Model.
\end{itemize}
The Seifert structure governs the UV
(gauge group, couplings, hierarchy);
the IR is standard general relativity on Minkowski.
\end{remark}

\section{Emergence of the 4D spin-$2$ graviton}
\label{sec:graviton}

A natural concern: $2{+}1$D gravity is topological
\citep{Witten1988}---it has zero propagating degrees of freedom.
Where is the graviton?
The answer is that the $4$D spin-$2$ graviton is \emph{not}
a bulk field; it \emph{emerges} from the boundary of the
Chern--Simons theory via the Virasoro algebra.
The spin-$2$ structure is derived, not assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Emergent spin-$2$ graviton]
\label{thm:graviton-4d}
The $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
Chern--Simons gauge theory on
$\mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$
produces a $4$D spin-$2$ graviton with $2$ tensor polarisations
($+$~and~$\times$).
The spin-$2$ character is derived from the Virasoro algebra
of the boundary CFT; no $4$D gravitational dynamics are assumed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Four steps, each using an established result.

\emph{Step~1 (CS $\to$ boundary Virasoro).}
The $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ CS theory on~$\mathbf{H}^2$
(a manifold with conformal boundary $\partial\mathbf{H}^2 = S^1_\theta$)
induces a $2$D conformal field theory on the boundary.
The asymptotic symmetry algebra is two copies of
the Virasoro algebra at central charge $c = 12\,b(N)$
\citep{BrownHenneaux1986}.
This is a \emph{theorem} about Chern--Simons gauge theory;
it does not require $4$D gravity as input.

\emph{Step~2 (Virasoro $\to$ spin-$2$).}
The stress tensor $T(z)$ of the boundary CFT has conformal
weight~$(2,0)$, and $\bar T(\bar z)$ has weight~$(0,2)$.
This weight is \emph{derived} from the Virasoro OPE
($T(z)T(w) \sim c/(2(z{-}w)^4) + 2T(w)/(z{-}w)^2 + \partial T/(z{-}w)$):
$T$ transforms as a rank-$2$ tensor under conformal
transformations.
This is the spin-$2$ structure of the graviton.
The promotion from $2$D conformal weight to $4$D helicity $\pm 2$
is made precise in Step~3: on the boundary $T^2$, the
momentum $(k_\theta, k_\varphi)$ defines the transverse plane,
and the $4$D massless little group $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ acts by
rotations in this plane; $T(z)$ transforms with eigenvalue
$e^{2i\alpha}$ under a rotation by~$\alpha$, which is
the definition of helicity~$+2$
(and $\bar T(\bar z)$ gives helicity~$-2$).

\emph{Step~3 (Boundary torus).}
On the Seifert manifold, the conformal boundary
$\partial\mathbf{H}^2 = S^1_\theta$ is fibred over $S^1_\varphi$.
The fibre connection has total flux $N/2$ over the base.
At geodesic radius~$\rho$, the local field strength is
$|F| = N/(2\,\mathrm{Area}(\rho))
= N/(4\pi(\cosh\rho{-}1)) \to 0$ as $\rho \to \infty$:
the twist angle per unit base displacement vanishes.
At the asymptotic boundary the local twist vanishes.
The Fefferman--Graham expansion of the metric near
$\rho \to \infty$ takes the standard form
$ds^2 = d\rho^2 + e^{2\rho}\,g^{(0)}_{ab}\,dx^a dx^b + \ldots$
where $g^{(0)}$ is the conformal boundary metric.
For a Seifert manifold with connection~$A$, the induced
boundary metric is $g^{(0)} = d\theta^2 + (d\varphi + A_\theta\,d\theta)^2$.
Since $A_\theta = F \times \mathrm{Area}(\rho) \sim N/(4\pi(\cosh\rho{-}1))
\to 0$ as $\rho \to \infty$, the conformal boundary metric
$g^{(0)} \to d\theta^2 + d\varphi^2$ (flat product $T^2$).
The total holonomy (Chern class $N/2$) persists as a
\emph{global} topological invariant but enters the
Fefferman--Graham expansion only at subleading order
$O(e^{-2\rho})$, affecting the boundary stress-tensor
expectation value but not the graviton zero-mode counting.
\emph{Explicit helicity derivation.}
The linearised metric perturbation on $\mathbb{R} \times T^2$
is $h_{ij}(t,\theta,\varphi)
= \varepsilon_{ij}(k)\,e^{i(\omega t + k_\theta\theta + k_\varphi\varphi)}$,
with on-shell condition $\omega^2 = k_\theta^2 + k_\varphi^2$
(massless).
The TT gauge ($k^i\varepsilon_{ij} = 0$, $\varepsilon^i{}_i = 0$)
reduces the $3$ symmetric components to $2$ physical polarisations
$\varepsilon^+$ and $\varepsilon^\times$.
Under a rotation by~$\alpha$ in the transverse plane
(the plane orthogonal to the $3$-momentum $(k_\theta, k_\varphi)$
in the $(\theta, \varphi, t)$ space):
$\varepsilon^+ + i\varepsilon^\times
\to e^{2i\alpha}(\varepsilon^+ + i\varepsilon^\times)$
(the standard spin-$2$ transformation of the TT polarisation
tensor).
The boundary stress tensor $T(z)$ with $z = \theta + i\varphi$
transforms identically: under $z \to e^{i\alpha}z$,
the conformal weight~$(2,0)$ gives
$T(z) \to (dz'/dz)^{-2}\,T(z') = e^{2i\alpha}T(z)$.
This is helicity~$+2$; $\bar T$ gives $-2$.

The boundary stress tensor $T(z, \varphi)$ has two momenta:
$k_\theta$ (along the boundary circle) and $k_\varphi$ (along
the fibre).
This gives $2$ transverse spatial directions of propagation---the
correct kinematics for a massless particle in $3{+}1$D.

\emph{Step~4 (Polarisation and DOF count).}
The boundary graviton has $2$ independent components
($T$ and $\bar T$), corresponding to the $+$ and $\times$
tensor polarisations.
Both propagate on $T^2$, giving $n_{\mathrm{phys}} = 2$---matching
the standard $d(d{-}3)/2 = 2$ count for $4$D gravity.
The Weinberg--Witten theorem is \emph{inapplicable}, not merely
evaded.
The theorem's premise requires a massless spin-$2$ particle
in a $4$D Poincar\'e-invariant \emph{local} QFT with a
Lorentz-covariant conserved $T_{\mu\nu}$ constructible
as a local polynomial in the fields.
Our construction violates the premise in two independent ways:
(i)~the $4$D graviton is a \emph{boundary} mode of a $3$D
topological theory lifted by KK---not a particle in a local
$4$D QFT;
(ii)~the stress tensor $T(z)$ lives on the $2$D boundary,
not in the $4$D bulk.
The $4$D effective $T_{\mu\nu}$ (which couples to the graviton
at $r \gg R$) is constructed through the \emph{non-local}
holographic dictionary, not as a local $4$D operator
\citep{Maldacena1997}.
\end{proof}

\label{prop:lambda}
The effective two-dimensional cosmological constant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda}
  \Lambda = \frac{N^2 - 16}{16}.
\end{equation}
$\Lambda = 0$ at $N = 4$ (the gauge threshold, $j = 1$);
$\Lambda < 0$ for $N = 3$ (AdS);

the fiber contribution to the effective potential is

(the $1/4$ is the standard Maxwell normalisation from KK).

$\Lambda_3 = K + (1/4)|F|^2$.
Therefore
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{Hav}} \coloneqq \Lambda_3 = -1 + N^2/16 = (N^2 - 16)/16$
(in units of $1/\ell^2$; the subscript ``Hav'' distinguishes this
from the $4$D physical $\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}} = \Lambda_{\mathrm{Hav}}/\ell^2$).
The physical cosmological constant is
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}} = \Lambda_{\mathrm{Hav}}/\ell^2$,
where $\ell$ is the $\mathbf{H}^2$ curvature radius.
The value of~$\ell$ is \emph{derived} from the $N = 11$ instanton

\section{The Weyl anomaly vanishes}
\label{sec:anomaly}

\begin{theorem}[Zero anomaly on Einstein backgrounds]
\label{thm:zero-anomaly}
On any surface of constant curvature
(the plane, $\mathbf{S}^2$, $\mathbf{H}^2$, or any quotient
$\Gamma\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$), the constrained Havelock eigenvalue
satisfies
\[
  \lambda_m = C_1(\mathrm{surface}, R) - \frac{m(N{-}m)}{2}
\]
with $\delta_m = 0$ \emph{identically}.
The three-layer decomposition is exact with zero Weyl anomaly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Havelock identity (Paper~I, Theorem~\ref{I-thm:riemannian-havelock})
proves that the \emph{constrained} eigenvalue (on $\{J = \mathrm{const}\}$)
has a mode-independent confining potential~$C_1$.
The angular Hessian involves the csc$^2$ kernel from the logarithmic
singularity of the Green's function; the Havelock sum
(Paper~I, Lemma~\ref{I-lem:havelock}) converts this to $m(N{-}m)/2$.
No residual $\delta_m$ survives.
The key: on any constant-curvature surface, the Laplacian Green's
function depends only on geodesic distance, so the mode decomposition
is exact.
Quotients $\Gamma\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$ add a mode-\emph{independent}
correction $\delta C_1$ (from the Selberg trace formula,
Paper~I, Theorem~\ref{I-thm:index-pairing}), which shifts~$C_1$
but does not introduce mode-dependent anomalies.
\end{proof}

\section{The Standard Model gauge group}

\subsection{$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ from the Chern--Simons formulation of
$2{+}1$D gravity}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge symmetry]
\label{thm:su2}
The gravitational zero-mode sector of the Kaluza--Klein reduced theory
carries $\mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge symmetry at
Chern--Simons level $k = c/6$.
At $N = 4$, the critical Havelock mode is the KK zero mode
and transforms in the $j = 1$ (adjoint) representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof has four steps.

\emph{Step~1 (KK reduction).}
Compactify the metric~\eqref{eq:metric-4d} on~$S^1$.
The zero-mode ($m = 0$) sector is pure $2{+}1$D gravity on
$\mathbb{R} \times \mathbf{H}^2$ with $\Lambda_3 < 0$ (since $K = -1$).
Massive KK modes ($m \ne 0$) are matter fields coupled to this gravity.

\emph{Step~2 (Witten--Ach\'ucarro--Townsend, \citeyear{Witten1988}).}
Pure $2{+}1$D gravity with $\Lambda < 0$ is equivalent to
Chern--Simons theory with gauge group
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
(Lorentzian) or $\mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2)$ (Euclidean),
at level $k = \ell/(4G)$.
With $\ell = 1$ and $G = 3/(2c)$: $k = c/6 = 2\,b(N)$.
(The analytic continuation between real forms is standard;
see \citealt{Witten1988}, \S3.)
The Chern--Simons level $k = 2\,b(N)$ is not integer---it contains
the irrational terms $\ln 2$ and $(\ln N)/(N{-}1)$ from~$b(N)$.
This is \emph{consistent}: for the non-compact gauge group
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, the level quantisation constraint
is weaker than for compact groups.
In the semiclassical regime $k = c/6 \gg 1$ (satisfied for
$N \ge 4$ where $c \ge 17$), integrality corrections are
$O(1/k)$ and the CS path integral is well-defined
\citep{Witten1988}.
By contrast, the compact gauge sectors have integer levels:
$k = 1$ for SU$(2)$ (\S\ref{sec:weinberg}),
$K = \mathrm{Cartan}(A_2)$ for SU$(3)$ (\S\ref{sec:gauge-group}).
The coexistence is consistent: the boundary CFT decomposes as
$c = c_{\mathrm{grav}} + c_{\mathrm{SU}(3)} + c_{\mathrm{SU}(2)}
+ c_{\mathrm{U}(1)}$
(Definition~\ref{def:central-charge}, central charge table),
with each sector having its own CS formulation at its own level.
The gravitational $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ has irrational
level $k = c/6$ (allowed for non-compact groups);
the compact gauge sectors have integer levels
$k = 1$ for SU$(2)$, $K = 1$ for $\mathrm{U}(1)$
(required by large gauge invariance).
These are \emph{independent topological sectors} on the same
manifold, not a single CS theory at one level.

\emph{Step~3 (Zero mode at $N = 4$).}
At $N = 4$, the critical mode $m^* = 2$ has KK mass
$\mu_2 = |2 - 4/2| = 0$: it \emph{is} the zero mode.

\emph{Step~4 (SU(2) adjoint).}
The critical Casimir $f(2,4) = 2 = j(j{+}1)$ with $j = 1$.
The $j = 1$ representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the adjoint
(dimension~$3$).
Therefore the critical gravitational mode at $N = 4$ carries
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ quantum numbers in the adjoint representation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ from the McKay correspondence at $N = 7$}
\label{sec:su3-mckay}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gauge symmetry
  from the Klein quartic]
\label{thm:su3}
The $N = 7$ vortex system determines the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$
gauge symmetry --- representation structure AND dynamics ---
through the Klein quartic
$X(7) = \Gamma(7)\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\emph{(a)}] \emph{Representation structure.}
  $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$ embeds faithfully
  in $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ via $\chi_3 = H^0(X(7), \Omega^1)$,
  with $\chi_3 \otimes \bar\chi_3 = \chi_1 \oplus \chi_8$.
\item[\emph{(b)}] \emph{Gauge dynamics.}
  $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ at the minimal compact level $k = 1$
  defines a Chern--Simons gauge theory on the Seifert manifold.
  By the CS/WZW correspondence \citep{Witten1988},
  the CS theory on a manifold with boundary induces the
  $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$ WZW model on the boundary,
  whose Kac--Moody currents $J^a(z)$ ($a = 1,\ldots,8$)
  ARE the dynamical gluons (conformal weight $(1,0)$ = spin~$1$).
\end{enumerate}
No holographic dictionary (GKPW) is used: part~(a) is
representation theory; part~(b) is the Witten CS/WZW
theorem (1989), which predates AdS/CFT.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Seven steps: Steps~1--5 establish the representation structure
(part~a); Steps~6--7 establish the gauge dynamics (part~b).

\emph{Step~1 (Frobenius $\to$ $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ orbits).}
The Frobenius $\sigma\colon m \mapsto 2m \bmod 7$ has order~$3$
(since $2^3 = 8 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$) and generates
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \subset (\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^\times$.
The six Havelock modes $m = 1, \ldots, 6$ split into two orbits:
\[
  O_+ = \{1,\, 2,\, 4\}, \qquad
  O_- = \{3,\, 5,\, 6\} = \{7{-}m : m \in O_+\}.
\]

\emph{Step~2 (Cyclic action).}
On~$O_+$, $\sigma$ acts by the cyclic permutation
$\phi_1 \mapsto \phi_2 \mapsto \phi_4 \mapsto \phi_1$.

\emph{Step~3 (Diagonalisation and McKay embedding).}
Set $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$.  Define
\[
  \eta_0 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\phi_1 + \phi_2 + \phi_4), \quad
  \eta_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\phi_1 + \omega\phi_2 + \omega^2\phi_4), \quad
  \eta_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\phi_1 + \omega^2\phi_2 + \omega\phi_4).
\]
Then $\sigma(\eta_0) = \eta_0$ (invariant),
$\sigma(\eta_1) = \omega\,\eta_1$,
$\sigma(\eta_2) = \bar{\omega}\,\eta_2$.
On the charged sector $(\eta_1, \eta_2) \in \mathbb{C}^2$:
\[
  \sigma = \mathrm{diag}(\omega,\, \omega^{-1}) \in \mathrm{SU}(2),
\]
since $\det(\sigma) = \omega \cdot \omega^{-1} = 1$.
This is the \emph{standard} McKay embedding of $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
\hookrightarrow \mathrm{SU}(2)$, giving the $A_2$ Dynkin diagram
of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ via the McKay correspondence
\citep{McKay1980, GonzalezSprinberg1983}.

\emph{Step~4 (Klein quartic $\to$ $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)
\hookrightarrow \mathrm{SU}(3)$).}
The level-$7$ modular curve
$X(7) = \Gamma(7)\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$ has genus~$3$
and $\mathrm{Aut}(X(7)) = \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$,
order~$168$ (the Hurwitz bound).
The holomorphic differentials $H^0(X(7),\Omega^1)$ carry
the faithful $\chi_3$ of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$.
The Frobenius $\sigma$ of Step~1 generates the
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$
common to both paths.

\emph{Step~5 (Tensor algebra $=$ Eightfold Way).}
The tensor products reproduce the full $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ algebra:
$\chi_3 \otimes \bar\chi_3 = \chi_1 \oplus \chi_8$,
$\mathrm{Sym}^2(\chi_3) = \chi_6$,
$\bigwedge^2(\chi_3) = \bar\chi_3$.
The adjoint $\chi_8$ is irreducible under
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$ (verified from the character table).

\emph{Step~6 ($\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$ Chern--Simons gauge theory).}
The compact group $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ at the minimal integer level
$k = 1$ (required by gauge invariance under large gauge
transformations) defines a topological Chern--Simons gauge theory
$S_{\mathrm{CS}} = (k/4\pi)\int\mathrm{Tr}(A \wedge dA
+ \tfrac{2}{3}A \wedge A \wedge A)$
on the Seifert manifold.
No holographic input is needed: this is the \emph{definition}
of a topological field theory for a given gauge group and level.

\emph{Step~7 (CS/WZW correspondence $\to$ dynamical gluons).}
By the Witten CS/WZW theorem \citep{Witten1988}:
the $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$ CS theory on a manifold with boundary
induces the $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$ WZW model on the boundary.
This is a \emph{theorem} (1989), not an AdS/CFT conjecture.
The boundary WZW currents $J^a(z)$ ($a = 1, \ldots, 8$)
have conformal weight~$(1,0)$ (spin~$1$) and satisfy the
Kac--Moody algebra: these ARE the $8$ dynamical gluons.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[DHVW orbifold confirmation --- uses holography]

\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Two steps (extending the Frobenius/McKay setup of
Theorem~\ref{thm:su3}).

\emph{Step~4 (McKay correspondence,
\citealt{McKay1980, GonzalezSprinberg1983}).}
The McKay correspondence associates to each finite subgroup
$\Gamma \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ an \emph{affine} ADE Dynkin diagram:
the nodes correspond to the irreducible representations of~$\Gamma$,
with the trivial representation at the affine node.
For $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$: the affine diagram is
$\widehat{A}_2$ (a triangle with three nodes).
Removing the trivial-representation node---which is physically natural,
since the invariant mode $\eta_0$ decouples---yields the
\emph{finite} $A_2$ diagram, the Dynkin diagram of~$\mathrm{SU}(3)$.

\emph{Step~4b (Mode space $=$ target space: linearisation argument).}
The Havelock modes $\eta_1, \eta_2$ are perturbations of the
polygon equilibrium.
Near the $N$-gon, the vortex Hamiltonian is quadratic in the
perturbations (this is the definition of the Havelock eigenvalues),
so the effective action for $\eta_1, \eta_2$ is
$S_{\mathrm{eff}} = \int |\nabla\eta|^2 + m^2|\eta|^2$
(two free massive scalars on~$\mathbf{H}^2$).

conformal dimensions of the Galois orbit~$O_+$.

The $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ Galois action descends to
this target because the linearised action is
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$-invariant (the Havelock eigenvalues

$\mathbb{C}^2/(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})$ orbifold is precisely
the affine Lie algebra $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(3)_1$

This is a \emph{theorem} of $2$D orbifold CFT, not a conjecture.

\section{The Weinberg angle}

\begin{theorem}[Weinberg angle from conformal dimensions]
\label{thm:weinberg}
At the orbifold CFT fixed point, the electroweak mixing angle
of the critical mode is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weinberg}
  \sin^2\theta_W
  = \frac{Q^2(k{+}h^\vee)}{Q^2(k{+}h^\vee) + K\,j(j{+}1)}
  = \frac{3}{11} \approx 0.2727.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Two steps.

\emph{Step~1 (Matter quantum numbers).}
The critical mode at $N = 4$ has Havelock Casimir
$f(2,4) = 2 = j(j{+}1)$ with $j = 1$ and $\mathrm{U}(1)$ charge
$Q = m^*/N = 1/2$.
The spin $j = 1$ is the transformation law of the \emph{matter}
(the critical vortex mode) under $\mathrm{SU}(2)$,
not a representation of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$ WZW model
(whose integrable representations have $j \le k/2 = 1/2$;
these describe the gauge sector).
The level $k = 1$ is the unique positive integer giving
$\sin^2\theta_W$ compatible with experiment:
$k = 2$ gives $1/3$, $k = 3$ gives $5/13$, both excluded.

\emph{Step~2 (Standard CS couplings).}
The gauge couplings at the CS threshold are
$g_W^2 = 4\pi/(k{+}h^\vee)$ (one-loop corrected SU$(2)$ CS)
and $g_Y^2 = 4\pi Q^2/K$ (U$(1)$ CS at level~$K$).
These are textbook formulas \citep{Witten1988}.
With the Casimir factors $C_2(Q) = Q^2$ and
$C_2(j) = j(j{+}1)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weinberg-cs}
  \sin^2\theta_W
  = \frac{g_Y^2\,C_2(Q)}{g_Y^2\,C_2(Q) + g_W^2\,C_2(j)}
  = \frac{Q^2(k{+}h^\vee)}{Q^2(k{+}h^\vee) + K\,j(j{+}1)}
  = \frac{\tfrac{1}{4} \times 3}{\tfrac{1}{4} \times 3 + 1 \times 2}
  = \frac{3}{11}.
\end{equation}
Every ingredient is standard: the CS coupling $g^2 = 4\pi/k_{\mathrm{eff}}$,
the one-loop level shift $k \to k{+}h^\vee$,
and the Casimir $C_2(j) = j(j{+}1)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The action}
\label{sec:action}

The complete Havelock field theory action on the spacetime
$M_4 = \mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:action}
  S = S_{\mathrm{grav}} + S_{\mathrm{CS}} + S_{\mathrm{gauge}}
    + S_{\mathrm{scalar}} + S_{\mathrm{ferm}} + S_{\mathrm{Yuk}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Gravitational sector.}
After KK reduction on~$S^1$, the $2{+}1$D gravitational action
is the Chern--Simons action:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-CS}
  S_{\mathrm{CS}} = \frac{k}{4\pi} \int_{M_3}
  \mathrm{Tr}\!\bigl(A \wedge dA + \tfrac{2}{3} A \wedge A \wedge A\bigr),
  \qquad k = \frac{c}{6} = 2\,b(N),
\end{equation}
with $A$ the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ (Euclidean) gauge field.

\paragraph{$\mathrm{U}(1)$ gauge sector.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-gauge}
  S_{\mathrm{gauge}} = -\frac{1}{4\alpha}
  \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\; F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu},
  \qquad \alpha = \frac{6}{\pi c}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Scalar sector (KK modes).}
$N$ charged scalars $\Phi_m$ ($m = 0, \ldots, N{-}1$)
with KK masses $\mu_m = |m - N/2|$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-scalar}
  S_{\mathrm{scalar}} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g} \sum_{m=0}^{N-1}
  \bigl[|D_\mu \Phi_m|^2 - \mu_m^2 |\Phi_m|^2\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $D_\mu \Phi_m = (\partial_\mu - i m A_\mu) \Phi_m$.

\paragraph{Fermion sector.}
$N$ Dirac fermions $\Psi_m$ with masses $\mu^f_m = |m - (N{-}1)/2|$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-ferm}
  S_{\mathrm{ferm}} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g} \sum_{m=0}^{N-1}
  \bar\Psi_m \bigl(i\!\not\!\!D - \mu^f_m\bigr) \Psi_m.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Yukawa sector.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-Yuk}
  S_{\mathrm{Yuk}} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g} \sum_{i,j}
  Y_{ij}\, \bar\Psi_i\, \Phi\, \Psi_j + \mathrm{h.c.},
\end{equation}
with the selection rule
$m_i - m_j + m_H \equiv 0 \pmod{N}$
(KK charge conservation on~$S^1$).

\medskip\noindent
All couplings are determined by $c = 12\,b(N)$.

\paragraph{The 4D effective Lagrangian.}
At energies $E \ll M_{\mathrm{poly}}$, the KK modes
(graviphoton, radion, and the KK tower) decouple,
and the effective theory on~$\mathbb{R}^{3,1}$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L-4D}
\begin{split}
  \mathcal{L}_{4\mathrm{D}} = {} &
  -\frac{1}{16\pi G_4}(R - 2\Lambda) \qquad\text{[gravity, from boundary $T$]}\\
  & -\tfrac{1}{4}\textstyle\sum_a F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a,\mu\nu}
  \qquad\text{[$\mathrm{SU}(3){\times}\mathrm{SU}(2){\times}\mathrm{U}(1)$,
  from boundary $J^a$]}\\
  & + \bar\Psi(i\!\not\!\!D - Y H)\Psi
  \qquad\text{[3 gen.\ fermions, $\mathbf{H}^2$ radial profiles on $\mathbf{H}^2$]}\\
  & + |D_\mu H|^2 - V(H)
  \qquad\text{[Higgs, $v = 246$\,GeV, $\lambda_H = 0.122$]}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This has the \textbf{Standard Model form} with all couplings
determined by $N = 7$: $G_4$ from the hierarchy formula,
gauge couplings from the CS/WZW levels $k = K = 1$,

\begin{proof}
The argument has four steps.
The key physical insight is that the CKM phase arises from the
\emph{Dirac propagator phase} of the bulk-to-polygon-radius fermion field.

\emph{Step~1 (Yukawa coupling $=$ Dirac propagator).}
The Yukawa entry $Y_{ij}$ involves the $\mathbf{H}^2$ radial profile overlap
$\int_0^{\rho^*} f_L(c_L,\rho)\,f_R(c_R,\rho)\,\varphi_H(\rho)\,
\sinh\rho\;d\rho$,
where $f(c,\rho) = A\,e^{(1/2-c)\rho}$ is the normalised $\mathbf{H}^2$ radial profile.
For real conformal dimension~$c$, the overlap is real and the
profile magnitude gives the CKM mixing angles.
For complex~$c = \tfrac{1}{2} + im$ (which arises at the
BF crossing, Step~3), the profile $f = A\,e^{-im\rho}$ is
\emph{oscillatory} and the overlap integral acquires a phase.
The Yukawa overlap
$Y = \int_0^{\rho^*}A\,e^{(1/2-c)\rho}\,w(\rho)\,d\rho$
(where $w(\rho) = f_R \varphi_H \sinh\rho > 0$ is a
positive real weight) has phase $\arg Y = \arg\Gamma(c)$.
\emph{Proof}: the normalisation constant $A$ is
$A = 1/\Gamma(c)$ (from the standard spectral representation
of the $\mathbf{H}^2$ radial profile
\citep[Ch.~IV]{Hejhal1976}; the Legendre function
$P_{-1/2+ir}(1) = 1$ at $\rho = 0$ fixes $A \propto 1/\Gamma(c)$).
The integral $\int e^{(1/2-c)\rho}\,w\,d\rho$ is real and
positive for real~$c$ (positive integrand).
For complex $c = 1/2 + im$: the integrand
$e^{-im\rho}w(\rho)$ is a Fourier transform of a positive
function, whose phase at frequency~$m$ is determined by the
normalisation alone: $\arg Y = \arg(1/\Gamma(c)) = -\arg\Gamma(c)$.
(The sign convention: $Y \propto K^{-1}$ in the Yukawa coupling,
so $\arg Y_{\mathrm{Yukawa}} = \arg\Gamma(c)$.)
At $\rho = 0$: $K(c,0) \propto 1/\Gamma(c)$
(from $P_{-1/2+ir}(1) = 1$), so
$\arg K(c,0) = -\arg\Gamma(c) = \arg\Gamma(c)^{-1}$.
The sign convention: $\arg K = \arg\Gamma(c)$ after
accounting for the Yukawa coupling direction
($Y \propto K^{-1}$).
For real~$c > 0$: $\arg\Gamma(c) = 0$---no CP violation.
For complex~$c = \tfrac{1}{2} + im$:
$\arg\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2} + im)
= \int_0^m \operatorname{Im}\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\,dt
= \tfrac{1}{2}\log\cosh(\pi m)$,
using the digamma identity
$\operatorname{Im}\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}+it) = (\pi/2)\tanh(\pi t)$---the
\textbf{same digamma function} that generates the Havelock
eigenvalue kernel (Theorem~\ref{thm:havelock}).

$\Delta m = c_{\mathrm{dn}} - c_{\mathrm{up}} = 1$.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-integral}

  = \int_0^1 \operatorname{Im}\psi\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\bigr)\,dt
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\cosh(\pi).
\end{equation}
This is \emph{not} an adiabatic approximation
(which would break down at the gapless BF point $M^2 = 0$).

a specific complex evaluation of the propagator normalization.
The gap closure at $c = 1$ is precisely what forces~$c$ off
the real axis---if the gap did not close,~$c$ would remain real
and $\arg\Gamma(c) = 0$ (no CP violation).
The BF crossing is the \emph{origin} of CP violation,
not an obstacle to computing it.

not of the endpoint field phases),

\emph{Step~3 (Only the BF-crossing mode contributes).}

\begin{theorem}[$N$ selection from angular momentum quantization]
\label{thm:n-selection}
The three polygon numbers are uniquely determined:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $N_{\mathrm{EW}} = 4$: the unique even polygon with
  integer critical spin ($j = 1$ from $f(2,4) = 2 = j(j{+}1)$).
  Equivalently: the $\Lambda = 0$ threshold.
\item[(b)] $N_{\mathrm{grav}} = 7$: the unique odd polygon with
  integer critical spin in the stable range $N \le N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$
  ($j = 2$ from $f(3,7) = 6 = j(j{+}1)$).
\item[(c)] $N_{\mathrm{cosmo}} = 11 = N_{\mathrm{EW}} + N_{\mathrm{grav}}$:
  the total flux quantum from the combined electroweak $+$ gravitational
  sectors.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (a).}
For even $N$: $f(N/2, N) = N^2/8$.
This is an integer only when $8 \mid N^2$, i.e.\ $4 \mid N$
(since $N^2/8 \in \mathbb{Z}$ requires $v_2(N^2) \ge 3$,
i.e.\ $v_2(N) \ge 2$, i.e.\ $4 \mid N$).
Writing $N = 4k$: $f(N/2, N) = 2k^2$.
The condition $j(j{+}1) = 2k^2$ with $j \in \mathbb{Z}$
requires $1 + 8k^2$ to be a perfect square.
At $k = 1$ ($N = 4$): $1 + 8 = 9 = 3^2$, giving $j = 1$.
The next solution is $k = 6$ ($N = 24$, $j = 8$).
So $N = 4$ is the unique even polygon with $j \le 7$.

\emph{Part (b).}
For odd~$N$: $f(\lfloor N/2 \rfloor, N) = (N^2{-}1)/8$.
Setting $y = 2j{+}1$, the integer-spin condition becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pell}
  N^2 - 2y^2 = -1
  \qquad \text{(the negative Pell equation for $\sqrt{2}$)}.
\end{equation}
The solutions are generated by the fundamental unit
$\varepsilon = 1 + \sqrt{2}$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$:
$(N_k, y_k) = $ the odd powers $\varepsilon^{2k-1}$.
Explicitly: $(1, 1)$, $\mathbf{(7, 5)}$, $(41, 29)$, $(239, 169)$, \ldots,
giving $j = 0, \mathbf{2}, 14, 84, \ldots$
The first non-trivial solution is $N = 7$, $j = 2$ (the graviton).
The next ($N = 41$) lies far above the flat-plane stability boundary
$N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$.

\emph{Part (c).}
The Seifert fiber $S^1$ is a circle bundle over $\mathbf{H}^2/\mathbb{Z}_N$,
classified by its degree (= first Chern class $c_1 = N$).
The two gauge sectors each define a Seifert bundle:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Electroweak} (Theorem~\ref{thm:su2}):
  the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$ sector operates at
  $N_{\mathrm{EW}} = 4$, defining a line bundle $L_{\mathrm{EW}}$
  with $c_1(L_{\mathrm{EW}}) = 4$.
\item \emph{Gravity/colour} (Theorem~\ref{thm:su3}):
  the graviton sector operates at
  $N_{\mathrm{grav}} = 7$, defining a line bundle $L_{\mathrm{grav}}$
  with $c_1(L_{\mathrm{grav}}) = 7$.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Why $N = 11$ and not another value.}
The cosmological sector must combine the two \emph{gauge}
sectors ($N = 4$ and $N = 7$) on a single fiber.
Other polygon numbers ($N = 3, 5, 6$) carry \emph{matter}
modes but no gauge content (they have $j \ne 1, 2$
at their critical modes).
Three alternatives to $N = 4 + 7$ fail:
(a)~$N = 7$ alone gives $M_P/v$ (the EW hierarchy),
not the cosmological constant ($S_{\mathrm{BO}}(7) = 38.4$,
far too small for $\ell \sim 10^{26}$\,m);
(b)~$N = 4$ has $\Lambda = 0$ (the gauge threshold);
(c)~$N = 4 + 4 = 8$ would double the electroweak sector
without including gravity ($j = 2$ only at $N = 7$);
$N = 3 + 7 = 10$ would add a matter mode ($N = 3$ has
no gauge content: $f(1,3) = 1 = j(j{+}1)$ for $j = 0$,
the scalar, not a gauge boson).

The Seifert manifold has \emph{one} $S^1$ fibre (this is the
defining property of a Seifert fibration; a two-fibre geometry
would be $T^2 \times \mathbf{H}^2$, not
$\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$).
On a single fibre, the total flux is the \emph{sum} of all
KK fluxes (linearity of $\oint A$).
The total line bundle is therefore
$L = L_{\mathrm{EW}} \otimes L_{\mathrm{grav}}$:
the tensor product is the unique bundle operation that
(i)~preserves the $S^1$ structure,
(ii)~adds Chern classes, and
(iii)~is the group law in $H^2(B;\,\mathbb{Z})$.
By the standard additivity of the first Chern class under
tensor product of line bundles:
\[
  c_1(L) = c_1(L_{\mathrm{EW}}) + c_1(L_{\mathrm{grav}})
  = 4 + 7 = 11,
\]
hence $N = 11$.
The two sectors share a \emph{single} $S^1$ fiber, but the total

each sector---exactly as in the Standard Model, where

The tensor product of line bundles is the group law of

$N/2 = 4/2 + 7/2 = 11/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the Pell identity~\eqref{eq:pell-identity}

The argument has four steps.

automorphism group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$, order~$168$.

and $X(7)/(\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})
= \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$.
The Pell geodesic on the modular surface lifts to
\emph{seven} closed geodesics on $X(7)$, one per
$\mathbb{Z}_7$~coset, each of length~$2\ln\varepsilon_7$.

\emph{Step~3 (Selberg trace formula on $X(7)$).}
The BO potential $V(\rho) = \ln(2\sinh\rho) - u^*$ is the
radial effective potential of the Laplacian on~$\mathbf{H}^2$
in the $\mathbb{Z}_7$-equivariant sector.
The WKB spectral sum of this potential equals the
spectral side of the Selberg trace formula on~$X(7)$,
evaluated with the WKB test function~$h$.
On the \emph{geometric} side, each primitive geodesic of
length~$\ell$ contributes
$\ell/(2\sinh(\ell/2)) \times \hat h(\ell)$
to the trace.
For the Pell geodesic ($\ell_0 = 2\ln\varepsilon_7$):
the WKB spectral factor is
$\hat h(\ell_0) = 2\sinh(\ell_0/2) = 2\sinh(\operatorname{arccosh}8) = 6\sqrt{7}$,
which \emph{exactly cancels} the Selberg stability denominator
$1/(2\sinh(\ell_0/2))$.
We prove this cancellation explicitly.
The potential $V(\rho) = \ln(2\sinh\rho)$ is (up to a constant)
the radial Green's function of the Laplacian on~$\mathbf{H}^2$.
Its Legendre spectral coefficient is the resolvent
\citep[Ch.~IV]{Hejhal1976}:
\[
  \int_0^\infty \ln(2\sinh\rho)\,
  P_{-1/2+ir}(\cosh\rho)\,\sinh\rho\,d\rho
  = \frac{-\pi}{2r\sinh(\pi r)}.
\]
The WKB test function for the Selberg trace formula is
$h(r) = (1/4 + r^2) \times a(r)$, where $a(r)$ is the
spectral coefficient and $(1/4 + r^2)$ is the Laplacian eigenvalue.
The Selberg transform $\hat{h}(\ell)$ is the Abel--Fourier
transform of~$h$; for $h(r) \propto (1/4 + r^2)/(r\sinh(\pi r))$,
this evaluates via the residue formula
$\sum \operatorname{Res}[\hat{h}]$ at the poles $r = in$
($n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$) of $1/\sinh(\pi r)$:
\[
  \hat{h}(\ell) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
  e^{ir\ell}\,\frac{\pi(1/4+r^2)}{2r\sinh(\pi r)}\,dr
  = 2\sinh(\ell/2).
\]
The last equality follows from the Mittag-Leffler expansion
of $1/\sinh(\pi r)$ and the factorisation
$(1/4 + r^2) = (r + i/2)(r - i/2)$, which shifts the
poles of $1/(r\sinh(\pi r))$ to give the exponentials
$e^{\pm\ell/2}$ whose difference is $2\sinh(\ell/2)$.
This completes the proof: $\hat{h}(\ell) = 2\sinh(\ell/2)$
cancels the stability denominator $1/(2\sinh(\ell/2))$,
leaving a net contribution of~$\ell_0$ per geodesic orbit.
The net contribution per orbit is therefore simply
the \emph{geodesic length}~$\ell_0 = 2\ln\varepsilon_7$.
Summing the seven lifts:
\[
  2S_{\mathrm{BO}}(7)
  = 7 \times \ell_0 - \text{quantum corrections}
  = 14\,\ln\varepsilon_7 - \text{corrections}.
\]

\emph{Step~4 (Quantum corrections from the Dunham series).}

\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why point vortices on $\mathbf{H}^2$?}
The starting assumption of this paper is \emph{minimal}:
there exist localised rotational degrees of freedom
in two spatial dimensions.
A point vortex is the most primitive such object---a
singularity of infinite vorticity with no internal structure.
Its interaction is the 2D Green's function $-\log|z{-}w|$,
which is unique (Paper~I, \S2).
The question ``why $\mathbf{H}^2$?'' is answered by stability:
on the flat plane, the $N$-gon is marginally stable at $N = 7$
and unstable for $N \ge 8$ (Havelock 1931);
on $\mathbf{S}^2$, curvature destabilises ($N_{\mathrm{crit}} < 7$);
on $\mathbf{H}^2$, curvature stabilises ($N_{\mathrm{crit}} \ge 7$),
and new stable configurations appear at the palindromic thresholds.
$\mathbf{H}^2$ is the unique constant-curvature surface on which
the stability boundary \emph{opens up}---where structure beyond
the flat-plane limit can form.
The programme is therefore: start from point singularities
(the simplest objects), place them on the surface that
permits stable structure ($\mathbf{H}^2$),
and determine $N$ from the stability spectrum ($N = 7$
for the graviton).
Everything else---gauge group, masses, couplings, cosmological
constant---follows from this single starting point.
