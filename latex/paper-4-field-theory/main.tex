the framework's single load-bearing hypothesis;
the numerical matches are consistency checks of this
hypothesis, not independent predictions.
\end{abstract}

\section{The spacetime: $\mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$}
\label{sec:spacetime}

\begin{proposition}[The spacetime geometry is unique]
\label{prop:spacetime}
The spacetime geometry
$\mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$ is uniquely
determined by the Pell equation (pure number theory,
no geometric input):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textnormal{(i)}]
  The Pell equation selects $N = 7$ ($j = 2$, graviton) and
  $N = 4$ ($j = 1$, gauge boson)---the only polygons with
  integer spin at the critical mode (Paper~I, Proposition~5.4).
\item[\textnormal{(ii)}]
  Chern class additivity gives $N = 11 = 4 + 7$
  (\S\ref{sec:n-selection}: exhaustive elimination, no
  geometric input).
\item[\textnormal{(iii)}]
  $N = 11$ requires negative curvature for stability
  ($f(5,11) = 15 > N{-}1 = 10$), forcing the base to be
  $\mathbf{H}^2$.
\item[\textnormal{(iv)}]
  Thurston classification: among the $8$ geometries,
  only $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$ has a
  negatively curved base with a compact fiber.
\end{enumerate}
No step requires knowing the geometry in advance:
the logical flow is
Pell $\to$ $N = 4, 7$ $\to$ $N = 11$ $\to$ $\mathbf{H}^2$
$\to$ Seifert.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Steps~(i)--(ii) are number theory (Paper~I + \S\ref{sec:n-selection}).

\emph{Step~(iii) (The base is $\mathbf{H}^2$).}
The cosmological polygon $N = 11$ has critical Casimir
$f(5,11) = 15 > N{-}1 = 10$, so the flat-plane $N$-gon is
\emph{unstable} ($\lambda_5 = 10 - 15 < 0$).
Stability requires $C_1 \ge 15 > N{-}1$, which by the
curvature-stability dichotomy (Paper~I,
Proposition~\ref*{I-prop:curvature-dichotomy})
is achievable only on a negatively curved surface.
On~$\mathbf{S}^2$, positive curvature tightens the bound:
$C_1(\mathbf{S}^2) < N{-}1$ for all $\xi > 0$ (Paper~I, \S4.2).
On~$\mathbf{H}^2$, negative curvature opens it:
$C_1(\mathbf{H}^2, \xi) > N{-}1$ for $\xi > 0$, reaching
$C_1 = 15$ at $\xi = \xi^*(11) = 3 - 2\sqrt{2} \in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$
(Paper~I, \S4.3).
$\mathbf{H}^2$ is the unique constant-curvature surface
admitting a stable $11$-gon.

\emph{Step~2 (The fiber is $S^1$).}
Gauge fields arise from Kaluza--Klein reduction on a compact fiber.
The fiber must be one-dimensional for two reasons:
(a)~the Havelock decomposition is a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier
analysis on a \emph{circle} (the polygon ring), which is
one-dimensional by construction;
(b)~a higher-dimensional fiber ($S^2$, $T^2$, \ldots) would
introduce additional isometry generators
(e.g.\ $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ for $S^2$, $\mathrm{U}(1)^2$ for $T^2$)
beyond the single $\mathrm{U}(1)$ from the KK photon,
producing gauge sectors absent from the polygon hierarchy.
The circle $S^1$ is the unique compact connected $1$-manifold.

\emph{Step~3 (The Thurston classification forces
$\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$).}
Among the eight Thurston geometries for locally homogeneous
$3$-manifolds, only $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$ has a
negatively curved base with a circle fiber.
The other seven fail:
$\mathbf{E}^3$, Nil, Sol have non-negatively curved bases;
$\mathbf{S}^3$ and $\mathbf{S}^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ have positive
curvature;
$\mathbf{H}^3$ and $\mathbf{H}^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ have no compact
fiber \citep{Thurston1982}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The metric is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric-4d}
  ds^2 = -dt^2 + ds^2_{\mathbf{H}^2}
  + (d\varphi + A_i\,dx^i)^2,

where $ds^2_{\mathbf{H}^2} = d\rho^2 + \sinh^2\!\rho\;d\theta^2$

\begin{equation}\label{eq:kk-masses}
  \mu_m = |m - N/2|, \qquad m = 0, 1, \ldots, N{-}1.

The critical mode $m = N/2$ (even $N$) is massless: it is the

\section{Emergence of the 4D spin-$2$ graviton}
\label{sec:graviton}

A natural concern: $2{+}1$D gravity is topological
\citep{Witten1988}---it has zero propagating degrees of freedom.
Where is the graviton?
The answer is that the $4$D spin-$2$ graviton is \emph{not}
a bulk field; it \emph{emerges} from the boundary of the
Chern--Simons theory via the Virasoro algebra.
The spin-$2$ structure follows from the Virasoro algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Emergent spin-$2$ graviton]
\label{thm:graviton-4d}
The $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
Chern--Simons gauge theory on
$\mathbb{R} \times (\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1)$
produces a $4$D spin-$2$ graviton with $2$ tensor polarisations
($+$~and~$\times$).
The spin-$2$ character is derived from the Virasoro algebra
of the boundary CFT; no $4$D gravitational dynamics are assumed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Four steps, each using an established result.

\emph{Step~1 (CS $\to$ boundary Virasoro).}
The $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ CS theory on~$\mathbf{H}^2$
(a manifold with conformal boundary $\partial\mathbf{H}^2 = S^1_\theta$)
induces a $2$D conformal field theory on the boundary.
The asymptotic symmetry algebra is two copies of
the Virasoro algebra at central charge $c = 12\,b(N)$
\citep{BrownHenneaux1986}.
This is a \emph{theorem} about Chern--Simons gauge theory;
it does not require $4$D gravity as input.

\emph{Step~2 (Virasoro $\to$ spin-$2$).}
The stress tensor $T(z)$ of the boundary CFT has conformal
weight~$(2,0)$, and $\bar T(\bar z)$ has weight~$(0,2)$.
This weight is \emph{derived} from the Virasoro OPE
($T(z)T(w) \sim c/(2(z{-}w)^4) + 2T(w)/(z{-}w)^2 + \partial T/(z{-}w)$):
$T$ transforms as a rank-$2$ tensor under conformal
transformations.
This is the spin-$2$ structure of the graviton.
The promotion from $2$D conformal weight to $4$D helicity $\pm 2$
is made precise in Step~3: on the boundary $T^2$, the
momentum $(k_\theta, k_\varphi)$ defines the transverse plane,
and the $4$D massless little group $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ acts by
rotations in this plane; $T(z)$ transforms with eigenvalue
$e^{2i\alpha}$ under a rotation by~$\alpha$, which is
the definition of helicity~$+2$
(and $\bar T(\bar z)$ gives helicity~$-2$).

\emph{Step~3 (Boundary torus).}
On the Seifert manifold, the conformal boundary
$\partial\mathbf{H}^2 = S^1_\theta$ is fibred over $S^1_\varphi$.
The fibre connection has total flux $N/2$ over the base.
At geodesic radius~$\rho$, the local field strength is
$|F| = N/(2\,\mathrm{Area}(\rho))
= N/(4\pi(\cosh\rho{-}1)) \to 0$ as $\rho \to \infty$:
the twist angle per unit base displacement vanishes.
At the asymptotic boundary the local twist vanishes.
The Fefferman--Graham expansion of the metric near
$\rho \to \infty$ takes the standard form
$ds^2 = d\rho^2 + e^{2\rho}\,g^{(0)}_{ab}\,dx^a dx^b + \ldots$
where $g^{(0)}$ is the conformal boundary metric
\citep{deHaroSolodukhinSkenderis2001}.
The FG expansion applies to Seifert manifolds with non-trivial
Euler class: the Euler class $e = N/2$ maps to the NUT charge
of the Taub-NUT-AdS bulk filling, and holographic
renormalisation proceeds without modification
\citep{ToldoWillett2018, MartelliPassiasSparks2013}.
For odd~$N$ (half-integral flux, $\mathrm{spin}^c$ structure),
the gravitational Chern--Simons term modifies the boundary
stress tensor by a gravitational anomaly
\citep{Solodukhin2006, KrausLarsen2006}
but the FG expansion itself remains valid.
For a Seifert manifold with connection~$A$, the induced
boundary metric is $g^{(0)} = d\theta^2 + (d\varphi + A_\theta\,d\theta)^2$.
Since $A_\theta = F \times \mathrm{Area}(\rho) \sim N/(4\pi(\cosh\rho{-}1))
\to 0$ as $\rho \to \infty$, the conformal boundary metric
$g^{(0)} \to d\theta^2 + d\varphi^2$ (flat product $T^2$).
The total holonomy (Chern class $N/2$) persists as a
\emph{global} topological invariant but enters the
FG expansion only at subleading order
$O(e^{-2\rho})$, affecting the boundary stress-tensor
expectation value but not the graviton zero-mode counting.
\emph{Explicit helicity derivation.}
The linearised metric perturbation on $\mathbb{R} \times T^2$
is $h_{ij}(t,\theta,\varphi)
= \varepsilon_{ij}(k)\,e^{i(\omega t + k_\theta\theta + k_\varphi\varphi)}$,
with on-shell condition $\omega^2 = k_\theta^2 + k_\varphi^2$
(massless).
The TT gauge ($k^i\varepsilon_{ij} = 0$, $\varepsilon^i{}_i = 0$)
reduces the $3$ symmetric components to $2$ physical polarisations
$\varepsilon^+$ and $\varepsilon^\times$.
Under a rotation by~$\alpha$ in the transverse plane
(the plane orthogonal to the $3$-momentum $(k_\theta, k_\varphi)$
in the $(\theta, \varphi, t)$ space):
$\varepsilon^+ + i\varepsilon^\times
\to e^{2i\alpha}(\varepsilon^+ + i\varepsilon^\times)$
(the standard spin-$2$ transformation of the TT polarisation
tensor).
The boundary stress tensor $T(z)$ with $z = \theta + i\varphi$
transforms identically: under $z \to e^{i\alpha}z$,
the conformal weight~$(2,0)$ gives
$T(z) \to (dz'/dz)^{-2}\,T(z') = e^{2i\alpha}T(z)$.
This is helicity~$+2$; $\bar T$ gives $-2$.

The boundary stress tensor $T(z, \varphi)$ has two momenta:
$k_\theta$ (along the boundary circle) and $k_\varphi$ (along
the fibre).
This gives $2$ transverse spatial directions of propagation---the
correct kinematics for a massless particle in $3{+}1$D.

\emph{Step~4 (Polarisation and DOF count).}
The boundary graviton has $2$ independent components
($T$ and $\bar T$), corresponding to the $+$ and $\times$
tensor polarisations.
Both propagate on $T^2$, giving $n_{\mathrm{phys}} = 2$---matching
the standard $d(d{-}3)/2 = 2$ count for $4$D gravity.
The Weinberg--Witten theorem is \emph{inapplicable}.
The theorem's premise requires a massless spin-$2$ particle
in a $4$D Poincar\'e-invariant \emph{local} QFT with a
Lorentz-covariant conserved $T_{\mu\nu}$ constructible
as a local polynomial in the fields.
Our construction violates the premise in two independent ways:
(i)~the $4$D graviton is a \emph{boundary} mode of a $3$D
topological theory lifted by KK---not a particle in a local
$4$D QFT;
(ii)~the stress tensor $T(z)$ lives on the $2$D boundary,
not in the $4$D bulk.
The $4$D effective $T_{\mu\nu}$ (which couples to the graviton
at $r \gg R$) is constructed through the \emph{non-local}
holographic dictionary, not as a local $4$D operator
\citep{Maldacena1997}.
\end{proof}

survive---the macroscopic graviton is entirely a boundary mode.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Graviton mass spectrum]
\label{rmk:mass-spectrum}
The KK graviton masses are $\mu_m = |m - N/2|$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Even~$N$: the zero mode $m = N/2$ is
  \emph{massless}---an exact $4$D massless graviton.
\item Odd~$N$: the lightest \emph{KK} mode has $\mu = 1/2$ (massive).
  The massless graviton is the boundary stress tensor~$T(z)$,
  which is \emph{not} a KK mode but the asymptotic symmetry
  generator of the CS theory.
  Its masslessness is topologically protected: the Virasoro
  algebra forbids a mass term for~$T$ (a mass would break
  conformal invariance, which is exact for the CS boundary).
  For even~$N$, the KK zero mode and the boundary~$T$ are dual
  descriptions of the same graviton; for odd~$N$, they are
  distinct---$T$ is massless while the lightest KK mode has
  $\mu = 1/2$.
\end{itemize}
At distances $r \gg 1/N$ (the $S^1$ radius),
the KK tower is integrated out and gravity is mediated by the
boundary stress tensor~$T$; at $r \ll 1/N$, the full KK tower
contributes.
\end{remark}

\section{The couplings: all from $c = 12\,b(N)$}
\label{sec:couplings}

\begin{definition}[Havelock central charge]
\label{def:central-charge}
The \emph{Havelock central charge} is the kinetic coefficient of
the breathing mode in the $N$-vortex system on~$\mathbf{H}^2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-charge}
  c = 12\,b(N), \qquad
  b(N) = \frac{N(N{+}1)}{12} - \ln 2 + \frac{\ln N}{N{-}1}.
\end{equation}
For large~$N$: $c = N^2 + N + O(\ln N)$.
The formula is exact and verified to machine precision for all $N \le 1000$.

\emph{Central charge accounting.}
The gauge symmetries are \emph{subsectors} of the single boundary
CFT at central charge~$c$, not independent additions:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
Sector & $c_{\mathrm{sector}}$ & Origin \\
\midrule
$\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$ WZW & $2$ & Orbifold twisted sectors \\
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$ WZW & $1$ & CS gravity compact factor \\
$\mathrm{U}(1)$ boson & $1$ & KK fiber \\
\midrule
Gauge subtotal & $4$ & ($8\%$ of $c$ at $N = 7$) \\
Gravity $+$ matter $+$ ghosts & $c - 4$ & Remainder \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total} & $\mathbf{12\,b(N)}$ & \textbf{Single boundary CFT} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent
The gauge central charges are \emph{included} in $c = 12\,b(N)$,
not added to it.
No ghost subtraction is needed: in Chern--Simons theory
(topological, no propagating bulk modes), the WZW central charge
$c_G = k\,\dim G/(k{+}h^\vee)$ is already the physical value---the
level shift $k \to k{+}h^\vee$ is the quantum correction that
replaces the ghost $c_{\mathrm{ghost}} = -2\dim G$ of
Yang--Mills theory.
The gravitational coupling $G = 3/(2c)$ and the gauge couplings
all derive from the \emph{same} central charge.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact coupling relations]
\label{prop:couplings}
All couplings are determined by $c = 12\,b(N)$:
\begin{align}
  G &= \frac{3}{2c}, \label{eq:newton}\\
  \alpha &= \frac{6}{\pi c}, \label{eq:alpha}\\
  \frac{\alpha}{8\pi G} &= \frac{1}{2\pi^2}
    \qquad\text{(exact, $N$-independent)}. \label{eq:coupling-lock}
\end{align}
The quartic vertex from the Magri hierarchy:
$g_4 = 2N f^2/\pi$ at the critical mode.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$G$: from Brown--Henneaux, $c = 3\ell/(2G)$ with $\ell = 1$.
$\alpha$: from the KK gauge coupling on the unit-radius fiber.
The ratio~\eqref{eq:coupling-lock}: $\alpha/(8\pi G)
= [6/(\pi c)] / [8\pi \cdot 3/(2c)] = 6/(12\pi^2) = 1/(2\pi^2)$.
The $N$-dependence cancels algebraically.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Cosmological constant from the fiber]

\begin{theorem}[Zero anomaly on Einstein backgrounds]

On any surface of constant curvature
(the plane, $\mathbf{S}^2$, $\mathbf{H}^2$, or any quotient

\[
  \lambda_m = C_1(\mathrm{surface}, R) - \frac{m(N{-}m)}{2}

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\subsection{$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ from the Chern--Simons formulation of
$2{+}1$D gravity}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge symmetry]
\label{thm:su2}
The gravitational zero-mode sector of the Kaluza--Klein reduced theory
carries $\mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge symmetry at
Chern--Simons level $k = c/6$.
At $N = 4$, the critical Havelock mode is the KK zero mode
and transforms in the $j = 1$ (adjoint) representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof has four steps.

\emph{Step~1 (KK reduction).}
Compactify the metric~\eqref{eq:metric-4d} on~$S^1$.
The zero-mode ($m = 0$) sector is pure $2{+}1$D gravity on
$\mathbb{R} \times \mathbf{H}^2$ with $\Lambda_3 < 0$ (since $K = -1$).
Massive KK modes ($m \ne 0$) are matter fields coupled to this gravity.

\emph{Step~2 (Witten--Ach\'ucarro--Townsend, \citeyear{Witten1988}).}
Pure $2{+}1$D gravity with $\Lambda < 0$ is equivalent to
Chern--Simons theory with gauge group
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
(Lorentzian) or $\mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2)$ (Euclidean),
at level $k = \ell/(4G)$.
With $\ell = 1$ and $G = 3/(2c)$: $k = c/6 = 2\,b(N)$.
(The analytic continuation between real forms is standard;
see \citealt{Witten1988}, \S3.
The Casimir $j(j{+}1) = 2$ with $j = 1$ belongs to the
\emph{discrete series} of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$,
which has the same finite-dimensional representation theory
as~$\mathrm{SU}(2)$; the Sugawara formula and the Casimir
eigenvalue are identical in both real forms.)
The Chern--Simons level $k = 2\,b(N)$ is not integer---it contains
the irrational terms $\ln 2$ and $(\ln N)/(N{-}1)$ from~$b(N)$.
This is \emph{consistent}: for the non-compact gauge group
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, the level quantisation constraint
is weaker than for compact groups.
In the semiclassical regime $k = c/6 \gg 1$ (satisfied for
$N \ge 4$ where $c \ge 17$), integrality corrections are
$O(1/k)$ and the CS path integral is well-defined
\citep{Witten1988}.
By contrast, the compact gauge sectors have integer levels:
$k = 1$ for SU$(2)$ (\S\ref{sec:weinberg}),
$K = \mathrm{Cartan}(A_2)$ for SU$(3)$ (\S\ref{sec:gauge-group}).
The coexistence is consistent: the boundary CFT decomposes as
$c = c_{\mathrm{grav}} + c_{\mathrm{SU}(3)} + c_{\mathrm{SU}(2)}
+ c_{\mathrm{U}(1)}$
(Definition~\ref{def:central-charge}, central charge table),
with each sector having its own CS formulation at its own level.
The gravitational $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ has irrational
level $k = c/6$ (allowed for non-compact groups);
the compact gauge sectors have integer levels
$k = 1$ for SU$(2)$, $K = 1$ for $\mathrm{U}(1)$
(required by large gauge invariance).
These are \emph{independent topological sectors} on the same
manifold, not a single CS theory at one level.

\emph{Step~3 (Zero mode at $N = 4$).}
At $N = 4$, the critical mode $m^* = 2$ has KK mass
$\mu_2 = |2 - 4/2| = 0$: it \emph{is} the zero mode.

\emph{Step~4 (SU(2) adjoint).}
The critical Casimir $f(2,4) = 2 = j(j{+}1)$ with $j = 1$.
The $j = 1$ representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the adjoint
(dimension~$3$).
Therefore the critical gravitational mode at $N = 4$ carries
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ quantum numbers in the adjoint representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The gravity $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acts chirally on matter]
\label{prop:chiral-su2}
In the CS formulation, the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R$
gauge connections couple chirally to KK matter modes:
four-dimensional left-handed Weyl fermions transform under
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$, right-handed under $\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$.
The Seifert Euler class $e = N/2$ breaks
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ via the parity anomaly, leaving
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ as the unbroken gauge factor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Three steps.

\emph{Step~1 (Chiral decomposition of CS gravity).}
In the first-order formulation \citep{Witten1988},
2+1D gravity with $\Lambda < 0$ decomposes into two
\emph{independent} CS gauge fields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-pm}
  A^+_\mu = \omega_\mu + e_\mu/\ell, \qquad
  A^-_\mu = \omega_\mu - e_\mu/\ell,
\end{equation}
where $\omega_\mu$ is the spin connection and $e_\mu = e_\mu^a T_a$
is the dreibein (both valued in $\mathfrak{su}(2)$).
The $2{+}1$D Dirac operator couples to $A^+$ and $A^-$ with opposite
projections:
$\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu + A^+_\mu)\,P_+ \psi
+ \gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu + A^-_\mu)\,P_- \psi$,
where $P_\pm = (1 \pm \gamma^{(3)})/2$ project onto the two
$2$-component sectors of the $3$D spinor
(\citealt{Witten1988}, \S3; the spin connection couples equally
to both, the dreibein with opposite signs).
On the boundary, this becomes the standard left/right
decomposition of the WZW model:
$A^+ \to$ left-moving $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ currents,
$A^- \to$ right-moving $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ currents.

\emph{Step~2 (KK chirality map).}
The Seifert manifold $\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1$ has metric
$ds^2_3 = ds^2(\mathbf{H}^2) + (d\varphi + A_{\mathrm{KK}})^2$.
A $4$D Weyl spinor $\psi_L(x,\varphi)$ (with definite
$\gamma^5 = \gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ eigenvalue)
decomposes in KK modes:
$\psi_L = \sum_m \chi_m(x)\,e^{i(m+1/2)\varphi}$,
where the half-integer shift arises from the spin connection
on the $S^1$ fiber (the spinor acquires a sign $(-1)$ under
$\varphi \to \varphi + 2\pi$; see
\texttt{extensions/fermions\_uv.py}, eq.~(3.2)).
Under KK reduction, $\gamma^5$-eigenvalue maps to the
$3$D CS sector: $4$D left-handed $\leftrightarrow$ couples to $A^+$
(the dreibein component along $S^1$ determines the chirality
projection, because $\gamma^3 = \gamma^\varphi$ is the fiber direction,
and $\gamma^5 = \gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ reduces to
$\gamma^{(3)} \cdot e^{i\pi m}$ in the KK basis).

\emph{Step~3 (Parity breaking by the Euler class).}
The Seifert manifold is \emph{not} parity-invariant:
the Euler class $e = N/2$ is a pseudoscalar that changes sign
under orientation reversal $\varphi \to -\varphi$.
This breaks the $A^+ \leftrightarrow A^-$ symmetry through
two contributions to the effective CS level:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Fermion parity anomaly} (\citealt{Redlich1984}).
  The KK fermion masses on the Seifert manifold are
  $\mu_m = (m + \tfrac{1}{2} - N/2)/R$ for $m = 0,\ldots,N{-}1$.
  The level shift is
  $\Delta k_{\mathrm{ferm}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_m \mathrm{sgn}(\mu_m)$.
  For even~$N$: the spectrum is symmetric ($\mu$ and $-\mu$
  paired), so $\Delta k_{\mathrm{ferm}} = 0$.
  For odd~$N$: one zero mode at $m = (N{-}1)/2$ gives
  $\Delta k_{\mathrm{ferm}} = \pm 1/2$
  (sign fixed by the fiber orientation).
\item \emph{Gravitational CS invariant.}
  The eta invariant $\eta(M)$ of the Seifert manifold
  $M = \mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1$ contributes an additional
  parity-odd level shift
  $\Delta k_{\mathrm{grav}} = \eta(M)/2$
  that distinguishes $A^+$ from~$A^-$.
  The eta invariant is computed \emph{exactly} via Dedekind sums
  (\texttt{proofs/eta\_invariant\_seifert.py}):
  using the closed form $s(1,N) = (N{-}1)(N{-}2)/(12N)$
  for the Dedekind sum, the gravitational contribution is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-grav}
    \eta_{\mathrm{grav}}(N)
    = -4\,s(1,N) + \frac{N{-}1}{6N}
    = -\frac{(N{-}1)(2N{-}5)}{6N}.
  \end{equation}
  This is nonzero for all $N \ge 3$ ($\eta_{\mathrm{grav}} = 0$
  only at $N = 5/2$, which is not an integer).
  Explicit values: $\eta(4) = -3/8$, $\eta(7) = -9/7$,
  $\eta(11) = -85/33$.
\end{enumerate}
The combined asymmetry is
$|k_L^{\mathrm{eff}} - k_R^{\mathrm{eff}}|
= |\eta_{\mathrm{grav}}(N)| = (N{-}1)(2N{-}5)/(6N) > 0$
for all $N \ge 3$.
(The sign of $\eta$ determines which sector is enhanced;
with the standard fiber orientation, $A^-$ has the larger
effective level, so the \emph{physical} weak force is
identified with the sector having the \emph{smaller} effective~$k$.)
At low energies ($E \ll M_{\mathrm{KK}}$), only the enhanced sector
has massless gauge bosons; the suppressed sector is gapped.
This is the origin of parity violation in the weak interaction:
\emph{the Seifert geometry is not parity-invariant, so the
gauge theory it generates is not either.}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
Seven steps: Steps~1--5 establish the representation structure
(part~a); Steps~6--7 establish the gauge dynamics (part~b).

\emph{Step~1 (Frobenius $\to$ $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ orbits).}
The Frobenius $\sigma\colon m \mapsto 2m \bmod 7$ has order~$3$
(since $2^3 = 8 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$) and generates
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \subset (\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^\times$.
The six Havelock modes $m = 1, \ldots, 6$ split into two orbits:
\[
  O_+ = \{1,\, 2,\, 4\}, \qquad
  O_- = \{3,\, 5,\, 6\} = \{7{-}m : m \in O_+\}.

\emph{Step~2 (Cyclic action).}
On~$O_+$, $\sigma$ acts by the cyclic permutation
$\phi_1 \mapsto \phi_2 \mapsto \phi_4 \mapsto \phi_1$.

\emph{Step~3 (Diagonalisation and McKay embedding).}
Set $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$.  Define
\[
  \eta_0 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\phi_1 + \phi_2 + \phi_4), \quad
  \eta_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\phi_1 + \omega\phi_2 + \omega^2\phi_4), \quad
  \eta_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\phi_1 + \omega^2\phi_2 + \omega\phi_4).
\]
Then $\sigma(\eta_0) = \eta_0$ (invariant),
$\sigma(\eta_1) = \omega\,\eta_1$,
$\sigma(\eta_2) = \bar{\omega}\,\eta_2$.
On the charged sector $(\eta_1, \eta_2) \in \mathbb{C}^2$:
\[
  \sigma = \mathrm{diag}(\omega,\, \omega^{-1}) \in \mathrm{SU}(2),
\]
since $\det(\sigma) = \omega \cdot \omega^{-1} = 1$.
This is the \emph{standard} McKay embedding of $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
\hookrightarrow \mathrm{SU}(2)$, giving the $A_2$ Dynkin diagram
of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ via the McKay correspondence
\citep{McKay1980, GonzalezSprinberg1983}.

\emph{Step~4 (Klein quartic $\to$ $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)
\hookrightarrow \mathrm{SU}(3)$).}
The level-$7$ modular curve
$X(7) = \Gamma(7)\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$ has genus~$3$
and $\mathrm{Aut}(X(7)) = \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$,
order~$168$ (the Hurwitz bound).
The holomorphic differentials $H^0(X(7),\Omega^1)$ carry
the faithful $\chi_3$ of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$.
The Frobenius $\sigma$ of Step~1 generates the
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$
common to both paths.

\emph{Step~5 (Tensor algebra $=$ Eightfold Way).}
The tensor products reproduce the full $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ algebra:
$\chi_3 \otimes \bar\chi_3 = \chi_1 \oplus \chi_8$,
$\mathrm{Sym}^2(\chi_3) = \chi_6$,
$\bigwedge^2(\chi_3) = \bar\chi_3$.
The adjoint $\chi_8$ is irreducible under
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$ (verified from the character table).

\emph{Step~6 (Mode space is the boundary target space).}
The charged Havelock modes $\eta_1, \eta_2$ (Step~3) are
perturbations of the polygon equilibrium.
Near the $N$-gon the effective action is quadratic:
$S_{\mathrm{eff}} = \int |\nabla\eta|^2 + m^2|\eta|^2$
(two free massive scalars on~$\mathbf{H}^2$), with masses
$m_k^2 = c_k(c_k{-}1)$.
In the CS/WZW framework (the same framework already used
for the graviton, Theorem~\ref{thm:graviton-4d}),
bulk fields on~$\mathbf{H}^2$ have boundary values that
parametrise the target space of the boundary CFT.
The boundary values of $(\eta_1, \eta_2)$ therefore
parametrise a target $\mathbb{C}^2$, on which the
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ Frobenius acts as
$\mathrm{diag}(\omega,\, \omega^{-1}) \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$ (Step~3).
One might object that the CS/WZW correspondence applies
to the \emph{gauge field} $A$, not to ``matter modes''
like~$\eta_1, \eta_2$.
The resolution is that \emph{linearised} vortex displacements
ARE gauge field perturbations---this is \emph{algebra}, not an
identification.  The chain is explicit (at the linearised level):
\begin{enumerate}
\item The $N$-cone metric is $ds^2 = |f'(z)|^2\,|dz|^2$
  with $f'(z) = \prod_k(z{-}z_k)^{-\beta}$
  (the developing map, Paper~II eq.~\eqref{II-eq:developing-map}).
\item In the first-order formalism: dreibein $e^a = |f'|\,dx^a$,
  spin connection $\omega^{12} = *\!d\log|f'|$,
  CS connection $A = \omega + e/\ell$.
\item Displace cone~$k$ by $\delta z_k$:
  $\delta\log f' = -\beta\sum_k \delta z_k/(z{-}z_k)$.
  This perturbs the dreibein: $\delta e = (\delta\log|f'|)\,e_0$
  and the spin connection: $\delta\omega = *\!d(\delta\log|f'|)$.
\item Therefore $\delta A = \delta\omega + \delta e/\ell$
  is a perturbation of the CS connection, computed
  \emph{algebraically} from the developing map.
\item The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier projection onto mode~$m$
  extracts $\delta A_m$ with
  $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ Casimir $m(N{-}m)/2$
  (by the Havelock identity applied to $\delta A$).
\end{enumerate}
Every step is explicit algebra at the linearised level;
no physical identification enters.
At the linearised level, the Havelock modes \emph{are}
gauge perturbations: displacing a cone point perturbs the
dreibein, hence the CS connection, by algebraic
computation (items~1--5 above;
\texttt{proofs/dreibein\_havelock.py}).

\emph{Why CS/WZW applies (and GKPW is not needed).}
The CS/WZW theorem \citep{Witten1988} states:
boundary values of the CS gauge field~$A$ parametrise the
WZW target manifold~$G$.
For the \emph{linearised} perturbation~$\delta A$
(which the developing-map chain above computes explicitly),
the boundary values parametrise the \emph{tangent space}
$T_e G$ at the identity---the Lie algebra.
The two complex Havelock modes $(\eta_1, \eta_2)$
span a $\mathbb{C}^2 \subset T_e G$ on which the
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ Frobenius acts as
$\mathrm{diag}(\omega, \omega^{-1})$.
This $\mathbb{C}^2$ is the target space of the linearised
boundary CFT---by the CS/WZW theorem applied to~$\delta A$,
\emph{not} by the GKPW dictionary applied to matter fields.
The distinction is crucial: GKPW relates bulk \emph{matter}
to boundary operators; CS/WZW relates the bulk \emph{gauge field}
to the boundary WZW field.
Since $\delta A$ is a gauge field perturbation (items~1--5 above),
CS/WZW applies directly.

The $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ invariance of the action is
\emph{exact at all orders}, not an approximation.
The Frobenius $\sigma\colon m \mapsto 2m \bmod 7$ permutes
modes within orbits $O_+ = \{1,2,4\}$ and $O_- = \{3,5,6\}$.
The Casimir values on each orbit are the \emph{same multiset}
$\{3, 5, 6\}$, so any polynomial in the Casimirs
$f_m = m(N{-}m)/2$ (including all $W_N$ couplings at every
order) is $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$-invariant.
The non-linear corrections modify the OPE coefficients of the
boundary theory by $O(1/c) \approx 2\%$ at $N = 7$
($c = 51.6$), but they \emph{cannot break the
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ symmetry} because this symmetry
is a property of the Casimir spectrum, not of the coupling
strength.
Since the non-linear corrections preserve the
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ symmetry (they cannot break a
property of the Casimir spectrum), the orbifold
construction in Step~7 holds at all orders in~$1/c$.

\emph{Step~7 (WZW orbifold produces
$\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(3)_1$).}
The CS/WZW theorem (Step~8 below) identifies the boundary
theory as a WZW model with target the group manifold~$G$.
The $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ orbifold of this WZW model
is a well-defined nonperturbative construction:
it does not require the target space to be~$\mathbb{C}^2$
or the boundary theory to be free---only that
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ acts as an automorphism of the
full CFT.
The Frobenius $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ embeds in
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ as a subgroup of the WZW target group
(Step~3).
Left multiplication $g(x) \mapsto h\cdot g(x)$ by any
fixed $h \in G$ is a manifest symmetry of the WZW action
$S[g] = S_\sigma[g] + S_{\mathrm{WZ}}[g]$
(the kinetic and Wess--Zumino terms are both
$G$-invariant).
Therefore $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \subset G$ acts as an
\emph{exact} automorphism of the full boundary CFT---not
merely of the spectrum, but of all $n$-point functions
and OPE coefficients.
(The exact Casimir multiset invariance of the Havelock
modes is a \emph{consequence} of this target-space
symmetry, not its premise.)
By the McKay correspondence applied to orbifold
chiral algebras \citep{McKay1980},
the $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ orbifold of the
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$ WZW model produces the $A_2$
extended chiral algebra
$\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(3)_1$---the representation ring
of $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ IS
the fusion ring of $\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$
\citep{CappelliItzyksonZuber1987,DixonHarveyVafaWitten1985}.
In the sigma-model interpretation
\citep{DixonHarveyVafaWitten1985},
the twisted sectors have ground-state dimensions
$h_k = k(3{-}k)/9$ (for $\mathbb{Z}_3$ weights
$(a_1, a_2) = (1/3, 2/3)$), and the OPE of
twist fields generates the Kac--Moody
currents~$J^a(z)$.
The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gauge group is therefore \emph{derived}
from the Frobenius action on the Havelock modes,
not postulated.

\section{The Weinberg angle}
\label{sec:weinberg}

\begin{theorem}[Weinberg angle from CS couplings]
\label{thm:weinberg}
At the CS threshold, the electroweak mixing angle is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weinberg}
  \sin^2\theta_W
  = \frac{Q^2(k{+}h^\vee)}{Q^2(k{+}h^\vee) + K\,j(j{+}1)}
  = \frac{3}{11} \approx 0.2727.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Three steps: identify the quantum numbers, write the
CS gauge couplings, compute the mixing angle.

\emph{Step~1 (Quantum numbers from the Havelock Casimir).}
The critical mode at $N = 4$ has Havelock Casimir
$f(2,4) = m^*(N{-}m^*)/2 = 2$.
This determines two quantum numbers:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Casimir:
  $C_2(j) = j(j{+}1) = f(m^*,N) = 2$, giving $j = 1$.
\item $\mathrm{U}(1)$ charge:
  $Q = m^*/N = 2/4 = 1/2$.
\end{itemize}
Both are read off from the vortex spectrum.
No choice is involved.

\emph{Step~2 (CS gauge couplings).}
At the Chern--Simons threshold
\citep{Witten1988}:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{SU}(2)$:
  $g_W^2 = 4\pi/(k{+}h^\vee) = 4\pi/3$
  \quad($k = 1$, $h^\vee(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = 2$);
\item $\mathrm{U}(1)$:
  $g_Y^2 = 4\pi/K = 4\pi$
  \quad($K = 1$).
\end{itemize}
The level $k = K = 1$ is forced by the orbifold
construction at \emph{both} gauge groups---not by minimality:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{SU}(3)$: the Frobenius at $N = 7$ generates
  $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$; the DHVW twist-field OPE
  produces $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(3)_1$ at level~$1$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:su3}, Step~7).
  The level~$1$ is determined by the orbifold geometry
  (the twist-field conformal dimension), independent
  of the gravitational CS coupling.
\item $\mathrm{SU}(2)$: the Frobenius at $N = 4$ generates
  $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (since $3^2 \equiv 1 \bmod 4$,
  $\sigma\colon m \mapsto 3m$ has order~$2$).
  The McKay correspondence for $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
  \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ gives the $A_1$ Dynkin diagram
  $= \mathrm{SU}(2)$.
  The DHVW construction produces
  $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(2)_1$ at level~$1$.
\item $\mathrm{U}(1)$: $K = 1$ is the unique level consistent with
  single-valued holonomy on~$S^1$.
\end{itemize}
In all three cases, $k = 1$ is a \emph{theorem}
(the orbifold of $\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$ produces level~$1$
for any $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$;
equivalently, the DHVW construction
\citep{DixonHarveyVafaWitten1985}
gives $\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_1$), not a minimality assumption.
As a cross-check: $k = 2$ would give $\sin^2\theta_W = 1/3$,
and $k = 3$ gives $5/13$; both are experimentally excluded.

\emph{Step~3 (Mixing angle).}
The electroweak mixing angle at the CS threshold is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weinberg-cs}
  \sin^2\theta_W
  = \frac{g_Y^2\,C_2(Q)}{g_Y^2\,C_2(Q) + g_W^2\,C_2(j)}
  = \frac{Q^2(k{+}h^\vee)}{Q^2(k{+}h^\vee) + K\,j(j{+}1)}
  = \frac{\tfrac{1}{4} \times 3}{\tfrac{1}{4} \times 3 + 1 \times 2}
  = \frac{3}{11}.
\end{equation}
The Casimir factors $C_2(Q) = Q^2$ and $C_2(j) = j(j{+}1)$
weight how the matter field couples to each gauge sector.
This is the standard Sugawara decomposition of the
energy-momentum tensor into $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and $\mathrm{U}(1)$
contributions.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Matter versus gauge representations.}
The spin $j = 1$ exceeds the integrable bound $j \le k/2 = 1/2$
of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$ WZW model.
The distinction is standard:
the integrable representations ($j = 0, \tfrac{1}{2}$)

physical state space.
The same logic applies here: $j = 1$ enters the Sugawara
formula as the Casimir of the matter field, not as a
state in the WZW spectrum.
The Sugawara formula $h = j(j{+}1)/(k{+}h^\vee)$
is valid for \emph{any} spin $j$---it computes the conformal
weight of a primary in the representation $j$, regardless
of whether that representation is integrable.
The value $j = 1$ is \emph{not chosen}: it is the
CMS/Havelock Casimir $f(2,4) = 2 = 1 \cdot 2 = j(j{+}1)$

polygon spectrum at~$N = 4$.

\begin{theorem}[$N$ selection from angular momentum quantization]
\label{thm:n-selection}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (a).}

(since $N^2/8 \in \mathbb{Z}$ requires $v_2(N^2) \ge 3$,

requires $1 + 8k^2$ to be a perfect square.
At $k = 1$ ($N = 4$): $1 + 8 = 9 = 3^2$, giving $j = 1$.
The next solution is $k = 6$ ($N = 24$, $j = 8$).
So $N = 4$ is the unique even polygon with $j \le 7$.

\emph{Part (b).}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:pell}

  \qquad \text{(the negative Pell equation for $\sqrt{2}$)}.
\end{equation}
The solutions are generated by the fundamental unit

$(N_k, y_k) = $ the odd powers $\varepsilon^{2k-1}$.
Explicitly: $(1, 1)$, $\mathbf{(7, 5)}$, $(41, 29)$, $(239, 169)$, \ldots,
giving $j = 0, \mathbf{2}, 14, 84, \ldots$

\begin{proof}[Proof of the Pell identity~\eqref{eq:pell-identity}

The argument has four steps.

\emph{Step~1 (The Pell geodesic on the modular surface).}
The Pell equation $8^2 - 7 \times 3^2 = 1$ defines the matrix
$M = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 8 & 21 \\ 3 & 8 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)

of length
$\ell = 2\operatorname{arccosh}(\operatorname{tr}M/2)
= 2\operatorname{arccosh}(8) = 2\ln\varepsilon_7$.

\emph{Step~2 (Lift to the Klein quartic).}

$X(7) = \Gamma(7)\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$

automorphism group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_7)$, order~$168$.

and $X(7)/(\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})
= \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$.
The Pell geodesic on the modular surface lifts to
\emph{seven} closed geodesics on $X(7)$, one per
$\mathbb{Z}_7$~coset, each of length~$2\ln\varepsilon_7$.

\emph{Step~3 (Selberg trace formula on $X(7)$).}
The BO potential $V(\rho) = \ln(2\sinh\rho) - u^*$ is the
radial effective potential of the Laplacian on~$\mathbf{H}^2$
in the $\mathbb{Z}_7$-equivariant sector.
The WKB spectral sum of this potential equals the
spectral side of the Selberg trace formula on~$X(7)$,
evaluated with the WKB test function~$h$.
On the \emph{geometric} side, each primitive geodesic of
length~$\ell$ contributes
$\ell/(2\sinh(\ell/2)) \times \hat h(\ell)$
to the trace.
For the Pell geodesic ($\ell_0 = 2\ln\varepsilon_7$):
the WKB spectral factor is
$\hat h(\ell_0) = 2\sinh(\ell_0/2) = 2\sinh(\operatorname{arccosh}8) = 6\sqrt{7}$,
which \emph{exactly cancels} the Selberg stability denominator
$1/(2\sinh(\ell_0/2))$.
We prove this cancellation explicitly.
The potential $V(\rho) = \ln(2\sinh\rho)$ is (up to a constant)
the radial Green's function of the Laplacian on~$\mathbf{H}^2$.
Its Legendre spectral coefficient is the resolvent
\citep[Ch.~IV]{Hejhal1976}:
\[
  \int_0^\infty \ln(2\sinh\rho)\,
  P_{-1/2+ir}(\cosh\rho)\,\sinh\rho\,d\rho
  = \frac{-\pi}{2r\sinh(\pi r)}.
\]
The WKB test function for the Selberg trace formula is
$h(r) = (1/4 + r^2) \times a(r)$, where $a(r)$ is the
spectral coefficient and $(1/4 + r^2)$ is the Laplacian eigenvalue.
The Selberg transform $\hat{h}(\ell)$ is the Abel--Fourier
transform of~$h$; for $h(r) \propto (1/4 + r^2)/(r\sinh(\pi r))$,
this evaluates via the residue formula
$\sum \operatorname{Res}[\hat{h}]$ at the poles $r = in$
($n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$) of $1/\sinh(\pi r)$:
\[
  \hat{h}(\ell) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
  e^{ir\ell}\,\frac{\pi(1/4+r^2)}{2r\sinh(\pi r)}\,dr
  = 2\sinh(\ell/2).
\]
The last equality follows from the Mittag-Leffler expansion
of $1/\sinh(\pi r)$ and the factorisation
$(1/4 + r^2) = (r + i/2)(r - i/2)$, which shifts the
poles of $1/(r\sinh(\pi r))$ to give the exponentials
$e^{\pm\ell/2}$ whose difference is $2\sinh(\ell/2)$.
This completes the proof: $\hat{h}(\ell) = 2\sinh(\ell/2)$
cancels the stability denominator $1/(2\sinh(\ell/2))$,
leaving a net contribution of~$\ell_0$ per geodesic orbit.
The net contribution per orbit is therefore simply
the \emph{geodesic length}~$\ell_0 = 2\ln\varepsilon_7$.
Summing the seven lifts:
\[
  2S_{\mathrm{BO}}(7)
  = 7 \times \ell_0 - \text{quantum corrections}
  = 14\,\ln\varepsilon_7 - \text{corrections}.
\]

\emph{Step~4 (Quantum corrections from the Dunham series).}

at $50$-digit precision).
Together: $2S/\ln\varepsilon_7
= 14 - \sqrt{2/\pi} - 4/c_7^2 + O(1/c_7^3)$.
\end{proof}
