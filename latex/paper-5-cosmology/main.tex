\section{The $N$ selection principle}
\label{sec:n-selection}

\begin{theorem}[$N$ selection from angular momentum quantization]
\label{thm:n-selection}
The three polygon numbers are uniquely determined:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $N_{\mathrm{EW}} = 4$: the unique even polygon with
  integer critical spin ($j = 1$ from $f(2,4) = 2 = j(j{+}1)$).
  Equivalently: the $\Lambda = 0$ threshold.
\item[(b)] $N_{\mathrm{grav}} = 7$: the unique odd polygon with
  integer critical spin in the stable range $N \le N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$
  ($j = 2$ from $f(3,7) = 6 = j(j{+}1)$).
\item[(c)] $N_{\mathrm{cosmo}} = 11 = N_{\mathrm{EW}} + N_{\mathrm{grav}}$:
  the two gauge sectors share a single $S^1$ fiber
  with KK fluxes $N_{\mathrm{EW}}/2 = 2$ and
  $N_{\mathrm{grav}}/2 = 7/2$; flux additivity gives
  $N_{\mathrm{total}}/2
  = 2 + 7/2 = 11/2$, giving $N = 11$.
  No geometric input enters---only the linearity of
  $\oint A$ on the fiber (exhaustive elimination below).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (a).}
For even $N$: $f(N/2, N) = N^2/8$.
This is an integer only when $8 \mid N^2$, i.e.\ $4 \mid N$
(since $N^2/8 \in \mathbb{Z}$ requires $v_2(N^2) \ge 3$,
i.e.\ $v_2(N) \ge 2$, i.e.\ $4 \mid N$).
Writing $N = 4k$: $f(N/2, N) = 2k^2$.
The condition $j(j{+}1) = 2k^2$ with $j \in \mathbb{Z}$
requires $1 + 8k^2$ to be a perfect square.
At $k = 1$ ($N = 4$): $1 + 8 = 9 = 3^2$, giving $j = 1$.
The next solution is $k = 6$ ($N = 24$, $j = 8$).
So $N = 4$ is the unique even polygon with $j \le 7$.

\emph{Part (b).}
For odd~$N$: $f(\lfloor N/2 \rfloor, N) = (N^2{-}1)/8$.
Setting $y = 2j{+}1$, the integer-spin condition becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pell}
  N^2 - 2y^2 = -1
  \qquad \text{(the negative Pell equation for $\sqrt{2}$)}.
\end{equation}
The solutions are generated by the fundamental unit
$\varepsilon = 1 + \sqrt{2}$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$:
$(N_k, y_k) = $ the odd powers $\varepsilon^{2k-1}$.
Explicitly: $(1, 1)$, $\mathbf{(7, 5)}$, $(41, 29)$, $(239, 169)$, \ldots,
giving $j = 0, \mathbf{2}, 14, 84, \ldots$
The first non-trivial solution is $N = 7$, $j = 2$ (the graviton).
The next ($N = 41$) lies far above the flat-plane stability boundary
$N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$.

\emph{Part (c).}
The Seifert fiber $S^1$ is a circle bundle over $\mathbf{H}^2/\mathbb{Z}_N$,
classified by its degree (= first Chern class $c_1 = N$).
The two gauge sectors each define a Seifert bundle:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Electroweak} (Theorem~\ref*{IV-thm:su2}):
  the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$ sector operates at
  $N_{\mathrm{EW}} = 4$, defining a line bundle $L_{\mathrm{EW}}$
  with $c_1(L_{\mathrm{EW}}) = 4$.
\item \emph{Gravity/colour} (Theorem~\ref*{IV-thm:su3}):
  the graviton sector operates at
  $N_{\mathrm{grav}} = 7$, defining a line bundle $L_{\mathrm{grav}}$
  with $c_1(L_{\mathrm{grav}}) = 7$.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Why $N = 11$ and not another value.}
The cosmological sector must combine the two \emph{gauge}
sectors ($N = 4$ and $N = 7$) on a single fiber.
Other polygon numbers ($N = 3, 5, 6$) carry \emph{matter}
modes but no gauge content (they have $j \ne 1, 2$
at their critical modes).
\paragraph{Logical status of the elimination criteria.}
The selection uses three layers, ranked by strength:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\emph{T}:] \emph{Theorems} (no freedom):
  KK flux additivity $N/2 = N_{\mathrm{EW}}/2 + N_{\mathrm{grav}}/2$;
  Thurston: one $S^1$ fiber; McKay: each~$N$ gives a unique gauge group.
\item[\emph{S}:] \emph{Structural requirements} (minimal freedom):
  each gauge sector appears exactly once (no duplication, which would
  introduce an undetermined relative scale);
  the cosmological sector must fix~$\ell$ (otherwise $\Lambda$ is a free parameter).
\item[\emph{P}:] \emph{Physical consistency}:
  $j = 0$ polygons carry no gauge content;
  non-integer~$j$ polygons have no spin interpretation.
\end{enumerate}
\noindent
Criteria \emph{T} are mathematical theorems.
Criterion \emph{S} is the requirement that the theory have
no more free parameters than the Standard Model.
Criterion \emph{P} uses the CMS/CS identification
(Proposition~\ref{III-prop:casimir-havelock} of Paper~III).
Any residual objection to the selection is an objection to
\emph{S} or \emph{P}, not to the mathematical structure.

The selection principle is \textbf{minimality}:
$N = 11$ is the unique value for which the cosmological
sector contains exactly the gauge content needed
($\mathrm{SU}(2)$ at $N = 4$, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ at $N = 7$)
with no redundancy and no extra free parameters.
Every alternative either duplicates a gauge sector
(introducing an undetermined relative scale) or includes
a non-gauge polygon (introducing unconstrained matter).

\paragraph{Exhaustive elimination.}
Three alternatives to $N = 4 + 7$ fail:
(a)~$N = 7$ alone gives $M_P/v$ (the EW hierarchy),
not the cosmological constant ($\mathcal{H}_7 = 38.4$,
far too small for $\ell \sim 10^{26}$\,m);
(b)~$N = 4$ has $\Lambda = 0$ (the gauge threshold);
(c)~$N = 4 + 4 = 8$ would double the electroweak sector
without including gravity ($j = 2$ only at $N = 7$);
$N = 3 + 7 = 10$ would add a matter mode ($N = 3$ has
no gauge content: $f(1,3) = 1 = j(j{+}1)$ for $j = 0$,
the scalar, not a gauge boson).
The two gauge sectors operate on the same $S^1$ fiber
(the Thurston classification allows only one compact fiber
direction, Step~iv of Proposition~\ref*{IV-prop:spacetime}).
On a single fiber, the total KK flux is the \emph{sum}
of individual fluxes (linearity of $\oint A$):
$N_{\mathrm{total}}/2 = N_{\mathrm{EW}}/2 + N_{\mathrm{grav}}/2
= 4/2 + 7/2 = 11/2$,
giving $N = 11$.
This is flux additivity---a consequence of the
$\mathrm{U}(1)$ gauge structure of the KK connection,
not a topological assumption.
$N = 11$ is the unique combination that includes each
gauge sector exactly once.
Higher multiples ($N = 14 = 2 \times 7$, $N = 28 = 4 \times 7$)
would require duplicating a gauge sector:
$N = 14$ contains two copies of the $N = 7$ gravitational bundle,
producing two independent BO potentials with two independent
tunnelling scales ($\ell_1, \ell_2$).
The theory would have an undetermined relative scale
$\ell_1/\ell_2$---an additional free parameter absent from the
$N = 11$ sector, which has a single BO potential and a single
prediction for~$\ell$.
$N = 28 = 4 \times 7$ is excluded similarly (two copies of each
gauge sector, doubling the parameter count).
All other additive combinations fail:
$N = 3 + 4 = 7$ (already the graviton sector itself);
$N = 3 + 7 = 10$ ($N = 3$ has $j = 0$, no gauge content);
$N = 5 + 7 = 12$ ($N = 5$ has $j \approx 1.79$, non-integer);
$N = 5 + 4 = 9$ (no graviton);
$N = 4 + 4 = 8$ (no graviton, doubled EW);
$N = 7 + 7 = 14$ (two BO potentials, extra parameter).
Multiplicative combinations $N = 3k$ for $k \ge 3$ or
$N = 5k$ always include matter polygons without gauge
content, adding free parameters without gauge sectors.
This completes the exhaustive elimination.

The Frobenius structure confirms the uniqueness:
$N = 11$'s Frobenius ($\sigma\colon m \mapsto 3m \bmod 11$,
order~$5$) gives $\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z} \to A_4

\emph{Step~3 (Selberg trace formula on $X(7)$).}

spectral side of the Selberg trace formula on~$X(7)$,
evaluated with the WKB test function~$h$.

length~$\ell$ contributes
$\ell/(2\sinh(\ell/2)) \times \hat h(\ell)$

  P_{-1/2+ir}(\cosh\rho)\,\sinh\rho\,d\rho

spectral coefficient and $(1/4 + r^2)$ is the Laplacian eigenvalue.

transform of~$h$; for $h(r) \propto (1/4 + r^2)/(r\sinh(\pi r))$,

$\sum \operatorname{Res}[\hat{h}]$ at the poles $r = in$
($n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$) of $1/\sinh(\pi r)$:
\[

  = 2\sinh(\ell/2).

The last equality follows from the Mittag-Leffler expansion
of $1/\sinh(\pi r)$ and the factorisation
$(1/4 + r^2) = (r + i/2)(r - i/2)$, which shifts the
poles of $1/(r\sinh(\pi r))$ to give the exponentials
$e^{\pm\ell/2}$ whose difference is $2\sinh(\ell/2)$.

cancels the stability denominator $1/(2\sinh(\ell/2))$,
leaving a net contribution of~$\ell_0$ per geodesic orbit.
The net contribution per orbit is therefore simply
the \emph{geodesic length}~$\ell_0 = 2\ln\varepsilon_7$.

\[

\end{proof}
