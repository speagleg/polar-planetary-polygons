\begin{abstract}
We derive the cosmological constant, Hubble parameter, dark
energy/matter fractions, neutrino mass ratio, and baryon
asymmetry from the Havelock-Pell criterion
($N = 4$ and $N = 7$; Paper~IV), which determines
the cosmological polygon $N = 11 = 4 + 7$ via
Kaluza--Klein flux additivity on the derived $S^1$ fiber.
The $N = 11$ Born--Oppenheimer tunneling action fixes the
hyperbolic curvature radius~$\ell$, giving
$H_0 = 67.4$\,km/s/Mpc (Planck 2018 \citep{Planck2018}: $67.4 \pm 0.5$),
$\Omega_\Lambda = 68.3\%$,
$\Omega_{\mathrm{DM}} = 26.7\%$,
$\Omega_b = 4.97\%$ ($0.4\sigma$),
$\Delta m^2_{\mathrm{sol}}/\Delta m^2_{\mathrm{atm}} = 1/31.5$ ($4\%$),
and $\eta_B = 5.7 \times 10^{-10}$
($7\%$ match to the observed
$6.12 \times 10^{-10}$).
The theory predicts normal neutrino hierarchy,
$w = -1$ exactly, and no neutrinoless double beta decay.
\end{abstract}

\section{The cosmological constant}
\label{sec:lambda}

\begin{proposition}[Cosmological constant from the fiber]
\label{prop:lambda}
The effective three-dimensional cosmological constant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda}
  \Lambda_3 = \frac{N^2 - 16}{16}.
\end{equation}
$\Lambda_3 = 0$ at $N = 4$ (the gauge threshold, $j = 1$);
$\Lambda_3 < 0$ for $N = 3$ (AdS);
$\Lambda_3 > 0$ for $N \ge 5$ (de~Sitter).
At $N = 7$: $\Lambda_3 = (49{-}16)/16 = 2.0625$ (in $\ell^{-2}$ units). The physical $\Lambda_4 = \Lambda_3/\ell^2$, with $\ell$ from the $N = 11$ instanton (\S\ref{sec:cc-instanton}), gives $\Lambda_4 \sim 10^{-122}\,M_P^4$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cosmological constant is the competition between the
negative curvature of~$\mathbf{H}^2$ (which wants $\Lambda_3 < 0$)
and the positive flux energy on the $S^1$ fiber
(which wants $\Lambda_3 > 0$); at $N = 4$ these cancel exactly.

In the standard KK reduction of the metric~\eqref{IV-eq:metric-4d},
the fiber contribution to the effective potential is
$(1/4)|F|^2$, where $F = dA$ is the gauge field strength.
With quantized flux $\oint dA = \pi N$
(from~\eqref{IV-eq:flux}), the energy density on a unit-area
base is $(1/4)(\pi N / (2\pi))^2 = N^2/16$.
The base Gaussian curvature: $K_{\mathbf{H}^2} = -1$
(Ricci scalar $R = 2K = -2$; the magnitude $|K| = 1$ sets
the curvature scale).
The KK reduction of $\int R^{(4)}\sqrt{g^{(4)}}$
with metric $ds^2 = g_{ij}dx^idx^j + (d\varphi{+}A)^2$
gives $\int[R^{(3)} - \tfrac{1}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}]\sqrt{g^{(3)}}$
(the $1/4$ is the standard Maxwell normalization from KK).
The 3D Einstein equations on the base
$G_{\mu\nu}^{(3)} + \Lambda_3\,g_{\mu\nu}^{(3)}
= 8\pi G_3\,T_{\mu\nu}^{(\mathrm{flux})}$
have $T_{\mu\nu}^{(\mathrm{flux})}
= (1/4)|F|^2\,g_{\mu\nu}^{(3)}$ (Maxwell stress from the
KK gauge field).
On a constant-curvature base with $K = -1$
(so $G^{(3)} = -K\,g = g$):
$\Lambda_3 = K + (1/4)|F|^2 = -1 + N^2/16$,
where $|F|^2 = (\pi N/(2\pi))^2 = N^2/4$ from the
quantized flux $\oint\!dA = \pi N$.
On $\mathbf{H}^2$ with $K = -1$:
$\Lambda_3 = -1 + N^2/16 = (N^2 - 16)/16$
(in units of $1/\ell^2$).
The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ cone points on the base do not
affect this formula: the KK reduction is a local
computation (the Einstein--Hilbert integrand is a
density), and the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbifold singularities
have zero measure.
The flux quantization $\oint dA = \pi N$ is
determined by the orbifold Euler class $e = N/2$,
which is a topological invariant of the Seifert
fibration (unchanged by resolving or smoothing the
cone points).
\end{proof}

\section{The $N$ selection principle}
\label{sec:n-selection}

\begin{derivation}[$N$ selection from angular momentum quantization]
\label{thm:n-selection}
The three polygon numbers are determined by three structural
requirements (integer spin, stability, flux additivity):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $N_{\mathrm{EW}} = 4$: the unique even polygon with
  integer critical spin ($j = 1$ from $f(2,4) = 2 = j(j{+}1)$).
  Equivalently: the $\Lambda_3 = 0$ threshold.
\item[(b)] $N_{\mathrm{grav}} = 7$: the unique odd polygon with
  integer critical spin in the stable range $N \le N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$
  ($j = 2$ from $f(3,7) = 6 = j(j{+}1)$).
\item[(c)] $N_{\mathrm{cosmo}} = 11 = N_{\mathrm{EW}} + N_{\mathrm{grav}}$:
  the two gauge sectors share a single $S^1$ fiber
  with KK fluxes $N_{\mathrm{EW}}/2 = 2$ and
  $N_{\mathrm{grav}}/2 = 7/2$.
  The total flux is the first Chern class of the
  Whitney sum of the two determinant line bundles:
  $c_1(L_{\mathrm{EW}} \oplus L_{\mathrm{grav}})
  = c_1(L_{\mathrm{EW}}) + c_1(L_{\mathrm{grav}})
  = 4 + 7 = 11$, giving $N = 11$.
  No geometric input enters, only the linearity of
  $\oint A$ on the fiber (exhaustive elimination below).
\end{enumerate}
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (a).}
For even $N$: $f(N/2, N) = N^2/8$.
This is an integer only when $8 \mid N^2$, i.e.\ $4 \mid N$
(since $N^2/8 \in \mathbb{Z}$ requires $v_2(N^2) \ge 3$,
i.e.\ $v_2(N) \ge 2$, i.e.\ $4 \mid N$).
Writing $N = 4k$: $f(N/2, N) = 2k^2$.
The condition $j(j{+}1) = 2k^2$ with $j \in \mathbb{Z}$
requires $1 + 8k^2$ to be a perfect square.
At $k = 1$ ($N = 4$): $1 + 8 = 9 = 3^2$, giving $j = 1$.
The next solution is $k = 6$ ($N = 24$, $j = 8$).
So $N = 4$ is the unique even polygon with $j \le 7$.

\emph{Part (b).}
For odd~$N$: $f(\lfloor N/2 \rfloor, N) = (N^2{-}1)/8$.
Setting $y = 2j{+}1$, the integer-spin condition becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pell}
  N^2 - 2y^2 = -1
  \qquad \text{(the negative Pell equation for $\sqrt{2}$)}.
\end{equation}
The solutions are generated by the fundamental unit
$\varepsilon = 1 + \sqrt{2}$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$:
$(N_k, y_k) = $ the odd powers $\varepsilon^{2k-1}$.
Explicitly: $(1, 1)$, $\mathbf{(7, 5)}$, $(41, 29)$, $(239, 169)$, \ldots,
giving $j = 0, \mathbf{2}, 14, 84, \ldots$
The first non-trivial solution is $N = 7$, $j = 2$ (the graviton).
The next ($N = 41$) lies far above the flat-plane stability boundary
$N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$.

\emph{Part (c).}
The Seifert fiber $S^1$ is a circle bundle over $\mathbf{H}^2/\mathbb{Z}_N$,
classified by its degree (= first Chern class $c_1 = N$).
The two gauge sectors each define a Seifert bundle:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Electroweak} (Paper~IV, Theorem~\ref*{IV-thm:su2}):
  the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$ sector operates at
  $N_{\mathrm{EW}} = 4$, defining a line bundle $L_{\mathrm{EW}}$
  with $c_1(L_{\mathrm{EW}}) = 4$.
\item \emph{Gravity/color} (Paper~IV, Theorem~\ref*{IV-thm:su3}):
  the graviton sector operates at
  $N_{\mathrm{grav}} = 7$, defining a line bundle $L_{\mathrm{grav}}$
  with $c_1(L_{\mathrm{grav}}) = 7$.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Why $N = 11$ and not another value.}
The cosmological sector must combine the two \emph{gauge}
sectors ($N = 4$ and $N = 7$) on a single fiber.
Other polygon numbers ($N = 3, 5, 6$) carry \emph{matter}
modes but no gauge content (they have $j \ne 1, 2$
at their critical modes).
The selection criteria are:
theorems (KK flux additivity, Thurston uniqueness, McKay);
structural requirements (no duplication, $\ell$ determined);
and physical consistency ($j$ integer, gauge content present).
The selection is \textbf{forced by the central charge budget}:
$c_{\mathrm{gauge}} = 2 + 1 + 1 = 4$ accounts for exactly
$\mathrm{SU}(3)_1 \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_1 \times \mathrm{U}(1)_1$
(Paper~IV, no-hidden-sector theorem).
These three gauge factors require the $N = 7$ gravitational
sector and the $N = 4$ electroweak sector (the unique polygons
with $j = 2$ and $j = 1$ respectively;
Derivation~\ref*{IV-thm:su2}
and Proposition~\ref*{III-prop:graviton}).
On a single $S^1$ fiber (Thurston uniqueness),
flux additivity gives $N = 4 + 7 = 11$.
No alternative exists: adding a third sector would exceed
the central charge budget; removing one would eliminate a
derived gauge factor.

\paragraph{Systematic elimination.}
On a single $S^1$ fiber (Thurston uniqueness,
Paper~IV, Proposition~\ref*{IV-prop:spacetime}),
flux additivity gives
$N/2 = N_{\mathrm{EW}}/2 + N_{\mathrm{grav}}/2 = 4/2 + 7/2 = 11/2$.
All alternatives violate either the Pell equation
(non-integer $j$: mathematical) or the single-sector
requirement (each gauge factor once: structural):

\toprule
$N$ & Decomposition & Reason excluded & Type \\

\section{The electroweak hierarchy}

\begin{derivation}[Electroweak hierarchy from the hybrid instanton]
\label{thm:hierarchy}
The Planck/electroweak hierarchy decomposes into three
independently computable contributions:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hierarchy}
  \ln\!\frac{M_P}{M_{\mathrm{EW}}}
  = \underbrace{2S_{\mathrm{BO}}(7)}_{\text{instanton}}
  + \underbrace{\Delta\varepsilon\;\ln\varepsilon_7}_{\text{mass gap}}
  + \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\!\bigl(c_{11}/(24\pi^2)\bigr)}_{\text{gravity}},
\end{equation}
where $S_{\mathrm{BO}}$ is the WKB tunneling action through
the Born--Oppenheimer potential $V_7(\rho) = \ln(2\sinh\rho) + b(7) - 6$
and $\varepsilon_7 = 8 + 3\sqrt{7}$ is the fundamental unit
of~$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{7}]$.
Each component is computed from $b(7)$, $f(3,7)$, and
$\varepsilon_7$ (all determined by $N = 7$; no observed
quantity enters the computation).
The three terms are:
$2S_{\mathrm{BO}}(7) = 36.548$ (the WKB tunneling action,
a definite integral of $\sqrt{2c_7\,|V_7|}$;
$c_7 = 12\,b(7)$, $V_7 = \ln(2\sinh\rho) + b(7) - 6$);
$\Delta\varepsilon\,\ln\varepsilon_7 = 2.224$
(the Pell unit $\varepsilon_7 = 8{+}3\sqrt{7}$ and
the BF conformal dimension gap $\Delta\varepsilon = 0.8031$
from Paper~IV);
and $\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(c_{11}/(24\pi^2)) = -0.313$
(the gravity one-loop prefactor with $c_{11} = 12\,b(11)$).
Each coefficient is algebraically fixed by~$N$.
Numerically: $36.548 + 2.224 + (-0.313) = 38.459$,
versus the observed $\ln(M_P/v) = 38.442$
($0.04\%$ match in the log; $1.5\%$ in the ratio).

\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
Each component is computed independently.

\paragraph{Why $c_7$ for the instanton but $c_{11}$ for the gravity
prefactor.}
The electroweak hierarchy involves the $N = 7$ sector
(the graviton polygon): $S_{\mathrm{BO}}(7)$ and $\varepsilon_7$
are computed from $b(7)$ and $f(3,7)$.
The gravity prefactor $c_{11}/(24\pi^2)$ uses
$c_{11} = 12\,b(11)$ because the Planck mass
$M_P = \sqrt{c_{11}/G}$ is set by the \emph{full} spacetime
geometry ($N = 11$), not the graviton sector alone.
The two sectors decouple: the $N = 7$ BO potential determines
the tunneling rate (which sets $v/M_P$), while $N = 11$
determines the overall gravitational coupling (which sets
$M_P$ in physical units).
This decoupling is guaranteed by the $\mathbb{Z}_{56}$
charge conservation (Paper~IV, Corollary~\ref*{IV-cor:proton-stability}):
the $N = 7$ and $N = 4$ sectors cannot exchange KK quanta.

\paragraph{Component~1 (Instanton).}
The Born--Oppenheimer potential for the $N = 7$ breathing mode is
$V_7(\rho) = \ln(2\sinh\rho) + b(7) - f(3,7)$,
which is negative for $\rho < \rho^* = 1.734$
and positive for $\rho > \rho^*$.
The WKB tunneling action:
$S = \int_0^{\rho^*} \sqrt{2c_7\,|V_7(\rho)|}\;d\rho = 18.274$
(numerical integration, $c_7 = 12\,b(7) = 51.57$).
The value $b(7)$ enters both $V_7$ (through the potential offset)
and $c_7$ (through the kinetic coefficient); these are the
\emph{same} quantity with \emph{different} roles, not
two independent inputs.
The bounce action is $2S = 36.548$.

\paragraph{Component~2 (Mass gap).}
The asymptotic mass gap of the WDW equation is
$\Delta\varepsilon = 0.8031$
(the numerical WDW eigenvalue gap, \S\ref{sec:mass-gap}).
The leading-order analytical approximation is
$\sqrt{2/\pi} = 0.7979$ (harmonic oscillator at the
BO potential minimum); the difference $0.0052$
is the anharmonic $O(1/c)$ correction.
The hierarchy uses the exact numerical value:
$\Delta\varepsilon\,\ln\varepsilon_7 = 0.8031 \times 2.769 = 2.224$.
The Pell identity~\eqref{eq:pell-identity} below uses
$\sqrt{2/\pi}$ (the analytical part that participates
in the algebraic cancellation).

\paragraph{Component~3 (Gravity prefactor).}
The Planck mass is $M_P = \sqrt{c_{11}/(24\pi^2 R\ell)}$
from the KK reduction $G_4 = G_3/(2\pi R)$
with $G_3 = 3\ell/(2c_{11})$ (Brown--Henneaux).
This gives $(1/2)\ln(c_{11}/(24\pi^2)) = -0.313$
with $c_{11} = 12\,b(11) = 126.56$.
The central charge is $c_{11}$ (not $c_7$) because
the Planck mass is determined by the full spacetime geometry,
which has total Chern class~$N = 11$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:n-selection}).

\paragraph{The Pell identity.}
Components~1 and~2 satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pell-identity}
  \frac{2S_{\mathrm{BO}}(7)}{\ln\varepsilon_7}
  = 2N_{\mathrm{grav}} - \sqrt{2/\pi}
  - \frac{4}{c_7^2} + O(1/c_7^3),
\end{equation}
where $\ln\varepsilon_7 = \operatorname{arccosh}(8) = \ln(8{+}3\sqrt{7}\,)$
is the regulator of the real quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})$,
arising from the Pell equation $8^2 - 7 \times 3^2 = 1$.
The identity is verified to $0.00005\%$.
\emph{Proof sketch.}
The Pell unit satisfies the exact algebraic identity
$\varepsilon_7 = (N{+}1) + \sqrt{(N{+}1)^2 - 1}
= 8 + \sqrt{63} = 8 + 3\sqrt{7}$,
so $\ln\varepsilon_7 = \operatorname{arccosh}(N{+}1)
= \operatorname{arccosh}(8) = 2.769$.
The leading-order identity
$2S_{\mathrm{BO}}(N)/\ln\varepsilon_7 = 2N - \sqrt{2/\pi} + O(1/c)$
follows because the WKB integrand
$\sqrt{2c\,|V(\rho)|}$ with $V = \ln(2\sinh\rho) + b - f$
is dominated at large~$c$ by the harmonic
frequency $\omega_0$ at the BO minimum,
and the ratio of this harmonic action to the
Pell regulator scales as $2N$ at leading order
with a Gaussian tail correction~$\sqrt{2/\pi}$.
Full proof in Supplement~S, \S\ref*{SP-sec:pell-proof}.

\begin{proposition}[Pell uniqueness at $N = 7$]
\label{prop:pell-unique}
$N = 7$ is the unique positive integer for which
$(N{+}1, \lfloor(N{-}1)/2\rfloor)$ is a Pell pair,
i.e.\ $(N{+}1)^2 - N \lfloor(N{-}1)/2\rfloor^2 = 1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For odd~$N$ with $m^* = (N{-}1)/2$:
$(N{+}1)^2 - N m^{*2}
= (-N^3{+}6N^2{+}7N{+}4)/4$.
Setting this to~$1$: $N(N{-}7)(N{+}1) = 0$.
The unique positive root is $N = 7$.
\end{proof}

The \emph{per-vortex} form of the identity is illuminating:
each of the $N = 7$ vortices contributes one Pell regulator
$\ln\varepsilon_7$ to the tunneling action, reduced by the
per-vortex quantum correction
$\sqrt{2/\pi}/(2N) + 2/(Nc_7^2)$.
The instanton provides $94.3\%$ of the Pell exponent;
the mass gap provides the remaining $5.7\%$.
Together: $e^{14\,\ln\varepsilon_7} = \varepsilon_7^{14}
= (8{+}3\sqrt{7}\,)^{14} = 6.82 \times 10^{16}$.

\paragraph{Cancellation.}
The $\sqrt{2/\pi}$ terms cancel between the instanton (component~1)
and the mass gap (component~2):
\[
  \ln\frac{M_P}{M_{\mathrm{EW}}}
  = \bigl(14 - \tfrac{4}{c_7^2}\bigr)\ln\varepsilon_7
  + \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\!\bigl(\tfrac{c_{11}}{24\pi^2}\bigr) + O(1/c_7^3).
\]
At leading order, the hierarchy depends on the Pell unit
$\varepsilon_7$ and the central charge; the mass gap
$\Delta\varepsilon = 0.8031$ enters only at subleading
order $O(\ln\varepsilon_7/c_7) \approx 0.04$, producing
the $0.04\%$ residual in the log.
\end{proof}

\noindent
\emph{Why} $\sim\!10^{17}$ and not larger:
$N_{\mathrm{grav}} = 7$ is the \emph{first} non-trivial Pell solution
(Theorem~\ref{thm:n-selection}).
At the next ($N = 41$):
$\varepsilon_7^{82} \sim 10^{99}$, no atoms could form.

\section{The cosmological constant from the $N=11$ instanton}
\label{sec:cc-instanton}

With the electroweak VEV $v = 242.0$\,GeV determined by the
$N = 7$ hierarchy (Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy}), the $N = 11$
tunneling action fixes the curvature radius~$\ell$.
The following two lemmas isolate the subleading corrections
to the tunneling action; the main theorem references them.

\begin{lemma}[One-loop determinant correction is $-\gamma/2$]
\label{lem:one-loop}
The one-loop functional determinant of the tunneling
amplitude contributes $\delta(\ln A) = -\gamma/2$
to the exponent, where $\gamma = 0.5772\ldots$ is the
Euler--Mascheroni constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The tunneling amplitude is
$A = e^{-S}\bigl(\det'(-\partial^2 + V'')/\det(-\partial^2+\omega^2)
\bigr)^{\!-1/2}$,
with $\det'$ the zero-mode-removed functional determinant.
The ratio is computed via spectral zeta regularization:
$\ln(\det'/\det) = -\zeta'_{\mathrm{rel}}(0)$.
The BF threshold at $c = 1$ has spectral flow $\mathrm{SF} = 1$.
\emph{Proof of $\mathrm{SF} = 1$}:
on $\mathbf{H}^2$, the radial effective potential
for conformal dimension~$c$ is $V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\rho)
= c(c{-}1)/\sinh^2\!\rho$.
As $c$ sweeps from $c_{\mathrm{up}} = 1/2$ to
$c_{\mathrm{dn}} = 3/2$ (the BF crossing of Step~1),
the mass-squared $M^2(c) = c(c{-}1)$ passes through zero
exactly once, at $c = 1$.
The crossing is simple: $dM^2/dc = 2c{-}1 = 1 \ne 0$ at $c = 1$.
By the standard spectral flow theorem (Atiyah--Patodi--Singer),
each simple zero crossing contributes $\mathrm{SF} = \pm 1$;
the sign is $\mathrm{sgn}(dM^2/dc) = +1$.
No other eigenvalue crosses zero in the interval
$c \in [1/2, 3/2]$ (the next zero of $c(c{-}1)$ is at
$c = 0$, outside the interval).
Therefore $\mathrm{SF} = 1$ exactly.
The Hurwitz zeta identity
$\zeta'_H(0, a) = \log\Gamma(a) - \tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)$
gives $\partial_a \zeta'_H(0,a)\big|_{a=1} = \psi(1) = -\gamma$.
The threshold contribution to the relative zeta is therefore
$\zeta'_{\mathrm{threshold}}(0) = -\gamma \times \mathrm{SF} = -\gamma$.
The bulk-spectrum contributions are regular at $s = 0$:
the BO potential
$V_{11}(\rho) = \log(2\sinh\rho) + b - f_{m^*}$
is confining ($V_{11} \to \rho$ as $\rho \to \infty$),
so both $H$ and the reference operator~$H_0$ have purely
discrete spectra $\{\lambda_n\}$ and $\{\lambda_n^0\}$
with Weyl asymptotics $\lambda_n \sim n^2$.
The eigenvalue differences satisfy
$\lambda_n - \lambda_n^0 = O(1/n)$ for large~$n$
(because $H$ and $H_0$ have the same confining potential
at infinity and differ only near the BF crossing).
The relative zeta
$\zeta_{\mathrm{rel}}(s) = \sum(\lambda_n^{-s} - \lambda_n^{0,-s})$
therefore converges for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1/2$
and continues meromorphically to $s = 0$
with no pole (the Weyl coefficients match).
The derivative $\zeta'_{\mathrm{rel}}(0)$
receives contributions from two sources:
(i)~the threshold crossing ($\mathrm{SF} = 1$),
which gives $-\gamma$ as computed above;
(ii)~the bulk spectral difference.
The heat-kernel expansion of $H = -\tfrac{1}{2c}\partial_\rho^2
+ V(\rho)$ on the half-line gives
$\mathrm{Tr}(e^{-tH} - e^{-tH_0})
= (a_0' - a_0^{0\prime})\,t^{-1/2}
+ (a_1' - a_1^{0\prime})
+ O(t^{1/2})$
as $t \to 0^+$.
The Seeley--DeWitt coefficients are:
$a_0' = (2c)^{1/2}/(4\pi)^{1/2}$
(volume, identical for $H$ and $H_0$ since both
act on the same half-line),
and $a_1'$ is determined by the \emph{regularized}
potential integral
$\int_0^\infty [V(\rho) - V_{\mathrm{asymp}}(\rho)]\,d\rho$,
where $V_{\mathrm{asymp}} = \rho + \ln 2 + b - f$
is the large-$\rho$ asymptote.
Since $V - V_{\mathrm{asymp}}
= \log(2\sinh\rho) - \rho - \ln 2
= \log(1 - e^{-2\rho})$ (independent of $f$) the
regularized integral is $\int_0^\infty\!\log(1{-}e^{-2\rho})\,
d\rho = -\pi^2/12$
(a standard Bose--Einstein integral)
for \emph{both} $H$ and $H_0$.
Equality $a_0' = a_0^{0\prime}$ (both operators on the same
half-line) and $a_1' = a_1^{0\prime}$
(the $f$-independent regularized integral $-\pi^2/12$
determines the $O(1)$ heat-kernel coefficient for
\emph{both} potentials) ensures that
$\zeta_{\mathrm{rel}}(s)$ has no pole at $s = 0$
or $s = 1/2$.
Explicitly: the heat-kernel remainder
$r(t) = \mathrm{Tr}(e^{-tH} - e^{-tH_0})
- (a_0' - a_0^{0\prime})t^{-1/2}
- (a_1' - a_1^{0\prime})$
satisfies $r(t) = O(t^{1/2})$ as $t \to 0^+$
(from the next Seeley--DeWitt coefficient)
and $r(t) = O(e^{-\delta t})$ as $t \to \infty$
(from the spectral gap $\delta > 0$ of both operators
on the confining half-line).
The Mellin transform
$\zeta_{\mathrm{rel}}(s) = \tfrac{1}{\Gamma(s)}
\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}\mathrm{Tr}(e^{-tH}-e^{-tH_0})\,dt$
therefore converges absolutely for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1/2$
and, after subtracting the two (vanishing) Seeley--DeWitt
terms, continues analytically to $\mathrm{Re}(s) > -1/2$
with no poles.
In particular, $\zeta'_{\mathrm{rel}}(0)$ receives no bulk
contribution, only the threshold crossing~(i).
The ratio $\det'/\det = e^\gamma$ follows.
With $(\det)^{-1/2}$:
$\delta(\ln A) = -\gamma/2$,
so $\ln(\ell\,v) = S_{\mathrm{BO}} - \gamma/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Non-adiabatic corrections vanish for Havelock eigenvectors]
\label{lem:nonadiabatic}
For the Havelock bath, the non-adiabatic coupling between
angular modes vanishes identically:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:no-nonadiabatic}
  \frac{\partial \lambda_m}{\partial\rho}
  = C_1'(\rho) \quad\text{for all } m
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \langle m | \partial H/\partial\rho | m' \rangle
  = C_1'(\rho)\,\delta_{mm'}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the BO potential is exact at all adiabatic orders;
the only corrections to $S_{\mathrm{BO}}$ are the WKB (Dunham)
anharmonicity series in powers of $1/c_{11}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvectors of the Havelock Hessian are the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier modes
$|m\rangle = N^{-1/2}\sum_j \omega^{mj}$, which are
$\rho$-independent (fixed by the group structure).
Hence the non-adiabatic coupling vanishes exactly:
$\langle m|\partial/\partial\rho|m'\rangle = 0$,
and the BO potential is exact at all adiabatic orders.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Precision budget for Lemma~\ref{lem:nonadiabatic}]
The only corrections to $S_{\mathrm{BO}}$ are the
Dunham anharmonicity series in $1/c_{11} \approx 0.008$.
The leading correction arises from the cubic anharmonicity
of $V(\rho) = \ln(2\sinh\rho)$ near the turning point
$\rho^*$: expanding $V(\rho) \approx V(\rho^*)
+ V'(\rho^*)\delta\rho + \tfrac{1}{2}V''\delta\rho^2
+ \tfrac{1}{6}V'''\delta\rho^3$,
the standard Dunham correction
\citep{Dunham1932} is
$\delta S = -V'''(\rho^*)/(24 c_N\,|V''(\rho^*)|^{3/2})$.
At $\rho^* \gg 1$: $V''(\rho^*) = -\operatorname{csch}^2(\rho^*)
\approx -4e^{-2\rho^*}$,
$V'''(\rho^*) = 2\operatorname{csch}^2(\rho^*)\coth(\rho^*)
\approx 8e^{-2\rho^*}$,
giving $\delta S \approx 1/(6c_N \times 2e^{-\rho^*})
= e^{\rho^*}/(12c_N)$.
For the $N = 11$ WDW potential,
$e^{\rho^*_{11}} \approx 2\sinh(\rho^*_{11}) = e^{f_{11} - b_{11}}$;
the numerical evaluation gives $\delta S = \ln 2/(2c_{11}) = 0.0027$.
The next term is $O(1/c_{11}^2) \approx 6 \times 10^{-5}$.
The comparison is also limited by the experimental
precision of~$M_P$ ($\sim 10^{-5}$).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Free energy decomposition}
The $N - 2$ angular Havelock modes with $m \ne m^*$
are \emph{frozen}: their eigenvalues $\lambda_m > 0$ at
the BF threshold prevent them from participating in
breathing-mode tunneling, so they contribute only through
their one-loop determinant.
The partition function $Z = e^{-F}$ of the BO
breathing mode then has three contributions:
\begin{align}
  F_{\mathrm{DE}} &= b(N) + \tfrac{N}{4}
    & &\text{(tree-level: vacuum $+$ radion zero-point)}, \label{eq:F-DE}\\
  F_{\mathrm{DM}} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\!\sum_{m \ne m^*}\!
    \ln\bigl|f(m^*) - f(m)\bigr|
    & &\text{(one-loop: frozen angular modes)}, \label{eq:F-DM}\\
  F_{\mathrm{M}}  &= \Delta E
    & &\text{(non-perturbative: WDW mass gap)}. \label{eq:F-M}
\end{align}
The decomposition is \emph{canonical}: each term arises from
a distinct order of the saddle-point expansion
(classical action, Gaussian fluctuation, tunneling correction).
No freedom exists in assigning terms to orders.
The \emph{physical labeling}
(DE $\leftrightarrow$ tree, DM $\leftrightarrow$ one-loop,
baryon $\leftrightarrow$ non-perturbative)
follows from the equation of state of each term:
\begin{itemize}
\item $F_{\mathrm{DE}}$ is $\rho$-independent at the saddle:
  a constant energy density has $\rho + p = 0$ ($w = -1$),
  which is the equation of state of the cosmological constant
  (dark energy).
\item $F_{\mathrm{DM}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum\ln|\lambda_m(\rho)|$
  is the one-loop determinant of the frozen angular modes.
  Unlike $F_{\mathrm{DE}}$ (which is $\rho$-independent),
  $F_{\mathrm{DM}}$ depends on the breathing mode~$\rho$
  through $\lambda_m = C_1(\rho) - f(m)$.
  This $\rho$-dependence is the key:
  a $\rho$-independent contribution would be vacuum energy
  ($w = -1$), but a contribution that depends on the
  dynamical variable~$\rho$ (which evolves with cosmic time)
  has zero pressure.
  Explicitly: the stress-energy tensor of the frozen modes is
  $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho_E + p)\,u^\mu u^\nu + p\,g^{\mu\nu}$
  with energy density $\rho_E = F_{\mathrm{DM}}(\rho)/(a^3 V_0)$
  (where $a$ is the FRW scale factor and
  $V_0$ the comoving volume) and pressure
  $p = -\partial F_{\mathrm{DM}}/\partial V$.
  Since $F_{\mathrm{DM}} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{m \ne m^*}
  \ln|\lambda_m(\rho)|$ depends on $\rho$ (the breathing mode,
  evolving with cosmic time via the WDW equation)
  but not on the spatial volume~$V$ of the $\mathbf{H}^2$ slice
  ($\lambda_m$ is determined by the polygon geometry,
  not the slice area):
  $p = -\partial F_{\mathrm{DM}}/\partial V = 0$, hence $w = 0$.
  After the $N$-selection phase transition, each frozen mode
  becomes a massive KK particle with
  $M_k = \sqrt{\lambda_k}\,M_{\mathrm{poly}}$,
  produced at rest in the comoving frame
  (zero peculiar velocity: the BO tunneling creates
  particles at the bottom of the angular potential well,
  not with kinetic energy).
  The stress-energy is
  $T_{\mu\nu} = \rho_E\,u_\mu u_\nu$ with $p = 0$
  (cold dark matter): massive, non-relativistic,
  non-interacting (orthogonal $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier modes
  have zero overlap by character orthogonality).
\item $F_{\mathrm{M}}$ is the non-perturbative
  WDW mass gap (the tunneling amplitude), localized and
  thermalized below $T_{\mathrm{EW}}$: baryonic matter ($w = 0$).
\end{itemize}
The equation-of-state assignment is determined by the
saddle-point order: constant ($w = {-}1$),
frozen massive ($w = 0$), localized tunneling ($w = 0$).
No freedom exists in assigning terms to cosmological
components.
The radion contribution $N/4$ in~\eqref{eq:F-DE} is the zero-point
energy of the $S^1$ fiber modulus, stabilized at unit radius by
the flux~$N/2$: the radion mass is $M_{\mathrm{rad}} = N/2$,
giving $(1/2)M_{\mathrm{rad}} = N/4$.
This is determined by the geometry.
The eigenvalues $\lambda_m(\rho^*) = f(m^*) - f(m)$
(where $f(m) = m(N{-}m)/2$) are the Havelock eigenvalues evaluated at
the BF threshold $\rho = \rho^*$, where the critical mode
$\lambda_{m^*} = 0$ and all remaining modes are positive.
The sum excludes both critical modes
($m = m^*$ and $m = N - m^*$ when $N$ is odd).
For $N = 11$: $f(m^*) = 15$, and $F_{\mathrm{DM}} = 5.193$.

adjacent flat connections on the Seifert manifold
have spectral flow $\pm 1$
(one eigenvalue crossing zero),
shifting the eta invariant by~$\pm 1$
and flipping the sign of the analytic torsion.
The $n$-instanton amplitude scales as $K^{2n}$ (not
$K^{2n}$ times an $n$-dependent prefactor) because
the CS action on the Seifert manifold is additive
across flat connections: the $n$-th flat connection
has Chern--Simons invariant $\mathrm{CS}_n = n \times
\mathrm{CS}_1$ (mod~$\mathbb{Z}$), so the tunneling
exponent is $e^{-2\pi n \,k_{\mathrm{frac}}} = K^n$.
The instanton--anti-instanton pair has amplitude $K^n
\times K^n = K^{2n}$.

property of analytic torsion on product connections
\citep{Rozansky1995}.

is $(-1)^n |\tau_1|^n K^{2n} = (-1)^n (|\tau_1| K^2)^n$.
