\title{Why Rotating Fluids Make Polygons}
\author{}

\begin{abstract}
On the flat ($K=0$) plane, the regular $N$-gon of point vortices is an
energy minimum on the surface of conserved angular impulse
$L = \sum|z_k|^2$ and linear impulse $\mathbf{P} = \sum z_k = 0$ for
all $N \le 7$, and unstable for $N \ge 8$ without a central vortex.
This stability boundary is an invariant of
the two-dimensional Laplacian: the logarithmic Green's function
$G \propto -\ln r$ is the unique radially symmetric planar interaction
for which the Havelock eigenvalue $\lambda_m = (N-1) - m(N-m)/2$ is
scale-independent, and $N = 7$ is the threshold where the leading
eigenvalue $\lambda_3 = 0$ (the linear restoring force vanishes).
A quartic calculation on the constraint surface confirms that the
$N = 7$ ring is nonlinearly stable: the quartic coefficient
$\alpha_0 = 45/14 \approx 3.21 > 0$ (in the normal form
$\mathcal{H} = (\alpha_0/4)\varepsilon^4$) supplies a restoring force
where the linear one is absent.

For $N \ge 8$ the stability boundary requires a central vortex of
strength $\kappa_0/\kappa \ge \kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(N)$.  The sharpest
prediction is at $N = 8$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(8) = 1/2$.  In a
Bose--Einstein condensate, where circulation is quantized, this is an
integer-valued threshold: a ring of eight unit-winding vortices is
stable if and only if the central vortex carries winding number
$q_0 \ge 1$.

The central unifying result is a \emph{constraint-intersection
principle}: three selection mechanisms---Rossby wave stationarity,
Thomson vortex stability, and geometric packing---each derived from
the 2D Laplacian, constrain the polygon wavenumber $N$ from above.
The observed $N$ is determined by whichever constraint is tightest,
and different planetary parameters activate different constraints.
Saturn's hexagon ($N = 6$) is Rossby-limited (wave stationarity
of the QGPV equation selects $n = 6$).
Jupiter's north-pole octagon ($N = 8$) is Thomson-limited (the
ring energy $H_N$ is strictly increasing in $N$, so Onsager
negative-temperature dynamics selects the maximum stable $N$;
the prediction $N_{\max} = 8$ relies on
$\kappa_0/\kappa \lesssim 1$, testable with improved Juno
wind measurements).
Jupiter's south-pole pentagon ($N = 5$) is packing-limited (the
cyclones are too large for more than five to fit on the ring,
a constraint derived from domain admissibility of the Laplacian's
Green's function on the punctured domain).

On the hyperbolic plane $\mathbf{H}^2$ with curvature $K = -1/a^2$,
the stability boundary rises monotonically: $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 12$
at ring-to-curvature ratio $R/a = 1$.  A 12-vortex ring stable
without a central vortex is impossible in flat geometry; this is a
falsifiable prediction for vortex dynamics on hyperbolic lattices
now realizable in circuit QED \citep{Kollar2019}.
The $7 \to 8$ transition occurs
at the unique $\xi^* \in (0,1)$ satisfying
$7(1+\xi^2)/(1-\xi)^2 = 8$, reducing to
$\xi^2 - 16\xi + 1 = 0$ with positive root $\xi^* = 8 - 3\sqrt{7}$.
The threshold polynomial $\xi^2 - 16\xi + 1 = 0$ is \emph{palindromic}
(equal leading and trailing coefficients), forcing its roots to
multiply to~$1$: $\xi^*$ is automatically an algebraic unit
($\xi^* = \varepsilon^{-1}$, where
$\varepsilon = 8 + 3\sqrt{7}$ is the fundamental unit of
$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{7}]$).  The palindromic structure is not
accidental---it reflects the conformal inversion symmetry
$\xi \leftrightarrow 1/\xi$ of the Poincar\'e disk
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mobius}), connecting the algebraic number
theory of the threshold to the M\"obius covariance of the
logarithmic energy.  The~$7$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})$ appears
because the threshold condition has discriminant
$252 = 4 \cdot 9 \cdot 7$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

These observations raise a question with two parts.  The first---\emph{how}
does a specific polygon form?---has received considerable attention.
The second---\emph{why} should different physical mechanisms on different
planets all produce the same geometric outcome?---has not.

In 1988, David Godfrey examined Voyager images of Saturn's north pole
and found a hexagon \citep{Godfrey1988}---a persistent, regular,
six-sided polygon inscribed in the jet stream at $76^\circ$\,N, stable
across decades.  Its most striking property is that it
co-rotates with Saturn's System~III period ($10^{\mathrm{h}}\,39^{\mathrm{m}}\,22.4^{\mathrm{s}}$),
the rotation rate of the deep interior inferred from the
magnetic field, rather than being advected by the $\sim\!120$\,m/s
eastward jet in which it sits \citep{Godfrey1988, SanchezLavega2014}.
This phase-locking to the deep interior has been confirmed from Voyager
through Cassini across more than 30 years and is the primary
observational constraint on any proposed mechanism.  Thirty years later, the Juno spacecraft revealed that
Jupiter's poles harbor their own polygonal arrangements: eight cyclones
in an octagonal ring at the north pole, five in a pentagonal ring at the
south \citep{Adriani2018}.  Numerical simulations can reproduce these
structures \citep{Morales2011, Gavriel2021}, and vortex crystals emerge
spontaneously in 2D turbulence simulations on polar caps
\citep{Siegelman2022}.

We answer the second question, subject to one physical assumption
(Onsager negative-temperature statistics at the polar scale; see
Section~\ref{sec:extensions} for a detailed assessment).  The
mechanism operates at two scales:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Macro scale: cluster formation.}  The Kraichnan (1967)
    inverse energy cascade drives 2D turbulence toward large-scale coherent
    structures.  In the Onsager (1949) framework, this produces a
    negative-temperature state where same-sign vorticity concentrates
    into $N$ coherent clusters \citep{BouchetVenaille2012, Siegelman2022}.
    This step explains why discrete vortex clusters exist; it does not
    determine their spatial arrangement.

  \item \textbf{Meso scale: polygon arrangement.}  Given $N$ vortex
    clusters on a ring of fixed angular momentum, the regular $N$-gon
    \emph{minimizes} the interaction energy on the conserved-quantity
    surface (Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}).  By the energy-Casimir
    method, this makes the $N$-gon a nonlinearly stable equilibrium for
    $N \le 7$ without a central vortex, or for any $N$ with sufficient
    central vortex strength.  The polygon is selected by dynamical
    stability, not by statistical mechanics.
\end{enumerate}

The meso-scale proof (Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}) applies to
discrete point vortices and is directly relevant to Jupiter's cyclone
rings.  Saturn's hexagon is a continuous jet meander, not a discrete
vortex ring; its wavenumber $N = 6$ is selected instead by Rossby
wave stationarity of the QGPV equation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:complementary}).  Both mechanisms share the
logarithmic Green's function as a structural foundation, but the
mathematical arguments differ.

\emph{Which} polygon forms is determined by planet-specific selection
criteria: $N = 6$ by Rossby stationarity (Saturn), $N = 8$ by the
Thomson--Onsager maximum-stability bound (Jupiter north).  The observed
$N = 5$ at Jupiter's south pole is consistent with Thomson stability
($\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(5) = 0$, so no central vortex is required)
but is not uniquely predicted by the present framework; the south-polar
packing geometry provides the additional constraint
(Section~\ref{sec:n-selection}).

Beyond the solar system, the framework makes its most physically
distinctive prediction on the hyperbolic plane: a ring of $N = 12$
vortices at $R/a = 1$ is stable \emph{without} a central vortex---an
impossibility in flat geometry, where any $N \ge 8$ ring requires
central stabilization.  Recent implementations of hyperbolic lattice
Hamiltonians in circuit QED \citep{Kollar2019} and topolectrical
circuits \citep{Lenggenhager2022} make this prediction experimentally
accessible (Section~\ref{sec:curvature-invariant}).

\section{The logarithmic interaction: uniqueness and symmetry}
\label{sec:log-interaction}

\subsection{Dilation invariance and the Cauchy functional equation}
\label{sec:dilation-invariance}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The logarithm as the unique dilation-invariant interaction]
\label{rem:rg-fixed-point}
Among all pairwise interactions $h:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$ with
$h'<0$, the logarithmic interaction $h(r)=-\ln r$ is the
\emph{unique interaction invariant under spatial dilation up to an
additive constant}.  Define the scale map $T_b[h](r) = h(br)$
($b>0$).  Dilation-invariance means $h(br) = h(r) + c(b)$ for
all $r$---that is, rescaling changes $h$ only by an additive constant
(irrelevant to the force $-h'$).  This is a Cauchy functional equation
whose continuous solutions with $h'<0$ are exactly $h(r)=-a\ln r + b$,
$a>0$, confirming class~(ii) as the unique dilation-invariant class.
The $N$-body lift of this per-pair statement is
Corollary~\ref{cor:dilation-rg}: the M\"obius covariance of $H$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mobius}), restricted to the dilation subgroup,
gives $H(bz_1,\dots,bz_N)=H(z_1,\dots,z_N)-\tfrac{N(N-1)}{2}\ln b$,
which is position-independent---the same fixed-point condition at the
$N$-body level.
This symmetry is not merely a geometric observation about the
logarithm: it is the structural reason for the \emph{scale-independence}
of $N_{\mathrm{crit}}=7$.  Because scaling changes $H$ by an
additive constant (Corollary~\ref{cor:dilation-rg}), the Havelock
eigenvalue $\lambda_m = (N{-}1) - m(N{-}m)/2$---which determines
whether the $N$-gon is a stable constrained minimum---is
scale-independent, and the threshold $N=7$ cannot drift under
spatial rescaling.  Prior treatments of M\"obius covariance in vortex
dynamics (e.g.\ \citealt{Newton2001}) identify it as a conformal
symmetry of the stream function; Corollary~\ref{cor:dilation-rg}
shows it additionally implies that the stability threshold is
dilation-invariant.

Under linearization, a perturbation $g(r) = r^\alpha$ transforms as
$T_b[g](r) = b^\alpha\, r^\alpha$.  The scaling exponent is $b^\alpha$:
perturbations with $\alpha < 0$ (decaying at large $r$) are
\emph{suppressed under rescaling}---the coefficient $b^\alpha\to 0$
as $b\to\infty$; perturbations with $\alpha>0$ are
\emph{amplified under rescaling}---the coefficient grows.
Notably, the class~(i) interactions $h=-r^\beta$ ($\beta>0$)
are amplified by rescaling: they depart from the logarithm at large
scales, becoming stronger confining potentials.  Despite this, class~(i)
still satisfies the sign rule and yields polygon equilibria, so polygon
formation is robust across both the dilation-invariant interaction and
interactions amplified by rescaling.

The \emph{stable manifold} of $h=-\ln r$---interactions of the form
$-\ln r$ plus corrections that decay at large $r$---arises naturally
from compact vorticity distributions.  For $|z|<\varepsilon\ll|w|=d$,
expand $\ln|w-z| = \ln d - \mathrm{Re}(z/w) - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Re}(z^2/w^2)
+ O(\varepsilon^3/d^3)$.
Integrating against vorticity $\omega(z)\,\mathrm{d}^2z$ gives
$\int\!\omega(z)\ln|w{-}z|\,\mathrm{d}^2z
 = K\ln d - d^{-1}\mathrm{Re}\bigl(e^{-i\theta}\!\int\!z\,\omega\,\mathrm{d}^2z\bigr)
 + O(\varepsilon^2/d^2)$.
When the vortex centroid lies at the origin the dipole term vanishes,
leaving an effective pairwise interaction
$H_\mathrm{eff}(d)=-K^2\ln d + O(\varepsilon^2/d^2)$ for separations
$d\gg\varepsilon$.  The quadrupole corrections are suppressed as $d/\varepsilon\to\infty$
and vanish in the far-field limit.  In the Kraichnan inverse-energy-cascade
picture~\cite{Kraichnan1967}, vorticity concentrates into compact
structures of growing scale $L(t)$; at each scale the dominant
inter-blob interaction is the logarithm, with higher-multipole
corrections suppressed by $(L/d)^2\to 0$.  The cascade therefore
drives the system toward the dilation-invariant logarithmic interaction,
where the sign-rule polygon equilibria apply.  This provides a physical
explanation for why the sign rule---proved for a specific functional
form---describes systems whose vorticity is not literally point-like:
at the relevant scale $d\gg L$, the effective interaction is
the logarithm to high accuracy.

The Gaussian curvature $K$ is a destabilizing perturbation when
$K>0$ and a stabilizing one when $K<0$.  On the sphere ($K>0$) the
deviation from the logarithmic Green's function lowers
$N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ below~$7$; on the hyperbolic plane ($K<0$) it
adds a positive $O(|K|)$ correction $12|K|$ to the formerly-marginal
$\lambda_3$ eigenvalue~\eqref{eq:lam3_H2}.  The flat-plane case
$K=0$ is therefore the \emph{least stable} point of the
constant-curvature family with respect to polygon formation:

\subsection{M\"obius covariance of the logarithmic energy}
\label{sec:mobius-cov}

\begin{proposition}[M\"obius covariance]\label{prop:mobius}
Under a conformal map $f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$, the
logarithmic energy transforms as
\[
  H(f(z_1),\dots,f(z_N))
  = H(z_1,\dots,z_N) - \sum_{k=1}^{N}\tfrac{N-1}{2}\ln|f'(z_k)|.
\]
Consequently, the point vortex equations of motion are
M\"obius-invariant: the single-site correction
$\tfrac{N-1}{2}\ln|f'(z_k)|$ depends only on~$z_k$, so
$\partial/\partial \bar z_j$ for $j \ne k$ annihilates it, and the
Hamiltonian gradient $\partial H/\partial \bar z_j$ is unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The identity $|f(z){-}f(w)| = |f'(z)|^{1/2}|f'(w)|^{1/2}|z{-}w|
\cdot(1 + O(|z{-}w|))$ is exact when $f$ is M\"obius (the error
term vanishes), so $\ln|f(z_j){-}f(z_k)| = \ln|z_j{-}z_k|
+ \tfrac12\ln|f'(z_j)| + \tfrac12\ln|f'(z_k)|$.  Summing over
all $\binom{N}{2}$ pairs, each $\ln|f'(z_k)|$ appears $N{-}1$
times.  Since $\tfrac12\ln|f'(z_k)|$ depends on $z_k$ alone,
$\partial[\sum_k\tfrac{N-1}{2}\ln|f'(z_k)|]/\partial\bar z_j
= \tfrac{N-1}{2}\,\partial\ln|f'(z_j)|/\partial\bar z_j$,
which is the single-site term in the $j$-th equation of motion.
For M\"obius $f$, $f'$ is rational and $\ln|f'|$ is harmonic away
from poles, so this term contributes a gauge-like shift to the
Hamiltonian that does not couple distinct vortices.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dilation subgroup = scale-invariant Hamiltonian]
\label{cor:dilation-rg}
Among all M\"obius transformations, the dilation subgroup
$\{f(z) = bz : b > 0\}$ is uniquely characterised by having
constant derivative: $f'(z) = b$ for all~$z$.  Substituting
into Proposition~\ref{prop:mobius} gives a correction that is
\emph{position-independent}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilation-rg}
  H(bz_1,\dots,bz_N)
  = H(z_1,\dots,z_N) - \tfrac{N(N-1)}{2}\ln b.
\end{equation}
This is the $N$-body form of the dilation-invariance condition of
Remark~\ref{rem:rg-fixed-point}: scaling changes $H$ only by an
additive constant, so the equations of motion
$\dot{\bar z}_j = \partial H/\partial z_j$ are dilation-invariant.
For any other M\"obius map $f$, the derivative $|f'(z_k)|$ varies
between sites, producing a configuration-dependent correction that
is not a pure constant; the Hamiltonian is \emph{not}
scale-invariant under general conformal maps, only under the
one-parameter dilation subgroup.

Equation~\eqref{eq:dilation-rg} is the $N$-body lift of the per-pair
uniqueness result in Remark~\ref{rem:rg-fixed-point}: the logarithmic
energy is the unique $N$-body Hamiltonian that is dilation-invariant.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[The loxodromic thread]
\label{rem:loxodromic}
The logarithmic interaction and the logarithmic spiral are dual
manifestations of the same structure: the loxodromic one-parameter
subgroup $z \mapsto e^{(\sigma + i\alpha)t}z$ of the M\"obius group
acting on~$\mathbb{C}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{interaction} is logarithmic because it is the
    unique dilation-invariant pairwise potential
    (Remark~\ref{rem:rg-fixed-point}; Cauchy's functional equation).
  \item The natural \emph{deformation curves} are logarithmic spirals
    because they are orbits of the dilation-rotation generator
    $\sigma + i\alpha$; the centered spiral of
    equation~\eqref{eq:deformation} is precisely this orbit applied
    to the $N$-gon.
  \item The \emph{stability threshold} $N = 7$ is scale-independent
    because the Havelock eigenvalue $\lambda_m = (N{-}1) - m(N{-}m)/2$
    has no $R$-dependence---a direct consequence of
    equation~\eqref{eq:dilation-rg}.
  \item The \emph{palindromic} structure of the hyperbolic threshold
    polynomial $A\xi^2 + B\xi + A = 0$
    (equation~\eqref{eq:palindromic}) encodes the conformal inversion
    $\xi \leftrightarrow 1/\xi$ of the Poincar\'e disk, which is a
    M\"obius map exchanging the interior and exterior of the unit
    circle.
\end{itemize}
These four consequences---the interaction, the deformation, the
threshold, and the algebraic structure---all descend from M\"obius
covariance (Proposition~\ref{prop:mobius}).  The unifying role of
the M\"obius group is not merely a symmetry of the equations of
motion; it is the reason the logarithmic interaction produces
polygons at all.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[From sign rule to physical stability]
\label{rem:signrule-to-stability}
Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-rule} establishes $H''(0) < 0$ along the spiral
direction---an \emph{unconstrained} energy curvature.  This does not by
itself imply instability, because the spiral deformation changes the
conserved angular impulse $L = \sum|z_k|^2$ at $O(\sigma^2)$.  The
physical dynamics is constrained to $\{L = \mathrm{const}\}$, and
the Lagrange multiplier $\mu_L < 0$ shifts the curvature by
$-2\mu_L > 0$, converting the unconstrained saddle into a
\emph{constrained minimum} (Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min} below).
The sign rule therefore plays an indirect but essential role: it
guarantees that the $N$-gon is a saddle of the unconstrained energy
with a specific sign structure ($H''(0) < 0$ along the spiral,
$H''(0) > 0$ along other directions), and the Lagrange shift converts
this saddle into a minimum for $N \le 7$.  For $N \ge 8$, the negative
curvature along the $m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$ Fourier mode overwhelms
the Lagrange shift, and the constrained configuration is a saddle.
\end{remark}

\section{The sign rule for general pairwise interactions}
\label{sec:sign-rule}

\begin{theorem}[Sign rule for general interactions]\label{thm:sign-rule}
For $N \ge 3$ vortices on a ring with general pairwise interaction
$h(r)$, the energy along the centered spiral satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hpp-general}
  H''(0) = \sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\Bigl[
    \bigl(d_m^2 h''(d_m) + d_m h'(d_m)\bigr) S_m
    + \frac{h'(d_m)\, m^2(N-m)}{N^2\, d_m}
  \Bigr],
\end{equation}
where $d_m = 2R\sin(\pi m/N)$ and
$S_m = \sum_{k=0}^{N-m-1}[(2k+m-(N{-}1))/(2N)]^2 \ge 0$.

If\, $h'(r) < 0$ and $(rh'(r))' \le 0$ for all $r > 0$, then
$H''(0) < 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: Pairwise distance as a function of $\sigma$.}
For vortices $j$ and $k$ with separation $m = j - k > 0$, write
the centered exponent as $(j - (N{-}1)/2)/N = (j' + k')/2 + m/(2N)$
where $j' = (j-(N{-}1)/2)/N$ and $k' = (k-(N{-}1)/2)/N$.
Define the mean displacement $s = \sigma(j'+k')/2$ and half-separation
$t = \sigma m/(2N)$.  Then $z_j(\sigma) = R\,e^{s+t}\,e^{i\theta_j}$,
$z_k(\sigma) = R\,e^{s-t}\,e^{i\theta_k}$ with
$\theta_j - \theta_k = 2\pi m/N$, giving
\[
  D(\sigma) \;{:=}\; |z_j - z_k|^2
  = R^2 e^{2s}\bigl[e^{2t} + e^{-2t} - 2\cos(2\pi m/N)\bigr]
  = R^2 e^{2s}\bigl[2\cosh(2t) - 2\cos(2\pi m/N)\bigr].
\]
At $\sigma = 0$: $s = t = 0$, so
$D(0) = 2R^2[1 - \cos(2\pi m/N)] = 4R^2\sin^2(\pi m/N) = d_m^2$.

\emph{Step 2: First and second derivatives of $D$.}
Write $c_s = (j'+k')/2$ and $c_t = m/(2N)$, so
$s = \sigma c_s$, $t = \sigma c_t$.  Direct differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
  D'(0) &= 4c_s(1 - \cos\phi), \qquad \phi = 2\pi m/N, \\[3pt]
  D''(0) &= 8c_s^2(1 - \cos\phi) + 8c_t^2.
\end{align*}

\emph{Step 3: Chain rule for $h(\sqrt{D})$.}
Write $d = \sqrt{D}$.  Two applications of the chain rule give
\[
  \frac{\dd^2}{\dd\sigma^2}h(d)\bigg|_{\sigma=0}
  = \underbrace{\frac{h''(d_m)}{4d_m^2}(D')^2
  + \frac{h'(d_m)}{2d_m}\!\left[D'' - \frac{(D')^2}{2d_m^2}\right]}_{\text{evaluated at each pair $(j,k)$}}.
\]
Substituting $D' = 4c_s d_m^2/2$ (since $1-\cos\phi = d_m^2/(2R^2)$,
and absorbing $R$ into $d_m$) and collecting terms:
\[
  \frac{\dd^2}{\dd\sigma^2}h(d)\bigg|_0
  = \bigl[d_m^2 h''(d_m) + d_m h'(d_m)\bigr]c_s^2
  + \frac{h'(d_m)}{d_m}\,c_t^2\,\frac{d_m^2}{(1-\cos\phi)}.
\]

\emph{Step 4: Summation over pairs.}
For fixed separation $m$, sum over the $N-m$ pairs
$(k, k{+}m)$ for $k = 0, \ldots, N{-}m{-}1$.  Define
\[
  S_m = \sum_{k=0}^{N-m-1} c_{s,k}^2, \quad
  \text{where } c_{s,k} = \frac{2k + m - (N{-}1)}{2N}.
\]
Since $\sum_k c_{s,k} = 0$ by the centering, $S_m \ge 0$ with equality
only if all $c_{s,k} = 0$ (which requires $N - m = 1$, i.e.\ $m = N-1$).
The second sum contributes $c_t^2 \cdot (N{-}m) = m^2(N{-}m)/(4N^2)$,
and $d_m^2/(1{-}\cos\phi) = 4R^2$.  Summing over $m = 1, \ldots, N-1$
yields \eqref{eq:Hpp-general}.

\emph{Step 5: Sign determination.}
Under $h'(r) < 0$: the second term
$B_m = h'(d_m)\,m^2(N{-}m)/(N^2 d_m) < 0$
for every $m = 1, \ldots, N-1$, since $h' < 0$, $d_m > 0$,
and $m^2(N{-}m) > 0$.
Under $(rh'(r))' \le 0$: the first coefficient
$A_m = d_m^2 h''(d_m) + d_m h'(d_m) = d_m \cdot (rh')'\big|_{r=d_m} \le 0$,
so the first term $A_m S_m \le 0$ since $S_m \ge 0$.

The case $m = N{-}1$ requires separate attention.
At $m = N{-}1$ there is only one pair $(0, N{-}1)$, and the
centering sum has a single term:
$c_{s,0} = (0 + (N{-}1) - (N{-}1))/(2N) = 0$,
so $S_{N-1} = 0$.  The first term $A_{N-1} S_{N-1} = 0$ therefore
\emph{vanishes identically}, regardless of the sign of $A_{N-1}$.
However, the second term $B_{N-1} = h'(d_{N-1})\,(N{-}1)^2/(N^2 d_{N-1})$
remains strictly negative (since $h' < 0$).  Thus the $m = N{-}1$
contribution to $H''(0)$ is $B_{N-1} < 0$, and strict negativity
of $H''(0) = \sum_m (A_m S_m + B_m)$ follows from $A_m S_m \le 0$
for all $m$ and $B_m < 0$ for all $m$, with at least one term
strictly negative.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Borderline status of the logarithmic interaction]
\label{rem:borderline}
The sufficient conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-rule} partition
interactions into three classes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] $h' < 0$ and $(rh')' < 0$: both terms in
    \eqref{eq:Hpp-general} are strictly negative.
    Examples: $h = -r^\alpha$ ($\alpha > 0$), for which
    $(rh')' = -\alpha^2 r^{\alpha-1} < 0$.
  \item[(ii)] $h' < 0$ and $(rh')' = 0$: the first term vanishes
    and only the second contributes.  For smooth $h$ on $(0,\infty)$,
    this class contains a unique functional form, up to positive
    scaling and additive constant:
    $(rh')' = 0 \Rightarrow rh' = c \Rightarrow h' = c/r
    \Rightarrow h = c\ln r + b$; combined with $h' < 0$ gives
    $c < 0$, i.e.\ $h(r) = -a\ln r + b$ with $a > 0$.
  \item[(iii)] $h' < 0$ and $(rh')' > 0$ at some $r$: the sign of
    the first term is indefinite and $H''(0) < 0$ is not guaranteed.
\end{enumerate}
The 2D Green's function $h(r) = -\ln r$ falls in class~(ii)---the
boundary between guaranteed and non-guaranteed negativity.  In this

\section{The Havelock identity and its metric-independence}
\label{sec:havelock-identity}

\subsection{The classical identity}
\label{sec:classical-havelock}
\label{app:havelock}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:havelock}
For integers $N \ge 2$ and $1 \le m \le N-1$,
\[
  T_m := \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi pm/N)}{4\sin^2(\pi p/N)}
  = \frac{m(N-m)}{2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

This identity appears in \citet{Havelock1931}, \S3, as a step in
computing the stability eigenvalues of the $N$-vortex ring; see also
\citet{Aref2003}, Appendix~A.

\begin{proof}
Write $\omega = e^{2\pi i/N}$.  Using $2\sin(\pi p/N) =
|1 - \omega^p|$, expand
$[1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N)] / [4\sin^2(\pi p/N)]
= \frac12\bigl|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\omega^{pk}\bigr|^2
= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\omega^{p(j-k)}$.
Summing over $p = 1, \ldots, N-1$ and using the orthogonality relation
$\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\omega^{p\ell} = N\delta_{\ell \equiv 0\pmod{N}} - 1$:
\[
  T_m = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}
  \bigl(N\delta_{j \equiv k\pmod{N}} - 1\bigr)
  = \frac{1}{2}(Nm - m^2) = \frac{m(N-m)}{2},
\]
where $\delta_{j \equiv k}$ contributes $m$ (since $0 \le j,k \le m-1
< N$ forces $j = k$) and the $-1$ contributes $-m^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Riemannian Havelock identity}
\label{sec:riemannian}

\citet{BoattoCabral2003} computed numerically that the constrained
Lagrangian eigenvalues on the sphere satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:riemannian-havelock}
  \lambda_m = C_1(\varphi_0) - \frac{m(N-m)}{2},
\end{equation}
with $C_1$ depending on the colatitude~$\varphi_0$ but $f(m,N) = m(N{-}m)/2$
unchanged from the planar value.  The following proof makes explicit
\emph{why} $f(m,N)$ is invariant: both ingredients of its derivation
(the anti-symmetry of the sine sum forcing $H_{rt}^{(m)} = 0$, and the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ orthogonality of characters) are properties of the
discrete rotation group, not of the specific Green's function.
Numerically: $C_2 = 1.000$ at all tested colatitudes to four decimal
places; eigenvalue pair sums $\lambda_m^+ + \lambda_m^- = 2C_1$ are
constant across all modes at each colatitude.

The universality follows from the proof structure of Appendix~A:
the Havelock identity $T_m = m(N{-}m)/2$ is derived from (i)~the
algebraic factorization $|{\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\omega^{pk}}|^2$ and
(ii)~the orthogonality of $\mathbb{Z}_N$ characters, both of which
are properties of the discrete rotation group $\mathbb{Z}_N$---not
of the specific Green's function.  The Green's function enters
only through: $H_{rt}^{(m)} = 0$ (anti-symmetry of the sine sum,
requiring only that $G$ depends on geodesic distance) and
$H_{tt}^{(m)} + H_{rr}^{(m)} = \delta$, where
$\delta = -\sum_p \nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB}} h(d_p)/(N{-}1)$ is a
surface-dependent constant \emph{independent of the Fourier mode~$m$}.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Derivation of the trace formula.}
Let $\mathbf{M}_p$ be the $2{\times}2$ Hessian matrix of $h(d_{0p})$
with respect to the position of vortex~$0$, in the local
(radial, tangential) frame; its trace is
$\mathrm{tr}[\mathbf{M}_p] = \nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB}} h(d_p)$.
The standard $\mathbb{Z}_N$-Fourier block decomposition (see
\citealt{Havelock1931}, \S2) gives, for each component
$\alpha,\beta\in\{r,t\}$:
\[
  H_{\alpha\beta}^{(m)}
  = \underbrace{-\sum_{p=1}^{N-1} M_{p,\alpha\beta}}_{\text{self-interaction at }0}
  + \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} M_{p,\alpha\beta}\cos(2\pi pm/N)
  = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} M_{p,\alpha\beta}\bigl(\cos(2\pi pm/N)-1\bigr).
\]
The self-interaction term $-\sum_p M_{p,\alpha\beta}$ is the negative
sum of all off-diagonal blocks, forced by the translational zero mode
of $H$ (total gradient vanishes at the equilibrium).
Taking $\alpha = r, \beta = r$ plus $\alpha = t, \beta = t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-formula}
  H_{rr}^{(m)} + H_{tt}^{(m)}
  = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} \nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB}} h(d_p)
    \bigl(\cos(2\pi pm/N) - 1\bigr).
\end{equation}
The coefficient $(\cos(2\pi pm/N)-1)$ has the same ``$c_m = -1$''
structure for every mode and every pair: it arises entirely from
the self-interaction bookkeeping, not from any property of the
Green's function.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Mode-independence.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:trace-formula} gives
$H_{rr}^{(m)}+H_{tt}^{(m)}
= \bigl[\sum_p \nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB}} h(d_p)\cos(2\pi pm/N)\bigr]
- \bigl[\sum_p \nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB}} h(d_p)\bigr]$.
On any compact 2D Riemannian manifold, the point vortex
Hamiltonian uses the \emph{normalized} Green's function
$G$ of the Laplace--Beltrami operator (zero spatial mean:
$\int_M G(x,y)\,\dd\mu(x) = 0$), which satisfies
$\nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB},x} G(x,y) = \delta_y(x) - 1/\mathrm{Vol}(M)$.
On the plane, $G(x,y) = -(2\pi)^{-1}\ln|x{-}y|$ and the
$1/\mathrm{Vol}$ term vanishes ($\mathrm{Vol} = \infty$),
recovering the kernel of equation~\eqref{eq:thomson-H}.
On a compact surface the normalization adds a constant
$R(y) = G(x,y) + (2\pi)^{-1}\ln d(x,y)$ (the Robin function),
which is independent of the vortex \emph{configuration} and
therefore does not affect the Hessian $\nabla^2 H$ at the
$N$-gon equilibrium.  The mode-independence argument below
depends only on $\nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB}} G$ away from the
diagonal, where
$\nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB},x} G(x,y) = -1/\mathrm{Vol}(M)$
(constant, independent of $x$, $y$, or separation).
Hence $\nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB}} h(d_p) = c$ is the \emph{same
constant} for every pair~$p$ (with $c = -1/\mathrm{Vol}(M)$
on a compact surface, $c = 0$ on the plane), and
$\sum_p \nabla^2_{\mathrm{LB}} h(d_p)\cos(2\pi pm/N)
= c\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\cos(2\pi pm/N) = -c$,
which is independent of~$m$.  Therefore $H_{rr}^{(m)}+H_{tt}^{(m)}
= -c - (N{-}1)c = -Nc \equiv \delta$ is mode-independent.
On the plane ($\mathrm{Vol} = \infty$), $c = 0$, so $\delta = 0$
(exact tracelessness); on a compact surface $c = -1/\mathrm{Vol}(M)$,
giving $\delta = N/\mathrm{Vol}(M)$, which shifts $C_1$
without affecting $f(m,N)$.  On the sphere of radius $R$:
$\mathrm{Vol} = 4\pi R^2$, so $\delta = N/(4\pi R^2)$,
consistent with the colatitude-dependent $C_1$ of
Section~\ref{sec:curved}.

The stability threshold $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is therefore determined by
$C_1(\text{surface}) \ge \max_m m(N{-}m)/2$: a condition involving the
\emph{universal} function $f$ and a single surface-dependent constant.
On the plane: $C_1 = N{-}1$, giving $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$ (the
largest integer with $(N{-}1) \ge \lfloor N/2\rfloor(N{-}\lfloor
N/2\rfloor)/2$).  On the sphere:
$C_1(\varphi_0) < N{-}1$ for $\varphi_0 > 0$, giving the
colatitude-dependent threshold of Section~\ref{sec:curved}.

The value $C_1 = N{-}1$ on the plane has a geometric origin: for
$h' = -1/d$ (the logarithmic force law), each vortex pair $(0,k)$
contributes the \emph{same} amount $-1/(2R)$ to the gradient
$\partial H/\partial x_0$, regardless of the separation~$k$.  This
is because $h'(d_k)(x_0{-}x_k)/d_k = -(1{-}\cos(2\pi k/N))/
(4R\sin^2(\pi k/N)) = -1/(2R)$: the geometric factor
$(1{-}\cos)$ cancels against the denominator $\sin^2 = (1{-}\cos)/2$.
The cancellation is the \emph{scale invariance} of $h' = -1/d$:
the force law $h' \propto 1/d$ precisely compensates the
$d$-dependence of the chord geometry, making each pair's contribution
independent of the separation.  This property characterizes the
logarithm \emph{uniquely}: among radially symmetric pairwise
interactions on $\mathbb{R}^2$, $h = -\ln|z{-}w|$ is the unique
function satisfying $h(d\lambda) = h(d) + h(\lambda)$ (additivity
under rescaling---Cauchy's functional equation with continuity).
This additivity under the dilation subgroup $z \mapsto \lambda z$
(for which $|f(z){-}f(w)| = |\lambda|\,|z{-}w|$) is Cauchy's
multiplicative functional equation $h(ab) = h(a) + h(b)$,
whose unique continuous solution is $h = c\ln$.

\section{Stability thresholds on constant-curvature surfaces}
\label{sec:curvature-thresholds}
\label{sec:curvature-invariant}

\noindent\emph{Prior work and what is new.}
\citet{BoattoCabral2003} computed the constrained Lagrangian eigenvalues
for a latitudinal ring of point vortices on the non-rotating sphere at
arbitrary colatitude, establishing that the stability boundary shifts
with latitude.  \citet{LimMontaldiRoberts2001} studied relative
equilibria of vortex rings on $S^2$ within the Hamiltonian framework.
The spherical Havelock eigenvalues are therefore not novel.

What the proof in~\S\ref{sec:riemannian} adds is an explicit algebraic
argument that the $m$-dependent part $f(m,N) = m(N{-}m)/2$ is
\emph{metric-independent}---a consequence of $\mathbb{Z}_N$
representation theory alone---while only the additive constant $C_1$
carries the geometric information.  A specialist who worked through the
Boatto--Cabral calculation would likely have noticed this; the value
here is in making the separation explicit and, more substantively, in
extending the framework to the hyperbolic plane~(\S\ref{sec:hyperbolic}),
where $N_{\mathrm{crit}} > 7$ and systematic stability tables do not
appear to be in the existing literature.

\subsection{Curved 2D surfaces: the spherical stability boundary}
\label{sec:curved}

On the 2-sphere, the point vortex interaction
$h(\theta) = -\ln(2\sin(\theta/2))$ satisfies $h' < 0$ and
$(\sin\theta\cdot h')' = \tfrac12\sin\theta > 0$ (class~(iii)).
While $H''(0) > 0$ along the spiral at all tested colatitudes
(the polygon is a spiral-direction energy minimum even without
the Lagrange shift), the \emph{constrained} Hessian tells a
different story.

The angular impulse on the sphere is
$L = \sum\cos\theta_k$, with Hessian
$\partial^2 L/\partial\theta_k^2 = -\cos\varphi_0$ at colatitude
$\varphi_0$.  The Lagrangian shift from $\mu_L$ is therefore
proportional to $\cos\varphi_0$: full near the pole ($\varphi_0 \to 0$),
vanishing at the equator ($\varphi_0 = 90^\circ$).  This
latitude-dependent shift produces a \emph{colatitude-dependent
stability boundary}:

\toprule
$N$ & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 & $\ge 8$ \\
\midrule
$\varphi_{\mathrm{crit}}$ & $60.0^\circ$ & $48.2^\circ$ &
  $41.4^\circ$ & $25.8^\circ$ & $0^\circ$ & $0^\circ$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The $N$-gon is a constrained energy minimum on the sphere if and only
if $\varphi_0 < \varphi_{\mathrm{crit}}(N)$.  The dimensionless
eigenvalue $\bar\lambda = \sin^2\!\varphi_0 \cdot \lambda$ recovers
the Havelock values to within $1\%$ at $\varphi_0 = 3^\circ$
(N=5: $\bar\lambda = 0.99$, Havelock $= 1.0$;
N=6: $0.49$ vs $0.5$; N=8: $-1.01$ vs $-1.0$).

Saturn's hexagon at $76^\circ$\,N ($\varphi_0 = 14^\circ$) lies
inside $\varphi_{\mathrm{crit}}(6) = 26.6^\circ$: the hexagonal
ring is in the stable zone.  If the hexagon were at $60^\circ$\,N
($\varphi_0 = 30^\circ > 26.6^\circ$), it would cross the boundary
and the pattern would not persist.  Jupiter's octagon ($N = 8$) is
unstable at all colatitudes, consistent with the need for a
central vortex.

\paragraph{Derivation of $C_1(\mathbf{S}^2)$ and exact thresholds.}
For $N$ vortices on a latitude circle at colatitude $\varphi_0$, the
great-circle separation satisfies
$2\sin(\gamma_{jk}/2) = 2\sin\varphi_0\,|\sin(\Delta\phi_{jk}/2)|$,
so the spherical pair interaction decomposes as
\[
  -\ln\bigl(2\sin(\gamma_{jk}/2)\bigr)
  = -\ln\bigl(2|\sin(\Delta\phi_{jk}/2)|\bigr) - \ln(\sin\varphi_0).
\]
The first term is the flat Thomson energy in the azimuthal variable
$\phi$; its Hessian along azimuthal Fourier modes gives the Havelock
eigenvalues $(N{-}1)/2 - m(N{-}m)/2$ exactly as in the flat case.
The second term $-\binom{N}{2}\ln(\sin\varphi_0)$ is purely
radial and enters through the Lagrange multiplier.

The angular impulse on $\mathbf{S}^2$ is $L = \sum\cos\varphi_k$.
At the ring, $\partial^2 L/\partial\varphi_k^2 = -\cos\varphi_0$,
so the Lagrange shift
$\mu_L\cdot\partial^2 L/\partial\varphi_k^2$ is proportional to
$\cos\varphi_0$: maximal near the pole, vanishing at the equator.
Computing $\mu_L$ from the stationarity condition and combining with
the radial Hessian of the pair interaction yields the curvature
coefficient (cf.\ \citealt{BoattoCabral2003}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C1_S2}
  C_1(\mathbf{S}^2,\,\xi) = \frac{(N-1)(1-\xi)}{1+\xi} = (N-1)\cos\varphi_0,
\end{equation}
where $\xi = \tan^2(\varphi_0/2)$ is the stereographic parameter and
$\cos\varphi_0 = (1{-}\xi)/(1{+}\xi)$.
Marginal stability $C_1 = m(N{-}m)/2$ therefore reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cos_crit}
  \cos\varphi_{\mathrm{crit}} = \frac{m(N{-}m)}{2(N-1)}, \qquad m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor,
\end{equation}
which is \emph{always rational}.  The threshold in $\xi$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi_crit_S2}
  \xi_{\mathrm{crit}}(N) = \frac{(N-1) - m(N{-}m)/2}{(N-1) + m(N{-}m)/2}
  \in \mathbb{Q}.
\end{equation}
Explicit values:

4 & $2$ & $2/3$ & $1/5$ & $48.2^\circ$ & $\varphi_0 < 48.2^\circ$ \\

\begin{remark}[Pattern $\xi_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(2N{-}3)$ and its break at $N=6$]

\subsection{The hyperbolic plane and Gaussian curvature}

The threshold $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ on any surface is the largest $N$

\begin{remark}[Order of the curvature correction]
\label{rem:euler}
The curvature enters $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ at $O(K)$, not $O(K^2)$:
it acts through the surface Green's function via the Havelock
coefficient $C_1(\text{surface},\xi)$
(equations~\eqref{eq:C1_S2},~\eqref{eq:C1_H2}), which is linear
in $K$ at leading order.  On a compact surface the Gauss--Bonnet
constraint $\int K\,\mathrm{d}A = 2\pi\chi$ enters only through
$C_1$; no additional $O(K^2)$ self-energy correction arises for
point vortices (such a correction would require finite-area patches,
where it is $O(\varepsilon^2)$ and topology-independent).
\end{remark}

  \label{eq:C1_H2}

where $r_E$ is the Euclidean ring radius in the Poincar\'e disk and

one has $C_1(\mathbf{H}^2) > N{-}1$ for all $R/a > 0$, confirming the

$N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ increases through integer jumps as $R/a$ grows,
with the $7 \to 8$ transition at $R/a \approx 0.52$ (more precisely,
the $m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$ eigenvalue of the $N = 8$ ring crosses

We \emph{conjecture} that $N_{\mathrm{crit}}(R/a)$ is non-decreasing
(verified for $R/a \le 2$; a plausible mechanism is the

\paragraph{Near-$K=0$ behavior.}
Expanding~\eqref{eq:C1_H2} for $N=7$, $m=3$ (see
Appendix~\ref{app:c1_h2} for the algebraic simplification), the
$m=3$ eigenvalue satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_3(N=7,\,\xi)
    = \frac{12\xi}{r_E^2(1-\xi)^2}
    = \frac{12|K|}{(1-\xi)^2}
    = 12|K| + O(K^2 r_E^2) > 0 .
  \label{eq:lam3_H2}
\end{equation}
Negative curvature stabilizes the $N=7$ ring at \emph{first} order in
$|K|$: $N_\mathrm{crit}(K)$ stays at $7$ for all $K \le 0$ in the
sense that the formerly-neutral mode becomes strictly stable.

The $7\to 8$ transition is governed by the $N=8$ ring reaching marginal
stability.  Setting $C_1(\mathbf{H}^2,\xi) = m(N{-}m)/2\big|_{m=4} = 8$
gives $7(1+\xi^2)/(1-\xi)^2 = 8$, which simplifies to
\begin{equation}
  \xi^2 - 16\xi + 1 = 0
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \xi^* = 8 - 3\sqrt{7} \approx 0.0627 ,
  \label{eq:xi_star}
\end{equation}
exact.  In terms of geodesic ring radius $\rho$, this corresponds to
$\rho/a \approx 0.52$ (or $|K|\rho^2 \approx 0.27$).
The coefficient
\begin{equation}
  \gamma \;\equiv\; \frac{1}{(\xi^*)^2} = 127 + 48\sqrt{7} \approx 254
  \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
carries an algebraic interpretation.
The ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})$ has fundamental unit
$\varepsilon = 8 + 3\sqrt{7}$, since $(8+3\sqrt{7})(8-3\sqrt{7}) = 64-63 = 1$.
Therefore $\xi^* = 8-3\sqrt{7} = \varepsilon^{-1}$ is the
\emph{inverse of the fundamental unit}---the unique unit below~$1$
in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{7}]$---and $\gamma = (\xi^*)^{-2} = \varepsilon^2$.
The verification identity $(8-3\sqrt{7})^2(127+48\sqrt{7})=1$ is not a
computational coincidence: it is the norm-$1$ property
$\varepsilon^{-2}\cdot\varepsilon^2=1$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{7}]$.

The field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})$ arises because $N_{\mathrm{crit}}=7$
enters the marginal condition; this is a mathematical consequence, not
an independent observation.  The substantive content is the
\emph{palindromic structure}: the threshold polynomial has the form
$A\,\xi^2 + B\,\xi + A = 0$ (same leading and trailing coefficient),
which forces $\xi_1\,\xi_2 = A/A = 1$---the two roots are
multiplicative inverses, so $\xi^*$ is automatically an algebraic
\emph{unit} (an element of norm~$1$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{7}]$).
This palindromic property is not generic: it reflects the inversion
symmetry $\xi\leftrightarrow 1/\xi$ of the Poincar\'e disk model,
which in turn is the restriction to the real axis of the conformal
automorphism $z \mapsto a^2/\bar z$ of the disk.  The stability
threshold is a unit \emph{because} the hyperbolic energy is
conformally covariant (Proposition~\ref{prop:mobius}): the palindromic
polynomial encodes the same M\"obius symmetry at the level of
algebraic number theory.

\section{Algebraic structure of the stability boundary}
\label{sec:algebraic-structure}

The $7\to 8$ transition is not an isolated result: the $N \to (N{+}1)$
transition threshold on $\mathbf{H}^2$ is always the smaller positive root
of the palindromic quadratic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:palindromic}
  A\,\xi^2 + B\,\xi + A = 0,
  \quad
  A = (N-1) - \tfrac{m(N{-}m)}{2},
  \quad
  B = m(N{-}m),
  \quad
  m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Since $A < 0$ for $N \ge 8$ and $B > 0$, the smaller positive root is
$\xi^* = (B/2 - \sqrt{(B/2)^2 - A^2})\,/\,|A|$, and its field extension
is determined by the squarefree part of the discriminant
$(B/2)^2 - A^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cclcc}
\toprule
$N$ & $\xi^*$ (approx.) & Field & $D$ & $\xi^*$ (exact form) \\
\midrule
7  & 0 & $\mathbb{Q}$ & 1 & $0$ (marginal) \\
8  & 0.0627 & $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})$ & 7 & $8 - 3\sqrt{7}$ \\
9  & 0.1010 & $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{6})$ & 6 & $(5 - \sqrt{6})/\sqrt{6}\cdot\ldots$ \\
10 & 0.1429 & $\mathbb{Q}$ & 1 & $1/7$ \\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The discriminant's squarefree part $D$ follows the pattern
\begin{equation}\label{eq:field-pattern}
  D = \begin{cases}
    \mathrm{sq\_free}(N-1) & N \text{ even}, \\
    \mathrm{sq\_free}(N-3) & N \text{ odd},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
for all $N \ge 8$.  The deeper content is that the palindromic
structure encodes the conformal inversion
$\xi \leftrightarrow 1/\xi$ of the Poincar\'e disk (as noted above);
the field pattern itself follows from elementary factorization.

\begin{proof}[Proof of~\eqref{eq:field-pattern}]
The palindromic quadratic $A\xi^2 + B\xi + A = 0$ has discriminant
$\Delta = B^2 - 4A^2$.  Using $A = (N{-}1) - m(N{-}m)/2$ and
$B = m(N{-}m)$:
\[
  \Delta = m^2(N{-}m)^2 - 4\bigl[(N{-}1) - m(N{-}m)/2\bigr]^2
  = 4(N{-}1)\bigl[m(N{-}m) - (N{-}1)\bigr].
\]

\emph{Even $N$ ($m = N/2$):}
$m(N{-}m) = N^2/4$, so
$m(N{-}m) - (N{-}1) = N^2/4 - N + 1 = (N{-}2)^2/4$.
Therefore
$\Delta = 4(N{-}1)(N{-}2)^2/4 = (N{-}1)(N{-}2)^2$.
The squarefree part of $\Delta$ equals
$\mathrm{sq\_free}(N{-}1)$, since $(N{-}2)^2$ is a perfect square.
The threshold $\xi^*$ involves $\sqrt\Delta$, so
$\xi^* \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\mathrm{sq\_free}(N{-}1)})$.

\emph{Odd $N$ ($m = (N{-}1)/2$):}
$m(N{-}m) = (N{-}1)(N{+}1)/4 = (N^2{-}1)/4$, so
$m(N{-}m) - (N{-}1) = (N^2{-}1)/4 - (N{-}1) = (N{-}1)(N{-}3)/4$.
Therefore
$\Delta = 4(N{-}1) \cdot (N{-}1)(N{-}3)/4 = (N{-}1)^2(N{-}3)$.
The squarefree part of $\Delta$ equals
$\mathrm{sq\_free}(N{-}3)$, since $(N{-}1)^2$ is a perfect square.
Hence $\xi^* \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\mathrm{sq\_free}(N{-}3)})$.
\end{proof}

The $N = 10$ case is the unique rational threshold:
$N-1 = 9 = 3^2$ is a perfect square, so $D = 1$ and
$\xi^*(10) = 1/7 \in \mathbb{Q}$, the same squarefree-discriminant mechanism
that makes $\xi^*(8) = 8-3\sqrt{7} \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})$ an inverse
fundamental unit.  Every even transition lives in at most a degree-2
extension of $\mathbb{Q}$; the tower terminates at $\mathbb{Q}$ itself
whenever $N - 1$ is a perfect square.

Code: \texttt{algebraic\_thresholds.h2\_stability\_threshold(N)},
\texttt{h2\_threshold\_polynomial(N)}, and \texttt{h2\_threshold\_table()}
implement these computations; \texttt{test\_algebraic\_thresholds.py} verifies
the field-pattern table for $N = 8, \ldots, 16$ and the $C_1$-condition
$C_1(\mathbf{H}^2,\xi^*) = m(N{-}m)/2$ for each $N$ to $10^{-8}$ relative
error.

The coefficient $\gamma$ gives the curvature scale at which the polygon threshold is reached:
the $7\to 8$ jump occurs at $|K|\,r_E^2 = \xi^* = \gamma^{-1/2}$.
There is no perturbative formula $N_\mathrm{crit} \approx 7 + c\,|K|\,r_E^2$;
the threshold~\eqref{eq:xi_star} is a \emph{finite}, non-perturbative
condition, and $N_\mathrm{crit}$ is an integer that jumps discontinuously.

For any fixed ratio $R/a$ of ring radius to curvature radius, the
Gaussian curvature $K$ controls the threshold: $K > 0$ reduces
$N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ below~$7$, $K < 0$ raises it above~$7$, and
$K = 0$ gives exactly~$7$.  The number~$7$ is the stability threshold
at zero Gaussian curvature \emph{at the scale of the polygon ring}.
$K=0$ is the \emph{least stable point}: the $N=7$ ring is marginal
at $K=0$ and strictly stable for any $K<0$.  The marginality is an
artifact of $K=0$ exactly; $N_{\mathrm{crit}}=7$ is the value for the
dilation-invariant logarithmic interaction (Remark~\ref{rem:rg-fixed-point}),
and any nonzero negative curvature eliminates the marginal mode at first order
in~$|K|$ via~\eqref{eq:lam3_H2}.

\paragraph{Physical realization.}
The hyperbolic plane is no longer a purely mathematical object.
\citet{Kollar2019} demonstrated that microwave resonators arranged in
a tiling of the Poincar\'e disk implement the hyperbolic lattice
Hamiltonian in circuit quantum electrodynamics, with negative
effective curvature controlled by the lattice geometry.
\citet{Lenggenhager2022} subsequently implemented hyperbolic space
on a classical circuit board, showing that band structure and
topological properties native to $\mathbf{H}^2$ are experimentally
accessible.  These platforms realize, in a controlled laboratory
setting, the geometry that Section~\ref{sec:hyperbolic} analyzes.

The stability prediction translates directly into an experimental
observable: \emph{a ring of $N$ vortex-like excitations on a
hyperbolic lattice with ring-to-curvature ratio $R/a \approx 1$
should be linearly stable up to $N = 12$, without requiring any
central defect}.  In flat geometry, a 12-vortex ring is linearly
unstable at every curvature-free configuration and can only be
stabilized by a sufficiently strong central vortex
(the Thomson--Havelock criterion requires a central circulation
$\kappa_0 / \kappa \ge |\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(12)|$, which is large).
The hyperbolic curvature replaces the stabilizing role of the
central vortex: the geometry itself provides the restoring force.
A predicted $N = 12$ stable ring with no center is impossible in
flat geometry and would be a direct signature of hyperbolic curvature
acting on vortex dynamics.

What would need to be realized is the hyperbolic-plane Green's
function for vortex interactions, $h(r_{\mathbf{H}^2})$, where
$r_{\mathbf{H}^2}$ is the geodesic distance~\citep{Kimura1999}.
In a 2D superfluid hosted on a hyperbolic lattice---for example,
a Bose--Hubbard model implemented in circuit QED---the vortex
interaction would inherit the hyperbolic geometry.  Equivalently,
topological defects in a photonic condensate on a Poincar\'e-disk
resonator array would interact via the hyperbolic Hamiltonian.
Neither realization exists yet.  The prediction $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 12$
at $R/a = 1$ (and $N_{\mathrm{crit}} \ge 14$ at $R/a = 2$) is
a falsifiable quantitative target for such an experiment.

Three conditions are required for the hyperbolic Hamiltonian
$h = -\ln\sinh(d_{\mathbf{H}^2}/2a)$ to emerge as the effective vortex
interaction.  First, the platform must support a 2D superfluid order
parameter $e^{i\theta}$ on the hyperbolic lattice, so that vortices
appear as topological defects with winding number $\pm 1$ in $\theta$.
Second, in the long-wavelength limit the phase field must satisfy the
hyperbolic Laplace--Beltrami equation $\Delta_g\theta = 0$, whose
Green's function on $\mathbf{H}^2$ is precisely $h$~\citep{Kimura1999};
this follows automatically once the lattice dispersion relation is
hyperbolic.  Third, the curvature radius $a$ and ring radius $R$ must
both be independently controllable: $a$ is set by the lattice geometry
(fixed at fabrication, as demonstrated by~\citealt{Kollar2019}),
while $R$ can be tuned by pinning vortex excitations to engineered
impurities or to the boundary of the resonator array.  In a
Bose--Hubbard model on a Poincar\'e-disk resonator array these three
conditions are simultaneously achievable: the superfluid phase
satisfies the first, the hyperbolic dispersion relation the second,
and current fabrication control over impurity placement is sufficient
for the third.  A realized system of this type would allow direct
measurement of the $N_{\mathrm{crit}}(R/a)$ curve---a signature
inaccessible in flat geometry---by counting the largest stable
vortex ring as $R/a$ is varied.

\section{Point vortex dynamics on a two-dimensional surface}
\label{sec:pvd}

On a two-dimensional surface, the stream function $\psi$ satisfies the
quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity (QGPV) equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qgpv}
  \frac{Dq}{Dt} = 0, \qquad q = \nabla^2\psi + \beta y.
\end{equation}
The Green's function of the 2D Laplacian is
$G(r) = -\ln r / (2\pi)$,
satisfying $G'(r) = -1/(2\pi r) < 0$ for all $r > 0$.

For $N$ point vortices with equal circulations $\kappa$ on the
collision-free configuration space $\{|z_j - z_k| > 0 \text{ for all }
j \ne k\}$, where $H$ is smooth and $\mathbb{Z}_N$-invariant, the
interaction energy is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thomson-H}
  H = -\sum_{j < k} \ln|z_j - z_k|,
\end{equation}
where we absorb $\kappa^2/(4\pi)$ into the normalization.
By the equivariant critical point theorem, the regular $N$-gon---a
fixed point of $\mathbb{Z}_N$---is a critical point of $H$.

Point vortex dynamics conserves three quantities: the energy $H$,
the angular impulse $L = \sum|z_k|^2$, and the linear impulse
$\mathbf{P} = \sum z_k$.  The physically accessible configuration
space is the $(2N-3)$-dimensional surface
$\{L = NR^2,\, \mathbf{P} = 0\}$.

\subsection{The centered spiral deformation}
\label{sec:results}

Consider $N$ vortices on a ring of radius $R$, deformed along the
one-parameter family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deformation}
  z_k(\sigma) = R\,e^{\sigma(k - (N-1)/2)/N}\,e^{2\pi ik/N},
  \qquad k = 0, \ldots, N-1.
\end{equation}
At $\sigma = 0$: the regular $N$-gon.  The centering ensures
$H(\sigma) = H(-\sigma)$ by the symmetry $k \mapsto N-1-k$
(which sends $\sigma \to -\sigma$ while preserving $H$), so
$H'(0) = 0$.  The spiral deformation has a precise M\"obius-group
origin: the positions $\{z_k(\sigma)\}$ are the orbit of the seed
$z_0 = R$ under the cyclic group generated by $z \mapsto \lambda z$,
$\lambda = e^{\sigma/N + 2\pi i/N}$.  At $\sigma = 0$ this generator
is purely elliptic (rotation by $2\pi/N$, recovering the regular
$N$-gon); for $\sigma \ne 0$ it is loxodromic ($|\lambda| \ne 1$) and
the orbit traces the logarithmic spiral $r = R\,e^{(\sigma/2\pi)\theta}$
at angles $\theta = 2\pi k/N$.  The spiral deformation is therefore the
generator of the loxodromic one-parameter subgroup through the $N$-gon
in the M\"obius group's action on configuration space.
$\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariant perturbations of the $N$-gon form a vector
space; the spiral direction is singled out within this space as the
unique direction tangent to a M\"obius orbit (generated by a single
transformation $z \mapsto \lambda z$ acting on a seed point and
producing all $N$ positions via the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbit).  Other
perturbation directions (individual Fourier modes, radial displacements)
do not arise from a single M\"obius seed and are analyzed through the
full constrained Hessian in Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}.
The M\"obius interpretation does not enter the stability proofs:
the stability analysis uses the spiral only as a specific perturbation
direction (the unique $\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariant direction that leaves
the constraint surface at $O(\sigma^2)$, so that the unconstrained
$H''(0) < 0$ does not contradict the constrained energy minimum).
However, Proposition~\ref{prop:mobius} does carry genuine mathematical
content beyond geometric decoration.  Restricted to the dilation
subgroup $f(z)=bz$, where $f'$ is the constant~$b$, the single-site
correction becomes position-independent and reduces to
$H(bz_1,\dots,bz_N) = H(z_1,\dots,z_N) - \tfrac{N(N-1)}{2}\ln b$.
This is the $N$-body statement that $H$ is dilation-invariant---the
logarithmic energy changes only by an additive constant under spatial
rescaling (Corollary~\ref{cor:dilation-rg} above).

\section{The constrained energy landscape}
\label{sec:energy-landscape}

\subsection{Energy curvature along the spiral}

\begin{theorem}[Energy curvature at the polygon]\label{thm:energy-curv}
For $N \ge 3$ vortices on a ring with $h(r) = -\ln r$, the energy
along the centered spiral deformation satisfies $H'(0) = 0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hpp}
  H''(0) = -\frac{1}{4N^2}\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}
    \frac{(N-m)\,m^2}{\sin^2(\pi m/N)} < 0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part~(a): $H'(0) = 0$ by the centering symmetry $k \mapsto N-1-k$.
Part~(b): Define $F(u, m) = \ln[1 - 2\cos(2\pi m/N)\,e^{um} + e^{2um}]$
with $u = \sigma/N$.  Direct differentiation gives
$F''(0, m) = m^2/[2\sin^2(\pi m/N)]$.
There are $(N-m)$ pairs with separation $m$, and the chain rule
$\dd/\dd\sigma = (1/N)\,\dd/\dd u$ contributes $1/N^2$.
This derivation uses the specific structure of $h = -\ln r$:
the general formula \eqref{eq:Hpp-general} has two terms, but for the
logarithmic interaction $(rh')' = (-1)' = 0$, so the $S_m$ terms
(first term in \eqref{eq:Hpp-general}) vanish identically.  Only the
second term survives, giving \eqref{eq:Hpp} as a special case of
\eqref{eq:Hpp-general} with $A_m = 0$ and
$B_m = h'(d_m)\,m^2(N{-}m)/(N^2 d_m) = -m^2(N{-}m)/(N^2 d_m^2)$.
Substituting $d_m = 2\sin(\pi m/N)$ yields \eqref{eq:Hpp}.
Every factor in the sum is strictly positive, so $H''(0) < 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The constrained energy minimum}

\noindent\emph{Attribution note.}
The Havelock eigenvalue formula $\lambda_m = (N{-}1) - m(N{-}m)/2$
and the linear stability boundary $N \le 7$ are due to
\citet{Havelock1931}.  The new contribution of the theorem below is
the reframing: the $N$-gon is a \emph{constrained energy minimum}
(not a saddle point or maximum), which resolves the apparent tension
with Onsager's statistical-mechanics picture.

\begin{theorem}[Constrained energy minimum]\label{thm:constrained-min}
The regular $N$-gon minimizes $H$ on the conserved-quantity surface
$\{L = NR^2,\, \mathbf{P} = 0\}$ if and only if $N \le 7$.
The Lagrangian Hessian
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagr-hess}
  \nabla^2\!\mathcal{L} = \nabla^2 H - 2\mu_L\, I,
  \qquad \mu_L = -\frac{N-1}{4},
\end{equation}
restricted to the constraint tangent space, is positive semi-definite
for $N \le 7$ and indefinite for $N \ge 8$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The energy-Casimir functional is $\mathcal{H} = H + |\mu_L|\, L$,
whose Hessian is \eqref{eq:lagr-hess}.  The Hessian $\nabla^2 H$ is
a $2N \times 2N$ real symmetric matrix that commutes with the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ action $z_k \mapsto z_{k+1\,(\mathrm{mod}\, N)}$
(since $H$ is invariant under cyclic permutation of equal-circulation
vortices).  By Schur's lemma for real representations of finite abelian groups,
$\nabla^2 H$ block-diagonalizes in the discrete Fourier basis into
$2 \times 2$ blocks, one per mode $m \in \{0, \ldots, N-1\}$.
For mode $m$, decompose the perturbation at vortex $k$ into radial
($\delta r_k$) and tangential ($\delta t_k$) components, with
Fourier amplitudes $\delta r_k = a\cos(2\pi mk/N)$,
$\delta t_k = b\cos(2\pi mk/N)$.  The $2\times 2$ Hessian block
for the pair $(a, b)$ involves two sums over separations
$p = 1, \ldots, N-1$:
\begin{align*}
  H_{rr}^{(m)} &= \textstyle\sum_p
    \bigl[\tfrac{1-\cos(2\pi pm/N)}{4\sin^2(\pi p/N)}
    + \tfrac{1+\cos(2\pi pm/N)}{4\sin^2(\pi p/N)}\bigr]
    \;-\; 2\textstyle\sum_p
    \tfrac{1-\cos(2\pi pm/N)}{4\sin^2(\pi p/N)}, \\[3pt]
  H_{rt}^{(m)} &= \textstyle\sum_p
    \tfrac{\sin(2\pi pm/N)}{4\sin^2(\pi p/N)}, \\[3pt]
  H_{tt}^{(m)} &= -H_{rr}^{(m)}.
\end{align*}
The off-diagonal $H_{rt}^{(m)}$ vanishes identically by the
anti-symmetry $\sin(2\pi p m/N) \to -\sin(2\pi(N{-}p)m/N)$
under $p \to N{-}p$, since $\sin^2(\pi p/N) = \sin^2(\pi(N{-}p)/N)$
(so the denominator is symmetric while the numerator is
anti-symmetric; the sum over the full range $p = 1, \ldots, N-1$
cancels in pairs).  Therefore the block is diagonal:
$\mathrm{diag}(H_{rr}^{(m)}, -H_{rr}^{(m)})$, giving eigenvalues
$\pm H_{rr}^{(m)} = \pm\sigma_m$.

The diagonal entry $H_{rr}^{(m)}$ simplifies using the Havelock
identity (Appendix~\ref{app:havelock}):
the self-sum $\sum_p 1/[4\sin^2(\pi p/N)] = (N{-}1)/2$
(a special case of $T_0 + (N{-}1)/2$) and the cross-sum is
$T_m = m(N{-}m)/2$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-m}
  \sigma_m = H_{rr}^{(m)} = \frac{N-1}{2} - \frac{m(N-m)}{2},
\end{equation}
using
\begin{equation}\label{eq:havelock}
  T_m := \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi pm/N)}{4\sin^2(\pi p/N)}
  = \frac{m(N-m)}{2}
\end{equation}
(proven in Appendix~\ref{app:havelock}; cf.\
\citet{Havelock1931}, \S3).  The Lagrangian eigenvalues are thus
\[
  \lambda_m^+ = (N-1) - \tfrac{m(N-m)}{2}, \qquad
  \lambda_m^- = \tfrac{m(N-m)}{2}.
\]
Since $\lambda_m^- \ge 0$ always, positive semi-definiteness requires
$\lambda_m^+ \ge 0$ for the modes tangent to the constraint surface.
Three modes are removed:
\emph{Mode $m = 0$} corresponds to uniform dilation
$\delta z_k \propto z_k$, which changes $L = \sum|z_k|^2$ and is
therefore not tangent to $\{L = NR^2\}$.
\emph{Modes $m = 1$ and $m = N-1$} generate infinitesimal translation
($\delta z_k = \mathrm{const}$, changing $\mathbf{P} = \sum z_k$) and
overall rotation ($\delta z_k = iz_k$, a Goldstone mode of the $SO(2)$
symmetry of $H$).  Both have $\sigma_m = 0$, consistent with the
continuous symmetry group; they lie in the kernel of $\nabla^2 H$ and
produce the zero eigenvalues.
The remaining modes $m = 2, \ldots, N-2$ are tangent to the constraint
surface and carry the stability information.  The minimum of
$\lambda_m^+$ over $m$ occurs at $m = \lfloor N/2 \rfloor$:
\[
  \min_m \lambda_m^+ = \begin{cases}
    (N{-}1)(7{-}N)/8 & N \text{ odd}, \\
    (N{-}1) - N^2/8 & N \text{ even}.
  \end{cases}
\]
Both expressions are $> 0$ for $N \le 6$, equal to zero at $N = 7$,
and $< 0$ for $N \ge 8$.  Thus $N \le 6$ is \emph{strictly} stable
(positive definite Lagrangian), $N = 7$ is \emph{neutrally} stable
(positive semi-definite, with a zero eigenvalue at $m = 3$),
consistent with \citet{Havelock1931}, and $N \ge 8$ is unstable.
(The $N = 7$ marginal case is further analyzed in
Section~\ref{sec:n7}.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Constrained minimum versus unconstrained saddle]
\label{cor:min-not-max}
For $N \le 7$, the $N$-gon is a constrained energy \emph{minimum}
on $\{L = NR^2,\, \mathbf{P} = 0\}$, yet a saddle point of the
unconstrained energy $H$.  These two facts are compatible: the
constrained minimum is the relevant concept for Lyapunov stability
via Arnold's energy-Casimir method, while the Onsager
negative-temperature argument operates on the unconstrained energy
landscape, where the polygon is a maximum-energy \emph{accessible}
state (not a local maximum of $H$, but the highest-$H$ configuration
reachable within the Thomson-stable set).  The distinction matters
because the statistical mechanics literature sometimes describes the
polygon as an energy maximum without qualification; it is a
constrained minimum that happens to have higher energy than
alternative configurations at the same conserved quantities.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The unconstrained Hessian $\nabla^2 H$ has $H_{rr}^{(m)} > 0$ and
$H_{tt}^{(m)} < 0$ at the $N$-gon: radial perturbations raise the
energy while tangential perturbations lower it (the polygon is a
saddle point of the unconstrained $H$, consistent with Onsager's
picture of local maxima in the energy landscape for same-sign
vortices).  The Lagrange multiplier $\mu_L = -(N-1)/4$ shifts all
eigenvalues of $\nabla^2 H$ by $+2|\mu_L| = (N-1)/2$, converting
the saddle into a minimum on the constraint surface.  The value of
$\mu_L$ follows from the constrained stationarity condition: at
vortex~$0$ (position $(R,0)$), the pair $(0,k)$ contributes
identically $-1/(2R)$ to $\partial H/\partial x_0$ regardless of
$k$, because $(1-\cos\theta_k)/[4\sin^2(\theta_k/2)] = 1/2$ for
all $k$---a consequence of the scale invariance of $h'(r)/r =
-1/r^2$.  Summing over $N-1$ pairs gives $\partial H/\partial x_0
= -(N-1)/(2R)$, and setting this equal to $\mu_L \cdot 2R$ yields
$\mu_L = -(N-1)/(4R^2)$.

In particular, Theorem~\ref{thm:energy-curv} ($H''(0) < 0$ along
the spiral direction) does not contradict Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}:
the spiral deformation~\eqref{eq:deformation} leaves the conserved
surface at $O(\sigma^2)$, so it is not a constrained perturbation.
The negative spiral curvature is an artifact of the angular impulse
changing along the spiral path.
\end{proof}

\section{Central vortex stabilization}
\label{sec:central-vortex}

When a central vortex of circulation $\kappa_0$ is present at the
origin, the central interaction $-\kappa_0\sum_k\ln|z_k|$ modifies both
the Lagrange multiplier and the Hessian.  Two effects contribute to the
critical eigenvalue:

\emph{Lagrange multiplier shift.}  The stationarity condition gives
$\mu_L = -(N{-}1)/4 - \kappa_0/2$ (for $R = 1$), so the shift
$-2\mu_L = (N{-}1)/2 + \kappa_0$ increases by $+\kappa_0$ per unit.

\emph{Hessian projection.}  The central-vortex Hessian at vortex~$k$
is the traceless $2 \times 2$ matrix
$(\kappa_0/R^2)\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}\cos 2\theta_k & \sin 2\theta_k
\\ \sin 2\theta_k & -\cos 2\theta_k\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$,
which has eigenvalue $+\kappa_0/R^2$ for radial perturbations
$(\cos\theta_k, \sin\theta_k)$ and $-\kappa_0/R^2$ for tangential.
For $N \ge 8$, the critical eigenvector of the ring-only Lagrangian
(the most negative eigenvalue direction, at mode $m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$)
is \emph{purely radial} at every vortex position.  This requires
the off-diagonal block $H_{rt}^{(m)}$ to vanish at the critical mode
$m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$, which we now verify explicitly.  From
equation~\eqref{eq:sigma-m}:
$H_{rt}^{(m)} = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\sin(2\pi pm/N)/[4\sin^2(\pi p/N)]$.
The substitution $p \to N{-}p$ sends
$\sin(2\pi pm/N) \to \sin(2\pi(N{-}p)m/N) = -\sin(2\pi pm/N)$
(anti-symmetric) while
$\sin^2(\pi p/N) \to \sin^2(\pi(N{-}p)/N) = \sin^2(\pi p/N)$
(symmetric).  Therefore each pair $(p,\,N{-}p)$ cancels, and
$H_{rt}^{(m)} = 0$ for \emph{every} $m$---in particular for the
critical mode $m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$.  The Hessian block is
diagonal at all modes: $\mathrm{diag}(H_{rr}^{(m)}, -H_{rr}^{(m)})$,
so radial and tangential perturbations decouple completely.  This
decoupling is specific to the logarithmic (harmonic) interaction,
for which $H_{tt} = -H_{rr}$.  Therefore
$\langle v_{\mathrm{crit}} | \nabla^2\!H_{\mathrm{center}} |
v_{\mathrm{crit}} \rangle = +\kappa_0$.

The total shift of the critical eigenvalue is $+2\kappa_0$ per unit
$\kappa_0$ (one from the Lagrange multiplier, one from the Hessian
projection).  Setting the shifted eigenvalue to zero:

\begin{proposition}[Critical central vortex strength]
\label{prop:kappa-crit}
For $N \ge 8$, the $N$-gon with central vortex is a constrained energy
minimum if and only if $\kappa_0/\kappa \ge \kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(N)$,
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa-crit}
  \kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(N) = \begin{cases}
    (N^2 - 8N + 8)/16 & N \text{ even}, \\[3pt]
    (N{-}1)(N{-}7)/16 & N \text{ odd}.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}, the minimum constrained
Lagrangian eigenvalue at $\kappa_0 = 0$ is
$\lambda_{\min} = (N{-}1) - \lfloor N/2\rfloor(N - \lfloor N/2\rfloor)/2$.
The central vortex shifts this eigenvalue by $+2\kappa_0$:
$+\kappa_0$ from the Lagrange multiplier (since $-2\mu_L$ increases by
$\kappa_0$) and $+\kappa_0$ from the central-vortex Hessian projection
(since the critical eigenvector is purely radial, and the central-vortex
Hessian has eigenvalue $+\kappa_0/R^2$ on radial perturbations).
Setting $(N{-}1) - m(N{-}m)/2 + 2\kappa_0 = 0$ with
$m = \lfloor N/2 \rfloor$ gives \eqref{eq:kappa-crit}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The first values are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccccc}
\toprule
$N$ & $\le 7$ & 8 & 9 & 10 & 11 & 12 & 13 & 14 \\
\midrule
$\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}$ & 0 & $\frac12$ & 1 &
  $\frac74$ & $\frac52$ & $\frac72$ & $\frac92$ & $\frac{23}{4}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
These agree with the classical Thomson dynamical stability analysis
\citep{Thomson1883, Havelock1931}, confirming that energy and dynamical
stability criteria are equivalent.

For $N = 6$, the exact critical ratio $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(6) = -1/4$
follows algebraically from the shifted-eigenvalue formula in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-crit}.  With $N = 6$ (even) and critical mode
$m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor = 3$, the Lagrangian eigenvalue with central vortex
at strength $r = \kappa_0/\kappa$ is
\[
  \lambda_3 \;=\; (N{-}1) - \tfrac{m(N{-}m)}{2} + 2r
  \;=\; 5 - \tfrac{9}{2} + 2r \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2} + 2r.
\]
Setting $\lambda_3 = 0$ gives $r = -1/4$.  The closed form
$\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}} = (N^2{-}8N{+}8)/16$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-crit} evaluates at $N = 6$ to
$(36-48+8)/16 = -4/16 = -1/4$, consistent with this.
(The Proposition is stated for $N \ge 8$; at $N = 6$ the formula
returns $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}} < 0$, meaning destabilisation requires
an anticyclonic central vortex with $r < 0$.)

The same threshold appears in the dynamical stability analysis.  The
$12\times 12$ linearisation of the hexagonal ring with central vortex
\citep{Havelock1931} has a characteristic polynomial containing the
low-frequency factor
\[
  9 + 36\kappa_0/\kappa + 16\pi^2\lambda^2.
\]
The constant term satisfies $9 + 36r = 18\lambda_3$, so the dynamical
condition $9 + 36r = 0$ is algebraically equivalent to $\lambda_3 = 0$,
both giving $r = -1/4$.  For $\kappa_0/\kappa > -1/4$ the hexagonal
configuration is stable; for $\kappa_0/\kappa < -1/4$ an anticyclonic
central vortex destabilises it.

The center-vs-ring geometry is essential: multi-ring configurations
are generically less stable (Section~\ref{sec:multiring}).

\section{The $N = 7$ bifurcation}
\label{sec:n7-bifurcation}
\label{sec:n7}

At $N = 7$, the minimum constrained Lagrangian eigenvalue is zero
(mode $m = 3$).  To classify the bifurcation, we compute the quartic
term of $H$ along the $m = 3$ neutral direction on the constraint
surface.  The $\mathbb{Z}_7$-equivariant normal form is
$H = (\lambda/2)|z|^2 + (\alpha/4)|z|^4 + \cdots$
where $z$ is the complex amplitude of the $m = 3$ mode and
$\lambda = 0$ at $N = 7$.  Numerical evaluation (tracing $H$ along
the Newton-projected constraint surface) gives
$\alpha > 0$.  The neutral subspace of the constrained Hessian consists
of two modes: the radial Fourier modes at $m = 3$ and $m = 4 = N{-}3$
(together forming the $2$-dimensional space of symmetry-breaking
perturbations), plus the rotation mode (trivially neutral by the
$\mathrm{SO}(2)$ symmetry of $H$).

For both neutral modes, the \emph{unconstrained} quartic
$\dd^4\!H/\dd t^4$ (where $t$ is the unit-eigenvector amplitude) takes
the same value by $\mathbb{Z}_7$ symmetry.  Each of the $21$ vortex
pairs contributes
\[
  \frac{6C^2}{A^2} - \frac{12CB^2}{A^3} + \frac{3B^4}{A^4},
\]
where $A = |\Delta z|^2$, $B = 2\operatorname{Re}(\Delta z\cdot
\overline{\Delta u})$, $C = |\Delta u|^2$ are evaluated at the
unit-ring N-gon ($|\Delta z|^2 = 4\sin^2(\pi p/N)$ for separation
class $p$).  Direct summation over all $21$ pairs gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quartic-153}
  \frac{\dd^4 H}{\dd t^4}\bigg|_{m=3} = \frac{153}{7}.
\end{equation}
The quartic sum is a polynomial in coordinates of the $N$-gon (whose
positions are $7$th roots of unity) and is $\mathbb{Z}_7$-invariant:
simultaneously shifting all vortex labels by one maps the ring to itself
and maps $u$ to a rotated copy.  By Galois theory, such an invariant
sum lies in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)^{\mathbb{Z}_7} = \mathbb{Q}$, so the
result is necessarily a rational number.  The value $153/7$ is then
identified by the following argument: the $21$ pairs decompose into
$6$ separation classes $p=1,\dots,6$ with $7$ pairs each, and the
per-pair quartic~\eqref{eq:quartic-153} depends on $p$ only through
$A_p = 4\sin^2(\pi p/7)$ and the mode overlap
$B_p = 2\operatorname{Re}(\Delta z\cdot\overline{\Delta u})$,
$C_p = |\Delta u|^2$.  These are algebraic numbers in
$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)$.  The Galois rationality argument is the
proof: $\mathbb{Z}_7$-invariant sums over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)$
lie in the fixed field $\mathbb{Q}$, so the quartic coefficient is
rational \emph{a priori}; direct algebraic summation
(\texttt{n7\_bifurcation.py}, independently confirmed by
$50$-decimal-place evaluation) identifies the value as $153/7$.
(The $\mathbb{Z}_7$ symmetry forces the degree-$4$ form on
the $2$-dimensional neutral subspace to be proportional to $|z|^4$,
so $153/7$ holds for \emph{any} unit vector in the $\{m{=}3,\,m{=}4\}$
subspace.)

The constraint-surface correction admits an exact closed form via
the \emph{log-scaling identity}.  The Newton projection onto
$\{L = N\}$ sends $\varepsilon \mapsto
z(\varepsilon) = (z + \varepsilon u)\,\sqrt{N/L(\varepsilon)}$, where
$L(\varepsilon) = N + \varepsilon^2$ exactly (since $\operatorname{Re}
\sum_k z_k\overline{u_k} = 0$ for the radial $m{=}3$ mode and
$\|u\|^2 = 1$).  Because the logarithmic energy is a sum of
$-\ln|\cdot|$ over $N(N-1)/2$ pairs, the overall scale factor
$s(\varepsilon) = \sqrt{N/(N+\varepsilon^2)}$ contributes an additive
term:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-scaling-identity}
  H_{\mathrm{constrained}}(\varepsilon)
  = H_{\mathrm{unconstrained}}(\varepsilon)
  + \frac{N(N-1)}{4}\ln\!\left(1 + \frac{\varepsilon^2}{N}\right).
\end{equation}
Expanding the logarithm:
$\ln(1+\varepsilon^2/N)
  = \varepsilon^2/N - \varepsilon^4/(2N^2) + O(\varepsilon^6)$,
so the 4th derivative of the correction term at $\varepsilon=0$ is
\[
  \frac{N(N-1)}{4}\cdot\left(-\frac{12}{N^2}\right)
  = -\frac{3(N-1)}{N}.
\]
For $N=7$ this is $-18/7$.  Combined with the unconstrained value
$153/7$, the constrained quartic is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constrained-quartic-exact}
  \left.\frac{\dd^4 H}{\dd t^4}\right|_{\text{constrained}}
  = \frac{153}{7} - \frac{18}{7} = \frac{135}{7},
\end{equation}
an exact rational result.  Richardson extrapolation (three two-point
pairs at $h \in \{0.05,0.04,0.03,0.02\}$, symmetric fourth-difference
formula $[2\,\delta\!H(2h)-8\,\delta\!H(h)]/h^4\to 24c_4$ with
log-ratio cancellation) gives $19.2857\pm 0.0005$, numerically
consistent with the exact value $135/7 = 19.285714\ldots$

\citet{KurakinYudovich2002} established nonlinear stability of the
heptagonal ring using Arnold's energy-Casimir method, proving
positivity of the quartic form by Dirichlet's criterion without
computing its value.  \citet{CabralSchmidt1999} and
\citet{Schmidt2004} independently proved stability via Birkhoff
normal form and computed a quartic coefficient of $9/28$ in
the \emph{Sylvester-normalized} coordinates (where all quadratic
terms are rescaled to $1/2$).  The present value $\alpha_0 = 45/14$
is expressed in the physical (un-normalized) radial-mode
coordinates on the constraint surface; both values confirm
$\alpha_0 > 0$ but measure different quantities.
The present derivation proceeds via the constrained energy-minimum
framework (Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}): the log-scaling
identity~\eqref{eq:log-scaling-identity} yields the exact
correction $-3(N{-}1)/N$, and the unconstrained value $153/7$ is
established by the Galois rationality argument combined with direct
algebraic summation.  In the normal form $\mathcal{H} = (\alpha_0/4)|z|^4$,
the exact constrained quartic gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha0-exact}
  \alpha_0 = \frac{135/7}{6} = \frac{45}{14} \approx 3.214 > 0\text{:}
\end{equation}
the energy-Casimir functional $\mathcal{H} = H + |\mu_L|L$ has a
strict local minimum along the $m = 3$ direction at quartic order,
$\mathcal{H} \ge \mathcal{H}_0 + (\alpha_0/4)\varepsilon^4$.  By the
Dirichlet stability criterion \citep{BoattoCabral2003}, the heptagonal
configuration is nonlinearly stable.  The quartic (rather than
quadratic) confinement explains why heptagonal rings are stable but
more easily perturbed than hexagonal ones.

\section{Multi-ring configurations and the center-ring dichotomy}
\label{sec:multiring-new}
\label{sec:multiring}

\begin{proposition}[Center-ring dichotomy]\label{prop:center-ring}
Let $N$ outer vortices sit at the regular $N$-gon on a ring of
radius $R_2$, and let $M \ge 2$ inner vortices sit at the regular
$M$-gon on a ring of radius $R_1 < R_2$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(i)] If $N \le 7$ (outer ring Thomson-stable), the two-ring
    configuration is \emph{unstable}: the Lagrangian Hessian has
    at least one negative eigenvalue.
  \item[(ii)] If $N \ge 8$ (outer ring Thomson-unstable), the inner
    ring can reduce but \emph{not eliminate} the instability:
    the two-ring Hessian retains at least one negative eigenvalue.
\end{itemize}
In contrast, a single central vortex ($M = 0$) at the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ fixed point produces a \emph{uniform} eigenvalue
shift $+\kappa_0/R^2$ across all Fourier modes and can achieve
full stabilization (Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-crit}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof splits into two cases.

\emph{Case 1: $N \ge 8$ (outer ring Thomson-unstable).}
The Havelock eigenvalue $\lambda_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}
= (N{-}1) - \lfloor N/2\rfloor(N{-}\lfloor N/2\rfloor)/2 < 0$,
so the outer $N$-ring alone has a negative constrained eigenvalue.
Adding $M \ge 2$ inner vortices introduces new degrees of freedom
but does not remove the existing instability: the negative outer
mode persists (its eigenvalue is a continuous function of the
coupling strength, starting negative at zero coupling).
This proves~(ii).

\emph{Case 2: $N \le 7$ (outer ring Thomson-stable).}
The two concentric regular polygons form an equilibrium at the
co-rotation ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-ring-limit}
  R_2/R_1 = \sqrt{(N{+}M{-}1)/(M{-}1)},
\end{equation}
determined by the Gauss mean-value theorem: the outer ring at
$R_2 > R_1$ encloses zero inner-ring circulation, so the inner
ring's angular velocity is $(M{-}1)\kappa/(4\pi R_1^2)$ (self-%
interaction only), while the outer ring sees the enclosed inner
circulation and rotates at $(N{+}M{-}1)\kappa/(4\pi R_2^2)$.
Equating gives~\eqref{eq:two-ring-limit}.

At this equilibrium, the constrained Lagrangian Hessian $\mathcal{H}$
is computed for all $12$ cases
($N \in \{5,6,7\}$, $M \in \{2,\ldots,N{-}1\}$).  Non-positive-%
definiteness is certified by two algebraic tests:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Determinant test} (8~cases with odd number of
    negative eigenvalues): $\det(\mathcal{H}) < 0$ implies an
    odd number of negative eigenvalues, hence $\ge 1$.
  \item \textbf{Sylvester minor test} (4~remaining cases with
    even number of negative eigenvalues): the $k$-th leading
    principal minor of $\mathcal{H}$ is negative for some
    $k \le \dim(\mathcal{H})$, so $\mathcal{H}$ is not positive
    semi-definite.
\end{itemize}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccrcl}
\toprule
$N$ & $M$ & $\lambda_{\min}$ & $n_{\mathrm{neg}}$ & Certificate \\
\midrule
$5$ & $2$ & $-0.375$ & $3$ & $\det < 0$ \\
$5$ & $3$ & $-1.410$ & $3$ & $\det < 0$ \\
$5$ & $4$ & $-3.085$ & $4$ & minor$(7) < 0$ \\
$6$ & $2$ & $-0.204$ & $2$ & minor$(8) < 0$ \\
$6$ & $3$ & $-0.795$ & $3$ & $\det < 0$ \\
$6$ & $4$ & $-1.688$ & $4$ & minor$(9) < 0$ \\
$6$ & $5$ & $-3.136$ & $5$ & $\det < 0$ \\
$7$ & $2$ & $-0.088$ & $1$ & $\det < 0$ \\
$7$ & $3$ & $-0.316$ & $3$ & $\det < 0$ \\
$7$ & $4$ & $-0.803$ & $3$ & $\det < 0$ \\
$7$ & $5$ & $-1.691$ & $5$ & $\det < 0$ \\

Both tests are evaluated at machine precision
($\sim\!10^{-14}$ per matrix entry); the determinant and minor
values exceed the accumulated floating-point error by at least

\texttt{two\_ring.py}.

\emph{Mechanism.}  A central vortex at the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ fixed
point contributes a uniform eigenvalue shift $+\kappa_0/R^2$
across all Fourier modes, compensating the negative Havelock
eigenvalues.  An inner ring of $M \ge 2$ vortices shares
the same Lagrange multiplier as the outer ring but has a
\emph{different} rotation rate, creating a coupling between the
inner tangential mode and the outer ring's response that
produces at least one negative eigenvalue at the equilibrium.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Jupiter's observed center-plus-octagon geometry is therefore
\emph{uniquely stabilized} among all configurations with a
secondary vortex structure: only the single central cyclone
achieves full stabilization of the $N = 8$ ring.

\section{From point vortices to continuous vorticity: the blob bridge}
\label{sec:blob-bridge}
\label{sec:blob}

The results of Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:discussion-new} are
stated for point vortices.  Two distinct bridges connect these to
continuous vorticity distributions, applicable in different regimes.

\subsubsection{The vortex blob bridge (Jupiter regime)}

For discrete cyclone configurations ($\beta R^3/\kappa \ll 1$),
replace each point vortex with a smooth blob of width $\varepsilon$.
The regularized interaction
$h_\varepsilon(d) = -\tfrac12\ln(d^2 + 2\varepsilon^2)$
recovers $-\ln d$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Blob stability correction]
\label{prop:blob}
The constrained Lagrangian eigenvalues for $h_\varepsilon$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blob-correction}
  \lambda_m(\varepsilon) = \lambda_m(0)
  + \bigl[-\tfrac{N^2-1}{12} + P_m\bigr]\,\varepsilon^2
  + O(\varepsilon^4),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_m(0)$ is the Havelock eigenvalue
(Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Lagrange multiplier shift.}  The stationarity condition
$\partial H_\varepsilon/\partial x_0 = \mu_L \cdot 2x_0$ gives
$\mu_L(\varepsilon) = \mu_L(0) + \delta\mu_L\cdot\varepsilon^2$
where $\delta\mu_L = (N^2{-}1)/24$, using
$\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\csc^2(\pi k/N) = (N^2{-}1)/3$.  The shift
$-2\delta\mu_L = -(N^2{-}1)/12$ is \emph{universal} (the same for
all Fourier modes).

\emph{Hessian correction.}  The $O(\varepsilon^2)$ corrections to
$h''$ and $h'/d$ are:
$\delta(h''(d)) = -6\varepsilon^2/d^4 + O(\varepsilon^4)$,
$\delta(h'(d)/d) = 2\varepsilon^2/d^4 + O(\varepsilon^4)$.
For a pair at distance $d_p = 2\sin(\pi p/N)$, the Hessian
correction per pair is $O(\varepsilon^2/d_p^4)$.
The projection $P_m = v_m^\top\,\delta\nabla^2\!H\,v_m$ of this
correction onto the critical eigenvector $v_m$ is computed by summing
over all $N-1$ pairwise distances with the Fourier mode weights.

\emph{Operator norm bound.}  Since $d_{\min} = 2\sin(\pi/N) > 0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blob-correction-op-norm}
\|\nabla^2\!H_\varepsilon - \nabla^2\!H_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le 6(2N{-}3)\,\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^4 = O(\varepsilon^2).
\end{equation}
By Weyl's perturbation theorem, each eigenvalue shifts by at most
this amount, giving uniform convergence at $O(\varepsilon^2)$.

\emph{Profile independence.}  For a blob with finite second moment
$\sigma^2$ (Gaussian, compact support), the Fourier transform
$|\hat\phi(\varepsilon k)|^2 = 1 - \sigma^2\varepsilon^2|k|^2
+ O(\varepsilon^4|k|^4)$
shows the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ correction is a constant in $k$-space
(= self-energy, not affecting pairwise eigenvalues).  The pairwise
correction is $O(\varepsilon^4)$---\emph{faster} than the Cauchy blob.
Since the Cauchy blob is the slowest-converging regularization, all
profiles with finite $\sigma^2$ inherit its stability conclusions.
\end{proof}

The Hessian projection $P_m$ and the total correction
$c_m = -(N^2{-}1)/12 + P_m$ (specific to the Cauchy blob
$h_\varepsilon = -\tfrac12\ln(d^2 + 2\varepsilon^2)$) are exact
rational numbers, computed as follows.  Differentiating the Cauchy
blob pair Hessian with respect to $\varepsilon^2$ at $\varepsilon=0$
gives the per-pair correction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blob_pair_correction}
  \delta H_{jj,xx} = \frac{2}{d_{jk}^6}\bigl((\Delta y)^2 - 3(\Delta x)^2\bigr),
  \;\;\;
  \delta H_{jk,xx} = -\delta H_{jj,xx},
\end{equation}
with analogous expressions for $yy$ and $xy$ (see proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:blob}).  Here $(\Delta x, \Delta y, d_{jk})$
are the pair separation components.  The critical radial Fourier
mode is $v_k \propto z_k\cos(2\pi mk/N)$ with $m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$.
The projection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pm_formula}
  P_m = v_m^\top\,\delta\nabla^2\!H\,v_m
\end{equation}
is a finite quadratic form: a sum over $N(N{-}1)$ pair
contributions, each involving trigonometric values at $N$-th roots
of unity.  Since $\sin(\pi p/N)$ and $\cos(2\pi p/N)$ are algebraic
for integer $p$ and $N$, every term is rational and so is the sum.
Explicit values (verified to machine precision in
\texttt{blob\_correction.compute\_Pm(N)} and notebook
\texttt{05\_Blob\_Correction.ipynb}):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
$N$ & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 & 8 \\
\midrule
$-(N^2{-}1)/12$ & $-\frac23$ & $-\frac54$ & $-2$ &
  $-\frac{35}{12}$ & $-4$ & $-\frac{21}{4}$ \\[3pt]
$P_m$ & $-\frac13$ & $+\frac14$ & $+1$ & $+4$ & $+8$ &
  $+\frac{65}{4}$ \\[3pt]
$c_m$ & $-1$ & $-1$ & $-1$ & $+\frac{13}{12}$ & $+4$ & $+11$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The Hessian projection $P_m$ changes sign between $N = 3$
($P_3 = -1/3$) and $N = 4$ ($P_4 = +1/4$): for the triangle, the blob
softening reduces the nearest-neighbor curvature (all pairs are at the
same short distance $d = \sqrt{3}$), while for the square and larger
polygons, the long-range pairs increasingly dominate the projection
and $P_m > 0$.  Despite this sign change,
$c_m = -1$ universally for $N \le 5$ because the Lagrange multiplier
shift $-(N^2{-}1)/12$ dominates $P_m$ in this range.  For $N \ge 6$,
$P_m$ grows faster and overwhelms the shift.

For blob profiles with finite second moment $\sigma^2$ (Gaussian,
compact support), the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ correction is confined to
the self-energy and does not affect pairwise eigenvalues
(Section~\ref{sec:blob}, profile independence argument).  The
pairwise correction is $O(\varepsilon^4)$ with a profile-dependent
coefficient.  Since the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ corrections from the Cauchy
blob table above preserve stability for $N \le 7$, and all
finite-$\sigma^2$ blobs have \emph{smaller} corrections at any given
$\varepsilon$, the stability conclusions hold \emph{a fortiori} for
all smooth profiles.

Three consequences follow:

\emph{(i) Stability preserved.}  For $N \le 5$:
$\lambda_0 \ge 1$ and $c = -1$, so
$\lambda(\varepsilon) = \lambda_0 - \varepsilon^2 > 0$
for $\varepsilon < 1$.
For $N = 6$: $c > 0$, so $\lambda(\varepsilon) > \lambda_0$
---the blob \emph{enhances} stability.

\emph{(ii) Marginal mode resolved.}  At $N = 7$,
$\lambda_0 = 0$ and $c = +4$, so
$\lambda(\varepsilon) = 4\varepsilon^2 > 0$ for any
$\varepsilon > 0$.  The heptagonal marginal stability is an artifact
of the singular point vortex limit.

\emph{(iii) Instability partially compensated.}  For $N = 8$:
$\lambda_0 = -1$ and $c = 11$, so the octagon is stabilized for
$\varepsilon > 1/\sqrt{11} \approx 0.30$.

\subsubsection{Full infinite-dimensional convergence}

Proposition~\ref{prop:blob} addresses center-position perturbations of
$N$ smooth blobs.  The full vorticity perturbation space is
infinite-dimensional, including blob \emph{shape} deformations.  A
complete stability analysis requires the Hessian of the 2D Euler energy
$E[\omega] = \tfrac12\iint\omega(x)G(x,y)\omega(y)\,\dd x\,\dd y$ at
the blob configuration
$\omega_\varepsilon = \sum_k \kappa\,\phi_\varepsilon(\cdot - z_k)$,
decomposed into three sectors:

\emph{(a) Center translations.}  The Hessian restricted to the
$2N$-dimensional subspace of center displacements is \emph{exactly}
the blob Hessian of Proposition~\ref{prop:blob}.  Convergence to the
point vortex Hessian at $O(\varepsilon^\alpha)$ follows from the
convolution estimates.

\emph{(b) Shape perturbations.}  Mean-zero, center-preserving
deformations of each blob's profile enter through the self-energy
$E_{\mathrm{self}} = \iint\phi_\varepsilon(x)\,G(x,y)\,
\phi_\varepsilon(y)\,\dd x\,\dd y$.  The second variation at
mean-zero $\delta\omega$ is, by Plancherel and the Fourier
representation $\hat G(\xi) = 1/(4\pi^2|\xi|^2)$ of the 2D
log-Green's function:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-energy-Hminus1}
  \delta^2 E_{\mathrm{self}}
  = \iint\delta\omega(x)\,G(x,y)\,\delta\omega(y)\,\dd x\,\dd y
  = \int_{\mathbb{R}^2}\frac{|\hat{\delta\omega}(\xi)|^2}%
    {4\pi^2|\xi|^2}\,\dd\xi
  = \frac{1}{4\pi^2}\,\|\delta\omega\|_{H^{-1}}^2.
\end{equation}
Since $\hat{\delta\omega}(0) = \int\delta\omega = 0$ (mean-zero),
the integrand is zero only on a set of measure zero, so
$\delta^2 E_{\mathrm{self}} > 0$ for all non-zero mean-zero
$\delta\omega$: the self-energy Hessian is positive definite
in $H^{-1}$.  The rescaling $\phi_\varepsilon(x)
= \varepsilon^{-2}\phi(x/\varepsilon)$ gives
$\hat\phi_\varepsilon(\xi) = \hat\phi(\varepsilon\xi)$, so
\[
  E_{\mathrm{self}}[\phi_\varepsilon]
  = \int\frac{|\hat\phi(\varepsilon\xi)|^2}{4\pi^2|\xi|^2}\,\dd\xi
  = \varepsilon^{-2}\int\frac{|\hat\phi(\eta)|^2}{4\pi^2|\eta|^2}\,\dd\eta
  = \frac{c}{\varepsilon^2},
\]
and the smallest shape eigenvalue scales as $c/\varepsilon^2$.
Shape modes are \emph{unconditionally stable}.

\emph{(c) Center-shape coupling.}  The cross terms involve overlap
integrals
\[
  C_{jk} = \iint \nabla\phi_\varepsilon(x{-}z_j)\cdot
  \nabla G(x,y)\,\delta\psi(y{-}z_k)\,\dd x\,\dd y
\]
between non-adjacent blobs $j \ne k$ ($|z_j - z_k| \ge d_{\min}
\gg \varepsilon$), where $\delta\psi$ is a mean-zero,
center-preserving shape perturbation.  Since both the monopole
$(\int\delta\psi = 0)$ and dipole $(\int y\,\delta\psi = 0)$
moments of $\delta\psi$ vanish, the leading term in the multipole
expansion of $G(x,y)$ around $z_k$ is the quadrupole:
\[
  G(x,y) = G(x,z_k) + \nabla_y G(x,z_k)\cdot(y{-}z_k)
            + O(|y{-}z_k|^2/d^2),\quad d = |x{-}z_k|.
\]
The first two terms contribute zero (by the vanishing moments),
so the coupling is determined by the quadrupole interaction:
$C_{jk} = O(\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^3)$.
(Numerically verified: $C_{jk} \approx 4\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^3$
for the Cauchy blob at $d_{\min} \ge 3\varepsilon$.)
This is \emph{algebraically small}---the correct bound for the
Cauchy blob, which has algebraic not exponential spatial
decay.  The ratio of coupling to shape eigenvalue
is $O(\varepsilon^4/d_{\min}^3) \to 0$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$
with $d_{\min}$ fixed, which is the only bound needed below.

\begin{proposition}[Inertia preservation]
\label{prop:inertia}
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon > 0$, the full Hessian of
$E[\omega_\varepsilon] + \mu_L\,L[\omega_\varepsilon]$ on the space of
$\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric vorticity perturbations has the same inertia
(number of positive, zero, and negative eigenvalues along the constrained
directions) as the point vortex Lagrangian Hessian of
Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}.  The point vortex model is therefore
the \emph{exact} limit of smooth vorticity dynamics for stability
analysis: no eigenvalue is lost, no sign is changed, and no new
instability arises from the infinite-dimensional shape degrees of
freedom.  The stability boundary $N \le 7$ is not an approximation of
the smooth result---it \emph{is} the smooth result in the
$\varepsilon \to 0$ limit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the constrained full Hessian in block form:
\[
  \mathcal{H} = \begin{pmatrix} H_{cc} & H_{cs} \\ H_{cs}^\top & H_{ss} \end{pmatrix},
\]
where the subscripts $c$ and $s$ denote the center and shape sectors.

\emph{Shape sector.}  By~\eqref{eq:self-energy-Hminus1},
$H_{ss} \ge (c/\varepsilon^2)\,\mathrm{Id}_{H^{-1}}$ with $c > 0$.
All shape eigenvalues are bounded below by $c/\varepsilon^2 > 0$.

\emph{Center sector.}  By~(a) and the convolution estimates of
Proposition~\ref{prop:blob}, the center-sector eigenvalues satisfy
$\lambda_c^{(k)}(\varepsilon) = \lambda_k^{\mathrm{Hav}} + O(\varepsilon^2)$,
where $\lambda_k^{\mathrm{Hav}}$ are the Havelock eigenvalues.

\emph{Coupling bound.}  From~(c), the off-diagonal block satisfies
$\|H_{cs}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = O(\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^3)$.
By the Schur complement, the full Hessian has the same inertia as
$H_{cc} - H_{cs} H_{ss}^{-1} H_{cs}^\top$.  The correction term is
\[
  \|H_{cs} H_{ss}^{-1} H_{cs}^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \le \|H_{cs}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\,\|H_{ss}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  = O\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon^4}{d_{\min}^6}\right)\cdot O(\varepsilon^2)
  = O\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon^6}{d_{\min}^6}\right),
\]
which goes to zero as $\varepsilon\to 0$ with $d_{\min}$ fixed.
Hence, for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, the Schur complement
eigenvalues are within $O(\varepsilon^6/d_{\min}^6)$ of the
center-sector eigenvalues $H_{cc}$, and in particular preserve
their signs.  Since $\|H_{cc} - H_{cc}^{\mathrm{Hav}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
= O(\varepsilon^2)$ (from the blob correction), for small enough
$\varepsilon$ the Schur-complement eigenvalues have the same signs
as the Havelock eigenvalues.  Therefore the full Hessian inertia
equals the Havelock inertia---which is the point vortex Lagrangian
inertia of Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:inertia} assumes a prescribed blob profile
$\phi_\varepsilon$.  For a \emph{general} $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric
vorticity $\omega$ concentrated in $N$ patches near the $N$-gon
vertices, the profile is not prescribed.  We show the same inertia
result holds for any such configuration.

\emph{Center extraction and dipole cancellation.}  Decompose
$\omega = \sum_k \omega_k$ via Voronoi cells and define the centroid
of each patch:
\[
  \bar z_k = \frac{1}{\kappa}\int\! x\,\omega_k(x)\,\dd x.
\]
Any perturbation $\delta\omega_k$ decomposes as
$\delta\omega_k = \delta\omega_k^{(c)} + \delta\omega_k^{(s)}$,
where $\delta\omega_k^{(c)} = -\delta\bar z_k\cdot\nabla\omega_k$
is the center-translation component and the residual
$\delta\omega_k^{(s)}$ is center-preserving: $\int x\,\delta\omega_k^{(s)}\,\dd x = 0$.
\emph{This is not an assumption but a consequence of the construction:}
\begin{align*}
  \int\! x\,\delta\omega_k^{(s)}\,\dd x
  &= \int\! x\,\delta\omega_k\,\dd x
    - \int\! x\,(-\delta\bar z_k\cdot\nabla\omega_k)\,\dd x \\
  &= \kappa\,\delta\bar z_k
    + \delta\bar z_k\cdot\int\! x\,\nabla\omega_k\,\dd x.
\end{align*}
Integration by parts gives
$\int\! x_i\,(\partial_j\omega_k)\,\dd x = -\int\!\delta_{ij}\omega_k\,\dd x
= -\kappa\,\delta_{ij}$
(the boundary term vanishes provided $\omega_k$ is compactly supported
or decays faster than $|x|^{-2}$ at infinity),
so $\int\! x\,\nabla\omega_k\,\dd x = -\kappa\,\mathrm{Id}$, and
the right-hand side is $\kappa\,\delta\bar z_k - \kappa\,\delta\bar z_k = 0$.
The dipole moment of $\delta\omega_k^{(s)}$ vanishes for any
profile $\omega_k \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^2)$ with finite first moment
$\int\!|x|\,|\omega_k|\,\dd x < \infty$.

\emph{Coupling bound for general $\omega_k \in L^1$.}
The center-shape coupling $C_{jk}$ involves the interaction between a
center translation of patch~$j$ and a shape perturbation
$\delta\omega_k^{(s)}$ of patch~$k$ ($j \ne k$).  Expanding the
Green's function $G(x,y) = -\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x{-}y|$ around
the centroid $\bar z_k$:
\begin{align*}
  G(x,y) &= G(x,\bar z_k) + \nabla_y G(x,\bar z_k)\cdot(y{-}\bar z_k) \\
          &\quad + \tfrac{1}{2}(y{-}\bar z_k)^\top
            \nabla_y^2 G(x,\bar z_k)\,(y{-}\bar z_k) + \cdots
\end{align*}
Integrating against $\delta\omega_k^{(s)}(y)\,\dd y$: the zeroth-order
term vanishes ($\int\delta\omega_k^{(s)} = 0$, mean-zero); the
first-order (dipole) term vanishes ($\int(y{-}\bar z_k)
\delta\omega_k^{(s)} = 0$, by the dipole cancellation proved above).
The leading contribution is therefore the quadrupole:
\[
  C_{jk} = \tfrac{1}{2}\int\!\int\!
  \nabla\phi_j(x)\cdot
  \bigl[(y{-}\bar z_k)^\top\nabla_y^2 G(x,\bar z_k)\,(y{-}\bar z_k)\bigr]
  \,\delta\omega_k^{(s)}(y)\,\dd y\,\dd x.
\]
The second derivatives $\nabla_y^2 G(x,\bar z_k) = O(1/d_{jk}^2)$
for $|x - \bar z_k| \ge d_{jk}$, and $|y - \bar z_k| \le \varepsilon$
within patch~$k$.  The integral over $y$ contributes
$\int |y{-}\bar z_k|^2\,|\delta\omega_k^{(s)}|\,\dd y
\le \varepsilon^2\,\|\delta\omega_k^{(s)}\|_{L^1}$, giving
\[
  |C_{jk}| \le \frac{C\,\varepsilon^2}{d_{jk}^2}\,
  \|\delta\omega_k^{(s)}\|_{L^1}\,\|\nabla\phi_j\|_{L^1}
  = O(\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^2)
\]
for any $\omega_k \in L^1$ with support in $B(\bar z_k, \varepsilon)$.
(For the Cauchy blob, which has $|x|^{-3}$ tails extending beyond
$\varepsilon$, the bound worsens to $O(\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^3)$
because $\|\nabla\phi_j\|_{L^1}$ grows as $1/d_{\min}$; the
$O(\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^2)$ bound holds for compactly supported
or exponentially decaying profiles.)

The self-energy Hessian~\eqref{eq:self-energy-Hminus1} is positive
definite for \emph{any} mean-zero profile.  The $\mathbb{Z}_N$
symmetry forces all patches to be congruent (up to rotation), so
the inter-patch Hessian depends on the profile only through
its convolution with $G$---the same structure as
Proposition~\ref{prop:blob}.

Therefore Proposition~\ref{prop:inertia} extends to arbitrary
$\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric vorticity patches satisfying the $L^1$
regularity condition above.  The phrase
\emph{sufficiently concentrated} is made quantitative as follows.
The operator norm bound~\eqref{eq:blob-correction-op-norm} is
derived for the Cauchy blob, which has algebraic ($|x|^{-3}$) rather
than exponential spatial decay and is therefore the
slowest-converging regularization among profiles with finite
second moment; profiles with faster decay (Gaussian, compact support)
satisfy the same bound \emph{a fortiori}, with a smaller implicit
constant.  The Cauchy-blob bound gives
$\|\nabla^2\!H_\varepsilon - \nabla^2\!H_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le 6(2N{-}3)\,\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^4$.
Sign preservation of the Havelock eigenvalues requires this to be
less than $\lambda_{\min}(N)$ (the smallest positive Havelock eigenvalue
at the configuration), yielding the explicit concentration condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:concentration-bound}
  \frac{\varepsilon}{d_{\min}} < \delta(N)
  := \sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\min}(N)}{6(2N-3)}},
\end{equation}
where $d_{\min} = 2\sin(\pi/N)$ is the nearest-neighbor separation
(unit ring).  Numerically:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
$N$ & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\
\midrule
$\lambda_{\min}(N)$ & $1$ & $1$ & $\frac{1}{2}$ & $0$ \\[3pt]
$\delta(N)$ & $0.18$ & $0.15$ & $0.096$ & $\text{---}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent
\emph{Consistency with the Cauchy coupling bound.}
The $\delta(N)$ values above are set by the center-sector perturbation
$\|H_{cc} - H_{cc}^{\mathrm{Hav}}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = O(\varepsilon^2)$,
not by the coupling $\|H_{cs}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$.  The weakened Cauchy
coupling $O(\varepsilon^2/d_{\min}^3)$ produces a Schur-complement
correction $O(\varepsilon^6/d_{\min}^6) = O(\delta(N)^6)$ when the
concentration condition~\eqref{eq:concentration-bound} is satisfied.
Since $\delta(N) \le 0.18$ for all $N \le 6$, the Schur correction is
$O(10^{-5})$---negligible compared to both $\lambda_{\min}(N)$ and
the center-sector perturbation.  The $\delta(N)$ table is therefore
unaffected by the Cauchy tail weakening.

\begin{corollary}[Inertia preservation for $N \le 6$]
\label{cor:inertia-N6}
For $N \le 6$ and any $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric vorticity distribution
with patch radius $\varepsilon/d_{\min} < \delta(N)$
(equation~\eqref{eq:concentration-bound}), the full Hessian inertia
equals the Havelock inertia.  The argument uses only the spectral-gap
condition $\lambda_{\min}(N) > 0$ and the Weyl perturbation bound; no
information about the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ blob correction is needed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Marginal-mode resolution for $N = 7$]
\label{cor:inertia-N7}
For $N = 7$, the concentration bound~\eqref{eq:concentration-bound}
does not apply ($\lambda_{\min} = 0$, so $\delta(7) = 0$).  A
separate, logically distinct argument is required.  The blob
correction $c_7 = +4 > 0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:blob}) lifts
the marginal eigenvalue to $+4\varepsilon^2$ for any
$\varepsilon > 0$.  The Schur-complement coupling is
$O(\varepsilon^6/d_{\min}^6) \ll 4\varepsilon^2$ for all
$\varepsilon < d_{\min}$, so the lifted eigenvalue remains positive.
The conclusion holds for any $\mathbb{Z}_7$-symmetric vorticity
with non-overlapping patches ($\varepsilon < d_{\min}$), a weaker
condition than Corollary~\ref{cor:inertia-N6}.  The $N = 7$ result
is correspondingly more delicate: it holds at $O(\varepsilon^2)$
order and could in principle be affected by $O(\varepsilon^4)$
corrections at large $\varepsilon/d_{\min}$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent
The two corollaries rest on different mechanisms:
Corollary~\ref{cor:inertia-N6} on a spectral gap,
Corollary~\ref{cor:inertia-N7} on a blob correction.  Both extend
beyond prescribed-profile blobs to general $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric
vorticity patches satisfying their respective conditions.

\subsubsection{The Rossby bridge (Saturn regime)}
\label{sec:rossby-bridge}

For continuous jet meanders ($\beta R^3/\kappa \gg 1$), the bridge
is not vortex concentration but \emph{Rossby wave quantization}.
The QGPV linearized about a zonal jet $U(y)$ gives the Rayleigh--Kuo
equation:
\[
  (U - c)\bigl(\hat\psi'' - k^2\hat\psi\bigr)
  + (\beta - U'')\hat\psi = 0.
\]
For a stationary wave ($c = 0$) at the jet center ($y = 0$) of a
Gaussian jet with $U''(0) = -U_{\max}/\sigma^2$:
\[
  \hat\psi'' + \Bigl[\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}}{U_{\max}}
  - \frac{n^2}{R^2}\Bigr]\hat\psi = 0,
  \qquad
  \beta_{\mathrm{eff}} = \beta + \frac{U_{\max}}{\sigma^2},
\]
where the effective PV gradient $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ includes the
jet curvature (which dominates $\beta$ by a factor $\sim\!13$ for
Saturn).  The stationary azimuthal wavenumber is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rossby-bridge}
  n^* = R_{\mathrm{S}}\cos\varphi
  \,\sqrt{\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}/U_{\max}},
\end{equation}
where $R_{\mathrm{S}}\cos\varphi$ is the ring radius at latitude
$\varphi$ ($\approx 1.31 \times 10^7\;\mathrm{m}$ for Saturn at
$76^\circ$\,N).  The nearest integer $n = \mathrm{round}(n^*)$ is
the selected polygon wavenumber.  For Saturn:
$\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}/U_{\max} \approx 1.72 \times
10^{-13}\;\mathrm{m}^{-2}$, giving $n^* \approx 5.4$ from the
leading-order dispersion relation.  The observed $n = 6$ is obtained
in numerical eigenvalue computations of the full Rayleigh--Kuo
equation \citep{SanchezLavega2014}; the discrepancy between the
leading-order $n^* \approx 5.4$ and the observed $n = 6$ reflects
corrections from the finite jet width (the meridional eigenfunction
structure contributes an $O(1/\sigma^2)$ correction to the effective
wavenumber that is not captured by the dispersion relation alone).

The discretization mechanism is \emph{azimuthal periodicity}: the wave
must fit an integer number of wavelengths around the ring.

The connection to the point vortex energy landscape proceeds through
the \emph{critical layer} of the saturated wave.  At finite amplitude,
the stationary Rossby wave develops a cat's-eye structure at the
critical line $U = c = 0$: vorticity is trapped into $N$ lobes of
width $\sim\sigma$ centered at the meander peaks.  As the jet sharpens
($\sigma/R \to 0$ at fixed wave amplitude), these lobes concentrate
into $N$ isolated patches near the $N$-gon vertices.

\begin{remark}[Cat's-eye Hessian convergence]
\label{rem:catseye}
The cat's-eye vorticity decomposes as
$\omega = \omega_{\mathrm{lobe}} + \omega_{\mathrm{braid}} +
\omega_{\mathrm{bg}}$, where the lobes are the trapped fluid inside
the separatrices, the braids are thin filaments along the separatrix
itself, and the background is the passing fluid outside the cat's-eye.

Two conditions are required for Proposition~\ref{prop:inertia} to
apply:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(i)] \textbf{Lobe concentration.}  The lobes satisfy the
    concentration condition~\eqref{eq:concentration-bound} with
    $\varepsilon \sim \sigma$ and the centroids converge to the
    $N$-gon vertices.  This is guaranteed by $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry.
  \item[(ii)] \textbf{Braid smallness.}  The absolute braid circulation
    $\Gamma_{\mathrm{braid}}$ is negligible compared to the total
    lobe circulation~$\Gamma_{\mathrm{lobe}}$.
\end{itemize}

Both conditions are verified numerically on the Stuart vortex
$\Psi = -\sigma^2\ln(\cosh(y/\sigma) - \eta\cos(n\theta))$,
an exact steady Euler solution with analytical cat's-eye structure,
where $\eta \in (0,1)$ is the Stuart amplitude parameter ($\eta = 0$:
linear wave; $\eta \to 1$: point-vortex concentration---note this is
the opposite convention from the blob width $\varepsilon$ in
Section~\ref{sec:blob}, where $\varepsilon \to 0$ is the point-vortex
limit).  As $\eta \to 1$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The lobe fraction of total circulation approaches unity:
    $\Gamma_{\mathrm{lobe}}/\Gamma_{\mathrm{total}} =
    1 - O(1{-}\eta)$, with
    $\Gamma_{\mathrm{braid}}/\Gamma_{\mathrm{total}} =
    O(1{-}\eta)$ (fitted exponent $1.08$, $R^2 = 0.997$).
  \item At $\eta = 0.99$, the lobes capture $99.6\%$ of the
    total circulation; braids carry $0.1\%$.
  \item The $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric lobe circulations are equal to
    within $0.01\%$, and the centroids lie on the $N$-gon vertices
    (both by the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry of the cat's-eye).
  \item The constrained Hessian of the lobe circulations placed on
    the $N$-gon ring has the \emph{same inertia} (number of positive,
    zero, and negative eigenvalues) as the Havelock Hessian, for all
    $\eta \in [0.3, 0.99]$.
\end{itemize}

(homogenized PV inside the lobes, discontinuous at the separatrix),
which is the physically relevant model for Saturn's long-lived
hexagon.  Code:
\texttt{catseye\_decomposition.py},
\texttt{catseye\_hessian.py}.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite Rossby deformation radius: the $K_0$ interaction}
\label{sec:k0-interaction}
\label{sec:deformation}

In quasi-geostrophic dynamics with finite Rossby deformation radius
$R_d$, the Green's function is $K_0(r/R_d)$ (modified Bessel function),
which recovers $-\ln r$ for $r \ll R_d$.  The interaction $h(r) =
K_0(r/R_d)$ satisfies $h'(r) = -K_1(r/R_d)/R_d < 0$ and
$(rh'(r))' = rK_0(r/R_d)/R_d^2 > 0$ (class~(iii)).  Numerical
computation of the constrained Lagrangian Hessian as a function of
$\eta = R/R_d$ (polygon ring radius over deformation radius) reveals
an $N$-dependent \emph{polygon-suppression transition}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccl}
\toprule
$N$ & Barotropic $\lambda_{\min}$ & Critical $R/R_d$ & Status \\
\midrule
$\le 5$ & $\ge 1$ & none & Stable for all $R/R_d$ \\
$6$ & $1/2$ & $\approx 1.29$ & Suppressed for $R/R_d > 1.29$ \\
$7$ & $0$ & $\approx 0$ & Unstable for any finite $R/R_d$ \\
$\ge 8$ & $< 0$ & N/A & Unstable (barotropic) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Polygons with $N \le 5$ are robust against stratification effects.
The hexagon ($N = 6$) is the first polygon sensitive to $R_d$: it
requires $R/R_d < 1.29$.  The heptagon ($N = 7$), neutrally stable
in the barotropic limit, is destabilized by any finite
$R/R_d$---providing an additional mechanism for its rarity beyond the
quartic confinement.

For Saturn, the barotropic deformation radius
($\sim\!2000$--$3000\,$km) gives $R/R_d \approx 8$--$12$ at the
hexagonal jet, placing it above the critical ratio.  The observed
hexagonal persistence therefore requires the dynamics to be effectively
barotropic over a deep layer---a requirement now directly supported by
observational data.  Cassini Grand Finale gravity measurements constrain
Saturn's differential rotation to a depth of $8{,}000$--$9{,}000$\,km
\citep{Iess2019}, implying a coherent barotropic column of order
$10^4$\,km, far exceeding the hexagon's vertical scale.  Saturn is
therefore the best-supported case in our sample for the applicability
of the 2D logarithmic Green's function: the columnar depth is
observationally established, not merely assumed.

\paragraph{Observable prediction: hexagon suppression signatures.}
The critical ratio $R/R_d \approx 1.29$ for $N = 6$ yields a
testable prediction.  If the effective barotropic depth were reduced
(by seasonal radiative forcing of the upper troposphere, or by a
hypothetical change in deep convective coupling), $R_d$ would
decrease and $R/R_d$ would increase toward the critical value.
Approaching the threshold, the framework predicts three observable
signatures: (1)~the minimum Havelock eigenvalue
$\lambda_{\min}(\eta) \to 0$, reducing the hexagonal mode's
restoring frequency and producing a \emph{longer oscillation period}
for meridional meander perturbations;
(2)~the hexagonal amplitude $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{obs}}$ would
\emph{grow}, since the restoring force weakens while convective
forcing remains constant; and
(3)~at $R/R_d > 1.29$, the hexagonal mode becomes linearly
unstable and the pattern would transition to a lower-$N$ polygon
($N = 5$, the next Thomson-stable configuration) or break up
entirely.
Saturn's hexagon has been observed continuously since Voyager~1
(1980--81); Cassini monitored it through northern spring equinox
(2009) to summer solstice (2017) with no sign of weakening
\citep{Antunano2015, SanchezLavega2014}.  This four-decade
persistence across half a Saturnian year places an empirical lower
bound on the effective barotropic depth: seasonal radiative forcing
does not reduce $R_d$ enough to approach the critical ratio, consistent
with the deep (${\sim}\,10^4\;\mathrm{km}$) barotropic column
implied by Cassini gravity.

For Jupiter the situation is different, and the 2D justification
requires separate examination.  Juno gravity measurements
\citep{Kaspi2018} constrain Jupiter's differential rotation to
$\sim\!3{,}000$\,km depth (${\approx}\,4\%$ of $R_J$), far shallower
than Saturn's.  Jupiter's polar cyclones therefore do \emph{not} rest
on deep barotropic Taylor columns; the relevant 2D dynamics is that of
a shallow weather layer above a near-solid-body rotating deep interior.
Two limiting cases bracket the problem:

\textit{Deep weather-layer limit ($H_{\rm eff} \sim 3{,}000$\,km).}
Taking the full differential-rotation depth as the effective layer
and using the equivalent-barotropic deformation radius
$R_d = \sqrt{g\,H_{\rm eff}}/f \approx \sqrt{25 \times 3\times10^6}\,/\,
(2\Omega_J\sin 84^\circ) \approx 25{,}000$\,km, we obtain
$R/R_d \approx 0.3 \ll 1$.  In this limit
$K_0(r/R_d) \to -\ln r + \mathrm{const}$, so the logarithmic
Green's function applies---though the physical justification is
a shallow equivalent-barotropic layer rather than a deep Taylor column.

\textit{Tropospheric limit ($H_{\rm eff} \sim 30$--$100$\,km).}
If the active polar-cyclone layer is shallower, reduced gravity
$g' \sim 0.1$--$1$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ gives
$R_d \sim 2{,}000$--$6{,}000$\,km and $R/R_d \sim 1$--$4$.
The $N = 8$ ring lies in the finite-$R_d$ regime.  Computing the
$\kappa_0$-shifted constrained Hessian eigenvalue for the $K_0$
interaction with $\kappa_0/\kappa = 0.7$ (the Juno central estimate):

\toprule

\midrule
$6$ & $0.7$ & $17.2$ & yes ($\gg 4$) \\
$7$ & $0.7$ & $4.2$  & marginal \\
$8$ & $0.7$ & $\mathbf{0.79}$ & \textbf{no} ($< 1$) \\

Here $(R/R_d)_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is the ratio at which the minimum

the critical ratio is $(R/R_d)_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 0.79$, which

\textbf{The $N = 8$ prediction therefore requires the deep

The effective layer depth is not directly constrained by current Juno
data, making Jupiter the \emph{least} observationally supported case
for the 2D assumption, in contrast to Saturn.  A direct measurement
of the depth of the polar cyclone roots---analogous to the gravity
constraints on the jets---would resolve this ambiguity.  The
computation above sets a quantitative target: $R_d > 9{,}700$\,km
is necessary for $N = 8$ stability under the $K_0$ interaction.

The critical ratio is defined implicitly by the equation
$\bar\lambda(\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}, N) = 0$, where the dimensionless
eigenvalue $\bar\lambda(\eta, N) = R^2 \cdot \lambda_{\min}(\eta)$
interpolates smoothly from the Havelock value
$\bar\lambda(0, N) = (N{-}1) - \lfloor N/2\rfloor
(N{-}\lfloor N/2\rfloor)/2$ to zero.

The Lagrange multiplier is determined by the stationarity condition
$\partial H/\partial R + \mu_L\,\partial L/\partial R = 0$.  For
$H = -\sum_{j<k}K_0(d_{jk}/R_d)$ with $d_{jk} = 2R\sin(\pi p/N)$
and $L = NR^2$, the radial derivative of each pair contributes
$-K_0'(d_{jk}/R_d)\cdot 2\sin(\pi p/N)/R_d
= K_1(d_{jk}/R_d)\cdot 2\sin(\pi p/N)/R_d$
(using $K_0' = -K_1$).  Projecting onto the constraint gradient
$\partial L/\partial R = 2NR$ and solving gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu-k0}
  -2\mu_L = \frac{\eta}{R}\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}
  \frac{(1-\cos\theta_p)\,K_1(2\eta\sin(\pi p/N))}
       {2\sin(\pi p/N)},
  \quad \theta_p = 2\pi p/N,
\end{equation}
where $\eta = R/R_d$ and the factor $(1{-}\cos\theta_p)$ arises from
the Fourier decomposition of the pairwise Hessian along the radial
mode.  As $\eta \to 0$: $K_1(x) \sim 1/x$ for small $x$, so each
summand reduces to
$(1{-}\cos\theta_p)/(4\eta\sin^2(\pi p/N)) \cdot (\eta/R)$
$= (1{-}\cos\theta_p)/(4R\sin^2(\pi p/N))$.  Summing over $p$ using
the Havelock identity $\sum_p (1{-}\cos\theta_p)/(4\sin^2(\pi p/N))
= (N{-}1)/2$ recovers the flat-plane value $-2\mu_L = (N{-}1)/(2R^2)$.  For $N = 6$, the critical mode $m = 3$
has zero Havelock weight at $p = 2, 4$ (since
$\cos(2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot 3/6) = 1$), so the critical eigenvalue
depends on $K_0$ and $K_1$ at only \emph{two} distinct arguments,
$\eta$ and $2\eta$, rather than three.

No closed-form analog of the Havelock identity is known for $K_0$.
The orthogonality mechanism that collapses the logarithmic sum to
$m(N{-}m)/2$ relies on the identity $\sum_p e^{2\pi i mp/N}\ln
|2\sin(\pi p/N)| = 0$, which has no counterpart for $K_0$:
the Bessel values $K_0(2\eta\sin(\pi p/N))$ are transcendental at
nonzero algebraic arguments by Siegel's theorem on $E$-functions,
but this does not preclude the full weighted sum from having a
closed form.  Whether $\sum_p (\cos\theta_{pm})
K_0(2\eta\sin(\pi p/N))$ admits a closed form for any $N$ and $m$
is an open question; no such form has been found, and all stability
computations for $K_0$ interactions are carried out numerically.
A perturbative expansion
$K_0(x) \approx -\ln x + O(x^2)$ gives $\eta_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx
0.9$ for $N = 6$---30\% below the exact value---because the critical
ratio lies in the non-perturbative regime where $K_0$ departs
significantly from $-\ln$.  The transition occurs precisely where the
exponential decay of $K_0$ (destroying long-range correlations between
non-adjacent vortices) overtakes the logarithmic regime, making the
suppression inherently non-perturbative.  The implicit equation is
nevertheless well-posed, and the critical $\eta$ is uniquely determined
by bisection for each $N$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The two phase boundaries.}
For $K_0$, two distinct boundaries exist in the $(N, \eta)$ plane:
(1)~the \emph{spiral sign change} $\bar H''(0) = 0$, where the
positive $A_m$ term from $(rh')' > 0$ overwhelms the negative $B_m$
term; and (2)~the \emph{constrained stability loss}
$\bar\lambda_{\min} = 0$, where the Lagrange multiplier shift
consumes the stability margin.  The constrained boundary
($\eta \approx 1.29$ for $N = 6$) is always \emph{inside} the
spiral boundary ($\eta \approx 3.2$ for $N = 6$) because the
Lagrange shift reduces the margin before $H''$ changes sign.
Physically, the polygon requires long-range correlations between
non-adjacent vortices.  When $\eta \gg 1$ and the $K_0$ interaction
decays exponentially, only nearest-neighbor correlations survive and
the polygon loses its energetic advantage.

For comparison, the Cauchy-blob regularization
$-\tfrac12\ln(d^2 + 2\varepsilon^2)$ has $H'' < 0$ for \emph{all}
$\varepsilon$---the soft-core never flips the spiral sign.  The
Gaussian-screened interaction $-\ln(d)\cdot e^{-d^2/L^2}$
shows an earlier transition ($\eta \approx 0.9$ for $N = 6$) due to
its faster-than-exponential decay.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The constrained stability boundary.}
The interaction class determines $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$, defined here as
the largest $N$ for which the \emph{constrained} Lagrangian Hessian
$\bar\lambda_{\min} \ge 0$ (i.e.\ the polygon is a constrained energy
minimum).  This is distinct from the spiral-direction boundary
$H''(0) = 0$, which occurs at larger $\eta$ (e.g.\ $\eta \approx 3.2$
vs.\ $1.29$ for $N = 6$ with $K_0$; see the two phase boundaries above):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Class & Condition & $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ (constrained) \\
\midrule
(i) & $(rh')' < 0$ & $\ge 7$ (increases with $|(rh')'|$) \\
(ii) & $(rh')' = 0$ (only $-\ln r$) & $= 7$ exactly \\
(iii) & $(rh')' > 0$ (e.g.\ $K_0$) & $\le 7$
  (decreases with screening) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent
Numerical computation confirms: for $h = -r^\alpha$ (class~(i)),
$N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$ at $\alpha \to 0$ and increases with $\alpha$
($N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 8$ at $\alpha = 1$, $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 14$
at $\alpha = 2$).  At $N = 7$, $\lambda_{\min}(\alpha) > 0$ for all
$\alpha > 0$ tested, with
$\dd^2\lambda_{\min}/\dd\alpha^2 = +2.5 > 0$ at $\alpha \to 0$:
the marginal stability at $N = 7$ is \emph{unique to the logarithmic
interaction}, and any class~(i) interaction makes the heptagon strictly
stable.  For $K_0(r/R_d)$ (class~(iii)), the dimensionless eigenvalue
$\bar\lambda(\eta)$ decreases monotonically in $\eta$ for each Fourier
mode (verified for $N = 5, 6$), consistent with the physical expectation
that screening weakens the stabilizing long-range correlations.
For
$N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 6$ at $R/R_d = 1$ and decreases to~$5$ at
$R/R_d = 3$.  The logarithmic interaction is the \emph{watershed}:
the critical case between interactions that stabilize polygons
(class~(i)) and interactions that suppress them (class~(iii)).

\begin{proposition}[Min/max duality]\label{prop:duality}
For any pairwise interaction $h(r)$, the constrained energy minimum
of $H = \sum h(d_{jk})$ and the constrained energy maximum of $-H$
have the same stability boundary $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Lagrange multiplier for $-H$ is $\mu_L^{-H} = -\mu_L^H$
(from $-\nabla H = (-\mu_L)\nabla L$).  The Lagrangian Hessian
$\mathcal{L}_{-H} = -\nabla^2\!H + 2\mu_L I = -\mathcal{L}_H$.
The eigenvalues are negated; constrained minimum (all $\ge 0$) of $H$
is equivalent to constrained maximum (all $\le 0$) of $-H$.
\end{proof}

For gravity ($h = -1/r$, $h' > 0$), the $N$-gon is a constrained
energy maximum.  The duality gives
$N_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{gravity}} =
N_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{Coulomb}}$.  Numerical computation of
the Coulomb ($h = +1/r$) constrained Hessian gives
$N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 5$: the minimum eigenvalue is $+0.73$ at $N = 5$
and $-0.02$ at $N = 6$.  This is consistent with Maxwell's (1859)
result that gravitating rings require a central mass for
$N \ge 7$; the one-unit difference ($N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 5$ vs
Maxwell's $6$) arises because our analysis does not include the
central mass (Maxwell's stability includes the stabilizing effect
of the central body).

The Coulomb/gravity $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 5$ is less than the
logarithmic $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$, confirming the class~(iii)
reduction: the $1/r$ interaction has $(rh')' = 1/r^2 > 0$, placing
it in the destabilizing regime relative to $-\ln r$.

\subsubsection{Resilience to circulation disorder}
\label{sec:disorder}

In both planetary regimes the vortex circulations $\kappa_k$ are not
perfectly equal; convection injects or removes circulation from individual
vortices continuously.  We model this as $\kappa_k = \kappa(1+\eta_k)$
with i.i.d.\ fractional disorders $\eta_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_\eta^2)$.

We work on $\mathbf{H}^2$ rather than the flat plane because the
hyperbolic setting simultaneously tests two effects: circulation
disorder and curvature stabilization.  In the flat limit
$\rho/a \to 0$ the $\mathbf{H}^2$ eigenvalues recover the Havelock
values exactly, so the flat-plane result is contained as a special
case; conversely, the curvature-stabilized configurations ($N \ge 8$
on $\mathbf{H}^2$) have no flat-plane analog and can only be probed
on the curved surface.

On $\mathbf{H}^2$, the disordered Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H_kappa}
  H_\kappa = -\sum_{j<k}\kappa_j\kappa_k\ln|z_j - z_k|
  + \tfrac12\sum_j\kappa_j(K_{\mathrm{tot}}-\kappa_j)\ln(a^2 - |z_j|^2),
\end{equation}
where $K_{\mathrm{tot}} = \sum_k\kappa_k$.  At $\eta_k \equiv 0$ this
reduces exactly to the uniform Hamiltonian $H_{\mathrm{hyp}}$.

Holding the ring positions at the uniform equilibrium (exact to $O(\eta)$;
the $O(\eta)$ shift of equilibrium produces only an $O(\eta^2)$ eigenvalue
correction), we compute the minimum Fourier-mode eigenvalue
$\lambda_{\min}(\eta)$ via finite differences.  Monte Carlo over
$n = 1000$ realizations estimates $P(\lambda_{\min} < 0)$ as a function
of $(\sigma_\eta, N, \rho)$.

\paragraph{Physical range of $\sigma_\eta$.}
Juno imagery \citep{Adriani2018,Gavriel2021} shows Jupiter's eight
north-polar cyclones have peak wind speeds ranging from
$\sim\!60$ to $\sim\!100$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ and diameters from
$\sim\!4{,}000$ to $\sim\!7{,}000$\,km.  Taking circulation
$\kappa \propto V_{\max}\,r_c$, the coefficient of variation across
the eight cyclones is $\sigma_\eta \approx 0.15$--$0.25$.  Saturn's
hexagonal jet is more uniform (Cassini wind profiles give
$\sigma_\eta \lesssim 0.05$ for the azimuthal circulation variation).
The physically relevant range is therefore
$\sigma_\eta \in [0.05, 0.25]$.

\medskip\noindent
Key findings (all uncertainties are $\pm 1\sigma$ binomial standard
errors $\sqrt{P(1{-}P)/n}$ with $n = 1000$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Stable configurations are robust.}  For $N = 6$ at
    $\rho = 0.5a$, the instability probability remains
    below $10\%\;(\pm 0.9\%)$ for $\sigma_\eta \le 0.2$, covering
    the full physically relevant range.

  \item \textbf{Curvature-stabilized rings ($N \ge 8$) are similarly
    robust.}  At $N = 8$, $\rho = 2a$ (well inside the stable region
    on $\mathbf{H}^2$), $P < 0.5\;(\pm 1.6\%)$ for
    $\sigma_\eta \le 0.1$.  At $\sigma_\eta = 0.2$ (Jupiter's
    estimated value), $P$ remains below $15\%$.

  \item \textbf{The $N = 7$ marginal ring is sensitive.}  At $N = 7$,
    $\rho = 0.05a$ (flat-limit regime), $P(\lambda_{\min} < 0)$
    increases monotonically with $\sigma_\eta$:
    $P \approx 0\;(\pm 0.3\%)$ at $\sigma_\eta = 0.1$ and $P > 0$ for
    $\sigma_\eta \ge 0.5$.  The marginal mode ($\lambda_0 = 0$ at
    $K = 0$) is the first to be destabilized by disorder, as expected.
    At Jupiter's estimated $\sigma_\eta \approx 0.2$, a hypothetical
    $N = 7$ ring would have $P \approx 0$---the marginal sensitivity
    manifests only at unphysically large disorder.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Thus the $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ boundary is a \emph{stability domain
boundary, not a knife-edge}: configurations comfortably inside the
stable region remain stable under the circulation fluctuations
observed at Jupiter ($\sigma_\eta \approx 0.2$) and Saturn
($\sigma_\eta \lesssim 0.05$).

\subsubsection{The regime criterion}

The dimensionless number $\beta R^3/\kappa$ is the ratio of two
timescales: the Rossby wave period $T_R \sim 1/(\beta R)$ and the
vortex turnover time $T_v \sim R^2/\kappa$.  When
$\beta R^3/\kappa = T_v/T_R \ll 1$, the vortex interactions complete
many orbits before the Rossby wave propagates appreciably, so the
dynamics is vortex-dominated.  When $\beta R^3/\kappa \gg 1$, the
Rossby wave dynamics operates faster than the vortex rearrangement,
so the wave mechanism dominates:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\toprule
Regime & $\beta R^3/\kappa$ & Bridge \\
\midrule
Vortex-dominated & $\ll 1$ & Blob convergence (Proposition~\ref{prop:blob}) \\
Wave-dominated & $\gg 1$ & Rossby stationarity (equation~\eqref{eq:rossby-bridge}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\textit{Jupiter's north pole.}
Throughout this regime calculation $\kappa$ denotes the
\emph{Rankine-core} circulation, which integrates only within the
solid-body rotation core and sets the characteristic vortex timescale
$T_v \sim R^2/\kappa$.  The full-cyclone circulation $\kappa_{\mathrm{full}}$
(which includes the outer irrotational skirt) is larger and is the
appropriate quantity for computing orbital periods and eigenfrequencies;
it is used there instead (Section~\ref{sec:dynamical-maintenance}).
The two estimates are numerically distinguished below.

At $84^\circ$N, the planetary vorticity
gradient is $\beta_J = 2\Omega_J\cos(84^\circ)/R_J \approx
5.1\times10^{-13}$\,m$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$ and the cyclone-ring radius
is $R \approx 7{,}470$\,km.  Each Juno-observed circumpolar cyclone has
Rankine core radius $r_c \approx 700$\,km and peak wind $V_{\max}
\approx 80$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ \citep{Adriani2018,Gavriel2021}, giving
$\kappa_{\mathrm{Rank}} = 2\pi r_c V_{\max} \approx 3.5\times10^{8}$\,m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$
(full-cyclone estimate: $r_c \approx 3{,}200$\,km,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{full}} \approx 1.6\times10^9$\,m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$).
Hence
\[
  \frac{\beta R^3}{\kappa}
  = \frac{5.1\times10^{-13}\,(7.47\times10^6)^3}{3.5\times10^8}
  \approx 0.61.
\]
This places Jupiter in the vortex-dominated regime, but only
moderately: $0.61$ is $O(1)$, not $\ll 1$.  Rossby-wave effects
are not negligible at this value, and a decrease in the effective
$\kappa$ (e.g.\ using the Rankine-core rather than full-cyclone
estimate) would push the ratio above unity.  The qualitative
conclusion---discrete cyclones, Thomson stability applies, blob
bridge proves convergence---is robust to this uncertainty because
the vortex-dominated bridge requires only that the cyclones be
identifiable as $N$ distinct vortices (which the Juno imagery
unambiguously confirms), not that $\beta R^3/\kappa$ be
parametrically small.  The ratio enters quantitatively only
through the Rossby-wave correction to the eigenvalues, which is
$O(\beta R^3/\kappa)$ and does not change the sign of
$\lambda_{\min}$ for $N = 8$ (where
$|\lambda_{\min}| = 1 \gg 0.61$).
Using the full-cyclone circulation
$\kappa_{\mathrm{full}} \approx 1.6\times10^9$\,m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$
gives $\beta R^3/\kappa \approx 0.13$, firmly in the
vortex-dominated regime.

\smallskip\noindent
\textit{Saturn's hexagon.}  At $76^\circ$N, $\beta_S =
2\Omega_S\cos(76^\circ)/R_S \approx 1.3\times10^{-12}$\,m$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$
and $R \approx 14{,}600$\,km.  Saturn's polar jet is
well-described by a Gaussian profile $U = U_{\max}\exp(-y^2/2\sigma^2)$;
its effective circulation is $\kappa = U_{\max}\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma$.
With $U_{\max} \approx 100$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ and $\sigma \approx 2{,}100$\,km
(Cassini VIMS wind profiles), $\kappa \approx 5.4\times10^{8}$\,m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$,
giving
\[
  \frac{\beta R^3}{\kappa}
  = \frac{1.3\times10^{-12}\,(1.46\times10^7)^3}{5.4\times10^8}
  \approx 7.6.
\]
Continuous jet meander, Rossby stationarity selects $n = 6$, periodicity
provides quantization.

\smallskip\noindent
Both mechanisms require the 2D logarithmic Green's function as the
common mathematical kernel.

\section{Predictions for quantum systems: BEC vortex clusters}
\label{sec:quantum}
\label{sec:predictions}
\label{sec:bec}

In a quasi-2D Bose--Einstein condensate in a circular trap, quantized
vortices interact logarithmically at scales above the healing length
$\xi$.  The circulation is quantized:
$\kappa = q \cdot h/m$ with integer winding number $q$.
The $\kappa_0/\kappa$ ratio of Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-crit}
becomes the winding number ratio $q_0/q$, yielding sharp integer-valued
predictions:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\toprule
Configuration & $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}$ & Prediction \\
\midrule
$N \le 7$ ring & 0 & Stable without central vortex \\
$N = 8$ ring & $1/2$ & Unstable alone; stable with $q_0 \ge 1$ \\
$N = 9$ ring & $1$ & Marginally stable with $q_0 = 1$;
  stable with $q_0 \ge 2$ \\
$N = 10$ ring & $7/4$ & Unstable with $q_0 = 1$;
  stable with $q_0 \ge 2$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The $N = 8$ prediction is the sharpest: a ring of $8$ unit-winding
vortices should be unstable alone and stable upon adding a single
central vortex ($q_0 = 1 > \kappa_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/2$).

The $N = 7$ case offers a distinct signature.  The quartic restoring
force ($\alpha_0 = 45/14$, Section~\ref{sec:n7}) produces an
\emph{amplitude-dependent} oscillation frequency for the $m = 3$ mode.
In the normal form $\mathcal{H} = (\alpha_0/4)\varepsilon^4$, the
nonlinear oscillation frequency is
$\omega_{m=3} = \alpha_0\,\varepsilon^2\,\kappa/(2\pi R^2)$, where
$\varepsilon$ is the dimensionless perturbation amplitude (radial
displacement / ring radius) and $\kappa = h/m_{\mathrm{atom}}$ is the
single-quantum circulation.  For $^{87}$Rb in a circular trap of
radius $R = 50\;\mu\mathrm{m}$:
$\kappa \approx 1.2\times 10^{-7}\;\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$,
$\kappa/(2\pi R^2) \approx 7.6\;\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, so
$\omega_{m=3} \approx 24\,\varepsilon^2\;\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.  At a
5\% radial displacement ($\varepsilon = 0.05$):
$\omega_{m=3} \approx 0.06\;\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ (period $\sim\!100\;\mathrm{s}$),
resolvable with standard BEC vortex imaging at $\sim\!10\;\mathrm{Hz}$
frame rates.  The key signature distinguishing the $N = 7$
quartic confinement from the $N \le 6$ quadratic regime is that the
$m = 3$ oscillation frequency \emph{vanishes} as $\varepsilon \to 0$
(rather than approaching a finite value), producing an
amplitude-dependent period that is directly measurable.

The healing length $\xi$ provides a natural blob width
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{blob}} = \xi/R$.  For typical BEC parameters
($\xi \sim 0.3\;\mu\mathrm{m}$, $R \sim 50\;\mu\mathrm{m}$):
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{blob}} \approx 0.006$, well within the point
vortex regime.  The blob correction (Proposition~\ref{prop:blob}) is
negligible: $\lambda(0.006) \approx \lambda_0 + O(10^{-4})$.

\emph{Finite-temperature effects.}  Current BEC vortex experiments
operate at finite temperature ($T/T_c \sim 0.3$--$0.7$), where a
thermal cloud coexists with the condensate and exerts a dissipative
drag on vortex motion \citep{Fetter2009}.  This drag damps the
oscillation modes on a timescale $\tau_{\mathrm{drag}} \sim
R^2/(D_v\,T/T_c)$ where $D_v$ is the vortex diffusion coefficient.
For the $N = 7$ quartic mode to be observable, the oscillation period
$(\sim\!100\;\mathrm{s}$ at $\varepsilon = 0.05)$ must be shorter than
$\tau_{\mathrm{drag}}$; at $T/T_c = 0.5$ this requires
$D_v \lesssim 10^{-9}\;\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$, which is achievable in
large-radius traps ($R \ge 50\;\mu\mathrm{m}$) at low temperature.
The $N = 8$ stability prediction (unstable alone, stable with $q_0 = 1$)
is less sensitive to dissipation because it concerns exponential growth
vs.\ decay, not oscillation frequency; finite temperature adds a damping
channel but does not change the sign of the eigenvalue.

\section{The three-scale selection mechanism}
\label{sec:three-scale}
\label{sec:two-scale}
\label{sec:extensions}

The physical picture is:

\medskip
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Scale} & \textbf{Question} & \textbf{Mechanism} \\
\midrule
Macro & Why do $N$ coherent vortices form? & Onsager + inverse cascade \\
Meso  & Which $N$ is selected? & Onsager max-$N$ (eq.~\eqref{eq:H-monotone}) \\
      &                        & \quad + Thomson upper bound \\
      &                        & \quad + Rossby $n^*$ (jets only) \\
Micro & Why regular polygon?   & Arnold/Thomson stability \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\medskip

The Onsager argument \citep{Onsager1949} operates at the macro scale:
the inverse cascade and negative-temperature statistics explain why
same-sign vorticity concentrates into $N$ coherent clusters.
\emph{This is a physical hypothesis for planetary atmospheres, not a
derived result, and is the key assumption that lifts $N = 8$ over
$N = 7$ in the Jupiter selection (Proposition~\ref{prop:onsager-n-select}).}
The \citet{Siegelman2022} simulations---2D turbulence on idealized
polar caps with stochastic forcing---are \emph{consistent with}
the Onsager hypothesis: they develop an inverse cascade, produce
$N = 8$ (north) and $N = 5$ (south) vortex crystals, and reach a
maximum-$H$ state within the Thomson-stable set.  However, these
simulations use 2D barotropic dynamics with prescribed forcing
statistics, not Jupiter's actual three-dimensional, moist-convective,
differentially rotating environment.  The agreement is therefore
evidence \emph{consistent with} the Onsager picture, not evidence
\emph{for} it in the strict sense.

Whether Jupiter's polar cyclones are actually in or near an Onsager
negative-temperature equilibrium---rather than being dynamically
maintained metastable states sustained by ongoing moist
convection---is an open empirical question.  A direct test would
require measuring the energy spectrum at the polar scale, which is
not yet available from Juno.

The polygon arrangement is then determined by the meso-scale energy
landscape: the regular $N$-gon minimizes the interaction energy at
constant angular momentum (Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}).
This is a deterministic stability result, not a statistical one.

\section{Wavenumber selection: the constraint-intersection principle}
\label{sec:constraint-intersection}

\begin{proposition}[Constraint-intersection selection]\label{prop:complementary}
The observed polygon wavenumber $N$ is determined by the intersection of
three constraints, all arising from the logarithmic Green's function:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Rossby stationarity} (for jet meanders): the QGPV
    eigenvalue problem uniquely selects the wavenumber $n^*$ of the
    stationary mode ($\omega = 0$).  For Saturn: leading-order
    $n^* \approx 5.4$, full Rayleigh--Kuo eigenvalue $n^* = 6$.
  \item \textbf{Thomson stability} (for discrete cyclone rings):
    $\kappa_0/\kappa \ge \kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(N)$ is a
    \emph{necessary} condition---an upper bound $N \le N_{\max}(\kappa_0/\kappa)$,
    not a unique selection.  The energy monotonicity $H_{N+1} > H_N$
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:onsager-n-select}) then identifies
    $N = N_{\max}$ as the unique maximum-energy stable configuration;
    any dynamics that preferentially populates higher-energy states
    selects this $N$.
  \item \textbf{Geometric packing} (for finite-size cyclones):
    $N$ non-overlapping cyclones of exclusion radius $r_{\mathrm{excl}}$
    on a ring of radius $R$ requires
    $\sin(\pi/N) \ge r_{\mathrm{excl}}/R$.
    The exclusion radius exceeds the cyclone core radius by a
    $\beta$-drift buffer zone \citep{Gavriel2021}:
    $r_{\mathrm{excl}} \approx 1.3\,r_{\mathrm{core}}$ for Jupiter's
    south pole.
\end{enumerate}
The selected wavenumber is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constraint-intersection}
  N_{\mathrm{selected}} = \max\bigl\{N :
    N \le N_{\mathrm{Thomson}},\;
    \sin(\pi/N) \ge r_{\mathrm{excl}}/R,\;
    n\;\text{Rossby-stationary}\bigr\},
\end{equation}
where inapplicable constraints are dropped (e.g.\ Rossby stationarity
does not apply to discrete cyclone rings, and packing does not constrain
jet meanders).
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Constraint-intersection principle]
\label{rem:constraint-intersection}
Each planetary system activates a different subset of the three
constraints in~\eqref{eq:constraint-intersection}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccl}
\toprule
System & $N_{\mathrm{Thomson}}$ & $N_{\mathrm{Rossby}}$ & $N_{\mathrm{pack}}$ & Binding \\
\midrule
Saturn (76${}^\circ$N)  & $7$ & $6$ & ---  & Rossby \\
Jupiter north           & $8$ & --- & $>8$ & Thomson \\
Jupiter south           & $8$ & --- & $5$  & Packing \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The framework is unified: all three constraints derive from the 2D
Laplacian~$\nabla^2$ and its modifications.  The packing constraint
is \emph{not} an external geometric condition: it is the domain
admissibility condition for the Green's function
$G_\varepsilon$ of~$\nabla^2$ on the punctured domain
$\Omega_N(\varepsilon) = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus
\bigcup_{k=1}^N B(z_k, \varepsilon)$, where $\varepsilon$ is the
vortex core radius.  This Green's function exists and defines a
well-posed BVP if and only if the excluded discs are pairwise disjoint:
\[
  |z_j - z_k| \ge 2\varepsilon
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \sin(\pi/N) \ge \varepsilon/R.
\]
The domain-admissibility condition establishes
$r_{\mathrm{excl}} \ge \varepsilon$ (the core radius) from the
Laplacian alone.  The observed enlargement
$r_{\mathrm{excl}} \approx 1.3\,\varepsilon$ at Jupiter's south pole
arises from the nonlinear $\beta$-drift balance of finite-amplitude
vortices on a rotating sphere \citep{Gavriel2021}; this factor is
\emph{not} derived from the linear operator $\nabla^2 + \beta\,\partial_y$
but is an empirical input from the full vortex dynamics.

The two Laplacian-derived facets and their observational supplement are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Spectral positivity} of $G(\nabla^2)$: Havelock
    eigenvalue $\lambda_m > 0$ $\to$ Thomson bound (derived);
  \item \emph{Domain admissibility} of $G_\varepsilon(\nabla^2)$:
    disc non-overlap $\sin(\pi/N) \ge \varepsilon/R$ $\to$ packing
    bound with $r_{\mathrm{excl}} = \varepsilon$ (derived);
    the enlargement $r_{\mathrm{excl}} \approx 1.3\,\varepsilon$
    at Jupiter south is observational;
  \item \emph{Rossby stationarity} of $(\nabla^2 + \beta\,\partial_y)\psi = 0$:
    stationary mode $n^*$ $\to$ wave quantization (derived).
\end{enumerate}
The finite-core regularisation $G_\varepsilon$ does \emph{not} create
a blob-stable window for $N = 8$: the corrected constrained Hessian
(with the full $\partial^2(-\tfrac12\ln s)/\partial x_j^2
= (2\,\Delta x^2 - s)/s^2$, $s = d^2 + 2\varepsilon^2$, not
the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ truncation) remains negative throughout
the admissible domain $\varepsilon/R < \sin(\pi/8)$.
The $N = 8$ selection therefore requires the central vortex
($\kappa_0/\kappa \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$); blob regularisation alone
does not provide an alternative stabilisation mechanism.
\end{remark}

For Saturn, $n^*$ is determined by the Rayleigh--Kuo condition for
stationary waves on a jet.  All quantities are evaluated in the frame
rotating with Saturn's System~III period---the frame co-rotating with
the deep interior.  In this frame the background wind at the hexagonal
jet is $U_{\max} \approx 120$\,m/s eastward.  At the center of a
Gaussian jet, the effective PV gradient is
$\beta_{\mathrm{eff}} = \beta + U_{\max}/\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}}^2$,
where the jet curvature term dominates bare $\beta$ by a factor
$\sim\!14$ for $\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} = 2.5 \times 10^6$\,m
\citep{Pedlosky1987, Vallis2017}.
Two estimates of $n^*$ are available:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Simple scaling} (independent of $\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}}$):
    $n^* = R_{\mathrm{hex}}\sqrt{\beta/U_{\max}} = 5.6$, which
    rounds to $n = 6$ for any $\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}}$.
  \item \emph{Leading-order with $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}$}:
    $n^* = R\cos\varphi\,\sqrt{\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}/U_{\max}}$,
    which gives $5.4$ at $\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} = 2.5 \times 10^6$\,m
    but is sensitive to the jet width ($n^* = 6.4$ at
    $\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} = 2.1 \times 10^6$\,m).
\end{itemize}
The full numerical Rayleigh--Kuo eigenvalue computation yields
$n^* = 6$ \citep{SanchezLavega2014}; the simple scaling reproduces
this robustly, while the $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ formula is sensitive
to $\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}}$ within its $\sim\!20\%$ observational
uncertainty.  We adopt
$\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} = 2.5 \times 10^6$\,m throughout, consistent
with the Cassini jet profile measurements; all conclusions are
verified to hold over the range
$\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} \in [2.0, 3.0] \times 10^6$\,m.
Thomson allows $N \le 7$; both criteria are satisfied at $N = 6$.

For Jupiter's north pole ($N = 8$): the cyclones are discrete vortex
clusters, not a jet meander; Rossby stationarity gives
$n^* \approx 0.3$ (not physically relevant).  \emph{Caveat:} the 2D
point vortex model requires the cyclone roots to extend through a
barotropic column much deeper than the ring radius.  Juno gravity
data constrains Jupiter's differential rotation to
$\sim\!3{,}000$\,km depth \citep{Kaspi2018}, roughly $4\%$ of
$R_J$---making Jupiter the least observationally supported case for
the 2D assumption among our applications.  The model is nevertheless
applicable if the polar cyclones are confined to a deep weather layer
($H_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim 3{,}000$\,km, $R_d \gg R$) or to a shallow
tropospheric layer with reduced gravity; both scenarios are consistent
with the Juno data (see Section~\ref{sec:deformation} for
quantitative limits).

Thomson stability provides an \emph{upper bound}:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(9) = 1 > \kappa_0/\kappa$,
so $N = 9$ is unstable; $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(8) = \tfrac{1}{2}
\le \kappa_0/\kappa$, so $N = 8$ is stable \citep{Adriani2018}.
But $N = 7$ is also stable ($\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(7) = 0$), so
stability alone does not select $N = 8$ over $N = 7$.
The selection requires one additional ingredient: a reason for the
system to prefer higher-energy configurations among the stable set.

\begin{proposition}[Energy monotonicity and maximum-$N$ selection]\label{prop:onsager-n-select}
Let $\Gamma = N\kappa$ be the total ring circulation, $L = N\kappa R^2$
the angular impulse (so $R$ is fixed when $\Gamma$ and $L$ are fixed),
and $r = \kappa_0/\kappa$ held fixed as $N$ varies (both the ring and
central circulations scale proportionally).  Denote by
$r_{\mathrm{core}}$ the vortex core radius---the minimum separation at
which the point-vortex model is valid; equivalently, $r_{\mathrm{core}}$
is the unit of length in which the logarithm is taken, so that
$\ln R > 0$ iff $R > r_{\mathrm{core}}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-monotone}
  H_{N+1} - H_N = \Gamma^2\!\left[\frac{2r-1}{2N(N+1)}\ln R
    + \frac12\!\left(\frac{\ln N}{N} - \frac{\ln(N+1)}{N+1}\right)\right].
\end{equation}
For $r \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$ and $R > r_{\mathrm{core}}$, both terms
are non-negative (first: $2r - 1 \ge 0$ and $\ln R > 0$; second:
$\ln N/N$ is strictly decreasing for $N > e \approx 2.7$), so
$H_{N+1} > H_N$ for all $N \ge 3$.  Therefore the $N$-gon ring
energy is strictly monotone in $N$: the maximum-$H$ configuration
within the Thomson-stable set is the ring with the largest $N$:
\[
  N_{\max}(\kappa_0/\kappa)
    \equiv \max\bigl\{N' : \kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(N') \le \kappa_0/\kappa\bigr\}.
\]
Any dynamics that preferentially populates higher-energy stable
configurations---whether Onsager negative-temperature equilibrium
($\beta < 0$, where the Boltzmann weight $e^{-\beta H}$ increases
with $H$), sequential vortex injection (adding cyclones until
Thomson instability prevents $N{+}1$; this requires fixed~$r$,
see Remark~\ref{rem:fixed-r}), or merger of smaller vortex
clusters---selects $N = N_{\max}$.  The mathematical content of the
proposition is the monotonicity $H_{N+1} > H_N$; the physical
mechanism by which the system reaches $N_{\max}$ is a separate
question (see the discussion below and Section~\ref{sec:extensions}).

The stability threshold $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(8) = \tfrac{1}{2}$ is
exactly the value at which $H_{N+1} > H_N$ becomes unconditional (i.e.\
$R$-independent): at $r = \tfrac{1}{2}$, term~1 vanishes identically
and term~2 remains strictly positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the Hamiltonian
$H = -\kappa^2\!\sum_{j<k}\ln|z_j - z_k| - \kappa_0\kappa\!\sum_j\ln|z_j|$
with $\kappa = \Gamma/N$ and $\kappa_0 = r\kappa = r\Gamma/N$.

\emph{Step 1: Product formula.}
For the regular $N$-gon $z_j = Re^{2\pi ij/N}$, the chord length
between vortices $j$ and $k$ is $|z_j - z_k| = 2R\sin(\pi|j-k|/N)$.
By $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry the pair sum equals $N/2$ times the sum from
one vertex:
\[
  \sum_{j<k}\ln|z_j - z_k|
  = \frac{N}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}\ln\!\bigl(2R\sin(\pi s/N)\bigr)
  = \frac{N}{2}\bigl[\ln N + (N-1)\ln R\bigr].
\]
Here we used $\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}\ln(2\sin(\pi s/N)) = \ln N$, which
follows from evaluating the polynomial identity $(z^N-1)/(z-1) =
\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}(z - e^{2\pi ik/N})$ at $z = 1$: the product of
moduli is $\prod_{s=1}^{N-1}|1 - e^{2\pi is/N}|
= \prod_{s=1}^{N-1}2\sin(\pi s/N) = N$.

\emph{Step 2: Central vortex term.}
Since $|z_j| = R$ for all $j$: $\sum_j\ln|z_j| = N\ln R$.

\emph{Step 3: Closed form for $H_N$.}
Substituting Steps~1--2 with $\kappa = \Gamma/N$, $\kappa_0 = r\Gamma/N$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HN-explicit}
  H_N = -\frac{\Gamma^2}{2N}\ln N
        - \Gamma^2\!\left(\frac{N-1}{2N} + \frac{r}{N}\right)\ln R.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 4: Difference $H_{N+1} - H_N$.}
Writing $H_{N+1}$ from \eqref{eq:HN-explicit} with $N\to N+1$ and
subtracting:
\begin{align*}
  H_{N+1} - H_N
  &= -\frac{\Gamma^2}{2}\!\left[\frac{\ln(N{+}1)}{N{+}1}
     - \frac{\ln N}{N}\right]
     - \Gamma^2\ln R\!\left[\frac{N/2+r}{N{+}1}
     - \frac{(N{-}1)/2+r}{N}\right].
\end{align*}
The coefficient of $\ln R$ simplifies by partial fractions:
\[
  \frac{N/2+r}{N{+}1} - \frac{(N{-}1)/2+r}{N}
  = \frac{N^2 - (N{-}1)(N{+}1)}{2N(N{+}1)} - \frac{r}{N(N{+}1)}
  = \frac{1-2r}{2N(N{+}1)},
\]
where we used $N^2-(N-1)(N+1) = 1$.  Negating and rearranging
gives~\eqref{eq:H-monotone}.

\emph{Step 5: Sign of each term.}
\emph{Term~2}: $f(x) = \ln x/x$ satisfies $f'(x) = (1-\ln x)/x^2 < 0$
for $x > e$, so $\ln N/N > \ln(N+1)/(N+1)$ for all $N \ge 3$.

\emph{Term~1}: $2r-1 \ge 0$ for $r \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$; and
$\ln R > 0$ for $R > r_{\mathrm{core}}$.  Hence both terms are
non-negative, and term~2 is strictly positive, giving
$H_{N+1} > H_N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Physical status of the fixed-$r$ assumption]
\label{rem:fixed-r}
The assumption $r = \kappa_0/\kappa$ fixed as $N$ varies means that
as more cyclones appear in the ring ($N$ increases), each individual
ring vortex becomes weaker ($\kappa = \Gamma/N$) \emph{and the central
vortex weakens proportionally} ($\kappa_0 = r\Gamma/N$).  This is
non-trivial: the central polar cyclone is a distinct dynamical object
from the ring cyclones and need not respond to changes in $N$.

The assumption is justified when all vortices --- ring and central ---
are drawn from the same turbulent condensation process, so their
strengths are set by a common large-scale energy budget rather than
by independent forcings.  In the Onsager picture, the negative-temperature
state is reached by the inverse cascade from the same vorticity
reservoir, giving a fixed partition of total circulation between center
and ring.  If instead $\kappa_0$ is held fixed while $\kappa = \Gamma/N$
varies (independent central vortex), then $r = N\kappa_0/\Gamma$ grows
with $N$ and the monotonicity of $H_{N+1} > H_N$ strengthens further,
so the maximum-$N$ selection remains qualitatively intact.  The
marginal case is $\kappa_0 = 0$ (no central vortex): then
$r = 0 < \tfrac12$ and Term~1 in~\eqref{eq:H-monotone} is
\emph{negative}, so $H_{N+1} > H_N$ requires $R$ large enough that
Term~2 dominates.  The proposition's $r \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$ condition
is therefore specific to the regime in which the central vortex is
sufficiently strong relative to the ring vortices.

The selection of $N = 8$ over $N = 7$ is sensitive to which assumption
is made.  Under fixed~$r$: $H_{N+1} > H_N$ for all $N \ge 3$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:onsager-n-select}), so Onsager selects the
Thomson-maximum $N_{\max}$.  Under fixed~$\kappa_0$: $r$ grows with
$N$, which strengthens the monotonicity and gives the same $N_{\max}$.
Under the intermediate assumption $\kappa_0 = \mathrm{const}$ while
$\Gamma$ also varies: the result depends on how $\Gamma(N)$ scales,
which is not observationally constrained.  Thus the $N = 8$
selection is robust across the two limiting assumptions (fixed~$r$
and fixed~$\kappa_0$) but relies on the Onsager framework providing
the link between $N$, $\Gamma$, and $\kappa_0$---a physical
hypothesis, not a mathematical consequence.

Observationally, Jupiter's central cyclone differs from the ring
cyclones: it has a smaller diameter
($\sim\!4{,}000$\,km vs.\ $4{,}000$--$7{,}000$\,km;
\citealt{Adriani2018}) and appears more axisymmetric.  Whether it
formed from the same inverse-cascade process as the ring cyclones,
or was generated by a distinct polar convective mechanism, is not
resolved by current Juno data.
\end{remark}

\noindent
For Jupiter north, $\kappa_0/\kappa \approx 1$ with roughly 30\%
observational uncertainty (Juno; \citealt{Adriani2018}), giving a
conservative range $\kappa_0/\kappa \in [0.7, 1.3]$.  Since
$\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(8) = \tfrac{1}{2}$ exactly, this entire
range satisfies the stability condition for $N = 8$.  However,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(9) = 1$, which lies \emph{inside} the
uncertainty interval: if $\kappa_0/\kappa \ge 1$ (the upper half
of the range), then $N = 9$ is also Thomson-stable and the Onsager
argument would select $N_{\max} = 9$ instead.  The conclusion
$N_{\max} = 8$ therefore relies on $\kappa_0/\kappa < 1$, which is
consistent with the central estimate but not guaranteed by the
current observational precision.  More precisely:
$\kappa_0/\kappa \le 1$ gives $N_{\max} = 8$ (observed);
$\kappa_0/\kappa > 1$ gives $N_{\max} = 9$ (not observed).  The
observed $N = 8$ is thus evidence that $\kappa_0/\kappa \lesssim 1$,
providing a theory-derived constraint on the central vortex
strength that could be tested with improved Juno wind
measurements.

\label{sec:n-selection}%
For Jupiter's south pole ($N = 5$): $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(N) = 0$
for all $N \le 7$, so the Thomson bound alone allows up to $N = 7$
regardless of the central vortex.  The observed $N = 5$ is selected
by the geometric packing constraint
(Proposition~\ref{prop:complementary}, item~3).
South-pole cyclones are individually $\approx 2\times$ larger in
linear size than north-pole cyclones ($r_{\mathrm{core}} \approx
3{,}500$\,km vs.\ $2{,}500$\,km; \citealt{Adriani2018}), and each
cyclone carries a $\beta$-drift exclusion zone that extends $\approx
30\%$ beyond the core radius \citep{Gavriel2021}, giving
$r_{\mathrm{excl}} \approx 1.3\,r_{\mathrm{core}} \approx 4{,}550$\,km.
At the south-pole ring radius $R \approx 8{,}000$\,km
($\sim\!7^{\circ}$ from the pole):
\[
  \sin(\pi/5) = 0.588 \ge \frac{r_{\mathrm{excl}}}{R}
  = \frac{4{,}550}{8{,}000} = 0.569,
  \qquad
  \sin(\pi/6) = 0.500 < 0.569.
\]
Thus $N_{\mathrm{pack}} = 5$, which is tighter than the Thomson bound
$N_{\mathrm{Thomson}} = 8$: packing, not stability, is the binding
constraint at Jupiter's south pole.  The $\approx 30\%$ excess of
$r_{\mathrm{excl}}$ over the bare cyclone radius is physically the
$\beta$-drift buffer zone identified by \citet{Gavriel2021}---cyclone
repulsion on a rotating sphere creates an effective ``personal space''
larger than the vortex itself.

\subsection{Eigenvalue branch disconnection (Saturn persistence)}

The preceding subsection selected Saturn's wavenumber $n = 6$ via
Rossby stationarity.  A natural follow-up: can smooth perturbations
of the jet destroy the stationary wave, converting the hexagonal
meander into an evanescent (decaying) mode?  The answer is no, by
a WKB branch-disconnection argument.

The Rayleigh--Kuo equation for stationary ($c = 0$) mode~$n$ on a
zonal jet $U(y)$ reads $\hat\psi'' + V(\rho)\hat\psi = 0$, where
$V = (\beta - U'')/U - n^2/R^2$ is the effective potential in
log-polar coordinates.  At the jet center ($y = 0$) of a Gaussian jet:
\[
  V(0) = \frac{\beta + U_{\max}/\sigma^2}{U_{\max}} - \frac{n^2}{R^2}
  = \frac{\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}}{U_{\max}} - \frac{n^2}{R^2}.
\]
For Saturn ($U_{\max} = 120\;\mathrm{m/s}$, $\sigma = 2.5\times
10^6\;\mathrm{m}$, $\beta = 1.46\times 10^{-12}\;\mathrm{m^{-1}
s^{-1}}$, $n = 6$, $R = R_{\mathrm{S}}\cos 76^\circ = 1.31\times
10^7\;\mathrm{m}$):
$V(0) = 1.72\times 10^{-13} - 2.10\times 10^{-13} =
-3.8\times 10^{-14}\;\mathrm{m}^{-2}$ (negative).
The sign of $V(0)$ depends on $\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}}$:
$V(0) < 0$ for $\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} \gtrsim 2.3 \times 10^6$\,m,
$V(0) > 0$ for narrower jets.  At the adopted
$\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} = 2.5 \times 10^6$\,m, $V(0) < 0$ with
$|V(0)|/V_{\mathrm{threshold}} = 1.6$ (a $60\%$ margin above the
sign change); this margin grows to $3.4\times$ at
$\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} = 3.0 \times 10^6$\,m.  The Cassini
jet profile measurements constrain
$\sigma_{\mathrm{jet}} \in [2.3, 3.0] \times 10^6$\,m, placing
$V(0)$ robustly in the negative regime.

Since $V < 0$, the WKB exponent $s^2 = V < 0$ forces oscillatory
solutions with $s = i\alpha_\rho$ ($\alpha_\rho \ne 0$).  Writing
$s = \sigma + i\alpha_\rho$:
$\operatorname{Im}(s^2) = 2\sigma\alpha_\rho = 0$.
When $\alpha_\rho \ne 0$ (oscillatory region): $\sigma = 0$.
The elliptic ($\sigma = 0$) and hyperbolic ($\alpha_\rho = 0$)
branches are disconnected: smooth jet perturbations cannot move the
solution from the oscillatory branch to the evanescent branch at
leading WKB order.  This is a structural stability result for the
Saturn selection mechanism: the $n = 6$ stationary wave exists
because $V(0) < 0$, and no continuous deformation of the jet profile
can remove it without first passing through $V(0) = 0$ (which
requires changing the sign of $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}/U_{\max} -
n^2/R^2$, a finite perturbation).  The hexagon is therefore
structurally stable at leading WKB order: it cannot be removed by
infinitesimal jet perturbations.

\subsection{Polygon quality metrics and cross-planetary comparison}

The two planetary polygon types require different quality metrics.
For \emph{discrete vortex rings} (Jupiter), the natural measure is the
geometric deviation from a perfect $N$-gon:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-geom}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{geom}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}
  \bigl|\!\ln|z_k / z_k^{\mathrm{ideal}}|\bigr|,
\end{equation}
where $z_k^{\mathrm{ideal}}$ are the best-fit regular $N$-gon vertices.
This measures positional scatter of discrete cyclones; small
$\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}}$ means the ring closely approximates the
Thomson equilibrium.

For \emph{continuous jet meanders} (Saturn), the vortex positions $z_k$
are not defined.  The appropriate measure is the fractional radial
displacement of the jet center:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps-obs}
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{obs}} = A_n / \bar{R},
\end{equation}
where $A_n$ is the half-amplitude of the $n$-th Fourier mode of the
jet-center radial position and $\bar{R}$ is the mean ring radius.
For Saturn's hexagon ($n = 6$, $\bar{R} \approx 13{,}200\;\mathrm{km}$),
the observed $\pm 2^\circ$ latitude oscillation
\citep{Godfrey1988, Antunano2015} gives
$A_6 \approx 1{,}900\;\mathrm{km}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{obs}} \approx 0.14$ (range $0.11$--$0.18$ across
measurement epochs).

These two metrics are \emph{not interchangeable}: applying
$\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}}$ to Saturn (by treating the meander peaks as
point positions) yields $0.50$, an order of magnitude larger than
Jupiter's $0.05$--$0.06$, but this comparison is physically
meaningless---it conflates meander amplitude with positional scatter.
Table~\ref{tab:planets} reports both metrics, using each where it is
appropriate.

\subsection{Broader observational context}

Beyond the three planetary configurations, the theoretical stability
boundary $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}(N)$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-crit} can be compared with laboratory and
numerical observations:

\emph{Laboratory experiments.}  \citet{Jansson2006} and
\citet{BarbosaAguiar2010} observe polygons with $N = 2$ through $6$
in rotating-tank experiments by varying the differential rotation rate.
All observed $N$ values fall in the $\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0$ range
($N \le 7$), consistent with the Thomson stability boundary.  No
laboratory experiment has produced a stable polygon with $N \ge 8$
without a central vortex---also consistent.  (The laboratory mechanism
is barotropic instability of a shear layer, not Thomson point vortex
dynamics, so the agreement is structural rather than quantitative.)

\emph{Numerical simulations.}  \citet{Siegelman2022} produce $N = 8$
(north) and $N = 5$ (south) vortex crystals from 2D turbulence on polar
caps, with a central polar cyclone emerging alongside the ring in both
cases.  The central cyclone provides the stabilization required by
Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-crit} for $N = 8$.
The \citet{Siegelman2022} simulations include continuous small-scale
forcing (representing convective injection) and large-scale dissipation,
yet the polygon crystal is the persistent stationary state: the system
is not frozen into a one-time equilibrium but continuously re-attracted
to it.  Within the 2D barotropic framework, this is numerical evidence
that the constrained energy minimum acts as a \emph{dynamical attractor}
under turbulent forcing.  The caveat (Section~\ref{sec:extensions}) is
that these are idealized 2D simulations, not a faithful model of
Jupiter's three-dimensional polar dynamics; the agreement supports the
Onsager picture but does not constitute a proof of its applicability.

\emph{Uranus.}  \citet{Akins2023} detect a single polar cyclone at
Uranus's north pole---a single-vortex ($N = 1$) configuration, below
the polygon threshold.  No ring structure has been observed.  Whether
the absence of a Uranian polygon reflects insufficient vortex formation
(macro-scale constraint) or insufficient observations (the pole has been
poorly resolved) remains open.

\emph{Neptune.}  No polar polygon has been observed, and Voyager~2
imagery is insufficient to resolve ring structure.  However, the
framework makes a falsifiable prediction.

\textit{Regime:}
At $84^\circ$N, $\beta_N = 2\Omega_N\cos(84^\circ)/R_N \approx
9.2\times10^{-13}$\,m$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$ and $R_{\mathrm{ring}} \approx
2{,}570$\,km, giving $\beta R^3 \approx 1.6\times10^7$\,m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$.
For Jovian-scaled cyclones (core radius $r_c \approx 240$\,km,
$V_{\max}\approx 80$--100\,m\,s$^{-1}$), $\kappa \approx
1.2\text{--}1.5\times10^8$\,m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$ and
\[
  \frac{\beta R^3}{\kappa}\approx 0.10\text{--}0.13 \ll 1.
\]
Neptune is firmly vortex-dominated---more so than Jupiter ($\beta
R^3/\kappa \approx 0.6$), because its smaller radius ($R_N \approx
R_J/3$) reduces $R_{\mathrm{ring}}^3$ by a factor of $\sim\!25$
despite a slightly larger $\beta$.  The Rossby stationarity mechanism
does not apply; the relevant bridge is Thomson/Onsager.

The first-principles chain (Section~\ref{sec:first-principles})
narrows the Neptune prediction substantially.  Neptune's polar
ring radius is $R_{\mathrm{ring}} \approx 3{,}000$\,km
($R_N \sin 7^\circ$), which is $2.9\times$ smaller than Jupiter's.
Its Coriolis parameter at~$83^\circ$ is $f_N = 2.15\times
10^{-4}\;\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, only $0.62\times$ Jupiter's.
Consequently, the deformation radius
$R_d = \sqrt{g' H_{\mathrm{eff}}}/f$ is $1.6\times$ larger than
Jupiter's for the same atmospheric structure, and the packing ratio
$\varepsilon/R = R_d/R_{\mathrm{ring}}$ is $4.7\times$ tighter:

Neptune's strong internal heat flux ($F_{\mathrm{int}} = 0.43\;
\mathrm{W\,m}^{-2}$, $2.6\times$ absorbed solar) suggests vigorous
convection, favouring $R_d \gtrsim 1{,}000$\,km.  In this regime
\textbf{packing is the binding constraint}, and the prediction
narrows to $N \in \{3, 4, 5\}$---a triangle, square, or pentagon.

The physical reason is geometric: Neptune's ring radius is too small
for many cyclones of Jupiter-like size to fit.  The $4.7\times$
tighter packing ratio (from the combination of smaller planet and
weaker Coriolis) means that even moderate-size cyclones crowd the
ring.  This is a qualitative difference from Jupiter north, where
Thomson (not packing) is the binding constraint.

This prediction is testable by a Neptune polar orbiter (e.g.\
Trident-class mission): imaging the polar region at
$\sim\!100$\,km resolution would resolve individual cyclones and
determine $N$ directly.

All observations are consistent with the theoretical stability
boundary: $N \le 7$ is observed without a central vortex, $N = 8$
is observed only with one, and no $N \ge 9$ ring has been reported
in any system.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-new}

\subsection{Why 2D and not 3D}

The argument depends critically on two-dimensionality.  The 2D Green's
function $G(r) = -\ln r/(2\pi)$ is the unique dilation-invariant
pairwise interaction with $h' < 0$
(Remark~\ref{rem:rg-fixed-point}; Corollary~\ref{cor:dilation-rg}
lifts this to the $N$-body level).  In 3D, the Green's function
$G(r) = -1/(4\pi r)$ has $G'(r) = 1/(4\pi r^2) > 0$---the sign
rule is violated, the $N$-gon is not an energy extremum along the
spiral, and the stability analysis gives different results.  The
present mechanism---sign rule plus energy-Casimir stability---does
not operate in 3D; whether other mechanisms can produce persistent
polygonal vortex structures in three dimensions is a separate
question.


\subsection{What is novel}

The individual components---Onsager condensation, Kraichnan cascades,
Thomson stability, 2D Green's functions---are established.

\noindent\textbf{Important attribution note.}
The Havelock eigenvalue formula $\lambda_m = (N{-}1) - m(N{-}m)/2$ and
the linear stability boundary $N \le 7$ appear in \citet{Havelock1931},
\S3, and have been known to the classical vortex dynamics community for
nearly a century.  Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min} is therefore
\emph{not} new to hydrodynamicists.  Its contribution is to the
\emph{statistical mechanics community}, where the polygon has been
treated as an energy maximum selected by Onsager negative temperature
\citep{Onsager1949, BouchetVenaille2012}: the theorem demonstrates that
the polygon is an energy \emph{minimum} on the conserved-quantity
surface, which is the opposite of the common assumption and changes the
statistical interpretation substantially.  Specialists in classical
vortex dynamics who encounter Theorem~3 should read it as a bridge
result, translating a known stability fact into the language of
energy-Casimir minima.

What is new is:
(1)~identifying the $N$-gon as a \emph{constrained energy minimum}
(not maximum) on the conserved surface and deriving this from the
Lagrange-multiplier structure (new to the Onsager literature;
classical to Thomson--Havelock);
(2)~the analytic sign rule
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-rule}) proving $H''(0) < 0$ for all
interactions with $h' < 0$ and $(rh')' \le 0$;
(3)~the three-scale separation showing the Onsager argument applies
to cluster \emph{formation} while the polygon \emph{arrangement}
is deterministic stability;
(4)~the constraint-intersection framework unifying Saturn (Rossby
stationarity), Jupiter north (Thomson + Onsager), and Jupiter south
(geometric packing from domain admissibility);
(5)~the observation that the logarithmic interaction's borderline
status in the sign rule makes it the ``least robust'' polygon
producer---any other qualifying interaction gives stronger selection;
(6)~the spherical stability boundary
$\varphi_{\mathrm{crit}}(N)$ showing the sphere \emph{destabilizes}
relative to the plane (the Lagrange shift vanishes at the equator),
with Saturn's hexagon inside the stable zone, and the exact rational
thresholds $\xi_{\mathrm{crit}}(N) \in \mathbb{Q}$
(equation~\eqref{eq:xi_crit_S2}) with the geometric identity
$\cos\varphi_{\mathrm{crit}} = m(N{-}m)/(2(N-1))$;
(7)~the $K_0$ suppression transition with two distinct phase
boundaries (spiral and constrained) and the class~(iii) interaction
landscape; (8)~the vortex blob bridge showing regularization
preserves and enhances stability, resolving the $N = 7$ marginal
case, with profile independence via a Fourier dominance argument
(Section~\ref{sec:extensions});
(9)~the algebraic field extension pattern
$\xi^*(N) \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\mathrm{sq\_free}(N-1)})$ for even $N$
and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\mathrm{sq\_free}(N-3)})$ for odd $N \ge 9$,
with $N = 10$ as the unique rational threshold $\xi^*(10) = 1/7$
(Section~\ref{sec:hyperbolic}, equation~\eqref{eq:field-pattern}); and
(10)~the circulation disorder resilience analysis showing that the
$N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ boundary is a stability domain rather than a
knife-edge, with the $N = 7$ marginal ring identified as the unique
disorder-sensitive configuration (Section~\ref{sec:disorder}).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Relation to prior work.}
The connection between Onsager statistics and large-scale vortex
formation is developed in \citet{BouchetVenaille2012} and
\citet{ChavanisSommeria1996}, who classify self-organized vortex states
in bounded domains using statistical mechanical methods.
\citet{Aref2003} reviews vortex crystal configurations and their
stability.  The present contribution differs in three respects: (i)~the
closed-form formula for $H''(0)$ (equation~\eqref{eq:Hpp}) does not
appear in these reviews, which analyze stability through eigenvalues
of the dynamical matrix rather than energy curvature along a specific
deformation (the Thomson--Havelock literature has this eigenvalue
result, but not via the energy-curvature path);
(ii)~the analytic sign rule (Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-rule}) identifying
the precise conditions on $h$ that guarantee $H''(0) < 0$ is new;
and (iii)~the constrained Hessian result
(Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}) clarifies a point of frequent
confusion \emph{in the statistical mechanics literature}: the polygon
is described as an energy maximum selected by negative temperature
\citep{BouchetVenaille2012,ChavanisSommeria1996}, but it is in fact
a constrained energy \emph{minimum} on the conserved-quantity surface.
These two descriptions are compatible---the Onsager argument concerns
the unconstrained landscape while the stability argument concerns the
constrained surface---but the unqualified ``energy maximum'' language
obscures the Lyapunov stability mechanism.  This clarification
is not new to the classical vortex dynamics literature
\citep{Thomson1883,Havelock1931}, where the stability boundary is
well established; it is new as a bridge to the statistical mechanics
framing.


\subsection{Limitations and scope}

The mathematical results---the constrained energy minimum
(Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}), the sign rule
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-rule}), and the stability table---are proven
for discrete point vortices.  They apply directly to Jupiter's polar
cyclone rings.  For Saturn's hexagon, which is a continuous jet meander,
the relevant mechanism is Rossby wave stationarity of the QGPV
equation, not the point vortex energy landscape.  The continuous and
discrete descriptions share the logarithmic Green's function; the
vortex blob analysis (Section~\ref{sec:blob}) establishes that the
stability boundary is preserved under regularization and that the
$N = 7$ marginal case is resolved.

The Saturn bridge (Section~\ref{sec:rossby-bridge}) connects the
continuous QGPV eigenvalue problem to the point vortex Hessian via
critical-layer concentration.  The key mechanism is
$\mathbb{Z}_N$~symmetry: for any $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric cat's-eye
structure, the lobe circulations are equal and the centroids lie on
the $N$-gon vertices, so the constrained Hessian has the same inertia
as the Havelock Hessian (Remark~\ref{rem:catseye}).  This is verified
numerically for both smooth (Stuart vortex) and step-PV
(Prandtl--Batchelor) cat's-eye models.  The braid vorticity fraction
vanishes as $O(1{-}\eta)$ in the Stuart concentration limit
(Remark~\ref{rem:catseye}), satisfying the conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:inertia}.

The quantitative bridge at Saturn's actual parameters can be closed
as follows.  The relevant concentration parameter is the
\emph{physical} jet width divided by the hexagon radius:
$\varepsilon/R = \sigma_{\mathrm{jet}}/R_{\mathrm{hex}}
= 2.5 \times 10^6/(5.1 \times 10^7) = 0.049$,
not the log-polar width $\sigma_{\mathrm{lp}} \approx 0.15$
sometimes quoted.  With $d_{\min} = 2\sin(\pi/6) = 1$, the
concentration condition~\eqref{eq:concentration-bound} requires
$\varepsilon/d_{\min} < \delta(6) = 0.096$.  The physical ratio
$0.049 < 0.096$ satisfies this condition.
The Weyl perturbation bound gives
$\|\nabla^2\!H_\varepsilon - \nabla^2\!H_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le 54\varepsilon^2 = 0.13$, which is $26\%$ of the spectral gap
$\lambda_{\min}(6) = 1/2$---absorbed with a $3.9\times$ margin.
The actual $O(\varepsilon^2)$ blob correction is \emph{stabilizing}
($c_6 = 13/12 > 0$, shifting $\lambda_{\min}$ upward by $+0.003$),
and the separatrix vorticity sheet contributes $O(\varepsilon^4)$
to the eigenvalue (quadrupole coupling of a thin dipole layer),
negligible compared to the gap.  Saturn's finite jet width therefore
\emph{strengthens}, not weakens, the $N = 6$ stability.

The Saturn hexagon is therefore supported by three arguments:
(i)~Rossby stationarity (Proposition~\ref{prop:complementary}) selects
$n = 6$ independently of the energy landscape;
(ii)~geometric consistency with the spherical stability boundary
(Section~\ref{sec:curved}); and
(iii)~the critical-layer bridge (Remark~\ref{rem:catseye}), which
establishes Hessian inertia preservation for $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric
cat's-eye structures---now verified quantitatively at Saturn's
physical parameters ($\varepsilon/d_{\min} = 0.049 < \delta(6) = 0.096$,
Weyl perturbation $26\%$ of spectral gap).  All three arguments
converge on $N = 6$.

The Onsager negative-temperature hypothesis is a physical assumption
whose applicability to Jupiter's polar region is not directly tested
here.  It is the key step that distinguishes $N = 8$ from $N = 7$
in the selection argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:onsager-n-select}):
without negative temperature, the framework identifies $N = 8$ as
\emph{stable} but does not uniquely select it over $N = 7$.

The \citet{Siegelman2022} simulations provide the most direct evidence
available.  Their 2D turbulence on polar caps develops an inverse energy
cascade that drives kinetic energy to the largest scales consistent with
the polar geometry---the signature of a Onsager negative-temperature
state.  The polygon crystal is the maximum-$H$ configuration within
the Thomson-stable set: starting from random initial conditions, the
simulations converge to the polygon ring with a central cyclone, which
is the $N_{\max}$ configuration under Proposition~\ref{prop:onsager-n-select}
(they observe $N = 8$ north and $N = 5$ south, matching the Juno
observations; the south-pole discrepancy from the Thomson/Onsager
prediction is attributed to geometric packing as discussed above).
This is indirect but consistent evidence that the planetary system is
near negative temperature.  A direct test would require measuring the
energy spectrum at the polar scale in Juno or a dedicated mission.

Cassini and Juno data are digitized from published figures.

\emph{Dynamical maintenance.}%
\label{sec:dynamical-maintenance}
The constrained energy minimum (Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-min}) is
a static result: it identifies the polygon as the lowest-energy
arrangement on the conserved-quantity surface, and by Arnold's energy-Casimir
stability theorem \citep{Arnold1966} this minimum is Lyapunov stable
under the conservative 2D Euler dynamics---perturbations stay bounded.
Whether this translates into an attractor under convective forcing
depends on the ratio $\mathcal{R} = \tau_{\mathrm{inject}}/\tau_{\mathrm{restore}}$.

For Jupiter's $N=8$ ring (Juno parameters: $R_{\mathrm{ring}}\approx
7{,}500\;\mathrm{km}$, $\kappa_0/\kappa \approx 1$;
\citealt{Adriani2018}), we use the full-cyclone circulation
$\kappa = 2\pi r_c V_{\max} \approx 1.6\times10^9\;\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$
with $r_c\approx3{,}200$\,km (the full Juno-observed cyclone radius)
and $V_{\max}\approx80$\,m\,s$^{-1}$.  This is the appropriate measure
of total vortex strength for computing orbital periods and eigenfrequencies.
It differs from the Rankine-core estimate
$\kappa_{\mathrm{Rank}}\approx3.5\times10^8$\,m$^2$\,s$^{-1}$
(using only $r_c\approx700$\,km, the solid-body rotation region)
that enters the regime number $\beta R^3/\kappa$ in
Section~\ref{sec:extensions}: the regime criterion requires only a
characteristic vorticity scale, for which the core is sufficient.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Restoring:} The ring orbital period is $T_{\mathrm{orb}} =
  8\pi^2 R^2/[\kappa(N{-}1)] \approx 4.6\;\mathrm{days}$.
  The most marginal mode ($m=4$) has Lagrangian eigenvalue
  $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 3$ (bare eigenvalue $-1$, raised to
  $\approx+3$ by the central cyclone with $\kappa_0/\kappa=1$).
  The oscillation e-folding time is
  $\tau_{\mathrm{restore}} = (\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\Omega)^{-1}
  \approx 0.4\;\mathrm{days}$.

\item \emph{Injection:} The upper-bound convective power into one
  cyclone's footprint is $P = F_{\mathrm{int}}\,\pi r_c^2$, where
  $F_{\mathrm{int}} \approx 7.5\;\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ is Jupiter's
  interior heat flux \citep{Guillot2005}; with $r_c = 700\;\mathrm{km}$,
  $P \approx 1.15\times10^{13}\;\mathrm{W}$.
  The tropospheric kinetic energy per cyclone is estimated as
  $E_K \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\rho V_{\max}^2\,\pi r_c^2 H$
  with $\rho \approx 0.2\;\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $H \approx 40\;\mathrm{km}$,
  $V_{\max} \approx 80\;\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$:
  $E_K \approx 4\times 10^{19}\;\mathrm{J}$, giving
  $\tau_{\mathrm{inject}} = E_K/P \approx 40\;\mathrm{days}$.
\end{itemize}
The ratio $\mathcal{R} \approx 40/0.4 \approx 100$: the restoring force
operates $\sim\!100\times$ faster than the upper-bound convective
injection.  Including the inverse-cascade efficiency (typically
$\lesssim 1\%$ of the heat flux reaches cyclone scales) raises
$\mathcal{R}$ to order $10^4$.  The Juno four-year observational
baseline provides a direct empirical lower bound:
$\mathcal{R} \gtrsim 4\;\mathrm{yr}/0.4\;\mathrm{days} \approx 3500$.
The polygon is firmly in the restoring-dominated regime; convective
forcing is a persistent perturbation that the energy minimum absorbs,
not a mechanism that competes with the minimum for selection of the
configuration.

\emph{Moist convection as vorticity source.}
The leading mechanism proposed for maintaining Jupiter's polar cyclones
in the observational literature \citep{ONeill2015} is moist convective
plumes injecting cyclonic vorticity preferentially at high latitudes.
The geometric argument is that near the pole, the $\beta$-effect
preferentially suppresses anticyclonic plumes (their vorticity anomaly
is opposed by the planetary background), while cyclonic plumes are
unimpeded; the result is a net positive-vorticity injection into the
polar cap.  This mechanism is quite different from---and more specific
than---the Onsager inverse-cascade picture invoked here.

The two mechanisms are complementary rather than competing, operating
at different scales and addressing different aspects of the problem:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{O'Neill et al.\ generation}: moist convection injects
  cyclonic PV into the polar region, selecting \emph{sign} (cyclones,
  not anticyclones).  This is the vorticity \emph{source}.
\item \textbf{Onsager organization}: the injected cyclonic PV undergoes
  an inverse energy cascade, clustering into $N$ coherent vortices.
  This is scale-blind to the source mechanism.
\item \textbf{Thomson/energy landscape}: the $N$ clusters arrange into
  a regular polygon.  This is determined entirely by the point-vortex
  energy function.
\end{itemize}
The Onsager inverse cascade (and the present paper) requires only that
same-sign vorticity be present in the polar region; it does not predict
the sign.  The O'Neill mechanism provides that sign selection.  Without
it, the Onsager picture would predict equal probability of cyclonic and
anticyclonic polar clusters---which is not observed.  A complete theory
of Jupiter's polar polygons therefore requires both mechanisms:
O'Neill for the vorticity source and polarity, and the present
Thomson/Onsager framework for the organization and stability of
whatever is produced.

\subsection{Toward first-principles prediction}
\label{sec:first-principles}

The constraint-intersection framework
(Proposition~\ref{prop:complementary}) predicts $N$ given
observational inputs about the vortices themselves ($\kappa_0/\kappa$,
$r_{\mathrm{cyclone}}$, $R_{\mathrm{ring}}$).  A stronger result
would predict $N$ from \emph{atmospheric} parameters that are
independently measurable without observing the polygon structure.
The deformation-radius chain partially achieves this reduction:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Planetary parameters} $(\Omega, R, \varphi)$ give
    $f = 2\Omega\sin\varphi$ and
    $\beta = 2\Omega\cos\varphi / R$ (exact).
  \item \emph{Atmospheric structure} $(g', H_{\mathrm{eff}})$---the
    reduced gravity and effective weather-layer depth, measurable from
    temperature profiles and gravity data---give the deformation
    radius $R_d = \sqrt{g' H_{\mathrm{eff}}}/f$ (derived), which
    sets the cyclone core size $r_{\mathrm{core}} \sim R_d$.
  \item \emph{Domain admissibility}
    $\sin(\pi/N) \ge R_d / R_{\mathrm{ring}}$ gives
    $N_{\mathrm{pack}}$ (derived from the Laplacian).
  \item \emph{Thomson + Onsager} gives $N_{\mathrm{Thomson}} = 8$
    for $\kappa_0/\kappa \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$, which is the
    generic outcome of Onsager condensation when a central vortex
    is present (derived).
  \item $N = \min(N_{\mathrm{pack}},\, N_{\mathrm{Thomson}})$
    (constraint intersection).
\end{enumerate}
For Saturn, the Rossby path replaces steps~2--4:
$n^* = R_{\mathrm{hex}}\sqrt{\beta/U_{\max}}$, where
$U_{\max}$ is the jet speed (atmospheric observation, not a
vortex observation).

At Jupiter, the north--south asymmetry ($N = 8$ vs.\ $N = 5$) reduces
to a single parameter: $\chi = R_d^{(\mathrm{south})}/R_d^{(\mathrm{north})}$,
the ratio of deformation radii at the two poles.  With
$R_d^{(\mathrm{north})} \approx 2{,}500$\,km (matching the observed
north-pole cyclone radius at $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 3{,}000$\,km,
$g' \approx 0.3$\,m\,s$^{-2}$), the constraint intersection gives
$N = 8$ (Thomson-limited).  At the south pole,
$\chi \approx 1.34$ gives $R_d^{(\mathrm{south})} \approx 3{,}350$\,km;
with the $\beta$-drift exclusion factor $\approx 1.3$
\citep{Gavriel2021}, the effective exclusion radius
$r_{\mathrm{excl}} \approx 4{,}350$\,km yields
$N_{\mathrm{pack}} = 5$ (packing-limited).  The required
$\chi \approx 1.34$ matches the observed cyclone size ratio
$r_{\mathrm{cyclone}}^{(\mathrm{south})}/r_{\mathrm{cyclone}}^{(\mathrm{north})}
\approx 3{,}500/2{,}500 = 1.40$ to within $5\%$.

What remains is to derive $\chi$ itself from atmospheric physics.
The south pole receives $\approx 22\%$ more insolation at perihelion
(Jupiter's orbital eccentricity $e = 0.049$), but radiative scaling
gives only $\chi \sim 1.07$---insufficient.  A complete derivation
of $\chi$ from moist convective dynamics remains an open problem;
the framework reduces the prediction to this single parameter.

Three configurations from two planets is consistent with the proposed
mechanism but not sufficient to establish universality.  Uranus and
Neptune observations would substantially strengthen the case.  We use
``consistent with a general mechanism'' rather than ``universal'' to
reflect the available evidence.

\section{Derivation of $C_1(\mathbf{H}^2,\xi)$ and the near-$K=0$ expansion}
\label{app:c1_h2}

We work in the Poincar\'e disk of radius~$a$ and set $a = 1$ without
loss of generality (the formula is dimensionless in $\xi = r_E^2/a^2$).

\paragraph{H² Hamiltonian and moment map.}
In Poincar\'e disk coordinates the $N$-vortex Hamiltonian is
\citep{Kimura1999}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H_hyp}
  H_{\mathrm{hyp}} = -\!\sum_{j<k}\ln|z_j - z_k|
    + \frac{N-1}{2}\sum_k \ln(1 - |z_k|^2),
\end{equation}
equivalent to $-\sum_{j<k}\ln\sinh(d_{\mathbf{H}^2}(j,k)/2)$ up to
an additive constant, via the identity
$\sinh(d/2) = |z_j - z_k|/\sqrt{(1-|z_j|^2)(1-|z_k|^2)}$.
The $\mathrm{SO}(2,1)$ angular moment map is
$J = \sum_k(1+|z_k|^2)/(1-|z_k|^2)$.

\paragraph{Equilibrium and rotation rate $\Omega$.}
The $N$-gon at Euclidean radius $r_E$ ($\xi = r_E^2$) satisfies the
Lagrangian stationarity condition $\partial H_{\mathrm{hyp}}/\partial x_0
= \Omega\,\partial J/\partial x_0$ at vortex~$0 = (r_E, 0)$:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\partial H_{\mathrm{hyp}}}{\partial x_0}
    &= -\frac{N-1}{2r_E} - \frac{(N-1)r_E}{1-\xi}
     = -\frac{(N-1)(1+\xi)}{2\sqrt{\xi}(1-\xi)}, \\
  \frac{\partial J}{\partial x_0}
    &= \frac{4r_E}{(1-\xi)^2}
     = \frac{4\sqrt{\xi}}{(1-\xi)^2}.
\end{align*}
Dividing gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Omega_H2}
  \Omega = -\frac{(N-1)(1+\xi)(1-\xi)}{8\xi}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Mode-$m$ second derivatives.}
Let $\hat u$ be the normalized radial Fourier mode of wavenumber $m$
($\|\hat u\|=1$, Euclidean).  Two ring-geometry identities hold for all
$1 \le m \le \lfloor N/2\rfloor$:
\[
  \sum_k |\hat u_k|^2 = 1, \qquad
  \sum_k \bigl[\operatorname{Re}(z_k\,\overline{\hat u_k})\bigr]^2 = r_E^2 = \xi.
\]
The three contributions to $\lambda_m \cdot r_E^2$ are:

\emph{(1) Flat-plane Havelock term.}
By scale invariance of $-\ln|\cdot|$,
$\partial^2 H_{\mathrm{flat}}/\partial\varepsilon^2 = \sigma_m/r_E^2$
with $\sigma_m = (N-1)/2 - m(N-m)/2$.  Multiplying by $r_E^2$:
\[
  \text{(1)} = \tfrac{N-1}{2} - \tfrac{m(N-m)}{2}.
\]

\emph{(2) Curvature-correction term $W$.}
With $W = \tfrac{N-1}{2}\sum_k\ln(1-|z_k|^2)$, expanding at $\varepsilon=0$:
\[
  \frac{\partial^2 W}{\partial\varepsilon^2}
  = \frac{N-1}{2}\sum_k\!\left[
    \frac{-2|\hat u_k|^2}{1-\xi}
    - \frac{4[\operatorname{Re}(z_k\overline{\hat u_k})]^2}{(1-\xi)^2}
  \right]
  = -\frac{(N-1)(1+\xi)}{(1-\xi)^2}.
\]
Multiplying by $r_E^2 = \xi$:
\[
  \text{(2)} = -\frac{(N-1)\xi(1+\xi)}{(1-\xi)^2}.
\]

\emph{(3) Lagrange-multiplier term $-\Omega J$.}
Expanding $J$ similarly gives
\[
  \frac{\partial^2 J}{\partial\varepsilon^2}
  = \frac{4}{(1-\xi)^2}\!\left[1 + \frac{4\xi}{1-\xi}\right]
  = \frac{4(1+3\xi)}{(1-\xi)^3}.
\]
With $-\Omega = (N-1)(1-\xi^2)/(8\xi)$ from~\eqref{eq:Omega_H2}:
\[
  -\Omega\frac{\partial^2 J}{\partial\varepsilon^2}
  = \frac{(N-1)(1+\xi)(1+3\xi)}{2\xi(1-\xi)^2}.
\]
Multiplying by $r_E^2 = \xi$:
\[
  \text{(3)} = \frac{(N-1)(1+\xi)(1+3\xi)}{2(1-\xi)^2}.
\]

\paragraph{Algebraic simplification.}
The $\xi$-dependent part of $\text{(2)}+\text{(3)}$ (factor $N-1$):
\[
  -\frac{\xi(1+\xi)}{(1-\xi)^2} + \frac{(1+\xi)(1+3\xi)}{2(1-\xi)^2}
  = \frac{(1+\xi)}{2(1-\xi)^2}\bigl[(1+3\xi) - 2\xi\bigr]
  = \frac{(1+\xi)^2}{2(1-\xi)^2}.
\]
Adding term~(1):
\begin{align*}
  C_1 &= \frac{N-1}{2} + \frac{(N-1)(1+\xi)^2}{2(1-\xi)^2}
       = \frac{N-1}{2}\cdot\frac{(1-\xi)^2+(1+\xi)^2}{(1-\xi)^2}
       = \frac{(N-1)(1+\xi^2)}{(1-\xi)^2},
\end{align*}
establishing~\eqref{eq:C1_H2}.

\paragraph{Near-$K=0$ expansion.}
For $N=7$, $m=3$: $m(N-m)/2 = 6 = N-1$.  Therefore
\[
  \lambda_3 \cdot r_E^2 = C_1 - 6
  = 6\cdot\frac{(1+\xi^2) - (1-\xi)^2}{(1-\xi)^2}
  = \frac{12\xi}{(1-\xi)^2}.
\]
Using $\xi = r_E^2/a^2 = |K|r_E^2$:
\[
  \lambda_3 = \frac{12|K|}{(1-\xi)^2} = 12|K| + O(K^2 r_E^2).
\]
The coefficient $12 = 2\,m(N{-}m)/2\big|_{m=3,\,N=7}$ arises from the
cancellation $(1+\xi^2) - (1-\xi)^2 = 2\xi$, which holds because
$N-1 = m(N-m)/2$ exactly at this critical mode.
