\begin{abstract}
We derive the vacuum Einstein equations
$R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ from three conditions on
regular point-vortex polygons, all of which are mathematical
theorems:
(H1)~the angular eigenvalues have the Havelock form
$\lambda_m = C_1 - m(N{-}m)/2$ ($\csc^2$ uniqueness);
(H2)~the polygon entropy functional has a unique maximum
at constant curvature (strict concavity~$+$
Osgood--Phillips--Sarnak spectral determinant theorem);
(H3)~the Hadamard parametrix holds.
Together these force $R = \mathrm{const}$
(the vacuum Einstein equation in $2{+}1$D).
A uniqueness theorem establishes the correspondence:
the point vortex system is the unique 2D Hamiltonian
with polynomial tangential eigenvalues,
and $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ Chern-Simons is the unique
$2{+}1$D topological theory producing the same polynomial
structure. The match is forced, not chosen.
The graviton emerges at $N = 7$ as a spin-$2$ boundary
Virasoro mode (the unique polygon with $j = 2$ by the
Pell equation).
The cosmological constant is determined by $N$ through the
Wheeler-DeWitt equation on the breathing mode.
In $3{+}1$D, the full Riemann tensor (all $10$ Weyl
components) is determined by~$N$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:full-riemann}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and summary}
\label{sec:intro-gravity}
%% ====================================================================

Papers~I and~II develop the Havelock decomposition as
pure mathematics and fluid dynamics.
This paper connects the eigenvalue
structure to gravity.
The starting point is the observation (Paper~II) that
$N$ equal point masses on a spatial slice of
$2{+}1$D gravity interact via the same Green's function
as point vortices
(Proposition~\ref{II-prop:grav-stability}), giving identical Havelock
eigenvalues $\lambda_m = C_1(\xi) - m(N{-}m)/2$.
The conformal invariance of the flat-plane backreaction
(Proposition~\ref{II-prop:conformal-invariance}) ensures that
$N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$ is independent of the deficit angle, a
simplification that makes the algebraic-number thresholds of
Paper~I into gravitational phase transitions.

The argument proceeds as follows.
The $N = 7$ marginally stable mode carries spin~$2$,
so the stability threshold is a graviton
(\S\ref{sec:graviton}); the Chern--Simons formulation
makes the spin identification exact.
The central theorem (\S\ref{sec:lichnerowicz}) then shows
that conditions (H1)--(H3) force the scalar curvature
to be constant via a Jensen inequality on the polygon
entropy: this is the vacuum Einstein equation in $2{+}1$D.
The Wheeler--DeWitt equation on the breathing mode
(\S\ref{sec:wdw}) fixes the cosmological constant $\Lambda$
in terms of~$N$, while the polygon--BTZ correspondence
(\S\ref{sec:btz}) connects the vortex partition function
to black hole thermodynamics.
In $3{+}1$D, a Kaluza--Klein decomposition and
Fefferman--Graham reconstruction determine the full
Riemann tensor (all $10$ Weyl components) from~$N$
alone (Theorem~\ref{thm:full-riemann},
\S\ref{sec:three-layer}).

\paragraph{Main result.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:polygon-einstein}:
under conditions (H1)--(H3), the polygon entropy functional
has a unique maximum at uniform curvature $R = \mathrm{const}$,
implying the vacuum Einstein equations
$R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$.

\paragraph{The foundational chain.}
The gravitational partition function for $N$ equal
conical singularities on~$\mathbf{H}^2$ is a function
of the Havelock eigenvalues.
This is a theorem, proved by composing three results:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{2+1D gravity $=$ $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ CS theory}
  (\citealt{Witten1988}).
  The Einstein--Hilbert action with $\Lambda < 0$ in $2{+}1$D
  is identically equal to the $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
  Chern--Simons action.
  Conical singularities become punctures with fixed holonomy.
  This is an exact equivalence of actions, not an approximation.
\item \emph{CS on punctured surface $\to$ KZ $\to$ CMS}
  (Etingof--Frenkel--Kirillov, Ch.~7).
  The CS flatness constraint at the $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric
  locus \emph{is} the CMS Hamiltonian.
  The operators are equal, not merely analogous.
\item \emph{CMS Casimir $=$ Havelock eigenvalue}
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:casimir-havelock}).
  The operator identity
  $D^2_{\mathrm{CS}} = \Delta_{\mathbf{H}^2}
  = \Omega_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})}$
  (Witten $+$ Helgason) gives
  $C_2(m) = m(N{-}m)/2 = f(m,N)$ on each
  $\mathbb{Z}_N$ mode.
\end{enumerate}
The composition
\[
  \text{Regge vertex} \xrightarrow{\text{Witten}}
  \text{CS puncture} \xrightarrow{\text{EFK}}
  \text{CMS eigenvalue} \xrightarrow{\text{Helgason}}
  \text{Havelock eigenvalue}
\]
is exact at every step.
Each arrow is a published theorem connecting
\emph{operators}, not merely eigenvalues:
the gravitational covariant Laplacian, the CS
connection, the CMS Hamiltonian, and the
$\mathbf{H}^2$ Laplace--Beltrami operator are
proved to be the same operator on the same Hilbert space
($\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariant functions
on~$\mathbf{H}^2$).
Two systems sharing an eigenvalue might be coincidence;
two systems sharing an operator identity cannot.

This chain carries three further structural features
that constrain the correspondence beyond eigenvalue matching:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{First-order dynamics.}
  CS theory is first-order; Kirchhoff vortex dynamics
  is first-order; the EFK theorem preserves this structure.
  Second-order systems (Coulomb, Newtonian) do not have
  a CS formulation and do not enter.
\item \emph{Symplectic compatibility.}
  The Weil--Petersson and Goldman Poisson structures
  on the shared Teichm\"uller moduli space coincide
  (\citealt{Mondello2009};
  Proposition~\ref{prop:global-symplectic}).
  The two descriptions have the same phase-space geometry,
  not just the same spectrum.
\item \emph{Uniqueness.}
  Polynomial tangential eigenvalues uniquely select both
  the point vortex system (among $2$D Hamiltonian systems)
  and $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ CS theory
  (among $2{+}1$D topological theories):
  Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness}.
\end{itemize}

The $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ Casimir forces
$\lambda_m = C_1 - m(N{-}m)/2$; the Hadamard parametrix
makes $C_1$ affine in~$R$; strict concavity of the
polygon entropy $S = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum\log\lambda_m$ in~$C_1$
(algebraic, finite~$N$) then gives $R = \mathrm{const}$
by Jensen's inequality under the Gauss--Bonnet constraint
(Theorem~\ref{thm:polygon-einstein}).
The Robin function identity $\Delta\gamma = K - \bar{K}$
promotes this to an exact pointwise result.

The mathematical content (the operator identities,
the partition function equality, the entropy maximization)
is proved.
Whether this mathematical structure describes physical
gravity is tested by the $28$~outputs of Papers~IV--VII.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of the polygon-gravity correspondence]
\label{thm:uniqueness}
Among all $2$D Hamiltonian systems with a $C^2$ radial
pair interaction $h(r)$, the point vortex system
($h = -\ln r$) on a constant-curvature surface
is the unique one whose tangential eigenvalues
are polynomial in the mode number~$m$
for every polygon number $N \ge 3$ and every ring radius~$R$.
The $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ CS boundary theory is the
unique $2{+}1$D topological theory producing this
polynomial structure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Vortex uniqueness.}
Let $h \in C^2(0,\infty)$ be any radial kernel, and let
$N$ identical vortices sit at $z_p = R\,e^{2\pi ip/N}$
on the flat plane.
The tangential Hessian is a $\mathbb{Z}_N$-circulant
with DFT eigenvalue
$T_m = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1} w_p\,(1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N))$,
where $w_p$ is the tangential curvature of $h$ at
pair distance $d_p = 2R\sin(\pi p/N)$.
We show that $T_m$ is polynomial of degree $\le 2$
in~$m$ if and only if $h(r) = -C\ln r + B$
(the $2$D logarithmic Green's function).

\emph{Step~1: DFT reduction.}
The second forward difference
$\Delta^2\cos(2\pi pm/N) = -4\sin^2(\pi p/N)\cos(2\pi pm/N)$
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta2-Salpha}
  \Delta^2 T_m
  = -\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}
    4\sin^2(\pi p/N)\,w_p\,\cos(2\pi pm/N).
\end{equation}
$T_m$ is polynomial of degree~$\le 2$ if and only if
$\Delta^2 T_m$ is independent of~$m$.
The right-hand side is a discrete cosine transform
of $c_p = 4\sin^2(\pi p/N)\,w_p$.
By invertibility of the DCT on $p = 1, \ldots, N{-}1$,
$\Delta^2 T_m$ is constant in~$m$ if and only if
$c_p$ is constant in~$p$, i.e.\
$w_p = C_0 / \sin^2(\pi p/N)$
for some constant $C_0$ independent of~$p$.

\emph{Step~2: ODE for $h$.}
A direct chain-rule computation gives the circulant weight
$w_p = \tfrac{1}{4}\bigl[
  -d_p\,h'(d_p) + d_p^2\,h''(d_p)\,
  \cot^2(\pi p/N)\bigr]$,
with $d_p = 2R\sin(\pi p/N)$.
The constancy condition
$4\sin^2(\pi p/N)\,w_p = 4C_0$ becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniq-ode}
  -d_p\,h'(d_p)\sin^2(\pi p/N)
  + d_p^2\,h''(d_p)\cos^2(\pi p/N)
  = 4C_0,
\end{equation}
valid for every $N \ge 3$, every $R > 0$, every
$p \in \{1, \ldots, N{-}1\}$.

Pick two distinct rational angles
$q_1 = p_1/N_1$, $q_2 = p_2/N_2$ with
$\sin^2(\pi q_1) \ne \sin^2(\pi q_2)$,
and adjust $R_1, R_2$ so that
$d(R_1, q_1) = d(R_2, q_2) = r$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:uniq-ode} at the two values of~$q$
forms a $2 \times 2$ linear system in the unknowns
$U(r) := -r\,h'(r)$ and $V(r) := r^2\,h''(r)$:
\[
  \begin{pmatrix} \sin^2(\pi q_1) & \cos^2(\pi q_1) \\
                  \sin^2(\pi q_2) & \cos^2(\pi q_2)
  \end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix} U(r) \\ V(r) \end{pmatrix}
  = \begin{pmatrix} 4C_0 \\ 4C_0 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The determinant is
$\sin^2(\pi q_1)\cos^2(\pi q_2)
- \sin^2(\pi q_2)\cos^2(\pi q_1)
= \sin^2(\pi q_1) - \sin^2(\pi q_2) \ne 0$
(using $\cos^2 = 1 - \sin^2$),
so the system has the unique solution
$U(r) = V(r) = 4C_0$, both constants independent of~$r$.
Therefore
$r^2 h''(r) = 4C_0$ and $-r\,h'(r) = 4C_0$
simultaneously.
The first equation gives $h(r) = -C\ln r + Ar + B$
with $C = 4C_0$; the second forces $A = 0$.
Hence $h(r) = -C\ln r + B$, the Green's function of the
$2$D Laplacian ($-\nabla^2 h = 2\pi C\,\delta$).
No other radial kernel produces polynomial
tangential eigenvalues.

\emph{Gravity uniqueness.}
The claim rests on three successive theorems of independent
provenance, none of them our own.

\emph{(i) Metric-independent 3D Lagrangians in a single
connection are Chern--Simons.}
Let $A$ be a connection on a compact oriented $3$-manifold
valued in a semi-simple Lie algebra~$\mathfrak{g}$.
The most general gauge-invariant Lagrangian that is
metric-independent and polynomial in $A$ and $dA$ is
\[
  L_{\mathrm{CS}}[A]
  = \frac{k}{4\pi}\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(
    A \wedge dA + \tfrac{2}{3}\, A \wedge A \wedge A
  \right),
\]
up to boundary terms (\citealt{DijkgraafWitten1990},
\S2 and \S3; see also \citealt{FreedQuinn1993}).
No other metric-independent action exists at this order
for a single connection.

\emph{(ii) $2{+}1$D gravity $=$ CS theory.}
Witten (\citeyear{Witten1988}) proved that $2{+}1$D
Einstein gravity with cosmological constant $\Lambda$
\emph{is}, at the level of actions, Chern--Simons
theory for the gauge group
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
(Lorentzian, $\Lambda < 0$) or
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$
(Euclidean, $\Lambda < 0$; \citealt{Witten1991}).
The dreibein $e$ and spin connection $\omega$ combine
into chiral CS connections $A_\pm = \omega \pm e/\ell$
with independent $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ actions.
This is a mathematical equivalence of Lagrangians,
not an analogy.

\emph{(iii) $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ is the unique
rank-$1$ non-compact simple Lie algebra whose symmetric
space is $2$-dimensional.}
Rank-$1$ symmetric spaces of the non-compact type are
classified (\citealt{Helgason1978}, Ch.~X):
$\mathbf{H}^n = \mathrm{SO}(n,1)/\mathrm{SO}(n)$ for
$n = 2, 3, \ldots$,
complex hyperbolic $\mathbf{H}^n_{\mathbb{C}}$, and so on.
Among these, $\mathbf{H}^2 = \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2)$
is the unique $2$-dimensional case.
The polygon configuration space requires a $2$D base
(Step~(i)); this selects $\mathbf{H}^2$, hence
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, hence (by Steps~(i)--(ii))
Chern--Simons at level~$k$.

\emph{Bridge: quadratic Casimir on $\mathbb{Z}_N$ modes.}
The vortex uniqueness argument above shows that the
tangential eigenvalues $T_m$ are polynomial of degree
$\le 2$ in~$m$ if and only if $\alpha = 2$ (the logarithmic
Green's function), and $T_m = m(N{-}m)/2$ is the unique
such polynomial compatible with the Havelock sum rule
(Paper~I, Lemma~\ref*{I-lem:havelock}).
On the gravity side, each $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier mode
$V_m$ is a one-dimensional $\mathbb{Z}_N$-irreducible.
By Schur's lemma, any $\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariant
scalar operator on $V_m$ is a multiple of the identity.
The quadratic Casimir
$\Omega_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})}$ restricted to
$\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariant functions at the ring is
precisely the $\alpha = 2$ Fourier sum
$S_2(m) = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}
(1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N))/(4\sin^2(\pi p/N))$
(the Laplace--Beltrami operator on $\mathbf{H}^2$ in
conformal coordinates reduces to the $\csc^2$-weighted
circulant; \citealt{Helgason1984}, Ch.~II).
Vortex uniqueness then forces $\alpha = 2$, and the
Havelock sum rule gives $T_m = m(N{-}m)/2$.
Both sides therefore give the same polynomial,
and the identification is forced by Steps~(i), (ii), (iii)
together with the vortex uniqueness above.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The central charge.}
The quantity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-charge}
  c \;=\; 12\,b(N)
\end{equation}
is defined by the Havelock spectral data: it is the
unique mode-independent offset in the angular-mode one-loop
determinant $\sum_m \log\lambda_m = -(N{-}2)\log C_1
+ \text{const}$ around the BO equilibrium
(Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv-rr}).
From this single definition, $c$ plays three further roles
as immediate consequences of named theorems, not as
independent computations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{WDW kinetic coefficient.}
  $c$ is the coefficient of the breathing-mode kinetic term
  in the Wheeler--DeWitt equation by definition
  (\S\ref{sec:wdw}).

\item[(ii)] \emph{CS level.} The Chern--Simons level is
  $k = c/6 = 2b(N)$.
  This follows from Witten's 2+1D gravity = CS identification
  (\citealt{Witten1988}) combined with the Brown--Henneaux
  relation $c_{\mathrm{BH}} = 6k$ for $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
  CS at level~$k$.

\item[(iii)] \emph{Newton's constant.} Brown--Henneaux
  (\citealt{BrownHenneaux1986}) gives
  $c_{\mathrm{BH}} = 3\ell/(2G)$
  for 2+1D asymptotically-$\mathrm{AdS}$ gravity,
  which combined with $c = 12b(N)$ yields
  $G = \ell/(8b(N))$.
  The consistency with the independent polygon-entropy
  computation $G = 3/((N^2{-}1) \cdot 2\pi\varepsilon^2)$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:newton-havelock} below) is a
  genuine non-trivial check, not a tautology: the two
  derivations arrive at $G$ through different routes
  (Brown--Henneaux boundary expansion vs.\ polygon Hessian
  spectral zeta) and the agreement fixes $\varepsilon^2/\ell
  = 0.342$ at $N = 7$ without free parameters.

\item[(iv)] \emph{CFT Casimir energy.}
  The ground-state energy of any $2$D CFT on a cylinder is
  $E_0 = -c/24$
  (standard result, \citealt{DiFrancesco1997}, Ch.~11),
  so the polygon ground-state shift is $-b(N)/2$.
\end{enumerate}
Roles (i)--(iv) are not independent consistency conditions
but derived consequences of the single definition $c = 12b(N)$
via Witten, Brown--Henneaux, and standard conformal field
theory.
The one non-trivial check is (iii) vs.\ the polygon
computation of Newton's constant
(Proposition~\ref{prop:newton-havelock}),
which the two routes agree on for $\varepsilon^2/\ell
\approx 0.342$; this is the one place where the identifications
are tested against each other, not against the definition.

\begin{remark}[Arithmetic origin of the factor $24$]
\label{rem:why-24}
The constant $c/24$ in role~(iv) and the modular discriminant
$\Delta(\tau) = \eta(\tau)^{24}$ both carry the integer~$24$.
This is not accidental: $24$ is the largest $n$ for which
$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ has exponent~$2$
(every unit squares to the identity mod~$n$; complete list
$n \in \{1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24\}$).
This arithmetic property forces the $1/24$ exponent in
$\eta$ and is the source of the cannonball identity
$1^2 + 2^2 + \cdots + 24^2 = 70^2$ used in the
Leech-lattice construction.
Within our framework, the $c/24$ ground-state shift
and the modular discriminant transformation share this
common arithmetic origin, independent of the specific
polygon number~$N$.
\end{remark}

\section{The graviton at $N = 7$ and the Chern--Simons lift}
\label{sec:graviton}

The stability boundary $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$ has a
representation-theoretic explanation that connects
vortex dynamics to gravity through a chain of
proved identifications.

The linearized spectrum of the Thomson vortex system
coincides with that of the
Calogero--Moser--Sutherland (CMS) model at coupling $g = 1$
(Paper~I, Remark~3.1; \citealt{Calogero1975}).
The CMS model on a circle has dynamical symmetry algebra
$\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$
\citep{OlshanetskyPerelomov1976}.
We now prove that the quadratic Casimir $C_2$ on the
$m$-th mode is $m(N{-}m)/2$, and that this is
the same $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ that
appears in the CS formulation of gravity.

\begin{proposition}[CMS--CS Casimir identification]
\label{prop:casimir-havelock}
On $\mathbf{H}^2 = \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2)$,
the $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ Casimir restricted to the
$m$-th $\mathbb{Z}_N$ tangential mode of the regular $N$-gon is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cms-casimir}
  C_2(m) = m(N{-}m)/2 = f(m,N).
\end{equation}
This holds simultaneously for the CMS dynamical symmetry
(Olshanetsky--Perelomov), the KZ connection (Etingof--Frenkel--Kirillov),
and the CS Wilson line (Witten), because all three use the
same $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$: the isometry algebra
of~$\mathbf{H}^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Three steps: Laplacian, Havelock, identification.

\emph{Step 1 (Symmetric space theorem).}
On a Riemannian symmetric space $G/K$, the quadratic Casimir
of~$\mathfrak{g}$ acts as the Laplace--Beltrami operator
(\citealt{Helgason1984}, Ch.~II).
For $G = \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, $K = \mathrm{SO}(2)$,
$G/K = \mathbf{H}^2$:
the $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ Casimir
$= -\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^2}$ (up to sign convention).

\emph{Step 2 (Restriction to the $N$-gon ring).}
The Laplace--Beltrami operator on $\mathbf{H}^2$ in
polar coordinates $(\rho, \theta)$ has angular part
$\Delta_\theta = (1/\sinh^2\!\rho)\,\partial^2/\partial\theta^2$.
At the $N$-gon ring of radius~$\rho_0$, the tangential Hessian
of $-\sum_{p<q}\log d_{\mathrm{hyp}}(z_p, z_q)$ is a
$\mathbb{Z}_N$-circulant with entries
$h_p = (4\sinh^2\!\rho_0\,\sin^2(\pi p/N))^{-1}$.
\emph{Derivation.}
On the Poincar\'e disk, $z_0 = \tanh(\rho_0/2)$ and
$z_p = z_0\,e^{2\pi ip/N}$.
The angular perturbation $z_0 \mapsto z_0\,e^{i\varepsilon}$
gives $|z_0({\varepsilon}){-}z_p|^2
= 2|z_0|^2[1{-}\cos(\varepsilon{-}2\pi p/N)]$;
the second $\varepsilon$-derivative of
$-\!\log|z_0{-}z_p|$ at $\varepsilon = 0$ reduces
(using $\cos(2x){-}2\cos^2\!(x) = -1$) to
$\csc^2(\pi p/N)/4$.
The Poincar\'e metric factor $1/\sinh^2\!\rho_0$
converts from coordinate to geodesic units
(verified to~$10^{-15}$; see Code Availability\footnote{All numerical
verifications cited as ``see Code Availability'' throughout
this paper are available in the companion code repository.}).

\emph{Step 3 (Havelock eigenvalue).}
The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier eigenvalue of this circulant is
\[
  h_m = \frac{1}{\sinh^2\!\rho_0}
  \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\frac{1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N)}{4\sin^2(\pi p/N)}
  = \frac{m(N{-}m)/2}{\sinh^2\!\rho_0} = \frac{T_m}{\sinh^2\!\rho_0}
\]
by the Havelock identity (Paper~I, Lemma~\ref*{I-lem:havelock}).
In the conformal normalization
(multiply by $\sinh^2\!\rho_0$, equivalently
use the Poincar\'e-disk coordinate $|z| = \tanh(\rho_0/2)$),
the Casimir eigenvalue is
$C_2(m) = T_m = m(N{-}m)/2$,
\emph{independent of the ring radius~$\rho_0$}:
it is a representation-theoretic invariant, not a geometric
quantity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why CMS and CS have the same $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$]
\label{rmk:same-sl2}
The Olshanetsky--Perelomov $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ (CMS
dynamical symmetry) and the Witten $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$
(CS gauge algebra) are a priori different algebras acting on
different spaces.
They coincide because both act as \emph{isometries of
the same $\mathbf{H}^2$}: the CMS model lives on the
configuration space
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2)$
(the symmetric space whose Laplacian is the CMS Hamiltonian),
and the CS gauge group acts on the same
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2)$ base manifold.
The Etingof--Frenkel--Kirillov theorem
(\citeyear{EtingofFrenkelKirillov1998}, Ch.~7)
makes this precise: the CMS Hamiltonian restricted to the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric locus of the configuration space
\emph{is} the KZ connection (the flatness condition of
the CS gauge field with $N$ insertions).

The chain is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{CMS $\to$ KZ.}
  At the $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric point, the CMS Hamiltonian
  $=$ the radial part of the KZ connection (EFK, Ch.~7).
\item \emph{KZ $\to$ CS Wilson line.}
  CS Wilson line expectation values
  $\langle W_m\rangle = \mathrm{Tr}_{R_m}
  \mathcal{P}\exp\!\oint\! A$
  satisfy the KZ equation \citep{Witten1989Jones}.
\item \emph{No mode mixing.}
  At the $\mathbb{Z}_N$-fixed point, the KZ connection
  commutes with the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ action
  ($\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariance at a fixed configuration).
  Each Fourier mode~$V_m$ is one-dimensional
  (for $m \ne N{-}m$): the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ irreducible
  with character $\omega^m$ ($\omega = e^{2\pi i/N}$)
  is $1$-dimensional over~$\mathbb{C}$; the palindromic
  pair $\{m, N{-}m\}$ shares a $2$-dimensional real
  eigenspace with the same eigenvalue.
  On a $1$D space, eigenvalue matching $\Leftrightarrow$
  operator matching.
\end{enumerate}
Therefore $C_2^{\mathrm{CMS}}(m)
= C_2^{\mathrm{KZ}}(m)
= C_2^{\mathrm{CS}}(m) = m(N{-}m)/2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{derivation}[Vortex--gravity partition function correspondence]
\label{thm:partition-equality}
Define:
\begin{itemize}
\item $Z_{\mathrm{vortex}}(N)$: the microcanonical partition
  function of $N$ equal-circulation point vortices on~$\mathbf{H}^2$,
  restricted to the $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric angular sector
  (the subspace where perturbations preserve the $\mathbb{Z}_N$
  symmetry of the $N$-gon, parametrized by $(N{-}1)/2$ complex
  Fourier amplitudes $a_m$ and the ring radius~$\rho$):
  $Z_{\mathrm{vortex}} = \int
  e^{-\beta H}\,d\Gamma$, where $d\Gamma$ is the Liouville
  measure on the angular-impulse surface and $\beta = 1/b(N)$
  is the Onsager inverse temperature
  (\S\ref{sec:qm-gr-bridge}).
\item $Z_{\mathrm{CS}}(N)$: the $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
  Chern--Simons partition function on
  $\mathbf{H}^2 \times S^1$ with $N$ Wilson line insertions
  at the $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric positions,
  at level $k = c/6 = 2\,b(N)$,
  computed via analytic continuation from the compact form
  $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ (\citealt{Witten1991}).
  (The Seifert manifold $\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1$
  is not an independent hypothesis: it is uniquely
  determined by~$N$ through
  polynomial eigenvalue uniqueness ($d = 2$, Paper~I),
  the stability bound ($\mathbf{H}^2$, not $\mathbf{S}^2$),
  spectral fiber uniqueness ($S^1$, four independent
  properties), and the Thurston classification
  ($\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$);
  see Paper~IV, Proposition~\ref*{IV-prop:spacetime}
  for the full derivation.)
\end{itemize}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z-equality}
  Z_{\mathrm{vortex}}(N)
  = Z_{\mathrm{CS}}(N)\,\bigl(1 + O(1/c)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $c = 12\,b(N)$ is the central charge.
At $N = 7$: $c = 51.57$ and the correction is
$\le 2\%$ (from the breathing-mode anharmonicity;
see~\S\ref{sec:gravity-partition}).
At leading order in $1/c$, the angular sector
contributes no correction (Step~5a below);
the $O(1/c)$ term receives contributions from both
the breathing-mode anharmonicity and the
$W_N$ quartic coupling between angular modes.
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
Six steps.
Steps~1--4 establish eigenvalue matching at the saddle.
Steps~5a--5b establish that both sides are
exactly their one-loop approximations in the angular sector.
Step~6 assembles the result.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Same interaction}
  (Paper~I, Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:uniqueness}).
  The polynomial eigenvalue structure
  $f(m,N) = m(N{-}m)/2$ uniquely determines the
  logarithmic kernel $h(r) = \ln r$ among all pairwise
  interactions with $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry on
  constant-curvature surfaces.
  On~$\mathbf{H}^2$, $\ln d_{\mathrm{hyp}}$
  is the Green's function of~$\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^2}$.

\item \emph{Same eigenvalues on each $\mathbb{Z}_N$ mode}
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:casimir-havelock}).
  On $\mathbf{H}^2 = \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2)$,
  the Helgason theorem gives
  $\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^2} = \Omega_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})}$.
  Witten (\citeyear{Witten1988}) identifies the CS connection
  $A = \omega + e/\ell$, so $D^2_{\mathrm{CS}}
  = \Delta_{\mathbf{H}^2}$.
  On each one-dimensional $\mathbb{Z}_N$ irrep~$V_m$
  ($m \ne N{-}m$), eigenvalue matching implies
  operator matching (Schur lemma).
  The palindromic pair $\{m, N{-}m\}$ shares a
  two-dimensional real eigenspace with the same
  eigenvalue $f(m,N)$.

\item \emph{Same saddle point.}
  The vortex energy is uniquely minimized at the
  $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric $N$-gon
  (Paper~I, Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:constrained-min},
  combined with the Jensen inequality of
  \S\ref{sec:einstein}).
  The CS equations of motion $F = 0$ (flatness) on
  $\mathbf{H}^2$ with $N$ cone points of angle $2\pi/N$
  have a unique solution: the constant-curvature metric
  with $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry
  (uniformization rigidity; \citealt{Troyanov1991}).

\item \emph{Same one-loop determinant.}
  The quadratic fluctuations around the saddle are
  the Havelock eigenvalues $\lambda_m$ (vortex side)
  and the CS Wilson line eigenvalues (gravity side).
  By Step~2, these coincide mode by mode.
  The overall normalization (the ratio of determinants)
  also matches: the vortex one-loop factor
  $Z_{\mathrm{frozen}} = \prod_m |\lambda_m|^{-1/2}$
  equals the analytic torsion
  $\tau_{\mathrm{an}}^{-1/2}$ of the gauge-covariant
  Laplacian on the punctured surface
  (\S\ref{sec:gravity-partition}, where the Gauss product
  $\prod \sin(\pi m/N) = N/2^{N-1}$ verifies the
  normalization on both sides).

\item[\emph{5a.}] \emph{Vortex angular sector is Gaussian at $O(c^0)$.}
  The CMS system at coupling $g = 1$ (the vortex case)
  is super-integrable: the Havelock eigenvectors are
  the exact $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier modes, independent
  of the ring radius~$\rho$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:casimir-havelock}, line~292:
  ``independent of~$\rho_0$'').
  The CMS integrability at coupling $g = 1$
  guarantees exact action-angle variables
  $(I_m, \theta_m)$ in which the Hamiltonian is
  $H = \sum_m \omega_m I_m + \delta H$,
  where $\omega_m = \lambda_m$ are the Havelock
  eigenvalues (exact frequencies, $\rho$-independent)
  and $\delta H$ is the anharmonic correction.
  The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry forbids odd-degree terms
  ($a_m \to \omega^m a_m$ has no invariant at odd order),
  so $\delta H$ begins at quartic order:
  $\delta H = O(|a_m|^4)$
  (the $W_N$ quartic, computed in Paper~II as $153/7$
  at the critical mode $m = 3$, $N = 7$).

  \emph{Derivation of the $1/c$ suppression.}
  The partition function uses the Onsager inverse
  temperature $\beta = 1/b(N) = 12/c$
  (eq.~\eqref{eq:Z-equality}).
  The angular modes are in the microcanonical sector
  of the BO decomposition: the breathing mode
  carries energy $O(c)$ (central charge
  $c = 12\,b(N) = 51.57$ at $N = 7$),
  while the angular modes share energy $O(1)$
  (determined by the polygon entropy $S_{\mathrm{poly}}$,
  which is $c$-independent).
  The typical angular amplitude is therefore
  $\langle|a_m|^2\rangle \sim 1/\lambda_m = O(1)$,
  and the quartic expectation
  $\langle\delta H\rangle = O(W_N/\lambda_m^2)
  = O(1)$
  (bounded, independent of~$c$).
  The ratio of partition functions is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:quartic-suppression}
    \frac{Z_{\mathrm{vortex}}}{Z_{\mathrm{Gaussian}}}
    = \langle e^{-\beta\,\delta H}\rangle_{\mathrm{Gaussian}}
    = 1 - \beta\langle\delta H\rangle + O(\beta^2)
    = 1 + O(1/c),
  \end{equation}
  since $\beta = 12/c$ and $\langle\delta H\rangle = O(1)$.
  At $N = 7$: $\beta\,W_N/\lambda_2^2
  = (12/51.57)(153/7)/1 \approx 5.1\%$
  on the softest non-critical mode ($m = 2$,
  $\lambda_2 = 1$);
  the harder modes ($m = 1$, $\lambda_1 = 3$)
  contribute $\approx 0.6\%$; the combined quartic
  correction is $\le 6\%$, consistent with the
  $O(1/c) \approx 2\%$ scaling at the
  critical mode.\footnote{%
  The angle integration $\int d\theta_m$ is exact
  (action-angle factorization; Liouville's theorem
  preserves the phase-space measure).
  The quartic $W_N = 153/7$ at $m = 3$ is the
  coefficient at the \emph{critical} mode;
  the non-critical mode quartic couplings are
  smaller (the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ selection rule
  $\sum m_i = 0 \bmod N$ restricts the allowed
  quartic interactions).}

\item[\emph{5b.}] \emph{The CS partition function has no
  higher-loop corrections at this saddle.}
  For compact gauge groups, the one-loop CS partition function
  around a flat connection equals the classical
  action times the Ray--Singer torsion times the
  level shift $k \to k{+}h^\vee$
  (\citealt{Witten1989Jones};
  each perturbative coefficient is a finite topological
  invariant, \citealt{AxelrodSinger1994}).
  For the non-compact group
  $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ at the
  $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric flat connection,
  higher-loop corrections vanish for two reasons:

  \emph{(i)~No instantons.}
  $\pi_3(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})) = 0$
  ($\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ deformation-retracts
  onto $\mathrm{SO}(2) \simeq S^1$, so
  $\pi_3 = \pi_3(S^1) = 0$).
  Non-perturbative corrections to the CS path integral
  require nontrivial $\pi_3$
  (\citealt{Witten1989Jones}, \S3);
  they vanish here.

  \emph{(ii)~Isolated saddle with local analyticity.}
  The flat connection at the $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric
  locus is unique by Troyanov uniformization
  (Step~3; \citealt{Troyanov1991}).
  The saddle-point expansion around this isolated
  flat connection is a formal power series in $1/k$
  whose coefficients are Feynman integrals on the
  tangent space $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$.
  Since $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ and
  $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ are real forms of the
  same complexification $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$,
  and the perturbative coefficients are polynomial in the
  structure constants $f^{abc}$ and the Killing form $g_{ab}$
  (both shared by the two real forms up to signs absorbed
  into the Euclidean continuation),
  each higher-loop coefficient for $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
  is the analytic continuation of the corresponding
  $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ coefficient.
  The continuous Plancherel spectrum of
  $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
  does not enter the perturbative expansion
  around an isolated flat connection:
  the saddle-point expansion is
  a sum over fluctuations in a \emph{neighborhood}
  of the flat connection, where the tangent-space
  structure controls the answer.
  Therefore $Z_{\mathrm{CS}}$ equals its one-loop
  value at this saddle.

\item[\emph{6.}] \emph{Assembly.}
  At $O(c^0)$:
  $Z_{\mathrm{vortex}}$ is Gaussian in the angular
  sector (Step~5a) and
  $Z_{\mathrm{CS}}$ is one-loop exact (Step~5b).
  The Gaussian/one-loop values coincide (Steps~1--4).
  Therefore $Z_{\mathrm{vortex}} = Z_{\mathrm{CS}}$
  at leading order.
  The $O(1/c)$ correction has two sources on the
  vortex side: (i)~breathing-mode anharmonicity
  ($\rho$-fluctuations around the BO saddle, suppressed
  by $1/c$ from the WDW kinetic coefficient;
  this becomes the cosmological sector of Paper~VI) and
  (ii)~the $W_N$ quartic coupling between angular modes
  (suppressed by $\beta = 12/c$,
  eq.~\eqref{eq:quartic-suppression};
  this becomes the matter sector of Paper~IV).
  Pure CS gravity, being topological, has no
  propagating degrees of freedom and hence no
  such corrections (one-loop exact, Step~5b).
  The combined vortex correction is $O(1/c)$,
  giving eq.~\eqref{eq:Z-equality}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the correspondence holds at $O(c^0)$]
\label{rmk:why-theorem}
For a generic pair of systems, eigenvalue matching
(Steps~1--4) would not imply partition function
equality: higher-order terms could differ.
Two features make the present case special at
leading order:
(i)~the CMS integrability makes the vortex angular
Hamiltonian linear in the action variables at leading order,
with the quartic correction $\delta H = O(1)$ entering the
partition function suppressed by
$\beta = 12/c$ (Step~5a,
eq.~\eqref{eq:quartic-suppression});
(ii)~CS is one-loop exact (Step~5b): no instantons
($\pi_3 = 0$) and the perturbative coefficients
are shared between $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ by complexification.
At $O(c^0)$, both sides equal the Gaussian/one-loop
value, and the one-loop values match (Steps~1--4).
The $O(1/c)$ correction in eq.~\eqref{eq:Z-equality}
is the \emph{matter and cosmological content}
of the theory.
Pure $2{+}1$D CS gravity has no propagating degrees
of freedom; the vortex system does.
The two sources of the $O(1/c)$ correction map onto
the two sectors developed in the subsequent papers:
\begin{itemize}
\item The $W_N$ quartic coupling between angular modes
  is the self-interaction of the KK matter fields
  (gauge bosons and fermions; Paper~IV).
\item The breathing-mode anharmonicity is the
  cosmological sector
  (the BO potential that determines $H_0$,
  $\Lambda$, and the dark-matter spectrum; Paper~VI).
\end{itemize}
The partition function therefore factorizes as
$Z_{\mathrm{vortex}}
= Z_{\mathrm{pure\,gravity}}
\times (1 + Z_{\mathrm{matter+cosmo}})$,
where $Z_{\mathrm{pure\,gravity}} = Z_{\mathrm{CS}}$
(leading order) and the matter$+$cosmological
content enters at $O(1/c)$.
At $N = 7$, the combined correction is $1/c \approx 2\%$,
smaller than the precision of any prediction derived
from the correspondence.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Global symplectic embedding]
\label{prop:global-symplectic}
The vortex--gravity identification extends beyond
the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ equilibrium.
Three independent results establish a global correspondence
between the vortex configuration space and the
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ CS moduli space:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Developing map.}
  Each configuration of~$N$ equal-circulation vortices
  on~$\mathbf{H}^2$ determines a cone metric on a punctured
  surface~$\Sigma_{g,N}$ with deficit angles
  $\delta_k = 8\pi G\kappa$
  (Troyanov uniformization,~\citeyear{Troyanov1991}).
  The developing map of this cone metric gives a
  holonomy representation
  $\pi_1(\Sigma_{g,N}) \to \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$,
  embedding the vortex configuration space into the
  character variety
  $\mathcal{X}(\Sigma_{g,N},\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}))$.
  The Hitchin component of~$\mathcal{X}$ is
  diffeomorphic to the Teichm\"uller space
  $\mathcal{T}(\Sigma_{g,N})$
  (\citealt{Goldman1980}; \citealt{Hitchin1992}).
\item \emph{Symplectic compatibility.}
  The Weil--Petersson Poisson structure on
  $\mathcal{T}(\Sigma_{g,N})$ (induced by the cone
  metric) equals the Goldman Poisson structure on
  $\mathcal{X}(\Sigma_{g,N},\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}))$
  (induced by the CS symplectic form),
  for all cone angles $< 2\pi$
  (\citealt{Mondello2009}).
  The two sides of the correspondence therefore have the
  same phase space geometry: Poisson brackets of
  observables agree on the full moduli space,
  not only at the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ equilibrium.
\item \emph{Hamiltonian matching.}
  In $2{+}1$D gravity with $\Lambda < 0$, the reduced
  particle Hamiltonian
  (after solving the CS flatness constraint)
  is a functional of the Liouville accessory parameters
  of the punctured surface
  (\citealt{DeserJackiwHooft1984};
  \citealt{CantiniMenotti2003}).
  The Kirchhoff vortex Hamiltonian
  $H = -\sum\kappa^2\,G_{\mathbf{H}^2}(z_i, z_j)$
  is a functional of the same Green's function on
  the same punctured surface.
  Both are first-order Hamiltonians
  (Kirchhoff: $\dot{\bar z}_k \propto \partial H/\partial z_k$;
  CS: $\dot{A} \propto F(A)$) on the same Teichm\"uller
  phase space with the same symplectic structure.
  At the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ equilibrium, the accessory
  parameters reduce to the Havelock eigenvalues
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:casimir-havelock}).
  The first nonlinear correction is $O(1/c)$
  from the $W_N$ quartic
  (eq.~\eqref{eq:quartic-suppression}).
\end{enumerate}
The Platonic extensions of Paper~I provide a
cross-surface consistency check:
on $\mathbf{S}^2$, the generalized Havelock formula
(Paper~I, Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:platonic-havelock})
gives the regular-polyhedron eigenvalues;
the bridge identity (Paper~I, Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:bridge})
connects $\mathbf{S}^2$ and~$\mathbf{H}^2$ eigenvalues
through the same $\csc^2$ kernel.
The ADE classification of the Platonic eigenvalues
matches the McKay correspondence for the corresponding
CS orbifold theories, extending the correspondence
from the $N$-gon on $\mathbf{H}^2$ to
all regular polytopes on all constant-curvature surfaces.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
The global symplectic embedding upgrades the correspondence
from ``eigenvalue matching at the $\mathbb{Z}_N$
equilibrium'' to ``phase-space isomorphism with matching
symplectic form, matching Green's function, and matching
linearized Hamiltonian, with $O(1/c)$ nonlinear correction.''
The $O(1/c)$ correction is not a gap in the correspondence
but its physical content:
the $W_N$ quartic is the matter self-coupling
(Paper~IV) and the breathing-mode anharmonicity
is the cosmological sector (Paper~VI).
Pure CS gravity, having no propagating degrees of freedom,
contains neither; the vortex system contains both.

Setting $j(j{+}1) = m(N{-}m)/2$ determines $j$ for each mode.

No other $N \le 30$ gives spin $j = 2$ at the critical mode.

The total $\csc^2$ sum

\begin{proposition}[Graviton identification]
\label{prop:graviton}
$N = 7$ is the unique polygon number $N \ge 3$ for which the
critical Havelock Casimir $f(\lfloor N/2\rfloor, N)$
equals $j(j{+}1)$ with $j = 2$ (the graviton spin).
Higher Pell solutions ($N = 41$, $j = 14$; $N = 239$, $j = 84$; \ldots)
give $j > 2$ and lie outside the stability range $N \le 7$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For even $N = 2k$: $f(k, 2k) = k^2/2$.
Then $j(j{+}1) = k^2/2$ gives
$j = (-1 + \sqrt{1 + 2k^2})/2$.
This is an integer iff $1 + 2k^2$ is a perfect square,
i.e.\ $n^2 - 2k^2 = 1$ (Pell equation).
Solutions: $(n,k) = (1,0), (3,2), (17,12), \ldots$
No even~$N$ yields $j = 2$: at $k = 2$ ($N = 4$), $j = 1$;
at $k = 12$ ($N = 24$), $j = 8$.
Setting $j = 2$ requires $n = 5$, giving $25 - 2k^2 = 1$,
i.e.\ $k^2 = 12$, which has no integer solution.

For odd $N = 2k{+}1$: $f(k, 2k{+}1) = k(k{+}1)/2$.
Then $j(j{+}1) = k(k{+}1)/2$ gives $j = (-1 + \sqrt{1+2k(k{+}1)})/2$.
At $k = 3$ ($N = 7$): $j(j{+}1) = 6$, $j = 2$.
At $k = 4$ ($N = 9$): $j(j{+}1) = 10$, $j \approx 2.70$ (not integer).
At $k = 5$ ($N = 11$): $j(j{+}1) = 15$, $j \approx 3.41$.
For $4 \le k \le 19$: $j$ is irrational (the next Pell
solution is $k = 20$, $N = 41$, $j = 14$).
Setting $y = 2j{+}1$ and $N = 2k{+}1$, the integer-spin
condition becomes the negative Pell equation $N^2 - 2y^2 = -1$.
The solutions are generated by $\varepsilon = 1 + \sqrt{2}$:
$(N,y) = (1,1),\, (7,5),\, (41,29),\, \ldots$,
giving $j = 0,\, 2,\, 14,\, \ldots$
The first non-trivial solution is $N = 7$, $j = 2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Physical content of the operator coincidence]
\label{rmk:cms-cs-physical}
The numerical verification
($N = 3,\ldots,12$; see Code Availability)
confirms the eigenvalue identity $C_2^{\mathrm{CMS}} =
C_2^{\mathrm{KZ}} = C_2^{\mathrm{CS}} = m(N{-}m)/2$.
Operator coincidence is necessary but not sufficient
in general dimensions.
In $2{+}1$D, it \emph{is} sufficient because
the Weyl tensor vanishes identically, so there are
\emph{no propagating gravitational modes}.
The only gravitational degrees of freedom are
\emph{boundary modes} (Brown--Henneaux 1986).
By the CS/WZW theorem \citep{Witten1988}, the boundary
modes of the CS gauge field are the WZW currents, which
are the polygon modes (Paper~IV, Theorem~\ref*{IV-thm:su3}, Step~6).
In $2{+}1$D, the boundary theory of CS gravity and the
Kirchhoff/Havelock vortex system have the same spectrum:
the CMS/CS operator coincidence is a spectral
isomorphism (eigenvalue matching on each
one-dimensional $\mathbb{Z}_N$ irreducible).
The dynamical identification (that both systems evolve
under the same Hamiltonian flow) follows from the
CS/WZW theorem: the boundary Hamiltonian of CS gravity
IS the Kirchhoff Hamiltonian of the vortex system,
because both are generated by the $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$
Casimir on the same $\mathbf{H}^2$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:casimir-havelock}).
The spectral match implies the dynamical match
\emph{in $2{+}1$D specifically}, because there are no
bulk propagating modes to break the correspondence.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The Chern--Simons lift.}
In $2{+}1$ dimensions, gravity with $\Lambda < 0$ is a
Chern--Simons gauge theory with group
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) \times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$
and level
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cs-level}
  k = \frac{\ell}{4G} = \frac{c}{6},
\end{equation}
where the Brown--Henneaux relation $c = 6k$ gives
$k = 2\,b(N)$ for the exact central charge $c = 12\,b(N)$
(with $b(N) = N(N{+}1)/12 - \ln 2 + (\ln N)/(N{-}1)$;
the proof via mean Casimir and the Gauss product
$\prod_{p=1}^{N-1}\sin(\pi p/N) = N/2^{N-1}$
is in Paper~I, \S5).
At leading order $c \approx N^2$, so $k \approx N^2/6$.
The CS level $k = c/6$ is not integer for most~$N$.
This is consistent:
$\pi_3(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})) = 0$
\citep[\S3]{Witten1988}, so no level quantization applies;
for the compact form $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, the partition
function $Z(k)$ is entire in~$k$ with nearest singularity
at $k = -h^\vee = -2$ \citep{Witten1991};
for the non-compact $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$,
the analytic continuation in~$k$ is controlled by
the Plancherel measure of the continuous series
\citep{Witten1991}
(the Plancherel density
$\mu(s) = s\tanh(\pi s)$ is entire in~$s$,
so $Z(k)$ extends meromorphically to $k \in \mathbb{C}$
with poles only at $k = -h^\vee + n$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\le 0}$);
the semiclassical expansion has corrections
$c_2^{\mathrm{adj}}/(12k^2) = 2/(12k^2) = 1/(6k^2)$
from the one-loop adjoint Casimir ($c_2 = 2$ for
$\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$),
giving $0.23\%$ at $N = 7$ ($k = 2\,b(7) = 8.6$).
The compact gauge sectors ($\mathrm{SU}(2)_1$,
$\mathrm{SU}(3)_1$) have integer levels;
only the gravitational $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ sector
carries the irrational level $k = 2b(N)$, which is
well-defined for the non-compact group.
The vortex polygon is a \emph{Wilson line}
$W_m = \mathrm{Tr}_{R_m}\,\mathcal{P}\exp\!\oint\! A$
in the representation $R_m$ with Casimir $f(m,N)$.
The Havelock eigenvalue is the Wilson line expectation
$\langle W_m \rangle = e^{-\lambda_m L}$; the three-layer
decomposition corresponds to
bulk (cosmological) $\times$ representation (matter) $\times$
one-loop (quantum) factors.
The CS partition function on Seifert manifolds is computed
via Stieltjes--Wigert polynomials \citep{Tierz2006},
linking the Havelock eigenvalue sequence to the
log-normal moment problem.

\subsection{Statement and prerequisites}

The Havelock eigenvalue structure characterizes the vacuum
Einstein equations.
In $d = 2$ ($2{+}1$D), the three conditions below follow
from the vortex dynamics (two by theorem, one by the Onsager
principle via the vortex-gravity isomorphism):
the Einstein equations emerge as the entropy-maximizing
geometry, not as an additional input.
In $d \ge 3$ ($3{+}1$D and higher), condition~(H1)
becomes \emph{equivalent} to the vacuum Einstein equations
(see Remark~\ref{rmk:higher-d}); the theorem then provides a new
characterization of Einstein manifolds via polygon stability, rather
than a one-directional derivation.
The full $4$D Riemann tensor (including all Weyl components)
is determined by~$N$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:full-riemann}).
We state the theorem first, then collect the proof.

\begin{remark}[Logical status of conditions (H1)--(H3)]
\label{rmk:logical-status}
Each condition is a mathematical theorem about
$2$D Riemannian geometry.
Their gravitational interpretation requires the
structural identification of polygon stability with
spacetime curvature (the foundational chain,
\S\ref{sec:graviton}).
\textbf{(H1)} and~\textbf{(H3)}: $\csc^2$ uniqueness
and Hadamard parametrix (standard).
\textbf{(H2a)}: strict concavity of~$S$ in~$C_1$
($d^2S/dC_1^2 = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum 1/\lambda_m^2 < 0$,
a finite-$N$ algebraic identity).
\textbf{(H2b)}: the entropy maximum is at $R = \mathrm{const}$
(Jensen's inequality on the constrained integral
$\int S(R(x))\,dA$).

The functional form $S_{\mathrm{poly}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{m \neq m^*}
\log\lambda_m$ is the microcanonical entropy of the
CMS angular modes in action-angle variables.
For an integrable system with frequencies $\omega_m = \lambda_m$,
the classical phase-space volume at energy~$E$ is
$\Omega(E) \propto E^n/\!\prod\omega_m$
(the volume of the simplex $\{I_m \ge 0,\,\sum\omega_m I_m \le E\}$),
so $\log\Omega = n\log E - \sum\log\omega_m + \mathrm{const}$.
The $\omega_m$-dependent part is $-\sum\log\lambda_m$;
the factor $1/2$ accounts for the palindromic pairing
$\lambda_m = \lambda_{N-m}$ (each real degree of freedom
contributes $\tfrac{1}{2}\log$).
In the $N \to \infty$ limit,
$S_{\mathrm{poly}} \to \log\det'\Delta + \mathrm{const}$
(Polyakov formula), confirming the functional form
independently.

The derivation of $R = \mathrm{const}$ requires
\emph{no smooth-metric assumption}.
The polygon entropy is a finite sum over $N{-}1$ modes.
Its strict concavity in~$C_1$ is algebraic (H2a).
The Hadamard expansion $C_1 = (N{-}1)[1 - R\varepsilon^2/4 + \cdots]$
makes $S$ strictly concave in~$R$ (H1$+$H3).
Jensen's inequality then gives: the unique maximum of
$\int S(R(x))\,dA$ subject to $\int R\,dA = 4\pi\chi$
(Gauss--Bonnet) is $R(x) = \mathrm{const}$.
This argument works for \emph{any} Riemannian metric
on a compact surface, including Regge metrics with $N$
conical singularities, because the Green's function,
the Hadamard parametrix, and the eigenvalues~$\lambda_m$
are all well-defined for cone metrics.

\emph{Three independent confirmations:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Jensen (primary, finite~$N$).}
  The argument above.  No smooth-metric limit needed.
\item \emph{OPS cross-check (smooth limit).}
  In the smooth ($N \to \infty$) limit,
  $S_{\mathrm{poly}} \to \log\det'\Delta + \mathrm{const}$
  (Polyakov formula, \citealp{Polyakov1981}),
  and the \citet{OsgoodPhillipsSarnak1988} theorem
  independently proves $\det'\Delta$ is maximized
  at $R = \mathrm{const}$.
  This confirms the finite-$N$ result in the
  continuous limit.
\item \emph{Onsager contraction (constructive, finite~$N$).}
  Corollary~\ref{cor:onsager-contraction}
  proves ${\ge}\,95\%$ variance reduction per iteration
  at $N = 7$ (contraction factor $0.042$ via the sharp Sobolev bound,
  or $0.044$ via the coarser $L^\infty$ bound; explicit in both cases).
  No smooth-metric assumption.
\end{enumerate}
The Onsager principle
\citep{Onsager1949,CagliotiFLMP1992,Kiessling1993}
provides the \emph{physical interpretation}:
the microcanonical ensemble at negative temperature
selects the entropy maximum.
No circularity arises: the derivation of
$R = \mathrm{const}$ uses (H1)--(H3), all
mathematical theorems about 2D Riemannian geometry.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Onsager prerequisites for the Regge system]
\label{lem:onsager-regge}
Let $S$ be a compact surface and $\mathcal{T}$ a
triangulation with $V$ vertices whose deficit angles
are all of the same sign
(positive curvature: $\chi > 0$; or all negative:
$\chi < 0$ on a compact quotient of~$\mathbf{H}^2$).
The Regge polygon system satisfies the three prerequisites
of the Onsager variational principle:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Compact phase space.}
  The configuration space $S^V$ is compact ($S$ compact).
  The non-degeneracy conditions (positive edge lengths
  and triangle areas) define an open dense subset, so
  the phase-space measure is finite.
\item \emph{Energy bounded above.}
  The vortex energy
  $H = -\sum_{i < j}\kappa_i\kappa_j\,G_S(z_i, z_j)$
  is bounded above on $S^V$ because $G_S$ is bounded
  below on any compact surface (the Green's function has
  only a logarithmic singularity at coincident points,
  which is integrable in 2D).
\item \emph{Negative temperature.}
  By (1) and (2), the density of states
  $\Omega(E) = \mathrm{Vol}(\{z : H(z) = E\})$
  is a decreasing function of~$E$ for $E > E^*$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{The Kiessling hypothesis, verified.}
\citet{Kiessling1993} proves
that $\Omega(E)$ is decreasing above a threshold $E^*$ for
any system of $N$ point particles with logarithmic
pair interaction on a compact $2$D domain.
His hypotheses are:
(H.K1)~$N$ point particles on a compact Riemannian
$2$-manifold~$\Sigma$ (or bounded domain with Dirichlet
boundary);
(H.K2)~pair interaction $V(x,y)$ with a logarithmic
singularity at $x = y$ and smooth elsewhere;
(H.K3)~bounded-above total energy on the configuration
manifold.
For the Regge system: (H.K1) holds with the vertices as
point particles on $S$; (H.K2) holds because the Laplacian
Green's function on any compact $2$-surface has the
universal expansion
$G_S(x,y) = -(1/2\pi)\log d(x,y) + \gamma_S(x,y)$
with $\gamma_S$ smooth, so the pair interaction
$-\kappa_i\kappa_j G_S(z_i,z_j)$ has exactly the logarithmic
singularity required; (H.K3) is (2) above.
Kiessling's theorem applies directly: its proof uses only
the static microcanonical measure on the compact
configuration space, not the Kirchhoff equations of motion,
and the hypotheses do not distinguish between vortex
circulations and Regge deficit angles (both act as
point sources of a 2D Laplacian Green's function).
The microcanonical temperature
$\beta = d\log\Omega/dE$ is therefore negative for
$E > E^*$, and the Onsager variational principle applies
to the Regge system.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Entropy variation forces constant curvature]
\label{prop:variation}
Fix the polygon radius~$\varepsilon$ (a geodesic length,
held constant as the conformal factor varies).
In 2D conformal gauge $g = e^{2\varphi}\,g_0$,
$\delta R = -(2R + 2\Delta_g)\,\delta\varphi$ and
$\delta(dA) = 2\,\delta\varphi\,dA$,
where $\Delta_g$ is the Laplace--Beltrami operator.
Using $dS/dC_1 = (1/2)\sum 1/\lambda_m$ and
$dC_1/dR = -(N{-}1)\varepsilon^2/4$ (from the Hadamard
expansion at fixed~$\varepsilon$),
the functional derivative is
\[
  \frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{total}}}{\delta\varphi(x)}
  = \frac{(N{-}1)\varepsilon^2}{4}
  \bigl(2R(x) + 2\Delta_g\bigr)
  \frac{1}{2}\sum\frac{1}{\lambda_m(x)}
  + 2\,S(x).
\]
On a closed surface the Laplacian term integrates to
zero: $\int\Delta_g(\cdot)\,dA = 0$.
At a critical point of $S_{\mathrm{total}}$
(which is the unique maximum by strict concavity,
$d^2S/dC_1^2 < 0$),
$\delta S_{\mathrm{total}}/\delta\varphi = \Lambda_\varphi$
(constant) for all~$x$; the pointwise equation
$F(R(x)) + (\text{Laplacian term}) = \Lambda_\varphi$
with $F$ strictly monotone ($dS/dC_1 > 0$, $dC_1/dR < 0$)
and elliptic regularity forces
$R(x) = \mathrm{const}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Polygon entropy maximization implies constant curvature]
\label{thm:polygon-einstein}
Let $(S, g)$ be a two-dimensional Riemannian manifold.
Place $N$ equal point vortices in a regular polygon ring
of geodesic radius~$\varepsilon$ centered at~$x \in S$,
interacting via the scalar Green's function of~$(S, g)$.
If:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(H1)}]
  \emph{Havelock structure} :
  the angular Hessian eigenvalues
  have the form $\lambda_m = C_1(x,\varepsilon) - m(N{-}m)/2
  + \delta_m(x,\varepsilon)$
  with $C_1$ independent of~$m$ and
  $\delta_m = 0$ for all interior modes $2 \le m \le N{-}2$.
  The only mode-dependent corrections are confined to the
  edge modes $m \in \{1, N{-}1\}$, where
  $|\delta_m| = O(K\varepsilon^2/C_1) \to 0$ as
  $\varepsilon \to 0$.

  \emph{Proof on general surfaces.}
  On any $2$D Riemannian manifold, the Green's function in
  normal coordinates at~$x$ has the Hadamard expansion
  $G(z,w) = -(1/2\pi)\ln|z{-}w| + \gamma(x)
  + (K(x)/12\pi)|z{-}w|^2 + O(|z{-}w|^4|\nabla K|)$,
  where $K(x)$ is the Gaussian curvature.
  The $-\ln|z{-}w|$ term gives $m(N{-}m)/2$
  (Paper~I, Lemma~\ref*{I-lem:havelock}).
  The constant $\gamma(x)$ has no tangential derivatives.
  For the curvature term: $|z_j{-}z_k|^2 = 4\varepsilon^2
  \sin^2(\pi(j{-}k)/N)$ at the ring, giving
  a circulant with entries
  $c_p = -(K\varepsilon^2/(6\pi))\cos(2\pi p/N)$.
  The DFT eigenvalue at mode~$m$ is
  \[
    \delta T_m
    = -\frac{K\varepsilon^2}{6\pi}
    \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\cos(2\pi p/N)\,
    \bigl(1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N)\bigr).
  \]
  Expanding the product and using
  $\sum_p \cos(2\pi(1{\pm}m)p/N) = -1$
  for $2 \le m \le N{-}2$
  (since $1{\pm}m$ is not divisible by~$N$):
  $\delta T_m = 0$ for all $2 \le m \le N{-}2$.

  The edge modes $m = 1$ and $m = N{-}1$ receive
  $\delta T_1 = K(x) N\varepsilon^2/(12\pi)$,
  which shifts $C_1$ at these two modes only.
  Since $\lambda_1 = C_1 - (N{-}1)/2$ is the
  largest eigenvalue ($\lambda_1 \ge (N{-}1)/2$ on the
  flat plane), the fractional correction is
  $O(K\varepsilon^2/C_1)$, negligible for small~$\varepsilon$.

  The first \emph{mode-dependent} correction to the
  interior eigenvalues enters at
  $O(\varepsilon^4\,|\nabla K|)$, two orders beyond
  the leading Havelock term.
  On constant-curvature surfaces ($\nabla K = 0$),
  $\delta_m = 0$ exactly for all~$m$
  (Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:riemannian-havelock}).
\item[\textup{(H2)}]
  \emph{Entropy maximization}
  \textbf{[strict concavity $+$ Jensen, finite~$N$]}:
  the polygon entropy functional
  $S_{\mathrm{poly}}[g] = \int S(x)\,dA$, where
  $S(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{m \ne m^*}\log\lambda_m(x)$
  is a finite sum over $N{-}2$ binding modes
  (excluding the critical mode $m^*$ where $\lambda_{m^*} = 0$
  at the threshold $N = 7$).
  On the domain $\mathcal{B} = \{g : \|\delta R\|_\infty \le 0.90\}$
  (established below as the Onsager contraction basin),
  all $\lambda_m$ with $m \ne m^*$ are bounded away from zero:
  $\min_{m \ne m^*} \lambda_m \ge (N{-}1) - m^*(N{-}m^*)/2 - \|\delta R\|_\infty/2
  \ge c > 0$.
  On~$\mathcal{B}$, $S$~is \emph{strictly concave} in~$R(x)$:
  $S$ is strictly concave in~$C_1$
  ($d^2S/dC_1^2 = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum 1/\lambda_m^2 < 0$,
  algebraic at finite~$N$),
  and $C_1(x) = (N{-}1)[1 - R(x)\varepsilon^2/4 + O(\varepsilon^4)]$
  is affine in~$R(x)$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:log-interaction}),
  so $S(C_1(R))$ is strictly concave in~$R$.
  Subject to the Gauss--Bonnet constraint
  $\int R\,dA = 4\pi\chi$ (scalar curvature convention,
  $R = 2K$),
  the unique maximum is $R(x) = \mathrm{const}$
  (Jensen's inequality).
  This argument works for \emph{any} Riemannian metric,
  including Regge metrics with $N$ conical singularities:
  the Green's function, the Hadamard parametrix, and the
  eigenvalues~$\lambda_m$ are all well-defined for cone
  metrics (Remark~\ref{rmk:logical-status}).
\item[\textup{(H3)}]
  \emph{Hadamard universality} :
  condition~\textup{(H1)} holds
  at every point~$x \in S$.
  The Hadamard parametrix
  $G(x,y) = -(1/2\pi)\log d(x,y) + \gamma_S(x) + O(d^2)$
  is universal on any Riemannian surface.
\end{enumerate}
Then the scalar curvature of~$S$ is constant:
$R(x) = R_0$ for all~$x$.
In $2{+}1$D, this gives the vacuum Einstein equations
$R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}$ (Schur's lemma;
the Weyl tensor vanishes identically in three dimensions).
\end{theorem}

\noindent
The cosmological constant $\Lambda$ is determined by a
separate dynamical argument (the WDW equation,
Proposition~\ref{prop:wdw-lambda} below), not by
the theorem above.
The theorem establishes the \emph{form} of the Einstein
equations; the WDW equation determines their
\emph{content} ($\Lambda$).

\subsection{Proof of the main theorem}

\paragraph{Proof architecture.}
The proof assembles three ingredients:
(1)~Lemma~\ref{lem:log-interaction} shows $C_1$ is
affine in~$R(x)$ via the Hadamard expansion;
(2)~Lemma~\ref{lem:regge-bridge} bridges discrete
Regge vertices to smooth metrics;
(3)~Corollary~\ref{cor:onsager-contraction} shows the
Onsager iteration contracts curvature variance
(factor~$0.042$ at $N = 7$).
The main proof (Theorem~\ref{thm:polygon-einstein},
page~\pageref{thm:polygon-einstein}) combines them via
Jensen's inequality and the Robin-function identity
$\Delta\gamma = K - \bar{K}$.
The reader may proceed directly to the main proof
and consult the lemmas as needed.
The ingredients are stated first:

\begin{corollary}[Onsager contraction, (H2b) as theorem]
\label{cor:onsager-contraction}
Define the Onsager iteration $T\colon g \mapsto g'$ on
compact metrics by:
(i)~compute the Onsager mean-field \emph{density}
$\rho(x)$ of $N$ equal-circulation vortices on~$(S,g)$
at negative temperature
(exists by the Onsager--Regge Lemma);
(ii)~map $\rho(x)$ to a curvature field $R'(x) = \delta \times \rho(x)$
via the vortex-gravity isomorphism (equal circulations
$\Rightarrow$ equal deficit angles $\Rightarrow$
$R \propto \rho$);
(iii)~define~$g' = T(g)$ as the metric with curvature~$R'$.

\emph{The iteration operates on the scalar field $R(x)$,
not on polygon geometry.}
The $N$ vortices are individual point masses whose positions
are drawn from the mean-field density~$\rho(x)$;
they do \emph{not} form a regular $N$-gon
(except on a constant-curvature surface, where $\rho = \mathrm{const}$
and the entropy-maximizing configuration is the polygon).
The energy landscape $V(x)$ that determines~$\rho$
depends on the local curvature~$R(x)$ through the
Hadamard parametrix $C_1(x) = (N{-}1)[1 - R(x)\varepsilon^2/4 + \cdots]$, a property of the Green's function of~$(S,g)$ at the point~$x$,
not of any global vortex configuration.

Then for all initial metrics with
$\mathrm{Var}(R(g)) \le 2$,
the curvature variance contracts:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onsager-contraction}
  \mathrm{Var}(R(T(g)))
  \le |\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}|^2 R_0^2\,\mathrm{Var}(R(g)),
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1/f(m^*,N)$ is the inverse
critical Casimir (equal to $1/C_1$ at the stability threshold),
$R_0 = 2\pi\chi/\mathrm{Vol}$ is the mean curvature
($|R_0| = 1$ for the Bolza surface), and
$V_1, V_2$ are defined in the proof below.
At~$N = 7$: $|\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}| R_0 = 1/f(3,7) = 1/6$,
giving contraction factor $(1/6)^2 \times e^{0.30} \times 1.12 = 0.042$
on the Bolza surface (Sobolev bound in the proof below;
the cruder basin estimate $\mathrm{Var} \le 2$ gives $0.044$).
The contraction improves with~$N$: $|\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}| R_0
= 1/8$ at $N = 8$, $1/15$ at $N = 11$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Onsager equilibrium distributes vortices with mean-field
density $\rho(x) \propto \exp(\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}\,V(x))$,
where $V(x) = V(C_1(R(x)))$ is the BO potential and
$\beta_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1/C_1$ is the inverse stiffness
(the angular bath's response to curvature perturbations is
controlled by $C_1 = f(m^*,N)$, not by the total energy).
For a perturbation~$\delta R$ around mean~$R_0$:
$\rho(x) = \rho_0\exp\!\bigl(\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}
(V_1\,\delta R + \tfrac{1}{2}V_2\,\delta R^2)\bigr)$
where $V_1 = \coth(\rho_*) = C_1'(\rho_*)$ and
$V_2 = -\operatorname{csch}^2(\rho_*) = C_1''(\rho_*)$,
evaluated at the palindromic threshold~$\rho_*$
($\rho_* = 1.73$ at $N = 7$, $2.40$ at $N = 8$).
The $N$ vortices have \emph{equal} circulations~$\kappa$,
so each deficit angle $\delta_k = 8\pi G\kappa$ is identical.
The new curvature field is therefore
$R'(x) = \delta \times \rho(x)$
where $\delta = 8\pi G\kappa$ is the deficit angle per vortex
(fixed by the identification;
Gauss--Bonnet $\int R\,dA = 8\pi(1{-}g)$ constrains
the mean density $\rho_0 = R_0/\delta$, and the
fluctuation $\delta R = R - R_0$ satisfies
$\int \delta R\,dA = 0$, so the contraction
operates on the zero-mean fluctuation field).
The new curvature variance is $\mathrm{Var}(R') = \delta^2\,\mathrm{Var}(\rho)$.
Expanding $\rho = \rho_0\,e^{\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}(V_1\delta R + V_2\delta R^2/2)}$
to second order in~$\delta R$ and bounding the variance of
the exponential by the elementary second-order Taylor estimate
\[
  \mathrm{Var}(e^{aY})
  \;\le\; a^2\,e^{2|a|\,\|Y\|_{L^\infty}}\,\mathrm{Var}(Y),
\]
valid for any bounded random variable~$Y$ with $|Y| \le \|Y\|_{L^\infty}$
(this is a direct consequence of $|e^{aY}-1-aY|\le\tfrac12 a^2 Y^2
e^{|a||Y|}$; see \citealt{BoucheronLugosiMassart2013}, \S2.3).
On a compact surface $\delta R = R - R_0$ is bounded with
$\|\delta R\|_{L^\infty} \le 2C_S$ (Sobolev embedding;
see below), so this bound applies.
Setting $Y = V_1\,\delta R + \tfrac12 V_2\,\delta R^2$
and $a = \beta_{\mathrm{eff}}$, we bound $\mathrm{Var}(Y)$
via the $L^2$ triangle inequality:
$\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(Y)}
\le |V_1|\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\delta R)}
+ \tfrac12 |V_2|\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\delta R^2)}$.
The $L^\infty$/$L^2$ interpolation
$\mathrm{Var}(\delta R^2) = E[\delta R^4] - (E[\delta R^2])^2
\le E[\delta R^4] \le \|\delta R\|_{L^\infty}^2\,\mathrm{Var}(\delta R)$
gives
$\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\delta R^2)}
\le \|\delta R\|_{L^\infty}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\delta R)}$.
Squaring the triangle inequality:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:var-Y-bound}
  \mathrm{Var}(Y)
  \le V_1^2\,\mathrm{Var}(\delta R)\,
  \Bigl(1 + \frac{|V_2|}{2|V_1|}\|\delta R\|_{L^\infty}\Bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
Combining with the Taylor-Lipschitz bound:
\[
  \mathrm{Var}(R') \;\le\;
  |\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}|^2 R_0^2\,
  e^{2|\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}|\,\|Y\|_{L^\infty}}\,
  \mathrm{Var}(\delta R)\,
  \Bigl(1 + \frac{|V_2|}{2|V_1|}\|\delta R\|_{L^\infty}\Bigr)^2.
\]
At~$N = 7$: $|\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}| R_0 = 1/6$,
$|V_2/V_1| = |\operatorname{csch}^2(\rho^*)/\coth(\rho^*)| = 0.125$
(with $\rho^* = 1.734$, the WDW turning point of
Proposition~\ref*{III-prop:equiv-rr}).
Within the basin ($\mathrm{Var} \le 2$, $\|\delta R\|_\infty \le 0.90$):
$\|Y\|_{L^\infty} \le |V_1|\cdot 0.90 + \tfrac12|V_2|\cdot 0.81
= 0.958 + 0.054 = 1.012$,
so $e^{2|\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}|\,\|Y\|_\infty} \le e^{0.337} = 1.40$.
The nonlinear factor
$(1 + (0.125/2)\cdot 0.90)^2 = (1.056)^2 = 1.12$,
giving total contraction
$(1/6)^2 \times 1.40 \times 1.12 = 0.044 < 1$.
The condition $|\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}| R_0 < 1$ is
$f(m^*,N) > |R_0|$, which holds for all $N \ge 4$
(since $f(m^*,N) \ge 2$ and $|R_0| = 1$ on the Bolza surface).
The contraction holds for all
initial $\mathrm{Var}(R) \le 2$,
establishing $R = \mathrm{const}$ as a locally attracting
fixed point with basin $\mathrm{Var}(R) \le 2$.
On the Bolza surface ($|R_0| = 1$, $\mathrm{Var}(R_0) = 0$),
the basin is $\mathrm{Var}(R) \le 2$ (an $L^2$ condition).
On the Bolza surface ($\mathrm{Vol} = 4\pi$,
$\lambda_1 = 3.839$; \citealt{Buser1992}), the
heat-kernel Sobolev bound at optimal time
$t = 1/\lambda_1$ gives, for any zero-mean
$u \in H^1(\Sigma)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sobolev-heat}
  \|u\|_{L^\infty}^2
  \le \frac{\lambda_1}{4\pi}\,\|u\|_{L^2}^2
  + \frac{1}{e\,\lambda_1}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2
  = 0.306\,\|u\|_{L^2}^2 + 0.096\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{equation}
Within the basin $\mathrm{Var}(R) \le 2$ and
$\|\nabla R\|_{L^2}^2 \le 2$
(both satisfied by metrics near the uniformized one):
$\|R - R_0\|_{L^\infty}^2 \le 0.306 \times 2 + 0.096 \times 2
= 0.803$, giving $\|R - R_0\|_\infty \le 0.90$
and Sobolev constant $C_S = 0.45$.
The $L^\infty$ bound is then
$\|\delta R\|_\infty \le 0.896$,
$\|Y\|_\infty \le 0.90$,
and the nonlinear factor (from~\eqref{eq:var-Y-bound})
becomes $(1 + (0.125/2)\cdot 0.896)^2 = (1.056)^2 = 1.12$,
tightening the contraction to
$(1/6)^2 \times e^{0.30} \times 1.12
= (1/6)^2 \times 1.35 \times 1.12 = 0.042 < 1$.
The Poincar\'e-uniformized metric ($R = \mathrm{const}$) has
$\mathrm{Var} = 0$, and the basin $\mathrm{Var} \le 2$
contains a metrically thick $H^1$ neighborhood:
$\|R - R_0\|_\infty \le 0.90 < |R_0| = 1$,
so the concavity domain ($\lambda_m > 0$ for all $m \ne m^*$)
is never exited within the basin.
The iteration $T^n(g_0) \to g^*$ converges geometrically
within this basin;
by~(H2a), $g^*$ has $R = \mathrm{const}$.

\emph{Extension to other compact surfaces.}
The Sobolev constant $C_S$ depends on the surface
through $\lambda_1$ and $\mathrm{Vol}$.
For any compact hyperbolic surface~$\Sigma$ of
genus~$g$ with $\lambda_1 > 1/4$
(proved for all arithmetic surfaces by Selberg~1965
and Gelbart--Jacquet~1978; the Bolza surface at $g = 2$
has $\lambda_1 = 3.839$, well above the bound):
$\lambda_1/(4\pi) \ge 1/(16\pi) \approx 0.020$
and $1/(e\lambda_1) \le 4/e \approx 1.47$.
The contraction constant
$|\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}|^2 R_0^2 \times
(1 + 2|V_2/V_1|\sqrt{\sigma^2})^2$
scales as $1/f(m^*,N)^2$, which is $\le 1/36$
at $N = 7$ regardless of the surface.
The basin condition $\mathrm{Var}(R) \le 2$ is
therefore sufficient on \emph{any} compact
hyperbolic surface with $\lambda_1 > 1/4$,
not just the Bolza surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Lax conservation alternative]
\label{rmk:lax-conservation}
The contraction can also be proved without the mean-field
(ergodic) step, using the CMS integrability of the
vortex system.
The Havelock Hessian is the CMS Hamiltonian
(Gaudin--Calogero--Moser identification); the
trigonometric CMS Lax matrix at the $N$-gon has
eigenvalues $\nu_m = g(2m{-}N{+}1)$ (equally spaced,
gap~$2g$), and these are conserved under the
CMS flow (isospectral evolution).
The BTZ phase requires vortex coalescence, which
forces $\|L\| \to \infty$ (the $\csc$ kernel diverges).
Since conserved Lax eigenvalues cannot diverge,

\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Logarithmic interaction and curvature expansion]
\label{lem:log-interaction}
Let $(S, g)$ satisfy conditions \textup{(H1)} and~\textup{(H3)}.
Then the vortex interaction is $V(z,w) = -G_S(z,w)$
(the scalar Laplacian Green's function), and the
mode-independent coefficient satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C1-expansion-proof}
  C_1(x, \varepsilon)
  = (N{-}1)\!\left[1 - \frac{R(x)\,\varepsilon^2}{4}
    + O(\varepsilon^4)\right],
\end{equation}
where $R(x)$ is the scalar curvature at~$x$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition~(H1) requires the angular Casimir
to be the exact polynomial $f(m,N) = m(N{-}m)/2$.
By the $\csc^2$ uniqueness theorem (Paper~I, Lemma~\ref*{I-lem:havelock}),
this polynomial arises \emph{only} from the weight function
$\csc^2(\pi p/N)$, which is the discrete Fourier transform
of the logarithmic kernel $-\log|z - w|$.
Condition~(H3) (Hadamard universality) ensures that the Laplacian
Green's function has exactly this logarithmic singularity on any
Riemannian surface.
Together, (H1)$+$(H3) force $V(z,w) = -G_S(z,w)$.

For the expansion: the Green's function has the short-distance
form $G_S(z,w) = -(1/2\pi)\log d(z,w) + \gamma_S(z) + O(d^2)$,
where $d(z,w)$ is the geodesic distance.
Summing over the $N$-ring at radius~$\varepsilon$ gives~\eqref{eq:C1-expansion-proof}.
The mode-independence of the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ correction is
\emph{automatic}: $C_1$ is mode-independent by the Riemannian
Havelock identity (Paper~I, Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:riemannian-havelock}),
so its expansion inherits this property at every order.
The coefficient $1/4$ is verified on the three model surfaces:
$\mathbf{H}^2$ ($R = -2$, $C_1/(N{-}1) = (1{+}\xi^2)/(1{-}\xi)^2$
with $\xi \approx \varepsilon^2/4$),
$\mathbf{S}^2$ ($R = +2$, $(1{-}\xi)/(1{+}\xi)$),
and the plane ($R = 0$, $C_1 = N{-}1$).
All three give $C_1/(N{-}1) = 1 - R\varepsilon^2/4 + O(\varepsilon^4)$
with the same coefficient $1/4$.
The Hadamard parametrix determines the
short-distance expansion of~$G$ through local curvature
invariants.  At $O(\varepsilon^2)$ the only
such invariant is~$R$, so $C_1/(N{-}1) = 1 - \alpha\,R\,\varepsilon^2 + \cdots$
for a universal constant~$\alpha$, and
matching any one model surface fixes $\alpha = 1/4$.
(The coefficient $1/4$ differs from the on-diagonal
Seeley--DeWitt value $a_1 = R/6$ because $C_1$ is the
\emph{off-diagonal} Green's function averaged over the
polygon chord geometry: the chord metric distortion
$d_p^2 = 4\varepsilon^2\sin^2(\pi p/N)
(1 - R\varepsilon^2\cos^2(\pi p/N)/6)$
and the Hadamard regular part each contribute, and their
angular averages combine to~$1/4$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Regge bridge to smooth metrics]
\label{lem:regge-bridge}
The entropy-maximizing Regge metric converges to the
entropy-maximizing smooth metric as the mesh size $h \to 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Any smooth 2D Riemannian manifold $(S, g)$ is
approximated by a simplicial complex $(S_h, g_h)$
in which curvature is concentrated at $V$ vertices,
each carrying a deficit angle
$\varepsilon_v \approx R(v)\,A_v$
(with $A_v$ the dual area).
The convergence $g_h \to g$ as $h \to 0$ is proved
\citep{CheegerMullerSchrader1984}; the Green's function
$G_h \to G$ in the $H^1$-norm (standard finite-element
convergence).
Each Regge vertex is a point vortex
(circulation $\kappa_v = \varepsilon_v/(2\pi)$,
by the isomorphism), so the Regge system is a
$V$-vortex configuration.
The vertex positions are \emph{simultaneously}
vortex positions and Regge metric degrees of freedom
(on a fixed triangulation, vertex positions determine
edge lengths, hence the simplicial metric).
This identification eliminates the apparent circularity
of~$H$: the Hamiltonian is a function of vertex positions
alone, and entropy maximization is a single finite-dimensional
optimization, not an iteration between metric and
vortex configuration.

The Onsager prerequisites for this Regge system
are established in Lemma~\ref{lem:onsager-regge}.
On a \emph{flat} background ($\Lambda = 0$),
conformal invariance
(Paper~II, Proposition~\ref{II-prop:conformal-invariance})
gives $G = -(2\pi)^{-1}\log|z{-}w|$,
independent of the deficit angles.
On a \emph{curved} background ($\Lambda \ne 0$),
the Hadamard expansion (Lemma~\ref{lem:log-interaction}) gives
$C_1 = (N{-}1)[1 - R(x)\varepsilon^2/4
+ \alpha_4 R^2\varepsilon^4 + O(\varepsilon^6)]$,
where $\alpha_4 = 1/48$ (the next Hadamard coefficient,
determined by the model-surface matching in
Lemma~\ref{lem:log-interaction}).
The entropy $S(C_1)$ is strictly concave in~$C_1$
($d^2S/dC_1^2 < 0$), and $C_1$ is a polynomial in~$R$
with leading term $-(N{-}1)\varepsilon^2 R/4$
and subleading correction $\alpha_4 R^2\varepsilon^4$.
By the chain rule, $d^2S/dR^2 = (dS/dC_1)(d^2C_1/dR^2)
+ (d^2S/dC_1^2)(dC_1/dR)^2$.
The first term has sign $(-)(+) = (-)$ (entropy increases
with $C_1$; the $R^2$ coefficient $\alpha_4 > 0$).
The second term is strictly negative.
So $d^2S/dR^2 < 0$ for all~$\varepsilon$:
strict concavity in~$R$ holds exactly, not just at
leading order.
The Gauss--Bonnet constraint $\int R\,dA = 4\pi\chi$
and Jensen's inequality give the unique maximum
at $R = \mathrm{const}$.

Given Lemma~\ref{lem:onsager-regge}, the Onsager
principle on the $V$-vertex Regge system selects the
entropy-maximizing vertex configuration, which by~(H2a)
has $C_1(v)$ uniform, hence $R = \mathrm{const}$.
In the continuum limit $V \to \infty$:
$S_{\mathrm{Regge}} \to S_{\mathrm{total}}[g]$
(by the FEM convergence of the Green's function,
sharpened to $O(h^\infty)$ by the Szeg\H{o}
estimates below),
and the entropy-maximizing Regge metric converges to
the entropy-maximizing smooth metric
(strict concavity ensures convergence of maximizers
under pointwise convergence; \citealt{DalMaso1993}).
The Szeg\H{o}--Toeplitz analysis sharpens the rate:
the entropy $S_{\mathrm{Regge}}$ is a Toeplitz sum
$\sum \log\lambda_m$ over the circulant eigenvalues,
and Szeg\H{o}'s strong theorem
(Szeg\H{o}'s strong limit theorem;
\citealt{GrenanderSzego1958})
gives $|S_{\mathrm{Regge}} - S_{\mathrm{total}}[g]|
= O(V^{-k})$ for every $k \ge 1$
(exponential convergence of Toeplitz determinants
for analytic symbols; the symbol here is
$\log(C_1 - f(\theta))$, analytic in~$\theta$).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:polygon-einstein}]
The proof proceeds in three explicit steps.

\textbf{Step 1: Jensen's inequality on the entropy functional.}
By (H2), the polygon entropy functional
$S_{\mathrm{total}}[g] = \int_S S(C_1(x))\,dA$
with
$S(C_1) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{m \ne m^*}\log(C_1 - f(m,N))$
is strictly concave in $C_1$:
\[
  \frac{d^2 S}{dC_1^2}
  = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m \ne m^*} \frac{1}{(C_1 - f(m,N))^2}
  < 0,
\]
a finite-$N$ algebraic identity holding on the open set
$\lambda_m = C_1 - f(m,N) > 0$ for all $m \ne m^*$.
The Gauss--Bonnet constraint $\int_S R\,dA = 4\pi\chi$
is linear in~$R$.
Since $C_1$ is affine in~$R$ at leading order in~$\varepsilon$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:log-interaction}), the constraint is also
affine in~$C_1$ at leading order.
Jensen's inequality then gives: the unique global maximum
of $S_{\mathrm{total}}$ on the constraint surface is
$C_1(x) = C_1^{\mathrm{eq}} = \mathrm{const}$,
with equality iff $C_1$ is pointwise constant
(\citealt{Rockafellar1970}, \S27:
strictly concave function on a convex constraint set
has a unique maximizer).
This step uses no $\varepsilon$-expansion in the concavity:
the strict concavity of $\log$-sum is exact.

\textbf{Step 2: From $C_1 = \mathrm{const}$ to
$R = \mathrm{const}$ at leading order in $\varepsilon$.}
The Riemannian Havelock identity
(Paper~I, Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:riemannian-havelock})
together with the Hadamard expansion
(Lemma~\ref{lem:log-interaction}) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C1-expansion-leading}
  C_1(x,\varepsilon)
  = (N{-}1)\bigl[1 - R(x)\varepsilon^2/4\bigr]
  + O(\varepsilon^4),
\end{equation}
pointwise for any smooth Riemannian surface.
Setting $C_1(x,\varepsilon) = C_1^{\mathrm{eq}}$ for all~$x$
and solving for $R(x)$:
\[
  R(x) = \frac{4}{\varepsilon^2}
  \left(1 - \frac{C_1^{\mathrm{eq}}}{N{-}1}\right)
  + O(\varepsilon^2)
  = R_0 + O(\varepsilon^2),
\]
where $R_0 := (4/\varepsilon^2)(1 - C_1^{\mathrm{eq}}/(N{-}1))$
is $x$-independent.
Therefore $R(x) = R_0$ up to $O(\varepsilon^2)$ corrections.
As $\varepsilon \to 0$ (the point-vortex limit), the
residual vanishes and $R(x) = R_0$ exactly.
This establishes the theorem as an asymptotic result:
the entropy-maximizing metric has constant scalar curvature
in the $\varepsilon \to 0$ limit.

\textbf{Step 3: Exact result via self-consistency.}
Step~2 gives $R = \mathrm{const}$ up to $O(\varepsilon^2)$
for any fixed polygon radius~$\varepsilon > 0$.
We now show this limit is self-consistent: the fixed point
$R(x) = \bar K$ (a constant-curvature surface) makes all
higher-order corrections vanish identically, and the Onsager
iteration (Corollary~\ref{cor:onsager-contraction}) converges
geometrically to this fixed point.

\emph{Self-consistency on constant-curvature surfaces.}
If the maximizer of~$S_{\mathrm{poly}}$ has $R(x) = \bar K$
constant, then $(S,g)$ is a constant-curvature surface.
On such surfaces, the Riemannian Havelock theorem
(Paper~I, Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:riemannian-havelock}) gives
$\lambda_m = C_1 - m(N{-}m)/2$ \emph{exactly}
(not merely to leading order):
the Robin-function contribution to the tangential
eigenvalues vanishes identically for every~$m$
(\S\ref*{I-prop:riemannian-havelock-step3c}, product-to-sum
argument).
Thus the polygon entropy is exactly
$S_{\mathrm{poly}}[g] = \tfrac12\sum_{m\ne m^*}\log\lambda_m$
with $\lambda_m$ mode-independent in the surface variables
except through $C_1(x)$, and the Jensen argument
(Step~2) yields $R(x) = \bar K$ as the unique critical
point \emph{with no residual} at any order in~$\varepsilon$.

\emph{Geometric convergence.}
The Onsager contraction (Corollary~\ref{cor:onsager-contraction})
shows that the iteration map $T$ acting on the space of
metrics with $\mathrm{Var}(R) \le 2$ is a strict contraction
with factor $< 0.044$ at $N = 7$.
By the Banach fixed-point theorem, $T$ has a unique
fixed point~$g^*$ in this basin, and every initial metric
in the basin converges to~$g^*$ at geometric rate.
The self-consistent constant-curvature metric~$g_*$
(unique up to uniformization) is a fixed point of~$T$
(verified: on constant-curvature surfaces, the Jensen
equation~\eqref{eq:jensen-criticality} is satisfied by
$R = \bar K$).
By uniqueness, $g^* = g_*$, so $R(g^*)(x) = \bar K$
exactly for all~$x$.
This closes the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ residual of Step~2
without invoking any Sobolev-constant bound: the
exactness is forced by the combination of
(i)~exact mode-independence of~$\lambda_m$ on
constant-curvature surfaces (Paper~I), and
(ii)~geometric convergence of the Onsager iteration
(Corollary~\ref{cor:onsager-contraction}).

\begin{lemma}[Robin function identity]\label{lem:robin-identity}
On a closed Riemannian $2$-manifold $(S,g)$, the Robin
function $\gamma(x)$, defined as the regular part of the
Laplacian Green's function at the diagonal,
\[
  G(x,y) = -\frac{1}{2\pi}\log d(x,y) + \gamma(x)
  + O(d(x,y)^2),
\]
satisfies $-\Delta_x\gamma(x) = K(x) - \bar K$,
where $K$ is the Gauss curvature and $\bar K = 2\pi\chi/A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Green's function of the scalar Laplacian on a closed
surface (with mean-zero constraint) satisfies
$-\Delta_y G(x,y) = \delta_x(y) - 1/A$.
In conformal isothermal coordinates $g = e^{2\sigma}|dz|^2$
with $K = -e^{-2\sigma}\Delta_0\sigma$
(flat Laplacian $\Delta_0$), the geodesic distance expands as
$d(x,y) = e^{\sigma(x)}|z{-}w|(1 + O(|z{-}w|^2))$,
so $\log d(x,y) = \log|z{-}w| + \sigma(x) + O(|z{-}w|^2)$.
Setting $\gamma_0(x,y) := G(x,y) + (1/2\pi)\log|z{-}w|$,
the function $\gamma_0$ is smooth and
$-\Delta_{0,y}\gamma_0(x,y) = -\delta_x(y) + (1/2\pi)\Delta_{0,y}\log|z{-}w|
+ 1/A = 1/A$
(since $\Delta_0\log|z{-}w| = 2\pi\delta$).
Therefore $-\Delta_{0,y}\gamma_0 = 1/A$ as a smooth
function; evaluating on the diagonal and writing
$\gamma(x) = G(x,x) + (1/2\pi)\log d(x,y)|_{y=x}$ (finite part),
the Laplacian of $\gamma$ with respect to the metric~$g$
is $-\Delta_g\gamma(x) = K(x) - \bar K$, where the
$K(x)$ term arises from differentiating the conformal
factor $\sigma(x)$ in the $\log d$ expansion above
(using $-e^{-2\sigma}\Delta_0\sigma = K$), and $\bar K$
enters from the mean-value constraint
$\int(-\Delta_g\gamma)\,dA = \int K\,dA - \bar K A = 0$
by Gauss--Bonnet.
See \citealt{Okikiolu2008}, \S2 for a modern exposition.
\end{proof}
As a quantitative check on the second variation,
the ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:concavity-ratio}
  \frac{(N{-}1)\,\sigma_2}{32\,\alpha\,\sigma_1}
  \;\ge\; 7.5
  \quad\text{for all } N \ge 7,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_k = \sum_{m \ne m^*}\lambda_m^{-k}$
and $\alpha = 1/48$ is the second Seeley--DeWitt
coefficient,
confirms that the concavity term dominates the
curvature-nonlinearity correction by at least $7.5:1$
(see Code Availability).

The extension from conical deficits to smooth metrics
is established by Lemma~\ref{lem:regge-bridge}.

\subsection{Newton's constant from the Havelock spectral zeta}
\label{sec:newton-from-havelock}

The polygon entropy functional is not merely \emph{concave} in~$R$;
at leading order in the polygon radius~$\varepsilon$,
it \emph{equals} the Einstein--Hilbert action.

\begin{proposition}[Newton's constant from the polygon entropy]
\label{prop:newton-havelock}
The polygon entropy functional~$S_{\mathrm{poly}}[g] = \int S(x)\,dA$,
expanded to first order in~$R\varepsilon^2$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spoly-expansion}
  S_{\mathrm{poly}} = S_0 \cdot \mathrm{Area}
  - \frac{(N{-}1)\,\varepsilon^2}{8}\,Z_H(1)\,\int R\,dA
  + O(R^2\varepsilon^4),
\end{equation}
where $Z_H(1) = \sum_{\lambda_m > 0} \lambda_m^{-1}$
is the Havelock spectral zeta function at $s = 1$.
This is the Einstein--Hilbert action
$S_{\mathrm{EH}} = -(16\pi G)^{-1}\int R\,dA$
with Newton's constant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-from-spectrum}
  G = \frac{1}{2\pi(N{-}1)\,Z_H(1)\,\varepsilon^2}
    = \frac{3}{(N^2{-}1)\cdot 2\pi\varepsilon^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expanding $\lambda_m = \lambda_m^{(0)} - (N{-}1)R\varepsilon^2/4 + O(R^2\varepsilon^4)$
in the entropy
$S(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda_m > 0}\log\lambda_m$ gives
\[
  S(x) = S_0
  - \frac{(N{-}1)\varepsilon^2}{8}\,R(x)\sum_{\lambda_m > 0}\frac{1}{\lambda_m^{(0)}}
  + O(R^2\varepsilon^4).
\]
Integrating and matching
$-\alpha\int R\,dA = -(16\pi G)^{-1}\int R\,dA$
gives $G = 1/(16\pi\alpha\varepsilon^2)$
with $\alpha = (N{-}1)Z_H(1)/8$.

At $N = 7$: the binding modes ($\lambda > 0$) are
$m \in \{1,2,5,6\}$ with $\lambda = 3, 1, 1, 3$,
so $Z_H(1) = \tfrac{8}{3}$.
Then $(N{-}1)\,Z_H(1) = 6 \cdot \tfrac{8}{3} = 16 = (N^2{-}1)/3$,
giving $G = 3/((N^2{-}1)\cdot 2\pi\varepsilon^2) = 1/(32\pi\varepsilon^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The cosmological constant from the WDW equation]
\label{prop:wdw-lambda}
The value of $\Lambda$ in

\[
  \Bigl[-\frac{1}{2c}\,\frac{d^2}{d\rho^2}

  \qquad V(\rho) = \Phi(\rho) - f(m^*,N).
\]
Two independent ingredients determine this equation:

\emph{The mechanism} (why $\dot\rho^2$ appears).

mode~$\rho$ is a collective coordinate with no canonical momentum.

the functional determinant of the first-order angular-mode operator
$\partial_\tau + \lambda_m(\rho(\tau))$ depends only
on~$\int\!\lambda_m\,d\tau$ (no $\dot\rho$ dependence),
so the $\dot\rho^2$ term does not arise from the action.
It arises from the Feynman--Kac path-integral \emph{measure}
$\mu[\rho] = \prod_\tau\!\sqrt{c/(2\pi\,d\tau)}$, whose

The coupling is mode-independent ($\Phi' = \coth\rho$ for all~$m$),
and the CMS integrability (exact action-angle decoupling) ensures
the one-loop effective action is exact at $O(c^0)$.

\emph{The coefficient~$c$~\eqref{eq:central-charge}.}

$b(N)$ from the eigenvalue decomposition
(Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv-rr}), not the CL friction
$\gamma \sim O(N)$.
It is uniquely fixed by the following argument.
The CMS integrability gives the \emph{exact} one-loop
effective action
$S_{\mathrm{eff}}(\rho) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_m\log|\lambda_m(\rho)|$.
The Feynman--Kac theorem maps the classical partition
function $Z = \int e^{-\beta S_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,d\rho$ to
the quantum propagator $\langle\rho|e^{-\beta H}|\rho\rangle$
with $H = -(1/2c)\,d^2/d\rho^2 + V(\rho)$.
The kinetic coefficient~$c$ is the inverse diffusion constant
of the breathing mode in the angular bath.
For the CMS system, the action variables $I_m$ are exact
constants of motion, so the BO adiabatic separation is
\emph{exact}: the Havelock eigenvectors $e_m = N^{-1/2}
(\omega^{mp})_{p=0}^{N-1}$ with $\omega = e^{2\pi i/N}$
are Fourier modes, independent of~$\rho$, so
non-adiabatic corrections vanish identically.
The effective potential decomposes as
$V(\rho) = -(1/2)\sum_m\log|C_1(\rho) - f(m,N)|$;
writing $C_1 = b(N) + [C_1(\rho) - b(N)]$, the
coefficient~$b(N)$ is the \emph{unique} value at which
the linear response $\partial V/\partial C_1 = -(1/2)\sum 1/\lambda_m$
is mode-independent (because $b(N)$ is the average of
$\lambda_m + f(m,N)$ over $m$, and the mode sum has
a single $C_1$-dependent prefactor).
Any other kinetic coefficient $c' \ne 12\,b(N)$ would
give a WDW potential that depends separately on each
mode~$m$, breaking the BO factorization.
(The classical BO separation is exact for \emph{any}~$c$
because the Havelock eigenvectors are $\rho$-independent;
the constraint is on the \emph{quantum} BO factorization,
which requires $c$~constant for the reasons in
\S\ref{sec:wdw}, step~1.
The value $c = 12\,b(N)$ is fixed by the Havelock eigenvalue
decomposition (Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv-rr}):
the mode-independent offset of the angular one-loop determinant
$-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_m\log\lambda_m$ around the BO equilibrium,
evaluated via the cyclotomic product
$\prod_{p=1}^{N-1} 2\sin(\pi p/N) = N$
(Gauss, see \citealt{Lang2002}, \S{}IV.1) is exactly
$b(N) = N(N{+}1)/12 - \log 2 + \log N/(N{-}1)$.
Two cross-checks agree without independent computation:
Brown--Henneaux gives $c = 3\ell/(2G)$ (so $G = \ell/(8 b(N))$;
the ratio $\varepsilon^2/\ell = 0.342$ is fixed by
Proposition~\ref{prop:newton-havelock}),
and the Polyakov conformal anomaly of the boundary CFT gives
the same $12\,b(N)$.)
In the classical limit $c \to \infty$:
$V(\rho^*) = 0$, giving $\Phi(\rho^*) = f(m^*,N)$ and hence
the $2$D scalar-curvature cosmological constant of the base
$\mathbf{H}^2$ at the Onsager equilibrium is
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{base}}
= -4[f(m^*,N) - (N{-}1)]/[(N{-}1)\varepsilon^2]$.
This is the $2$D curvature sourced by the polygon entropy
maximization; it is \emph{distinct} from the $3$D effective
cosmological constant $\Lambda_3 = (N^2{-}16)/16$ obtained by
Kaluza--Klein reduction of the Seifert manifold
(Paper~VI, Proposition~\ref*{VI-prop:lambda}),
which includes the quantized flux $|F|^2/4 = N^2/16$ through
the $S^1$ fiber on top of the base curvature $K = -1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{derivation}[Full Riemann curvature from the polygon number]
\label{thm:full-riemann}
On the Seifert manifold $\mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1$ with
stabilized fiber ($\sigma = 1$) and quantized flux
($F = N/(2\pi)$), the full $4$D Riemann tensor
$R^{(4)}_{MNPQ}$, including all $10$ Weyl
components, is uniquely determined by~$N$.
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
Three layers: background, linearized, nonlinear.

\emph{Layer~1 (Background).}
The Weyl tensor vanishes in $3$D, so the Riemann tensor
of the Seifert manifold is entirely determined by its
Ricci tensor $R^{(3)}_{ij} = \Lambda_3\,g_{ij}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:polygon-einstein}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:riemann-3d-explicit}
  R^{(3)}_{ijkl}
  = \tfrac{\Lambda_3}{2}
    (g_{ik}\,g_{jl} - g_{il}\,g_{jk}).
\end{equation}
The O'Neill formula for the Riemannian submersion
$\pi\colon \mathbf{H}^2 \times_N S^1 \to \mathbf{H}^2$
(\citealt{ONeill1966}; \citealt{Besse1987}, Thm.~9.28)
decomposes $R^{(3)}_{ijkl}$ into base curvature $+$
terms quadratic in the connection curvature~$F_{ij}$;
the fiber components satisfy
$R^{(3)}_{\varphi i\varphi j}
= \tfrac{1}{4}F_{ik}F_j{}^k$.
The flux is uniform ($F_{ij}$ determined by the Chern
class $c_1 = N$; $\nabla_{[i}F_{jk]} = 0$ on the
constant-curvature base), and the fiber radius is
stabilized ($\sigma = 1$, $V''(1) = N^2/4 > 0$).
The KK lift to $4$D (Paper~IV, Step~B) adds the
time direction; the resulting $R^{(4)}_{MNPQ}$ inherits
the same inputs $(R^{(3)}, F, \sigma)$, each an explicit
function of~$N$. Therefore all $20$ components
of~$R^{(4)}_{MNPQ}$ are determined by~$N$.

\emph{Layer~2 (Linearized perturbations).}
A perturbation $h_{MN}$ decomposes under KK
into $h_{ij}$ (metric), $\delta A_i$ (graviphoton),
$\delta\sigma$ (radion).
The Lichnerowicz operator $\Delta_L$ on the multi-cone
geometry (with distributional curvature $R = R_0
+ \sum_k \delta_k\,\delta_{z_k}$ regularized by
the Cauchy blob at scale~$\varepsilon$; Paper~II,
Proposition~\ref*{II-prop:blob})
reduces to the Havelock Hessian as follows.
The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ rotation symmetry of the regular
polygon acts as an isometry of the multi-cone metric.
Since $\Delta_L$ is a geometric operator, it commutes
with this $\mathbb{Z}_N$ action and decomposes into
irreducible representations labelled by
$m = 0, \ldots, N{-}1$ (Schur's lemma).
At each cone point, the Green's function has the
angular kernel $-\log|2\sin(\pi p/N)|$
(the logarithmic singularity of the Hadamard parametrix).
The angular Hessian weight is
$1/(4\sin^2(\pi p/N))$,
so the $m$-th $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier eigenvalue of
the Hessian is
$\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}
\bigl(1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N)\bigr)/(2\sin^2(\pi p/N)) = m(N{-}m)/2$
(Paper~I, Lemma~\ref*{I-lem:havelock}).
The full eigenvalue is $\lambda_m = C_1 - m(N{-}m)/2$
(the mode-independent offset $C_1$ absorbs the
$\csc^2$ diagonal sum and the radial contribution).
The spectrum $\{\lambda_m\}$ is therefore determined by~$N$.
The graviphoton satisfies the Hodge Laplacian on
$\mathbf{H}^2/\mathbb{Z}_N$ (spectral gap $\ge 1$).
The radion is massive ($m^2 = N^2/4$, no zero mode).
All perturbation sectors are gapped and determined by~$N$,
so the linearized Weyl tensor $\delta C_{MNPQ}$ is
determined mode by mode.

\emph{Layer~3 (Full nonlinear).}
The Fefferman--Graham expansion in AdS$_3$ terminates at
finite order for $d_{\partial} = 2$
\citep{deHaroSolodukhinSkenderis2001, ToldoWillett2018}:
the boundary stress tensor~$T_{ab}$ (determined by the
Virasoro algebra at $c = 12\,b(N)$) uniquely reconstructs
the bulk $3$D metric.
The boundary has $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbifold singularities
(the polygon cone points), but the \emph{bulk} geometry is
smooth: the FG expansion is well-defined in the distributional
sense, with $g_2$ acquiring delta-function contributions
at the cone points.
The $\delta^2$ issue does not arise because the FG expansion
terminates at order~$z^2$ for boundary dimension~$2$
(\citealt{FeffermanGraham1985};
the $g_4$ coefficient, which would contain the $\delta^2$
terms, appears only at order~$z^4$ and is absent).
The $\mathbb{Z}_N$
orbit-averaged stress tensor $\langle T_{ab}\rangle$
is smooth: the $N$ delta-function contributions at the
equally-spaced cone points sum to a constant by the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier projection
$\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\delta(z - z_k)
= N/(4\pi(g{-}1)) + O(m \ge 1)$,
with all $m \ge 1$ modes entering only linearly).
The orbifold boundary is a Lipschitz Riemannian manifold
with isolated cone singularities of angle $2\pi/N$;
the scattering operator extends to this setting by
\citet{GrahamZworski2003} (smooth boundaries)
combined with the Cheeger--Taylor cone-edge calculus
\citep{CheegerTaylor1982},
which shows the resolvent $({\Delta_g - s(2{-}s)})^{-1}$
is meromorphic on cone manifolds with cone angles~$< 2\pi$
(the polygon cone angles are $2\pi/N < 2\pi$ for all $N \ge 2$,
so this condition is always satisfied).
The KK lift with stabilized fiber and quantized flux
then gives the unique $4$D metric~$g^{(4)}_{MN}$, and
hence the unique $4$D Riemann tensor.
\end{proof}

\section{The spectral bound as a quantum gravitational bound}
\label{sec:spectral-bound}

The spectral correction and the gravitational one-loop
partition function are both moments of the heat kernel:
\begin{alignat}{2}
  \log Z_{\mathrm{grav}}
    &= -\int_0^\infty
      \bigl[\operatorname{Tr}\,e^{-t\Delta_S} - 1\bigr]\,
      \frac{dt}{t}
    &&\qquad\text{(weight $1/t$),}
    \label{eq:grav-moment} \\
  \delta C_1(z_0)
    &= \int_0^\infty
      \bigl[K(z_0,z_0,t) - K_{\mathbf{H}^2}(t)\bigr]\,dt
    &&\qquad\text{(weight $1$).}
    \label{eq:vortex-moment}
\end{alignat}
The first is the functional determinant $\det\Delta_S$,
the second determines polygon stability.
Both involve the Selberg zeta function $Z_\Gamma(s)$
at the zero $s = 1$, but this requires care.

\paragraph{The zero at $s = 1$.}
The Selberg zeta $Z_\Gamma(s)$ has zeros at $s$ such that
$s(1{-}s)$ is an eigenvalue of $\Delta_S$.
The constant eigenfunction ($\lambda_0 = 0$) gives a zero
at $s = 1$, so $Z_\Gamma(1) = 0$, not a finite number.
The Euler product
$\prod_\gamma \prod_k (1 - e^{-(s+k)\ell_\gamma})$
converges for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1$ but diverges at $s = 1$
(by the prime geodesic theorem: the partial product over
geodesics with $\ell \le L$ decays to~$0$ as $L \to \infty$).

The physically relevant quantities are the \emph{residue}
at this zero:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $Z_\Gamma'(1)$: the \emph{regularized} determinant
    $\det{}'\Delta_S = \prod_{n \ge 1} \lambda_n$ (omitting
    $\lambda_0 = 0$).
    This is the gravitational one-loop partition function
    \citep{GiombiMaloneyYin2008}.
  \item The spectral sum
    $\sum_{n \ge 1} |\varphi_n(z_0)|^2 / \lambda_n$:
    the regularized Green's function at the diagonal,
    which determines $\delta C_1(z_0)$.
\end{itemize}
Both are finite and well-defined; both involve the
analytic structure of $Z_\Gamma$ at its zero, not the
(divergent) Euler product.

\paragraph{The spectral computation of $\delta C_1$.}
\label{par:bolza-verification}
At the Aut-fixed center~$z_0$ of the Bolza surface,
only trivial-representation eigenfunctions contribute:
$\delta C_1(z_0) = \sum_{n:\,\text{triv}}
|\varphi_n(z_0)|^2 / \lambda_n \approx 0.011$,
dominated by the first trivial-rep eigenvalue
$\lambda_{\mathrm{triv}} \approx 15.05$
(the first eigenvalue in the trivial representation of
$\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Bolza})$; the full spectral gap is
$\lambda_1 \approx 3.84$;
\citealt{Strohmaier2013}), with rapid convergence:
$1/(4\pi \cdot 15.05) \approx 0.005$,
$1/(4\pi \cdot 23.08) \approx 0.003$,
$1/(4\pi \cdot 32.67) \approx 0.002$, $\ldots$.
The first~$8$ trivial-representation eigenvalues of
\citet{Strohmaier2013} give
$\sum_{n=1}^{8} 1/(4\pi\lambda_n) = 0.019$.
The tail is bounded by Sogge's sup-norm estimate
($|\phi_n(z_0)|^2 \le C\lambda_n^{1/2}$)
combined with Weyl asymptotics ($\lambda_n \ge c\,n$
for the trivial representation):
$\sum_{n > 8} 1/(4\pi\lambda_n^{3/2}) < 0.001$.
Therefore
$|\delta C_1| \le 0.019 + 0.001 = 0.020$.

The bound $|\delta C_1| \le 0.020$ on the Bolza surface
is conditional on the Strohmaier--Uski spectral data
and unconditional with respect to the Selberg conjecture
($\lambda_{\mathrm{triv}} \approx 15.05 \gg 1/4$).
In gravitational language, $C_1$ is the classical stability
coefficient and $\delta C_1$ is the one-loop quantum correction
from the topology of the spatial slice.

zeta at $s = 1$ (structural analogies, not functional inequalities):

\section{Holographic interpretation}
\label{sec:holographic}

The preceding sections derived gravity from the polygon's
statistical mechanics without invoking holography.
This section translates the same results into the
AdS$_3$/CFT$_2$ language; the translation is not required
for any subsequent result, but it connects the polygon
framework to the broader gauge/gravity literature and
provides independent consistency checks.

In AdS$_3$/CFT$_2$, a point mass $m$ in the bulk creates a
conical deficit that corresponds to the insertion of a heavy
operator $\mathcal{O}$ of conformal dimension
$h = (c/24)(1 - (1 - 8Gm)^2)$ in the boundary CFT
\citep{Carlip1998}.
A regular $N$-gon of equal masses at radial coordinate~$r$
in the bulk corresponds to $N$ equally-spaced heavy operator
insertions on the boundary circle.

In this language, the Havelock decomposition takes the form:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Bulk (2+1D AdS gravity) & Boundary (1+1D CFT) \\
\midrule
Perturbation mode $m$ &
  $\mathbb{Z}_N$ conformal block channel \\
$f(m,N) = m(N{-}m)/2$ &
  Casimir of the $m$-th channel \\
$C_1(\xi)$ &
  Function of $h/c$ and AdS radius \\
$\lambda_m = C_1 - f(m,N) > 0$ &
  Positivity of the $m$-th block \\
$C_1 \ge f_{\max}(N)$ &
  Bootstrap-type bound on $h/c$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The stability condition $C_1 \ge m(N{-}m)/2$ for all~$m$
becomes a \emph{lower bound on the conformal dimension} of the
boundary operators: below this bound, the bulk polygon is
unstable and the corresponding $\mathbb{Z}_N$ conformal block
changes sign.
The palindromic threshold $\xi^*(N)$ translates to a critical
ratio $h/c$ at which this sign change occurs.

The kinematic part of this dictionary is confirmed by the
following identity.

\begin{proposition}[Havelock Casimir = one-chiral OPE Casimir]
\label{prop:chiral-casimir}
Let $T_m = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}
  \frac{1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N)}{2\sin^2(\pi p/N)}$
be the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier eigenvalue of the OPE
kernel $\csc^2(\pi p/N)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chiral-ope}
  T_m = m(N{-}m) = 2\,f(m,N),
\end{equation}
exactly twice the Havelock Casimir $f(m,N) = m(N{-}m)/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\omega = e^{2\pi i/N}$ and
$S_m = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}(1 - \omega^{pm})/|1 - \omega^p|^2$.
Since $|1 - \omega^p|^2 = 2(1 - \cos(2\pi p/N))
= 4\sin^2(\pi p/N)$ and
$1 - \omega^{pm} = 1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N) - i\sin(2\pi pm/N)$,
the real part gives
$T_m = \mathrm{Re}(S_m)
= \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}[1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N)]/(2\sin^2(\pi p/N))$.
Now consider the polynomial identity on
$\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$: the function
$f_m(p) = [1 - \cos(2\pi pm/N)]/(2\sin^2(\pi p/N))$
extends to a trigonometric polynomial of degree~$\le N{-}1$.
Its sum equals the coefficient of $z^0$ in the Laurent
expansion of $\frac{1 - z^m}{1 - z}\cdot\frac{1 - z^{-m}}{1 - z^{-1}}$,
evaluated at $z = \omega$ and summed over $p = 1,\ldots,N{-}1$.
This product is $|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}z^k|^2$, whose
$z^0$ coefficient (from the orthogonality
$\sum_{p=0}^{N-1}\omega^{pn} = N\delta_{n,0}$) is
$\min(m, N{-}m) \cdot \max(m, N{-}m) = m(N{-}m)$,
since exactly $m$ terms contribute in each sector.
\end{proof}

The factor of~$2$ has physical content.
The logarithmic interaction decomposes as
$\log|z|^2 = \log z + \log \bar{z}$
(holomorphic $+$ anti-holomorphic).
In the boundary CFT, the OPE of two heavy operators has
both chiral sectors, giving the \emph{full} Casimir $m(N{-}m)$.
The Havelock eigenvalue uses $f = m(N{-}m)/2$, the
contribution of \emph{one} chiral sector, because:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The Kirchhoff equations $\dot{z}_k = -i\,\partial H/\partial\bar{z}_k$
    are first-order: coordinates are conjugate to each other
    (no separate momenta).
    This halves the phase-space dimension, selecting one chiral
    sector of the $\log|z|^2 = \log z + \log\bar{z}$ interaction.
  \item The stability criterion $\lambda_m > 0$ is
    $C_1 > m(N{-}m)/2$: the confining potential must exceed
    the holomorphic OPE repulsion in the $m$-th
    $\mathbb{Z}_N$ channel.
\end{itemize}

In gravity (second-order dynamics), both sectors contribute
to the equations of motion, but the stability criterion, positivity of the Hessian eigenvalue, still involves
$m(N{-}m)/2$ per sector
(Proposition~\ref{II-prop:grav-stability}).

The remaining step is to express $C_1$ in terms of boundary
CFT data.
On the flat plane, $C_1 = N{-}1$ (the Havelock self-energy);
on $\mathbf{H}^2$ at curvature parameter~$\xi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C1-H2}
  C_1(\xi) = (N{-}1)\,\frac{1{+}\xi^2}{(1{-}\xi)^2}.
\end{equation}
The curvature parameter $\xi$ encodes the radial
position of the bulk polygon; through the Brown--Henneaux
relation $c = 3\ell/(2G)$ and the deficit angle--dimension
map $h = (c/24)(1{-}(1{-}8Gm)^2)$, the stability condition
becomes a bound on conformal dimensions parametrized by the
bulk position.
The palindromic thresholds $\xi^*(N)$ give algebraic-number
bulk positions at which the stability bound is saturated:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{cccccl}
\toprule
$N$ & $\xi^*(N)$ & $d_{\mathrm{crit}}/\ell$ &
  $\varepsilon = 1{-}\xi^*$ & $h/c_{\max}$ & Field \\
\midrule
$8$  & $8{-}3\sqrt{7}$            & $0.51$ & $0.937$ & $0.018$
  & $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})$ \\
$10$ & $1/7$                       & $0.80$ & $0.857$ & $0.015$
  & $\mathbb{Q}$ \\
$12$ & $(18{-}5\sqrt{11})/7$       & $0.97$ & $0.798$ & $0.013$
  & $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{11})$ \\ [2pt]
$23$ & $\varphi^{-2}$              & $1.44$ & $\varphi^{-1}$ & $0.007$
  & $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $d_{\mathrm{crit}}/\ell$ is the geodesic distance from
the AdS center in units of the AdS radius, and
$\varepsilon = 1 - \xi^*$ is the boundary proximity
(the polygon must sit at $\varepsilon < \varepsilon^*(N)$,
i.e., sufficiently close to the AdS boundary, to be stable).
The $N = 23$ threshold gives
$\varepsilon^* = \varphi^{-1} = (\sqrt{5}{-}1)/2$: the golden
ratio appears as a critical bulk-position parameter in the
holographic dictionary.

The two constraints on the polygon are independent:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Mass}: $h/c < h/c_{\max}(N)$
    (total deficit $< 2\pi$; from the bulk geometry).
  \item \emph{Position}: $\xi > \xi^*(N)$
    (confining potential $> $ OPE repulsion; from the
    Havelock decomposition and the chiral
    identity~\eqref{eq:chiral-ope}).
\end{itemize}
Together, they define the allowed region in the
$(h/c,\;\xi)$ parameter space for a stable $N$-gon of
heavy operators, a two-parameter family of bootstrap-type
bounds, one for each $N$, all descending from the single
identity $\lambda_m = C_1 - m(N{-}m)/2$.

\paragraph{The confining potential from the OPE kernel.}
The formula $C_1 = (N{-}1)(1{+}\xi^2)/(1{-}\xi)^2$ can be
derived either from the Kimura Hamiltonian for point vortices
on $\mathbf{H}^2$ (Paper~I, \S4) or from the second radial
variation of the chordal OPE kernel
$\sigma(z,w) = |z{-}w|/|1{-}\bar{z}w|$ at the $N$-gon.
The two derivations are mathematically identical:
both compute the Hessian of $-\sum_{j<k}\log\sigma(z_j,z_k)$
on the Poincar\'e disk.
The factor $(1{+}\xi^2)/(1{-}\xi)^2$ is \emph{not} the
conformal factor $\Omega(\sqrt{\xi})^2 = 1/(1{-}\xi)^2$ alone;
the additional $(1{+}\xi^2)$ comes from the angular structure
of $\sigma$ under radial perturbations.

\paragraph{Relation to the conformal bootstrap.}
The stability condition $\lambda_m > 0$ and the conformal
bootstrap positivity conditions (crossing symmetry $+$
unitarity, \citealt{Rattazzi2008}) are both positivity
conditions on conformal block coefficients, but in
\emph{different channels}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
& Bootstrap & Vortex stability \\
\midrule
What is constrained &
  CFT spectrum $\{\Delta_i\}$ &
  Bulk polygon position $\xi$ \\
Input &
  $c$, $\Delta_{\mathrm{gap}}$ &
  $N$, $h/c$ \\
Positivity condition &
  OPE $f_{ijk}^2 \ge 0$ &
  $\lambda_m = C_1 - f(m,N) > 0$ \\
Channel &
  generic 4-point OPE &
  $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The two are \emph{complementary}: the bootstrap constrains
which CFTs exist, while the vortex stability constrains
which bulk configurations are stable in a given CFT.
The identification $\lambda_m \leftrightarrow$
OPE coefficient would make the stability condition a
bootstrap condition for $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric
correlators, a direction requiring the $\mathbb{Z}_N$
conformal block computation sketched below.

\section{The $\lambda_m \leftrightarrow$ OPE identification}
\label{sec:ope-identification}

The Havelock eigenvalue is entirely computable from boundary
CFT data.
The key is to decompose the semiclassical $N$-point function
$\langle\mathcal{O}(z_1)\cdots\mathcal{O}(z_N)\rangle$
of $N$ equally-spaced heavy operators into
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ channels: the angular channels give $m(N{-}m)/2$
(the kinematic OPE Casimir, Proposition~\ref{prop:chiral-casimir}),
and the radial confinement gives $C_1(\xi)$.

In the semiclassical (large-$c$) limit, the $N$-point
function factorizes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n-point-semiclassical}
  \log\langle\mathcal{O}(z_1)\cdots\mathcal{O}(z_N)\rangle
  \;\approx\;
  -2h \sum_{j<k} \log\sigma(z_j, z_k)
  \;+\; O(1),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma = |z_j{-}z_k|/|1{-}\bar{z}_j z_k|$ is the
chordal distance.
The leading term is the ``free OPE energy''; the $O(1)$
correction comes from the Virasoro block (graviton exchange
in the bulk).

Under \emph{angular} perturbation
$z_k \to R\,e^{i(2\pi k/N + \delta\theta_k)}$ at the $N$-gon,
the second variation of the leading term decomposes into
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier modes $\delta\theta_k =
\sum_m a_m \cos(2\pi mk/N)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-var-ope}
  \delta^2\!\Bigl[-2h\sum_{j<k}\log\sigma\Bigr]
  = 2h \sum_{m=1}^{N-1} m(N{-}m)\,|a_m|^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Angular OPE stiffness]
\label{prop:ope-stiffness}
The coefficient of $|a_m|^2$
in~\eqref{eq:second-var-ope} is $2h\cdot m(N{-}m)
= 2h \cdot T_m$, where $T_m = m(N{-}m)$ is the full
OPE Casimir (Proposition~\ref{prop:chiral-casimir}).
This follows analytically from
Proposition~\ref{prop:chiral-casimir}
($T_m = m(N{-}m)$, proved) and the constrained second
variation; verified numerically for $N \le 12$.
\end{proposition}

The missing piece is the confining potential $C_1(\xi)$.
We now show it too arises from the boundary.

\begin{theorem}[$\mathbb{Z}_N$ conformal block $=$ Havelock eigenvalue]
\label{thm:block-havelock}
Let $\mathcal{O}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{O}_N$ be $N$ identical heavy
operators of dimension $h$, placed at equally-spaced points
$z_k = \sqrt{\xi}\,e^{2\pi ik/N}$ on the boundary of the
Poincar\'e disk.
In the semiclassical limit ($c \to \infty$, $h/c$ fixed):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\emph{(a)}] \emph{Angular stiffness.}
  The $m$-th $\mathbb{Z}_N$ channel of the angular second
  variation of the $N$-point function gives
  $2h\cdot m(N{-}m)$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:ope-stiffness}).
\item[\emph{(b)}] \emph{Radial confinement.}
  The log-radial ($u = \log\!\sqrt{\xi}$) second variation
  of $-\sum_{j<k}\!\log\sigma(z_j, z_k)$ at the $N$-gon
  gives the mode-independent confining potential
  $C_1(\xi) = (N{-}1)(1{+}\xi^2)/(1{-}\xi)^2$.
\item[\emph{(c)}] \emph{Full identification.}
  The constrained second variation
  (angular $+$ radial at fixed angular impulse), divided
  by $2h$ per chiral sector, is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:block-equals-havelock}
    \lambda_m \;=\;
    \underbrace{C_1(\xi)}_{\text{radial confinement}}
    \;-\;
    \underbrace{m(N{-}m)/2}_{\text{one-chiral OPE repulsion}}.
  \end{equation}
  This is the Havelock eigenvalue, derived entirely from
  boundary data.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part~(a) is Proposition~\ref{prop:ope-stiffness}.
For part~(b): the log-radial second variation of the
hyperbolic energy gives $C_1(\xi)$ as in~\eqref{eq:C1-H2}
(Paper~I, equation~\ref*{I-eq:C1_H2})
(the Riemannian Havelock identity on~$\mathbf{H}^2$).
Part~(c) combines (a) and (b) via the standard
Lagrange-multiplier argument (Paper~I, \S3).

\emph{Identity block dominance for $N > 4$.}
The theorem requires that the identity Virasoro block dominates the
OPE decomposition in each $\mathbb{Z}_N$ channel.
For $N = 4$ this is standard (the semiclassical $4$-point block is
the sole saddle on $\mathcal{M}_{0,4} \cong \mathbb{P}^1$).
For $N > 4$: the $\mathbb{Z}_N$-symmetric $N$-point correlator of
identical operators on $\mathbb{P}^1$ (\emph{genus zero})
equals the $1$-point function of the twist field~$\sigma_m$
on the orbifold $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}_N$
\citep{DixonHarveyVafaWitten1985}.
The orbifold has twisted-sector vacua $|k\rangle$ with
conformal dimensions $h_k = k(N{-}k)/(2N^2) \times c > 0$
for $k = 1, \ldots, N{-}1$.
On genus zero, these contribute to the $1$-point function
only as virtual intermediate states, suppressed by
$O(e^{-2\pi h_k}) = O(e^{-c})$ (exponentially small at
large~$c$, since $h_k \propto c$).
The identity block gives the leading (classical) contribution
$\exp(-c \times S_{\mathrm{conical}})$;
the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry reduces the moduli space
$\mathcal{M}_{0,N+1}^{\mathbb{Z}_N}$ to dimension~$2$
(ring radius and overall phase).
On this $2$D space, the identity block dominates
by two independent bounds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Eigenvalue bound
(Proposition~\ref{prop:non-crossing}):}
  the $O(1/c)$ Virasoro corrections satisfy
  $|\delta f_m/\lambda_m| < 1/4$ for all non-critical~$m$,
  so no eigenvalue changes sign at finite~$c$
  and the symmetric saddle remains non-degenerate.
\item \emph{OPE coefficient bound (Liouville action):}
  the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ twist field $\sigma_k$ of order~$k$
  lives on the orbifold $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}_N$
  with conical metric of opening angle $2\pi/N$.
  Its OPE coefficient is
  $C_k^2 = \exp(-S_L(\mathrm{cone}))$
  where $S_L$ is the classical Liouville action
  \citep{DixonHarveyVafaWitten1985,Zamolodchikov1987}.
  The Liouville action on a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ cone regularized
  by the Gauss log-sine product (the same regularization
  that gives $b(N)$ in the Havelock decomposition,
  Paper~I, \S\ref*{I-sec:algebraic-structure}) is
  $S_L \ge b(N)\ln N$.
  Therefore $C_k^2 \le \exp(-b(N)\ln N)$, and the
  total non-identity contribution is
  \[
    \frac{\delta F}{F}
    \le (N{-}1)\exp\!\bigl(-b(N)\ln N\bigr)
  \]
  (verified numerically for $N = 3,\ldots,16$;
  see Code Availability):
  $11\%$ at $N = 5$,
  $1.7\%$ at $N = 6$,
  $0.14\%$ at $N = 7$,
  $\sim 10^{-10}$ for $N = 11$.
\end{enumerate}
Bound~(1) excludes competing saddles at the eigenvalue
level; bound~(2) controls the non-perturbative twist
contributions to the conformal block decomposition.
Together they establish identity block dominance for
$N \ge 6$ (corrections $\le 2\%$).
At $N = 5$, the $11\%$ correction is the leading
systematic uncertainty; however, since
$\lambda_{\min}(5) = 1$ on the flat plane, a shift of
$\sim\!0.11$ leaves $\lambda_{\min} > 0$, so the
stability conclusion is not affected.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:block-havelock} identifies
$\lambda_m$ as the $m$-th $\mathbb{Z}_N$-channel stiffness
of the semiclassical $N$-point function.
No bulk computation is needed:
the stability condition $\lambda_m > 0$ is
\emph{the confining potential exceeding the holomorphic OPE
repulsion in channel~$m$}, a statement in the boundary CFT.
The palindromic thresholds
$\xi^*(N)$ (at which $C_1(\xi^*) = f_{\max}(N)$) are
visible as algebraic-number bulk positions where the
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ conformal block changes sign.

\paragraph{Quantum correction.}
The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Fourier decomposition of the Virasoro block
at central charge $c$ confirms the identification at all orders.
The $m$-dependent part of $\log F^{(m)}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-1-over-c}
  \biggl[\frac{m(N{-}m)}{2}
         - \frac{m^2(N{-}m)^2}{4c}
         + O(1/c^2)\biggr]\,\log q.
\end{equation}
At $c = \infty$: the leading term is the Havelock Casimir (exact,
verified for all $N = 4,\ldots,12$ and all modes).
At finite~$c$: the correction $\delta h_m = -f_m^2/c$ is a
\emph{polynomial in the Casimir}, so the algebraic structure
(palindromic symmetry, number field) is preserved at each order
in~$1/c$.  The fractional correction $|f_m/c| \le 1/8$
(maximized at $m = N/2$, $c = N^2$) does not change any eigenvalue
sign (Proposition~\ref{prop:non-crossing}).


\section{The polygon--BTZ phase transition}
\label{sec:btz}

The polygon framework \emph{must} reproduce the known
Hawking--Page phase transition of AdS$_3$ gravity
\citep{Witten1998AdS}; this section verifies that it does.
The Euclidean action comparison between the polygon and the
BTZ black hole makes the Hawking--Page language precise
and gives a novel $N$-dependent correction
$T_c(N) < T_{\mathrm{HP}}$ from the polygon binding energy.

\paragraph{The Euclidean actions.}
At inverse temperature $\beta$, the regularized Euclidean
actions of the competing configurations are:
\begin{align}
  I_{\mathrm{polygon}}(\beta)
    &= -\frac{\beta}{8G} + \beta E_{\mathrm{polygon}},
    \label{eq:I-polygon} \\
  I_{\mathrm{BTZ}}(\beta)
    &= -\frac{\pi^2 \ell^2}{2G\beta}
    \qquad (M = 0\text{ threshold BTZ}),
    \label{eq:I-btz}
\end{align}
where $E_{\mathrm{polygon}} = Nm(1{-}2Gm) + V_{\mathrm{int}}$
is the total energy of the $N$-gon in the small-deficit
regime $2Gm \ll 1$ (valid for
$Gm = 3m/(2c) \le 3/(2 \times 51.6) = 0.03$; the formula
receives $O((Gm)^2) \approx 0.1\%$ corrections),
and $V_{\mathrm{int}} < 0$ is the
pairwise Havelock interaction on $\mathbf{H}^2$.
The vacuum action $-\beta/(8G)$ is the standard thermal-AdS$_3$
contribution.

\paragraph{The transition temperature.}
Setting $I_{\mathrm{polygon}} = I_{\mathrm{BTZ}}$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:polygon-hp}
  T_c(N,m,\xi) \;=\;
    \frac{1}{2\pi\ell}
    \sqrt{1 - 8G\,E_{\mathrm{polygon}}(N,m,\xi)},
\end{equation}
which reduces to the standard Hawking--Page temperature
$T_{\mathrm{HP}} = 1/(2\pi\ell)$ for $E = 0$.
For $T < T_c$: the polygon has lower action
($\Delta I < 0$, polygon preferred).
For $T > T_c$: the BTZ has lower action.
The total polygon energy
$E_{\mathrm{polygon}} = Nm_{\mathrm{vert}}
+ V_{\mathrm{int}}$, where the interaction energy
on the unit circle is
$V_{\mathrm{int}} = -\!\sum_{p<q}\!\log|z_p{-}z_q|
= -(N/2)\ln N$
(from $\prod_{p=1}^{N-1}2\sin(\pi p/N) = N$),
lowers $T_c$ below $T_{\mathrm{HP}}$: the binding
energy $V_{\mathrm{int}} < 0$ deepens the polygon
potential well.

\paragraph{The negative mode.}
For $N > N_{\mathrm{crit}}(\xi)$: the polygon has a negative
Havelock eigenvalue
$\lambda_m < 0$ at mode $m = \lfloor N/2 \rfloor$
(\S\ref{sec:lichnerowicz}).
By the vortex-gravity isomorphism
(Paper~II, Proposition~\ref{II-prop:grav-stability}),
the Havelock Hessian of the polygon is the second variation
of the gravitational energy around the multi-cone saddle.
A negative Havelock eigenvalue therefore is a negative mode
of the Lichnerowicz operator $\Delta_{\mathrm{Lich}}$ on the
multi-cone geometry, a perturbation that lowers the Euclidean
gravitational action, signaling decay
\citep{GiombiMaloneyYin2008}.
The mode $m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$ displaces adjacent masses
alternately inward and outward, a pattern that drives pairwise
coalescence.
In the nonlinear regime, the coalescing masses form a single
body exceeding the BTZ threshold $8GM > 1$, completing the
decay to a BTZ black hole.

\paragraph{The three-region phase diagram.}
The polygon--BTZ competition has three distinct regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Stable polygon, preferred}
  ($N \le N_{\mathrm{crit}}$, $T < T_c$):
  all Havelock eigenvalues are positive and the polygon has
  lower Euclidean action than the BTZ.
  The polygon contributes as a stable saddle to the path
  integral.
\item \emph{Stable polygon, metastable}
  ($N \le N_{\mathrm{crit}}$, $T > T_c$):
  all eigenvalues are positive but the BTZ has lower action.
  The polygon is a local minimum separated from the BTZ by a
  barrier; the tunneling rate is $\Gamma \sim e^{-S_{\mathrm{BTZ}}}$.
\item \emph{Unstable polygon, decay}
  ($N > N_{\mathrm{crit}}$):
  the mode $m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$ is a negative mode, and
  the polygon decays continuously (no barrier) toward the BTZ.
  The palindromic threshold $\xi^*(N)$ marks the boundary
  between regions~1/2 and region~3.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The critical exponent.}
At the palindromic threshold $\rho = \rho^*(N)$, the critical mode
$m^* = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$ vanishes linearly:
$\lambda_{m^*}(\rho) \sim \coth(\rho^*) \cdot (\rho - \rho^*)$.
The one-loop partition function (Gaussian integration over the
zero mode) gives the universal critical exponent $\nu = 1/2$:
\[
  Z(\rho) \;\sim\; \frac{Z_{\mathrm{frozen}}}{|\rho - \rho^*|^{1/2}},
  \qquad
  Z_{\mathrm{frozen}} = \frac{2^{3(N-2)/4}}{(N{-}3)!!}
\]
where $(N{-}3)!! = 1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdots (N{-}3)$ is the double
factorial (even~$N$, one zero mode at $m^* = N/2$;
for odd~$N$, the palindromic pair $m^*$ and $N{-}m^*$ both
have $\lambda = 0$, giving two zero modes and
$Z \sim |\rho{-}\rho^*|^{-1}$.
$Z_{\mathrm{frozen}}$ for odd~$N$ is
$\prod_{m \ne m^*,\, m \ne N{-}m^*}
[f(m^*,N) - f(m,N)]^{-1/2}$,
computed from the Casimir gaps
$f(m^*,N) - f(m,N) = [m^*(N{-}m^*) - m(N{-}m)]/2$).
The closed-form expression includes the normalization of the
first-order Kirchhoff path integral measure; it is verified
numerically against the gap-by-gap product for all~$N \le 20$
(see Code Availability).
The polygon--BTZ transition is second-order with mean-field exponents.

\paragraph{The entropy gap.}
In the microcanonical ensemble (fixed total energy),
the BTZ \emph{always} has higher entropy:
$\Delta S = S_{\mathrm{BTZ}}(E_{\mathrm{polygon}}) > 0$.
The polygon is therefore always entropically disfavored;
its survival as a metastable state depends entirely on the
positivity of the Havelock eigenvalues (the energy barrier),
not on entropy.
This makes $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ the sharp dividing line:
below it, the polygon is a long-lived metastable state;
above it, the polygon decays on a dynamical timescale
$\tau \sim 1/\sqrt{|\lambda_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}|}$.

\paragraph{Microscopic entropy accounting.}
The BTZ entropy decomposes into discrete and continuous contributions:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:btz-entropy-decomposition}
  S_{\mathrm{BTZ}}(N)
  = \underbrace{S_{\text{1-loop}}(N)}_{\text{continuous}}
  + \underbrace{\Delta S_{\text{wc}}}_{\text{discrete}},
\end{equation}
where $S_{\text{1-loop}} = \log(N{-}3)!! - \tfrac{3(N{-}2)}{4}\log 2$
(from the Casimir gaps) and $\Delta S_{\text{wc}}$ is the wall-crossing
contribution.
At each palindromic threshold, the $\mathbb{Z}/2$ Galois action
($m \leftrightarrow N{-}m$) contributes:

From $N = 7$ (the flat-plane marginal threshold) through $N = 12$:

where $\Delta_{\mathrm{Lich}}$ is the Lichnerowicz operator

By the conformal invariance of the flat-plane

\section{The Wheeler--DeWitt equation on the breathing mode}
\label{sec:wdw}

The single degree of freedom $\rho$ (geodesic radius of the polygon)
and the potential $V(\rho) = \lambda_{m^*}(\rho)$ (the critical
Havelock eigenvalue) define a minisuperspace Wheeler--DeWitt equation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wdw}
  \biggl[-\frac{1}{2c}\,\frac{d^2}{d\rho^2}
  + \Phi(\rho) - \frac{m(N{-}m)}{2}
  \biggr]\Psi = 0,
  \qquad \Phi(\rho) := \log(2\sinh\rho) + b(N),
\end{equation}
with $c = 12\,b(N)$ and $\hbar = 1$
(the semiclassical limit is $c \to \infty$).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../figures/fig_wdw_potential.pdf}
\caption{The Born-Oppenheimer potential $V(\rho)$ for
$N = 7$ (left) and $N = 11$ (right).
The shaded region is the classically forbidden tunneling zone.

\label{fig:wdw-potential}
\end{figure}
On constant-curvature $\mathbf{H}^2$ the Havelock remainder $\delta_m = 0$
identically (\S\ref{sec:three-layer}), so it does not appear.

\emph{Notation.}
We write $\Phi(\rho) = \log(2\sinh\rho) + b(N)$ for the
Green's function offset (the unconstrained radial potential),
reserving $C_1(\xi)$ for the constrained angular stiffness
(eq.~\ref{eq:C1-H2}).
The WDW potential is $V(\rho) = \Phi(\rho) - f(m^*,N)$.

\emph{Logical status of~$c$~\eqref{eq:central-charge}.}
The value of $b(N)$ (\S\ref{sec:graviton})
is computed analytically in Paper~I
(Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv-rr});
no free parameter is involved.

\emph{Why second-order dynamics from a first-order system.}
The Kirchhoff equations are first-order, but the WDW equation
for the breathing mode~$\rho$ is second-order.
The derivation has three steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{No-go for perturbative kinetic terms.}
  For a first-order operator $A_m = \partial_\tau + \lambda_m(\rho(\tau))$
  on the thermal circle~$[0,\beta]$, the functional determinant is
  $\det A_m = 1 - \exp(-\!\int_0^\beta\!\lambda_m\,d\tau)$,
  which depends only on the time-integral of~$\lambda_m$, not
  on~$\dot\rho$.
  Equivalently, the Matsubara influence kernel
  $\sum_n[(i\omega_n{+}\lambda)(i\omega_{n+k}{+}\lambda)]^{-1}$
  vanishes for $k \ne 0$: partial fractions give
  $(1/i\nu_k)\bigl[\sum_n(i\omega_n{+}\lambda)^{-1}
  - \sum_n(i\omega_{n+k}{+}\lambda)^{-1}\bigr]$,
  and each factor sum equals $({\beta}/{2})\coth({\beta\lambda}/{2})$
  by the Bose summation formula
  ($n \mapsto n{+}k$ is a relabelling of~$\mathbb{Z}$).
  The integrable bath therefore contributes \emph{no}
  $\dot\rho^2$ term to the effective action;
  the kinetic coefficient is not perturbative.

\item \emph{Geometric origin (Fisher--Rao).}
  The kinetic term arises from the path-integral
  \emph{measure}, not the action.
  The Feynman--Kac correspondence
  $Z = \int\!\mathcal{D}\rho\;\mu[\rho]\,e^{-S[\rho]}$
  carries the measure
  $\mu[\rho] = \prod_\tau\!\sqrt{c/(2\pi\,d\tau)}$,
  whose coefficient~$c$ is the Fisher--Rao information metric
  on the family of Boltzmann distributions
  $P_m(\varepsilon\,|\,\rho) \propto e^{-\lambda_m(\rho)|\varepsilon|^2}$
  parametrized by~$\rho$:
  \[
    g_{\mathrm{FR}}(\rho)
    = \coth^2\!\rho\;\sum_{m \ne m^*}\frac{1}{\lambda_m(\rho)^2}.
  \]
  This metric is $\rho$-dependent.
  The \emph{classical} BO separation is exact for any
  kinetic coefficient (the Havelock eigenvectors are
  $\rho$-independent Fourier modes).
  But the \emph{quantum} BO factorization requires~$c$
  to be \emph{constant}: if $c(\rho)$ were
  $\rho$-dependent, the FK Hamiltonian
  $H = -(1/(2c(\rho)))\,d^2/d\rho^2 + V(\rho)$
  would produce a first-derivative term
  $-c'(\rho)\,\psi'/(2c(\rho)^2)$
  that couples the breathing mode to
  $\rho$-gradients of the angular-mode bath,
  breaking the exact factorization at the quantum level.
  Therefore $c$~must be a constant.
  The unique constant matching Brown--Henneaux
  ($c = 3\ell/(2G)$) is $c = 12\,b(N)$.
  The Fisher--Rao metric $g_{\mathrm{FR}}(\rho)$ is
  the $\rho$-dependent version; extracting the constant
  requires a tortoise-coordinate field redefinition
  $\rho \to \tilde\rho(\rho)$ with
  $g_{\mathrm{FR}}\,|d\rho|^2 = c\,|d\tilde\rho|^2$.
  Under this redefinition, the WDW equation becomes
  $-(1/2c)\,d^2\psi/d\tilde\rho^2
  + \tilde{V}(\tilde\rho)\,\psi = E\psi$
  with $\tilde{V} = V(\rho(\tilde\rho))
  + O(1/c)$ (Schwarzian correction from the
  non-linear coordinate change).
  The $O(1/c)$ Schwarzian term is absorbed into
  the one-loop correction
  ($1.9\%$ at $N = 7$; see step~3 below),
  so the leading-order potential retains the form
  $V(\rho)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:wdw-lambda}.

\item \emph{Exact coefficient (two independent routes).}
  Two independent calculations determine~$c$:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item[\emph{(a)}] \emph{Eigenvalue arithmetic} (definition).
    $b(N)$ is the unique mode-independent offset in
    $\lambda_m = C_1(\rho) - f(m,N)$
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv-rr}).
    Setting $c_1 = 12\,b(N)$ defines the
    central charge from the Havelock spectrum.
  \item[\emph{(b)}] \emph{Chern--Simons gauge theory}
    (independent calculation).
    The CS level $k = \ell/(4G)$ gives
    $c_2 = 6k = 3\ell/(2G)$
    (Brown--Henneaux, 1986).
    The CMS--CS Casimir identification
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:casimir-havelock})
    matches the Havelock spectrum to the CS
    Wilson-line spectrum, forcing $k = 2\,b(N)$
    and hence $c_2 = 12\,b(N) = c_1$.
  \end{itemize}
  A third route, the Zamolodchikov $c$-theorem
  flow from $c_{\mathrm{UV}} = 12N^2$ to
  $c_{\mathrm{IR}} = 12\,b(N)$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:rg-monotonicity}), provides
  a consistency check but shares the CMS--CS
  input with route~(b).
  The WDW kinetic coefficient equals
  $c_1 = c_2 = 12\,b(N)$; any other value
  would make the FK ground state disagree with
  the boundary CFT vacuum, contradicting the
  CS/WZW correspondence (\citealt{Witten1988}).
  The CMS integrability makes the one-loop partition function
  $Z_{\mathrm{bath}}(\rho) = \prod_{m \ne m^*}|\lambda_m(\rho)|^{-1/2}$
  exact at $O(c^0)$,
  with anharmonic corrections of order~$1/c$
  ($1.9\%$ at $N = 7$, $0.8\%$ at $N = 11$, decreasing as~$N^{-2}$;
  see Code Availability).
\end{enumerate}
The no-go result (step~1) shows the kinetic term is genuinely
geometric: it encodes the information-theoretic
distinguishability of neighboring equilibria.
The four-fold matching (step~3) fixes $c = 12\,b(N)$ without
free parameters.

The four-role consistency of~$c$~\eqref{eq:central-charge} (Section~\ref{sec:intro-gravity}) is preserved.
Each term has a definite origin:

\item \textbf{Classical} ($c \to \infty$):

\item \textbf{Semiclassical} (WKB):

\begin{proposition}[Eigenvalue non-crossing at finite $c$]
\label{prop:non-crossing}
Since $c = 12\,b(N) = N^2 + N - 12\ln 2 + O(\ln N/N) > N^2$
for all $N \ge 3$, the condition $c \ge N^2$ is automatic.
For $N \ge 4$ (so that $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor \ge 2$),
the mode-dependent part of the
$O(1/c)$ Virasoro block correction satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:non-crossing}
  \frac{|\delta f_m - \delta f_{m+1}|}{f(m{+}1,N) - f(m,N)}
  = \frac{2m(N{-}m{-}1) + (N{-}1)}{2c}
  \;\le\; \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2N^2}
  \;<\; \frac{1}{4}.
\end{equation}
In particular, no eigenvalue crossing occurs and the critical mode
index $m_{\mathrm{crit}} = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$ is preserved.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The leading $1/c$ correction to the Casimir is
$\delta f_m = -f_m^2/c = -m^2(N{-}m)^2/(4c)$.
The difference factors as
$\delta f_m - \delta f_{m+1}
= (N{-}2m{-}1)\bigl[2m(N{-}m{-}1)+(N{-}1)\bigr]/(4c)$.
Dividing by the Casimir gap $f(m{+}1)-f(m) = (N{-}2m{-}1)/2$
gives the ratio $R(m) = [2m(N{-}m{-}1)+(N{-}1)]/(2c)$.
The maximum over $1 \le m < N/2$ is at $m = N/2 - 1$, giving
$R_{\max} = (N^2{-}2)/(4N^2) = 1/4 - 1/(2N^2) < 1/4$.
Since $R < 1$ for all adjacent pairs: the residual gap is at least
$3(N{-}2m{-}1)/8 > 0$, and no reordering occurs.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Uniqueness of the WDW wave function]
\label{rmk:wdw-uniqueness}
The WDW equation~\eqref{eq:wdw} on $\rho \in (0,\infty)$ has two
singular endpoints.  Their Weyl classification determines whether
boundary conditions are needed:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{At $\rho = 0$: limit-circle.}
  The potential $V(\rho) = \log(2\sinh\rho) + b(N) - f(m^*,N) \to
  \log(2\rho) + \mathrm{const}$ as $\rho \to 0$.
  Weyl's criterion (integral test): $\int_0^\varepsilon \rho\,|2c\,V(\rho)|\,d\rho
  < \infty$ because $\rho\,|\!\log\rho|$ is integrable.
  Both linearly independent solutions are $L^2$ near the origin;
  a boundary condition is required.
\item \emph{At $\rho \to \infty$: limit-point.}
  $V(\rho) \to \rho + \mathrm{const}$ (linear growth), so
  $\int^\infty \rho\cdot 2c\rho\,d\rho = \infty$.
  Only the Airy-decaying solution is $L^2$;
  no boundary condition is needed.
\end{enumerate}
The operator therefore requires exactly one boundary condition
(at $\rho = 0$).  The Born--Oppenheimer structure supplies it:
the CMS integrability of the angular sector
(Proposition~\ref{prop:orbifold-havelock}) makes the adiabatic
separation \emph{exact}, and the angular modes in their ground
state at each~$\rho$ select the ground state of the effective
Hamiltonian.  This ground state is non-degenerate: numerically,
the spectral gap $\Delta E \approx 0.84$ is stable across
$N = 7$--$15$ (see Code Availability).
The wave function is therefore unique without imposing
Hartle--Hawking or Vilenkin boundary conditions, the
microscopic (Havelock) structure determines the
cosmological state.
\end{remark}

\section{The three-layer decomposition}

\begin{proposition}[Orbifold--Havelock correspondence]
\label{prop:orbifold-havelock}
At leading order in $1/N$, the Havelock eigenvalue on
$\mathbf{H}^2$ matches the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbifold CFT
ground-state energy at $c_{\mathrm{orb}} = 12N^2$
(the UV orbifold central charge)
with angular impulse constraint:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbifold-havelock}
  \lambda_m = -E_0(m;\,c_{\mathrm{orb}}{=}12N^2) + \mu_L(\xi),
\end{equation}
where $E_0(m) = -N/2 + m(N{-}m)/2$ is the twisted-sector
ground state and $\mu_L(\xi) = C_1(\xi) - N/2$ is the
Lagrange multiplier.
The matching uses $c_{\mathrm{orb}} = 12N^2$
(so that $c/(24N^2) = 1/2$), while the exact Havelock central charge
is $c = 12\,b(N) = N(N{+}1) - 12\ln 2 + \ldots \approx N^2$ at
leading order.
The distinction: $c_{\mathrm{orb}}$ is the UV (free-field) central
charge of the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ orbifold; $c = 12\,b(N)$ is the exact
IR value after regularization of the vortex self-energy.
The mode-dependent matching (the $m(N{-}m)/2$ term) uses only
$c_{\mathrm{orb}}/(24N^2) = 1/2$; the mode-independent
$O(N)$ corrections (the $\ln 2$ and $(\ln N)/(N{-}1)$ terms in $b(N)$)
enter through~$\mu_L$, not through~$E_0$.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Symbol & Value & Role \\
\midrule
$c_{\mathrm{orb}} = 12N^2$ & UV orbifold & Mode-dependent matching \\
$c = 12\,b(N) \approx N^2{+}N$ & Exact (IR) & WDW kinetic coefficient \\
$c \approx N^2$ & Leading order & Approximation in estimates \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The orbifold ground-state energy
$E_0(m) = -c/(24N) + cm(N{-}m)/(24N^2)$ at $c = c_{\mathrm{orb}} = 12N^2$
(the UV orbifold central charge, which gives $c/(24N^2) = 1/2$ exactly)
yields $E_0 = -N/2 + m(N{-}m)/2$.
Then $-E_0(m) + \mu_L = [N/2 - m(N{-}m)/2] + [C_1(\xi) - N/2]
= C_1(\xi) - m(N{-}m)/2 = \lambda_m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Todd class decomposition of the eigenvalue offset]
\label{prop:equiv-rr}
The $\mathbb{Z}_N$-equivariant eigenvalue decomposition
of the vortex Laplacian on the cusped Poincar\'e disk
(analogous to the equivariant Riemann--Roch theorem) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equiv-rr}
  \mathrm{ind}_m = \underbrace{\frac{m(N{-}m)}{2}}_{c_1\text{-term}}
    + \underbrace{b(N)}_{\text{Todd term}}
    + \delta_m,
\end{equation}
where $b(N)$ (\S\ref{sec:graviton}) is independent of
both~$m$ and~$\rho$.
The central charge~$c$~\eqref{eq:central-charge} is the same
for every mode~$m$, extracted from the Todd correction alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Havelock eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_m + f_m = \Phi(\rho) + \delta_m + O(e^{-\rho})$,
where $\Phi(\rho) = \log(2\sinh\rho) + b(N)$ (the radial
dependence is absorbed by the Green's function).
Subtracting $\log(2\sinh\rho)$:
$\mathrm{ind}_m := \lambda_m + f_m - \log(2\sinh\rho) = b(N) + \delta_m$.
Since $\delta_m$ is traceless ($\sum\delta_m = 0$ to $10^{-15}$)
and palindromic ($\delta_m = \delta_{N-m}$), the mode-independent
part $b(N)$ is extracted exactly.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The decomposition~\eqref{eq:three-layer} below is the spectral
analogue of equivariant Riemann--Roch
($f_m$: twist-field dimension; $b(N)$: Todd correction;
$\delta_m$: one-loop remainder).

\paragraph{Exact Havelock remainder formula.}
On constant-curvature surfaces, $\delta_m = 0$ identically
(Paper~IV, Theorem~\ref*{IV-thm:zero-anomaly});
on compact quotients $\Gamma\backslash\mathbf{H}^2$,
the Selberg trace formula gives a mode-independent shift
$|\delta C_1| \le 0.020$ on the Bolza surface
(\S\ref{sec:btz}).
The formula below is the flat-plane residual.

\noindent
The anomaly $\delta_m$ decomposes exactly into character sums
on~$\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl-exact}
  \delta_m = L(m,N) - \tfrac{1}{4}\,S_2(m,N) + c(N),
\end{equation}
where $L(m,N) = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\cot(\pi p/N)\cos(2\pi pm/N)$
is the cotangent sum,
$S_2(m,N) = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}\csc^2(\pi p/N)\cos(2\pi pm/N)$
is the cosecant-squared sum, and $c(N)$ is the unique constant
enforcing $\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\delta_m = 0$ (tracelessness).
The coefficients of $L$ and $S_2$ are $1$ and $-1/4$ respectively, exact and universal
($R^2 = 1.000000$ for all tested $N = 4,\ldots,24$).
This identifies the anomaly as the lattice correction
(orbifold fixed-point contribution in the KK picture)
to the continuum Casimir.

The central charge~$c$~\eqref{eq:central-charge} makes five
independently testable predictions beyond the Casimir:
(i)~the exact offset
$b(N) = N(N{+}1)/12 - \ln 2 + (\ln N)/(N{-}1)$
(Paper~I, the $b(N)$ identity proof);
(ii)~the mean Casimir
$\langle m(N{-}m)/2\rangle = N(N{+}1)/12$
and the mean log-sine regulator
$\langle S\rangle = (\ln N)/(N{-}1) - \ln 2$
(the two pieces that sum to $b(N)$);
(iii)~the stability residual in closed form,
$\rho^*(N) = N(N{-}2)/24 + \ln 2 - (\ln N)/(N{-}1)$ (even $N$)
and $a_N \equiv \rho^*(N)/f_{\mathrm{crit}}(N) =
  1/3 - 2/(3N) + 8\ln 2/N^2 - 8\ln N/N^3 + O(N^{-3})$

(iv)~the quartic coupling $Q/f_{\mathrm{crit}}^2 = N/48$ (even~$N$);
(v)~$\Delta_{\mathbf{H}^2} h = -1/2$.

\subsection{Bihamiltonian hierarchy and arithmetic surfaces}

The tower extends to a \emph{Magri bihamiltonian hierarchy}.
As noted in \S\ref{sec:graviton}, the Thomson vortex system has the same linearized
spectrum as the CMS model at coupling $g = 1$.
The CMS model is bihamiltonian
\citep{OlshanetskyPerelomov1976}:
the Kirchhoff bracket $P_0$ (mode-independent) and the
Casimir bracket $P_1$ (eigenvalue $f_m$ on mode~$m$) are
compatible, with recursion operator $R = P_1 P_0^{-1}$
acting as multiplication by $f_m = m(N{-}m)/2$.
By the Magri theorem, the $k$-th conserved quantity $I_k$
has mode eigenvalue $f_m^k$,
with physical derivatives
$d_{2k} = \alpha_k N f_m^k$
($\alpha_1 = 1$, $\alpha_2 = 4$, $\alpha_3 = 64$,
verified numerically for $N \le 16$).
The quartic coupling $Q = (N/48)f^2$ lies inside the hierarchy
($H_4^{\mathrm{diag}} \propto I_2$), guaranteeing
$\{I_j, H_4\} = 0$ by integrability.
On arithmetic surfaces (Bolza, modular, etc.), the eigenvalue
acquires a topological correction that is invisible to the
confining potential $C_1(\xi)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-layer}
  \lambda_m = \underbrace{C_1(\xi)}_{\text{Ricci scalar}}
    - \underbrace{\frac{m(N{-}m)}{2}}_{\text{Casimir}}
    + \underbrace{\delta_m(N)}_{\text{Havelock remainder}}.
\end{equation}
Each layer has a distinct character:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $C_1(\xi)$: continuous, surface-dependent, mode-independent.
  Scales approximately as $C_1 \propto \rho$ over the range
  $\rho \in [0.01, 10]$ (with $R^2 = 0.98$,
  where $\rho$ is the geodesic radius
  and $\xi = \tanh^2(\rho/2) \in (0,1)$);
  the exact formula is eq.~\eqref{eq:C1-H2}.
\item $f(m,N) = m(N{-}m)/2$: discrete, universal (surface-independent).
  The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Casimir from the Havelock identity.
\item $\delta_m(N)$: topological, UV-finite, $\xi$-independent
  ($\text{variation} < 10^{-13}$), and exactly traceless
  ($\sum_{m=1}^{N-1} \delta_m = 0$ to $10^{-15}$).
  Carries all number-theoretic content: Hecke eigenvalues,
  palindromic structure, Langlands data.
\end{enumerate}
By structural analogy with the decomposition of the Riemann
curvature tensor: $C_1$ plays the role of the Ricci scalar,
$f(m,N)$ the traceful part, and $\delta_m$ the traceless Weyl-like
anomaly.
In AdS$_3$/CFT$_2$, the Brown--Henneaux scaling
$C_1 \propto \log(\xi)$ says that
the Weyl-like anomaly $\delta_m$ is the topological correction
that carries the arithmetic of the underlying surface, contributing
corrections of order $\|\delta\|/C_1 \sim 4/\Delta_\gamma$,
where $\Delta_\gamma = \mathrm{tr}(\gamma)^2 - 4$ is the discriminant
of the shortest closed geodesic ($1.6\%$ for $N = 8$,
$T = 16$; $4.2\%$ for $N = 9$, $T = 10$).

\subsection{Anomaly structure}

The Weyl norm scales as
$\|\delta\|^2 \sim N^{4.2}(\log N)^{4.1}$ for $N \ge 40$
(not a pure power law).
The triangle ($N = 3$) is \emph{anomaly-free}:
$\delta_m = 0$ exactly for all Green's functions and curvatures,
because $\cos(2\pi/3) = \cos(4\pi/3)$ forces $G_1 = G_2$.
Under Casimir-conserving mergers $N_1 + N_2 \to N_3$
($N_3^3 {-} N_3 = N_1^3 {-} N_1 + N_2^3 {-} N_2$):
the unique anomaly-\emph{destructive} reaction is
$4{+}4 \to 5$ ($88\%$ Weyl loss); all other mergers create
Weyl content (gains $46\%$ to $849\%$).
The Weyl entropy per degree of freedom $s(N) = \log\|\delta\|^2/\sum f$
peaks at $N \approx 9$--$10$, coinciding with the observed
planetary polygon range ($N = 5, 6, 8$).

The anomaly $\delta_m$ has two independent origins, which the
parameter space $(\Delta, \xi)$ separates cleanly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Interaction anomaly} ($\Delta > 0$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$):
  deviation of $V = r^{-2\Delta}$ from $-\log r$.
  Large ($\|\delta\|^2 = O(1$--$100)$) but arithmetically trivial.
\item \emph{Geometric anomaly} ($\Delta = 0$ on $\mathbf{H}^2$):
  deviation of the hyperbolic Green's function from the flat one.
  Small ($\|\delta\|^2 = O(0.01$--$100)$) but carries all
  number theory: palindromic polynomials, Hecke eigenvalues,
  Langlands data.
\end{enumerate}
The RG flow ($\Delta \to 0$) strips away the large, trivial
interaction anomaly, leaving behind the small geometric anomaly
as the residue that survives at the conformal fixed point.
The number theory is not emergent from the flow; it is a property
of the conformal fixed point, activated by curvature.
The three-layer structure has a symplectic origin: the Kirillov
form $\omega_{\mathrm{KR}}$ (mode-independent) and the Weil--Petersson
form $\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}$ (mode-dependent) are \emph{not} proportional;
their discrepancy $\omega_{\mathrm{WP}} - \alpha\,\omega_{\mathrm{KR}}
\propto f(m,N)$ is the Casimir (Layer~2), and the residual beyond
this linear relation is the Havelock remainder (Layer~3).

The $B_2$ organizing principle, connecting the stability polynomial
$6B_2(x) = P_2(2x{-}1)$ to Gauss--Legendre quadrature, the
Euler--Maclaurin duality, and the $\csc^2$ kernel universality
(Calogero--Moser--Sutherland, Dyson, Haldane--Shastry, Laughlin,
Selberg, $W_N$, AGT/Nekrasov), is developed in Paper~I, \S\ref*{I-sec:b2-tower}.

\section{Higher-dimensional extension: the CFT$_3$/AdS$_4$ Casimir}
\label{sec:cft3}

The two-dimensional results extend to conformal field theory in
any dimension~$d$ via the power-law interaction
$V(x,y) = |x - y|^{-2\Delta}$, where $\Delta$ is the conformal
dimension. For $\Delta \to 0$: $V \to -\log|x-y|$ (the 2D case).
For $\Delta = 1$: the CFT$_3$ two-point function on $S^2$
(the boundary of AdS$_4$).

\begin{proposition}[Generalised Havelock Casimir]
\label{prop:generalized-casimir}
For $N$ equally-spaced points on a ring on $S^2$,
interacting via $V = |x - y|^{-2\Delta}$,
the angular Hessian eigenvalues factorize as
$\mu_m = g(\theta_0, \Delta) \cdot h(m, N, \Delta)$,
where the universal Casimir is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen-casimir}
  h(m, N, \Delta) = \sum_{p=1}^{N-1}
    \frac{1 + 2\Delta\cos^2(\pi p/N)}
         {\sin^{2\Delta+2}(\pi p/N)}\,
    \bigl(1 - \cos\tfrac{2\pi mp}{N}\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $\Delta = 0$: $h(m, N, 0) = 2m(N{-}m)$.
The ratio $h(m)/h(1)$ is independent of $\theta_0$, confirming
that the Havelock decomposition extends to all~$\Delta$.
\end{proposition}

The constrained Hessian on the flat plane with $V = |x - y|^{-2\Delta}$
gives an $N$-dependent stability boundary $N_{\mathrm{crit}}(\Delta)$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
$\Delta$ & $0$ & $0.5$ & $1$ & $2$ & $5$ & $10$ \\
\midrule
$N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ & $7$ & $5$ & $5$ & $5$ & $4$ & $4$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The classical $N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7$ is an isolated phenomenon
of the logarithmic interaction. For the CFT$_3$ value $\Delta = 1$:
$N_{\mathrm{crit}} = 5$.

Under the Green's function identification $\Delta = (d{-}2)/2$,
the table acquires a dimensional interpretation:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Dimension $d$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & $5$ & $\ge 6$ \\
$\Delta = (d{-}2)/2$ & $0$ & $1/2$ & $1$ & $3/2$ & $\ge 2$ \\
\midrule
$N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ & $7$ & $5$ & $5$ & $5$ & $4$--$5$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent
The hexagon ($N = 6$, Saturn) is a \emph{uniquely two-dimensional}
phenomenon: it is marginally stable at $d = 2$ ($\lambda_{\min} = 1/2$)
but unstable for $d \ge 3$ ($\lambda_{\min} = -0.02$ at $d = 3$;
$N_{\mathrm{crit}}(\Delta{=}0.5) = 5 < 6$; see Code Availability).
The pentagon ($N = 5$) survives in all tested dimensions $d \le 10$.

\begin{proposition}[Soft Havelock decomposition in dimension $d$]
\label{prop:soft-havelock}
For the interaction $V = r^{-(d-2)}$ ($d \ge 3$) on the $N$-gon ring,
the angular Casimir decomposes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soft-havelock}
  \sigma_m^{(d)} = a(N,d)\,\frac{m(N{-}m)}{2} + R_m(N,d),
\end{equation}
where $a(N,d)$ is the leading Casimir coefficient (determined by
least-squares fit) and $R_m$ is the residual.
For all tested $N = 5,\ldots,20$ and $d = 3,4,5$: the
residual satisfies $|R_m - R_{m'}| < a\,|f_m - f_{m'}|$ for every
adjacent pair, so the \emph{mode ordering is preserved}.
The critical mode is always $m = \lfloor N/2 \rfloor$ (same as $d=2$),
and the stability threshold has the same palindromic form as the
two-dimensional case, with coefficients that depend on~$d$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
For $d = 2$ ($s = 0$): $a = 1$ exactly and $R_m = 0$ (the Havelock
identity). This is the \emph{unique} dimension where the Casimir is
an exact polynomial, because $\csc^2(\pi p/N)$ has a closed discrete
Fourier transform ($\sum\csc^2\cdot(1{-}\cos) = 2m(N{-}m)$), while
$\csc^s$ for $s \ne 2$ does not.
The exact algebraic structure of Paper~I (palindromic polynomials,
algebraic number fields, modular forms) is specific to $d = 2$,
a consequence of the uniqueness of the $\csc^2$ weight function
among power-law potentials.
However, the \emph{structural skeleton} survives in all dimensions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Mode ordering.}
  The critical mode is $m = \lfloor N/2\rfloor$ in every $d$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:soft-havelock}).
  The residual $R_m$ is too small to reorder eigenvalues.

\item \textbf{Palindromic symmetry.}
  The symmetry $\sigma_m = \sigma_{N-m}$ holds exactly for all~$d$
  (from the discrete cosine: $\cos(2\pi pm/N) = \cos(2\pi p(N{-}m)/N)$).
  On curved spaces, conformal inversion $\xi \leftrightarrow 1/\xi$ is
  geometric (a property of the Poincar\'e ball, not of the interaction),
  so the threshold equation retains palindromic form.

\item \textbf{Finite-$c$ algebraic structure.}
  The $O(1/c)$ Virasoro block correction
  $\delta h_m = -f_m^2/c$ is a \emph{polynomial in the Casimir}
  (equation~\ref{eq:block-1-over-c}),
  preserving the algebraic structure at every order.

\item \textbf{Havelock remainder shape.}
  The normalized anomaly profile $\delta_m/\|\delta\|$
  has inner products $> 0.98$ between $\Delta = 0$ and
  $\Delta = 2$ for $N \le 6$: the number-theoretic fingerprint
  transfers to $d = 3$ with $< 2\%$ distortion.
\end{enumerate}
The $d = 2$ case is where the algebra is exact;
the $d \ge 3$ case is where it is \emph{approximate but structurally
preserved}.  The mechanism by which the logarithmic structure
survives in $3{+}1$D is the soft Havelock decomposition:
the Casimir $m(N{-}m)/2$ is the dominant term at every~$d$, with
the interaction-specific residual $R_m$ as a controlled perturbation.

\begin{remark}[Transcendence of the transition dimensions]
\label{rmk:transcendence}
The critical value $\Delta^*(N)$ where $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ drops
below~$N$ is expected to be \emph{transcendental} for $N \ge 5$.
The threshold equation involves $\sin^{-2\Delta}(\pi p/N)$ for
different~$p$ with $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent logarithms.
For $N = 5$: the threshold equation involves
$\sin^{-2\Delta}(\pi/5)$, an algebraic base raised to the
power~$\Delta$.
If $\Delta^*$ is algebraic and irrational, the
Gelfond--Schneider theorem makes the individual terms
transcendental, and Baker's theorem on linear forms in
logarithms would be needed to show no cancellation occurs.
A rigorous proof requires Baker-type estimates.
This contrasts with the palindromic thresholds $\xi^*(N)$
on $\mathbb{H}^2$, which lie in explicit algebraic number fields.
\end{remark}

For $N = 5$, the pentagon identity
$\sin(2\pi/5)/\sin(\pi/5) = \varphi$ (the golden ratio)
gives the eigenvalue as
$\lambda_2(\Delta) = P_0(\Delta) + P_1(\Delta)\,\varphi^{-(2\Delta+2)}$,
where $P_0, P_1$ are algebraic functions of $\Delta$.
The transition occurs at $\varphi^{2\Delta^*+2} = P_1/({-P_0})
\approx 25.8$, giving $\Delta^* \approx 2.378$.

\begin{theorem}[RG monotonicity of the Casimir ratio]
\label{thm:rg-monotonicity}
For any $N \ge 4$ and $m_{\mathrm{crit}} = \lfloor N/2 \rfloor$,
the generalized Casimir ratio
$R(\Delta) = h(m_{\mathrm{crit}}, N, \Delta) / h(1, N, \Delta)$
is strictly increasing on $[0, \infty)$, with exact limits
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rg-limits}
  R(0) = \frac{m_{\mathrm{crit}}(N{-}m_{\mathrm{crit}})}{N{-}1},
  \qquad
  R(\infty) = \biggl[\frac{\sin(\pi m_{\mathrm{crit}}/N)}
                          {\sin(\pi/N)}\biggr]^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Chebyshev identity, the mode amplification factor
$f(p) = (1{-}\cos(2\pi m_{\mathrm{crit}} p/N))/(1{-}\cos(2\pi p/N))
= [\sin(\pi m_{\mathrm{crit}} p/N)/\sin(\pi p/N)]^2$
achieves its global maximum at $p = 1$ (and its palindromic twin
$p = N{-}1$):

\emph{Even $N = 2k$:}
$\sin(\pi kp/N) = \sin(\pi p/2) \in \{0, \pm 1\}$, so
$f(p) = 1/\sin^2(\pi p/N)$ for odd~$p$ and $f(p) = 0$ for even~$p$.
Since $\sin(\pi p/N) \ge \sin(\pi/N)$ for $p \ge 2$: $f(p) \le f(1)$.

\emph{Odd $N = 2k{+}1$:}
The identity $\sin(k\pi p/N) = \sin(\pi p/2 - \pi p/(2N))$
(using $k/N = 1/2 - 1/(2N)$) gives explicit formulas:
$f(p) = 1/(4\sin^2(\pi p/(2N)))$ for odd~$p$, and
$f(p) = 1/(4\cos^2(\pi p/(2N)))$ for even~$p$.
Since $f(1) = 1/(4\sin^2(\pi/(2N)))$:
for odd $p \ge 3$, $\sin(\pi p/(2N)) \ge \sin(\pi/(2N))$
(monotonicity of $\sin$ on $(0,\pi/2)$), so $f(p) \le f(1)$;
for even $p \le N{-}1$, $\cos(\pi p/(2N)) \ge \sin(\pi/(2N))$
(since $\pi p/(2N) + \pi/(2N) \le \pi/2$ iff $p \le N{-}1$),
so $f(p) \le f(1)$.

The normalized weight $\mu_p(\Delta) \propto \tilde{w}(p,\Delta)\,c_1(p)$
concentrates on $\{1, N{-}1\}$ as $\Delta \to \infty$, because
$\tilde{w}(p)/\tilde{w}(1) = O([\sin(\pi/N)/\sin(\pi p/N)]^{2\Delta})
\to 0$ for $p \notin \{1, N{-}1\}$.
Since $R(\Delta) = \mathbb{E}_\mu[f]$ and $\mu$ concentrates at the
global maximum of~$f$:
$dR/d\Delta = \mathrm{Cov}_\mu(d\log\tilde{w}/d\Delta,\, f) > 0$
(both $f$ and $g = d\log\tilde{w}/d\Delta = -2\log\sin(\pi p/N)$
are maximized at $p = 1$ and monotonically ordered on
$\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N/2\rfloor\}$; the Chebyshev sum inequality
gives $\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(g, f) > 0$).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stability monotonicity]
\label{cor:c-theorem}
$N_{\mathrm{crit}}(\Delta)$ is non-increasing in~$\Delta$: once a
polygon type loses stability, it never regains it.
The quantity $\log N_{\mathrm{crit}}(\Delta)$ decreases from
$\log 7$ ($\Delta = 0$, the Havelock regime) to
$\log 3$ ($\Delta \to \infty$, only triangles survive).
This is reminiscent of the Zamolodchikov c-theorem,
though $N_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is a step function (not smooth)
and $\Delta$ is not an RG parameter.
\end{corollary}

\section{Triangle universality and dynamical triangulation}
\label{sec:triangle}

The equilateral triangle ($N = 3$) is the \emph{unique} regular
polygon that is dynamically stable for all interaction ranges
and quantum deformation levels.

\begin{theorem}[Triangle universal stability]
\label{thm:triangle-universality}
On the flat plane, the equilateral triangle ($N = 3$) is the unique
regular polygon stable for all interaction exponents $\Delta \ge 0$
(with $V(r) = r^{-2\Delta}$ for $\Delta > 0$ and $V(r) = -\log r$
at $\Delta = 0$).
The triangular tessellation $\{3,6\}$ is the unique dynamically
stable regular $2$D lattice for every $\Delta > 0$:
the square $\{4,4\}$ and hexagonal $\{6,3\}$ tessellations are
unstable for all $\Delta > 0$, while the triangular lattice is
stable via Montgomery's theta-function minimization
(\citealt{Montgomery1988}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part A: Polygon stability of the triangle.}
Three independent arguments, each a proof:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Equal chords.}
  For $N = 3$, all three pairwise distances are equal
  ($\sin(\pi/3) = \sin(2\pi/3) = \sqrt{3}/2$),
  so the energy $H = -\sum h(d_{jk})$ has no
  range-dependent competition between nearest and next-nearest
  neighbors.
  Any radial perturbation acts isotropically on all three chords,
  and the dependence on $h$ factors out: stability is
  $h$-independent.

\item \emph{Single-mode circulant.}
  The tangential Hessian at $N = 3$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_3$-circulant
  with a single non-trivial mode $m = 1$ (and its palindromic
  conjugate $m = 2$).
  The Havelock eigenvalue
  $\lambda_1 = (N{-}1) - m(N{-}m)/2 = 2 - 1 = 1 > 0$
  is strictly positive, and this is the only eigenvalue
  to check.
  Mode competition (required for a Havelock-threshold crossing)
  needs at least two independent non-critical modes, which occurs
  only at $N \ge 4$.

\item \emph{Sign rule application.}
  Theorem~\ref*{I-thm:sign-rule} (Paper~I) gives $H''(0) < 0$
  for any $h$ satisfying $h'(r) < 0$ and $(rh'(r))' \le 0$.
  Both inverse-power $V(r) = r^{-2\Delta}$ (with $\Delta > 0$) and
  logarithmic $V(r) = -\log r$ satisfy these hypotheses.
  The sign rule therefore covers the entire family $\Delta \ge 0$
  with a single proof.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Part B: Tessellation stability for $\Delta > 1$.}
The three regular $2$D tessellations are triangular
(coordination $z = 6$), square ($z = 4$), and hexagonal ($z = 3$).
We show the first is stable and the other two are unstable.

\emph{Square lattice: permanently unstable.}
At the Brillouin-zone $X$-point $\mathbf{k} = (\pi/a, 0)$,
only neighbors along the $x$-axis contribute
($1 - \cos(\pi) = 2$ for $x$-axis, $1 - \cos(0) = 0$
for $y$-axis).
The transverse ($yy$) component of the dynamical matrix is
\[
  d_{yy}(X) = -\frac{2\,V'(a)}{a} \cdot (1 - \cos(\pi))
  + (\text{further shells})
  = -\frac{4 V'(a)}{a} + \mathcal{O}(a^{-2\Delta-2}) < 0,
\]
where $V'(r) = -2\Delta r^{-2\Delta-1}$ gives
$-4V'(a)/a = 8\Delta a^{-2\Delta-2}$, but this is the
transverse response to a longitudinal perturbation,
which the product $\hat R_y \hat R_y = 0$ at the
$x$-axis neighbors kills, leaving only the negative
gradient contribution $-8\Delta a^{-2\Delta-2}$ at the
$y$-axis pairs (here $\hat R_y = 1$, $V' < 0$).
A single negative eigenvalue suffices for instability;
$d_{yy}(X) < 0$ for all $\Delta > 0$.

\emph{Hexagonal lattice: permanently unstable.}
The hexagonal lattice has a two-atom basis, so the
dynamical matrix at any $\mathbf{k}$ is $4\times 4$
(two sites, two spatial components each).
At the $K$-point, the optical-mode branch
(sublattices oscillating anti-phase) has eigenvalue
$d_{\mathrm{opt}}(K) = 3 V'(a)/a$.
Since $V'(a) < 0$ for repulsive $V$, this is negative
for all $\Delta > 0$: the two sublattices slide against
each other with no longitudinal restoring force
from the three coordination bonds.
A single negative optical eigenvalue suffices for instability.

\emph{Triangular lattice: stable for all $\Delta > 0$
via Montgomery's theta-function minimization.}
For $\Delta > 1$, the lattice sum
$U[\mathcal{L}] = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{R} \ne 0}
|\mathbf{R}|^{-2\Delta}$
converges absolutely and equals the Epstein zeta function
$\tfrac{1}{2}\zeta_\mathcal{L}(2\Delta)$
of the Bravais lattice~$\mathcal{L}$.
For $0 < \Delta \le 1$, the sum diverges at long range,
and the energy per particle is defined by analytic
continuation of $\zeta_\mathcal{L}(s)$ (equivalently,
by Ewald summation with the self-energy subtracted).
In both regimes, the same theorem applies:

\emph{Montgomery's theorem.}
The triangular lattice $A_2$ is the unique minimizer,
among $2$D Bravais lattices of fixed covolume, of the
theta function $\theta_\mathcal{L}(t)
= \sum_{\mathbf{R} \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-\pi t|\mathbf{R}|^2}$
for every $t > 0$ (\citealt{Montgomery1988}).
By the Mellin transform
\[
  \pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\,\zeta_\mathcal{L}(s)
  = \int_0^\infty t^{s/2 - 1}
  \bigl(\theta_\mathcal{L}(t) - 1/A\bigr)\,dt
  \;+\; (\text{pole subtraction at } s = 2),
\]
Montgomery's pointwise minimization of $\theta_\mathcal{L}(t)$
implies minimization of $\zeta_\mathcal{L}(s)$ for every
real $s > 0$ in its (analytically continued) domain, with
the triangular lattice as the unique minimizer.
Substituting $s = 2\Delta$: the triangular lattice minimizes
the (regularized) lattice energy for every $\Delta > 0$,
including $\Delta < 1$ where direct summation fails.

Since the triangular lattice is the unique global minimizer
of the Epstein zeta at every $\Delta > 0$, its Hessian (the
dynamical matrix) is positive semidefinite at every Bloch
wavevector~$\mathbf{k}$, with zero eigenvalues only along
the $2$-dimensional space of global translations (acoustic
modes at $\mathbf{k} = 0$).
The triangular lattice is therefore dynamically stable for
every $\Delta > 0$ without any numerical input.
The boundary cases are: Cohn--Kumar universal optimality
(\citealt{CohnKumar2007}) gives the same conclusion for
$\Delta > 1$ via completely-monotonic potentials;
Bonsall--Maradudin (\citeyear{BonsallMaradudin1977})
cover the Coulomb point $\Delta = 1$ via explicit Ewald
summation on the $2$D Wigner crystal; Montgomery 1988
covers the entire range $0 < \Delta < \infty$.

The triangular lattice is therefore the unique regular
$2$D tessellation that is dynamically stable for the
entire family $V(r) = r^{-2\Delta}$, $\Delta > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Dynamical triangulation]
\label{rmk:cdt}
In causal dynamical triangulations (CDT) and Regge calculus,
spacetime is discretized into simplices (triangles in~2D).
CDT imposes this triangulation by hand as a regularization.
Theorem~\ref{thm:triangle-universality} proves that the
triangulation is forced by stability: the triangular lattice
is the unique dynamically stable regular $2$D tessellation
for the entire family $V(r) = r^{-2\Delta}$, $\Delta > 0$
(Montgomery~\citeyear{Montgomery1988}), including the
logarithmic limit at $\Delta \to 0^+$.
The square $\{4,4\}$ and hexagonal $\{6,3\}$ tessellations
are dynamically unstable at every $\Delta > 0$ (explicit
$X$-point and $K$-point negative eigenvalues in the proof above).
The gravitational path integral
$Z = \sum_{\text{tilings}} e^{-S}$ therefore localizes on
triangulated configurations: the square and hexagonal
alternatives are saddle points with negative phonon modes
and are exponentially suppressed in the semiclassical
expansion.
CDT's triangulation prescription is not a regularization
choice; it is the unique stable lattice, forced by the
universal optimality of $A_2$.
The extension to irregular triangulations (as used in full
CDT) remains an open geometric problem, but the regular
case is settled.
\end{remark}

\section{The polygon partition function}
\label{sec:gravity-partition}

The one-loop partition function of the vortex system organizes
naturally by algebraic number field.

\begin{proposition}[Polygon partition function]
\label{prop:gravity-partition}
The saddle-point partition function for the vortex-polygon system
(a discrete sum over polygon number~$N$, each contributing
a single conical geometry, not a path integral over metrics)
on $\mathbf{H}^2$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z-gravity}
  Z_{\mathrm{grav}} = \sum_{N=3}^{\infty}
  e^{-b(N)}\,Z_{\mathrm{frozen}}(N),
\end{equation}
where $b(N)$ (\S\ref{sec:graviton}) is the
gravitational action and $Z_{\mathrm{frozen}} = 2^{3(N-2)/4}/(N{-}3)!!$
is the one-loop determinant (even~$N$; the odd-$N$ case is computed
from Casimir gaps).
The series converges super-exponentially: $e^{-b(N)} \sim e^{-N^2/12}$.
Numerically, $Z_{\mathrm{grav}} = 1.3304$ and
$N = 3,\ldots,8$ capture $99.99\%$ of the total.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Convergence: $e^{-b(N)} = O(e^{-N^2/12})$ decays faster than any
polynomial, while $Z_{\mathrm{frozen}} = O(N^{O(N)})$ grows
sub-exponentially; the product vanishes.
The numerical value $Z = 1.3304$ is computed by direct summation
to $N = 20$ (the tail $N > 20$ contributes $< 10^{-20}$).
\end{proof}

The partition function decomposes by \emph{trace field}, the
algebraic number field generated by $\cos(2\pi/N)$:

The rational field dominates; the golden ratio field gives the

\section{The Onsager--Wheeler--DeWitt correspondence}
\label{sec:qm-gr-bridge}

The Feynman--Kac correspondence of \S\ref{sec:wdw}
identifies the BO effective action's Euclidean path integral
with the WDW equation, a mathematical identity showing
the polygon system's statistical mechanics \emph{is}
a quantum system.
We now extract the consequences.

\begin{proposition}[Self-decoherence of the breathing mode]
\label{prop:decoherence}
The $N{-}2$ angular Havelock modes decohere the
breathing mode~$\rho$ with decoherence rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decoherence-rate}
  \gamma = \frac{[\Phi'(\rho)]^2}{4}
  \sum_{m \ne m^*} \frac{1}{\lambda_m^2}.
\end{equation}
The reduced density matrix after tracing out the bath is
\[
  \varrho_{\mathrm{red}}(\rho, \rho')
  = \Psi_0(\rho)\,\Psi_0^*(\rho')\,e^{-\gamma(\rho-\rho')^2}.
\]
For $\gamma \gg 1$ the off-diagonal elements are
exponentially suppressed and $\varrho_{\mathrm{red}}$
is effectively diagonal:
$\varrho_{\mathrm{red}} \approx |\Psi_0(\rho)|^2\,
\delta(\rho - \rho')$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The CL influence functional for the Gaussian bath
with $\rho$-dependent frequencies $\omega_m(\rho)
= \sqrt{\lambda_m(\rho)}$ gives a decoherence kernel
$\exp(-\gamma(\rho{-}\rho')^2)$ with
$\gamma = \sum_m (g_m/\omega_m)^2$, where
$g_m = \partial\omega_m/\partial\rho
= \Phi'/(2\sqrt{\lambda_m})$ is the system-bath coupling.
Substituting: $\gamma = (\Phi')^2/4 \times
\sum 1/\lambda_m^2$.
For $N = 7$ (all modes stable on $\mathbf{H}^2$):
$\gamma \approx 7$--$12$ across the stable range.
For $N = 11$ at the stability threshold
$\rho^*_{11} \approx 0.88$ (the cosmologically relevant
operating point, where all $N{-}2 = 9$ bath modes
have $\lambda_m > 0$): $\gamma \gg 1$.
Near the threshold, the critical mode $m^*$ is excluded
from the bath (it is the ``system'' variable).
For even~$N$, $m^* = N/2$ is self-palindromic
($N - m^* = m^*$), so only one mode is excluded.
For odd~$N$, the palindromic pair $\{m^*, N{-}m^*\}$
share the eigenvalue $\lambda_{m^*}$;
excluding only~$m^*$ leaves the partner $N{-}m^*$
in the bath with $\lambda_{N-m^*} \to 0$,
so $1/\lambda^2 \to \infty$ and
$\gamma$ grows without bound as the threshold is
approached.
The polygon is therefore \emph{strongly decohered}
at the physical operating point: the soft bath mode
near the stability threshold drives $\gamma \gg 1$
structurally.

\emph{Irreversibility from near-integrability.}
The CMS angular bath is integrable \emph{in isolation}.
The coupling to the breathing mode~$\rho$ through the
$\rho$-dependent frequencies $\lambda_m(\rho)$
breaks exact integrability at $O(1/c)$
(the $W_N$ quartic coupling,
$\sim\!2\%$ at $N = 7$).
This converts exact Poincar\'e recurrences
(which would periodically restore coherence)
into diffusive mixing.
The Nekhoroshev theorem bounds the mixing
timescale from below:
$\tau_{\mathrm{mix}} \ge
\exp(c_0\,c^{a})$ for some $c_0 > 0$, $a > 0$
(with $\varepsilon = O(1/c)$ as the perturbation
parameter).
Even this exponentially long mixing time satisfies
$\tau_{\mathrm{mix}} \ll \tau_{\mathrm{tunnel}}
\sim e^{S_{\mathrm{BO}}}$,
provided $c_0\,c^a < S_{\mathrm{BO}}$
(for $c \approx 50$--$130$ and
$S_{\mathrm{BO}} \approx 38$--$103$, this is
satisfied for generic Nekhoroshev exponents
$a \le 1/2$).
A second bound comes from the Poincar\'e recurrence time
of the full $(N{-}2)$-mode system,
$\tau_{\mathrm{rec}} \sim \exp(O(N{-}2))
\sim e^5$--$e^9 \approx 150$--$8000$,
again $\ll \tau_{\mathrm{tunnel}}$.
The decoherence is therefore \emph{irreversible}
on all cosmologically relevant timescales.

This is consistent with the exact BO result
(Paper~VI, eq.~\eqref{VI-eq:no-nonadiabatic}):
the mode-independence of~$\Phi$ makes the BO
potential exact (no non-adiabatic transitions),
while the $O(1/c)$ $W_N$ quartic, which breaks the
exact CMS integrability, provides the mode mixing
needed for irreversible decoherence.
The two results operate at \emph{different orders in $1/c$}
and are not contradictory:
at $O(c^0)$, the CMS integrability gives exact BO separation
(Havelock eigenvectors are $\rho$-independent, the angular
modes decouple completely);
at $O(1/c)$, the $W_N$ quartic coupling breaks exact
integrability and provides the system-bath coupling needed
for decoherence.
The decoherence is a perturbative $O(1/c)$ correction to an
exactly decoupled $O(c^0)$ system, the standard situation in
atomic/molecular BO theory, where non-adiabatic corrections
at $O(m_e/m_p)$ allow energy transfer between electronic and
nuclear sectors without contradicting the BO separation.
\end{proof}

\emph{Implications.}
The CL decoherence selects the $\rho$-basis as the pointer basis
($\gamma \gg 1$ at the physical operating point),
with $|\Psi_0(\rho)|^2 = e^{-\beta V}/Z$
as the Boltzmann weight \citep{Zurek2003}.
The polygon decoheres \emph{itself}: no external
observer or environment is required.
The $N{-}2$ angular modes form a Caldeira--Leggett bath for
the breathing mode~$\rho$.
The four CL conditions hold:
(i)~harmonic bath (Havelock diagonalization, exact);
(ii)~uniform parametric coupling
($\partial\lambda_m/\partial\rho = \Phi'(\rho)$ for all~$m$);
(iii)~timescale separation
($\tau_{\mathrm{tunnel}} \sim e^{S_{\mathrm{BO}}} \gg \tau_m \sim O(1)$);
(iv)~FDT (automatic for Gaussian systems).
The resulting Onsager--Machlup action is
$S_{\mathrm{OM}} = \int[\tfrac{c}{2}\dot\rho^2 + V(\rho)]\,d\tau$,
where $\dot\rho^2$ is the noise kernel (not a kinetic energy).

The Markovian approximation requires
$\tau_{\mathrm{bath}} \ll \tau_{\mathrm{tunnel}}$.
The bath correlation time is
$\tau_{\mathrm{bath}} = 1/\omega_{\min}
= 1/\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}^{(\mathrm{bath})}}$
where $\lambda_{\min}^{(\mathrm{bath})}$ is the
smallest \emph{positive} eigenvalue among the bath modes
(excluding the marginal mode(s) $m^*$
and, for odd~$N$, its palindromic partner $N{-}m^*$).
At the palindromic threshold $C_1 = f(m^*,N)$:
$\lambda_{\min}^{(\mathrm{bath})}
= f(m^*,N) - f(m^*{-}1,N)
= (N{-}2m^*{+}1)/2$,
which equals $1$ (odd~$N$) or $1/2$ (even~$N$).
Thus $\tau_{\mathrm{bath}} \le \sqrt{2}$.
The tunneling time $\tau_{\mathrm{tunnel}}
\sim e^{S_{\mathrm{BO}}}$ with $S_{\mathrm{BO}} \gg 1$
(Paper~VI), so the separation
$\tau_{\mathrm{bath}}/\tau_{\mathrm{tunnel}}
= O(e^{-S_{\mathrm{BO}}})$ is exponentially sharp, and
non-Markovian corrections are $O(e^{-S_{\mathrm{BO}}})$;
the coefficient $c = 12\,b(N)$ is classical
(Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv-rr}).
The Feynman--Kac transfer matrix gives $H\Psi_0 = 0$
(the WDW equation, Proposition~\ref{prop:wdw-lambda}), with Born rule

\begin{derivation}[Quantum structure from polygon dynamics]
\label{thm:qm-from-polygons}
Starting from the classical Kirchhoff equations for
$N$ point vortices on~$\mathbf{H}^2$, with no quantum
postulate, the following quantum-mechanical structures
are derived:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(Q1)}] \emph{Hilbert space.}
  $\mathcal{H} = L^2(\mathbb{R},\,d\rho)$
  (Feynman--Kac, Proposition~\ref{prop:wdw-lambda}).
\item[\textup{(Q2)}] \emph{Hamiltonian.}
  $H = -\tfrac{1}{2c}\,d^2/d\rho^2 + V(\rho)$
  with $c = 12\,b(N)$ (CL mechanism $+$ orbifold matching).
\item[\textup{(Q3)}] \emph{Physical state.}
  The WDW constraint $H\Psi_0 = 0$ selects the ground
  state (the additive constant in~$V$ is fixed by
  $V(\rho^*) = 0$ at the BO equilibrium, giving
  $E_0 = 0$; excited states $E \ne 0$ do not satisfy
  the constraint).
\item[\textup{(Q4)}] \emph{Ground-state position distribution.}
  $|\Psi_0(\rho)|^2 = e^{-\beta V}/Z$
  (mathematical identity: the Boltzmann weight of the
  classical Onsager--Machlup path integral equals the
  quantum ground-state probability in the $\rho$-basis).
\item[\textup{(Q5)}] \emph{Pointer basis.}
  The angular bath decoheres~$\rho$ with
  $\gamma \gg 1$ near the stability threshold
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:decoherence}).
  The $\rho$-basis is the pointer basis.
\item[\textup{(Q6)}] \emph{Uncertainty principle.}
  $\Delta\rho\,\Delta p = 1/2$ (minimum-uncertainty
  ground state near the turning point;
  $\Delta\rho = 1/\!\sqrt{2c\omega}$,
  $\Delta p = \sqrt{c\omega/2}$,
  $\omega^2 = |V''(\rho_1)| = \mathrm{csch}^2(\rho_1)$).
\item[\textup{(Q7)}] \emph{Entanglement.}
  The entanglement entropy between~$\rho$ and
  the angular bath is $S_{\mathrm{ent}} \gg 1$~bit
  (from the CL squeezing parameters
  $r_m = (\Phi'/(2\lambda_m))^2$;
  see Code Availability).
\item[\textup{(Q8)}] \emph{Irreversibility.}
  The breathing-angular coupling breaks exact CMS
  integrability at $O(1/c)$, converting exact
  recurrences into diffusive mixing.
  The Nekhoroshev bound gives
  $\tau_{\mathrm{mix}} \ge \exp(c_0 c^a)$;
  even this exponentially long mixing time satisfies
  $\tau_{\mathrm{mix}} \ll \tau_{\mathrm{tunnel}}
  \sim e^{S_{\mathrm{BO}}}$ for generic exponents.
  Decoherence is irreversible on all cosmologically
  relevant timescales.
\end{enumerate}
Within the WDW framework ($H\Psi = 0$, unique state,
no external time), items \textup{(Q1)--(Q5)} are derived
from the Feynman--Kac correspondence; items
\textup{(Q6)--(Q8)} are standard consequences of the
WDW Hamiltonian structure (not specific to the polygon
system).
\end{derivation}

\begin{proof}
Items (Q1)--(Q4) follow from the five-step chain above
(Steps~1--5: Havelock $\to$ CL $\to$ Onsager--Machlup
$\to$ Feynman--Kac $\to$ WDW).
Item (Q5) is Proposition~\ref{prop:decoherence} with the
mode-mixing irreversibility.
Item (Q6): the WDW ground state near the turning point
$\rho_1$ is $\Psi_0 \propto
\exp(-c\omega(\rho{-}\rho_1)^2/2)$
with $\omega^2 = |V''(\rho_1)| = \mathrm{csch}^2(\rho_1)$;
the widths $\Delta\rho = 1/\!\sqrt{2c\omega}$ and
$\Delta p = \sqrt{c\omega/2}$ give
$\Delta\rho\,\Delta p = 1/2$ (minimum uncertainty).
Item (Q7): the CL influence functional at zero temperature
gives squeezing parameter
$r_m = (\Phi'/(2\lambda_m))^2$ for each bath mode;
the entanglement entropy is
$S_{\mathrm{ent}} = \sum_m [(r_m{+}1)\ln(r_m{+}1)
- r_m\ln r_m]$.
Item (Q8): the $O(1/c)$ $W_N$ quartic coupling
breaks exact CMS integrability; the Nekhoroshev
bound gives $\tau_{\mathrm{mix}} \ge \exp(c_0 c^a)$,
and $\exp(c_0 c^a) \ll e^{S_{\mathrm{BO}}}$
for generic exponents $a \le 1/2$ at
$c \approx 50$--$130$, $S_{\mathrm{BO}} \ge 38$.
\end{proof}
